ABSTRACT
Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease driven by oxidative stress, necessitating advanced antioxidant nanozymes capable
of broad-spectrum reactive oxygen species (ROS) elimination. Here, we address this challenge through the rational design
of a ternary synergistic nanocluster, Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP, engineered via “atomic engineering” of an optimal AgAu alloy core
and “molecular engineering” via Fe‑porphyrin ligand grafting. This dual-level engineering creates a metal–ligand synergistic
interface that confers excellent multi-enzyme mimetic activity for scavenging H2O2, ∙O2
−, and ∙OH. Coupled with enhanced skin
penetration, the nanozyme demonstrates both therapeutic and preventive efficacy in a psoriasis mouse model, rivaling the first-
line clinical drug betamethasone/calcipotriol, without observable systemic toxicity. Transcriptomic profiling reveals that its potent
effect originates from a dual-pathway immunomodulation: simultaneous suppression of the pro-inflammatory IL‑23/Th17/IL‑17
and NF‑κB axes and enhancement of the anti-inflammatory Treg/Th2 pathways. This work establishes a generalizable “precision
synergy” paradigm for designing high-performance nanocluster-based therapeutics against oxidative stress-related inflammatory
diseases.
1
Introduction
The pursuit of effective and safe therapies for chronic inflam-
matory diseases like psoriasis remains a formidable challenge in
modern medicine [1, 2]. Affecting millions worldwide, psoriasis is
driven by a self-perpetuating cycle of oxidative stress and aberrant
immune activation, characterized by elevated levels of reactive
oxygen species (ROS) that fuel pathogenic signaling pathways
[3], including NF-κB, interleukin-17 (IL-17), IL-23, and T helper
17 (Th17) cells [4, 5]. Current clinical management primarily
includes topical treatments (e.g., corticosteroids and vitamin
D3 analogs), phototherapy, and systemic immunosuppressants
(e.g., methotrexate and cyclosporine) [6]. While conventional
treatments and biologics offer symptomatic relief, they often
fall short of addressing the root oxidative microenvironment
and carry significant limitations, including systemic toxicity,
high cost, and compromised long-term efficacy. This critical gap
underscores an urgent need for innovative strategies capable of
precisely disrupting the disease at its molecular origin.

pathological oxidative stress [7, 8]. However, the vast majority of
reported nanozymes lack atomic-level precision in their structure
and composition. This fundamental ambiguity obscures clear
structure-activity relationships and mechanistic understanding,
thereby impeding the rational design of high-performance,
disease-targeted antioxidant agents. The field thus stands at a
pivotal juncture: transitioning from empirical material discovery
to a paradigm of precision nanozyme engineering, where
function is dictated by exact atomic composition and tailored
surface chemistry.
Atomically precise metal nanoclusters (NCs) present an ideal
platform for this paradigm shift [9–15]. Their well-defined struc-
tures offer an unprecedented opportunity to decode, at the
atomic and molecular scale [16–22], how specific parameters—
such as the type [23–27], number [28–31], and arrangement of
metal atoms in the core [32–35], coupled with the chemical
nature and spatial presentation of surface ligands [36–42]—
collectively orchestrate catalytic performance. Yet, harnessing
this potential to create synergistic, multi-enzyme systems for
complex diseases requires a deliberate strategy that moves beyond
single-component tuning.
Herein, we demonstrate a “precision synergy” strategy by
constructing a ternary Ag/Au/Fe nanozyme through the con-
certed engineering of both the metallic core and the organic-
inorganic interface. We first establish an atomically precise
bimetallic library, Ag25−xAux (x = 1–25), identifying the optimal
Au-doping level that maximizes intrinsic superoxide dismu-
tase (SOD)- and catalase (CAT)-like activities. This atomically
engineered core is then functionally augmented via molecular
engineering, through the covalent grafting of a Fe-porphyrin
(FeTP) complex, a proficient peroxidase mimic, onto its sur-
face ligands (Figure 1). This rational design creates a coop-
erative ternary (Ag/Au/Fe) catalytic center, enabling broad-
spectrum and potent ROS elimination that significantly sur-
passes the performance of any single-metal or binary coun-
terparts. When applied to an imiquimod (IMQ)-induced psori-
asis model, this precision-engineered nanozyme demonstrates
remarkable preventive and therapeutic efficacy, rivaling that
of a first-line clinical drug betamethasone/calcipotriol (BC). A
comprehensive transcriptomic analysis reveals its dual-pathway
mechanism: simultaneously suppressing the pro-inflammatory
IL-23/Th17/IL-17 and NF-κB axes while enhancing the anti-
inflammatory Treg/Th2 pathways. This work achieves two
pivotal advances: (1) it presents a novel and highly effective
nanotherapeutic agent for psoriasis, validated through deep
mechanistic insight; and (2) it establishes a generalizable “core
doping–ligand synergy” blueprint for the rational design of
next-generation, multifunctional nanozymes, moving the field
from serendipitous discovery toward true atomic-level precision
medicine.
2
Results and Discussion
2.1
Synthesis and Characterization of Ternary
Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP NCs
To precisely investigate the influence of metal composition on
the ROS-scavenging capability of NCs, we first designed and
synthesized a library of atomically precise, 6-mercaptohexanoic
acid (MHA)-protected bimetallic NCs with the general formula
Ag25−xAux (x = 1–25). This series maintains the characteristic
M25(SR)18 structure—an M13 icosahedral core surrounded by
six dimeric [M2(SR)3] motifs (M denotes metal, i.e., Au and
Ag; Figure 2a)—ensuring that any variation in catalytic per-
formance stems primarily from changes in electronic structure
due to Au doping, rather than from gross structural differ-
ences. Comprehensive characterization by UV-vis absorption
spectroscopy, electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-
MS), and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed
the successful synthesis of ultrasmall, monodisperse NCs and
identified five major compositional families: Ag25, Ag24Au1,
Ag14–20Au5–11, Ag6–10Au15–19, and Ag0–2Au23–25 (Figures S1–S5, Sup-
porting Information S1: Note I).
Preliminary evaluation of antioxidant capacity (detailed in Sec-
tion 2.2) identified the Ag14–20Au5–11 composition as possessing the
most potent overall ROS-scavenging activity, prompting its selec-
tion for further functionalization and study. The Ag14–20Au5–11
NCs exhibited a light-brown color in solution (Figure 2b, inset)
and displayed characteristic molecular-like UV-vis absorption
peaks at approximately 330, 463, and 598 nm (Figure 2b).
The peak at ∼330 nm is attributed to ligand-to-metal charge
transfer (LMCT) within the [M2(SR)3] shell. The peaks at
∼463 nm and ∼598 nm correspond to core-based electronic tran-
sitions (HOMO–1→LUMO and HOMO→LUMO, respectively)
[43]. Notably, these core-level transitions are significantly blue-
shifted compared to those of the homometallic Ag25 NCs (485
and 660 nm, Figure S1a), a direct spectroscopic signature of
the electronic perturbation induced by the incorporation of Au
atoms into the Ag13 core. The similarity of these transitions
to those of the well-defined Ag24Au1 NC (with a central Au
atom, Figure S2a) strongly suggests the presence of a central
Au dopant within the M13 core of Ag14–20Au5–11 [44]. A detailed
analysis of Au doping sites, based on tandem mass spectrometry
(MS/MS) fragmentation patterns (Figure 2c,d), is provided in the
Supporting Information (Supplementary Note II). This analysis
confirms a non-random distribution of Au atoms across the
core and shell, a key factor underlying the optimized electronic
structure.
To construct a ternary synergistic system, we grafted a Fe-
porphyrin complex, 5,10,15,20-tetrakis(4-aminophenyl)porphyrin
iron(III) chloride (FeTP), onto the surface of the Ag14–20Au5–11
NCs
via
1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide/N-
hydroxysuccinimide (EDC/NHS) coupling chemistry, yielding
the composite Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP. Successful conjugation was
confirmed by multiple techniques. The solution color deepened
to dark brown (Figure 2b, inset), and the UV-vis spectrum showed
the emergence of characteristic FeTP absorptions (Figure 2b and
Figure S6): a strong Soret band at 420 nm and weaker Q bands
at 585 and 626 nm [45, 46]. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR)
spectroscopy provided direct evidence for the formation of amide
bonds between the carboxyl groups of MHA and the amino
groups of FeTP (Figure S7). Transmission electron microscopy
(TEM) revealed an increase in the average particle size from
<2 nm for the pristine NCs to 3.2 nm for Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP
(Figure 2e, Figure S3b), attributable to the covalent linkage of
multiple NCs by the tetra-functional FeTP molecule, leading to
controlled aggregation.

the alloyed core and the grafted FeTP, we performed X-ray photo-

observed after FeTP grafting, concomitant with a negative shift in
the Fe 2p signal compared to free FeTP (Figure S8). This reciprocal
shift provides direct spectroscopic evidence for electron transfer
from the Ag/Au bimetallic core to the Fe center of the surface-
grafted porphyrin. This interfacial electronic coupling is central to
the proposed ternary synergy, which we hypothesize will enhance
multi-enzyme catalytic activity for ROS elimination.
2.2
Enzyme-Mimetic Activities and
ROS-Scavenging Performance of Ag14–20Au5-1@FeTP
A critical design goal for an effective anti-psoriatic nanomedicine
is the ability to broadly neutralize the key ROS—namely, hydroxyl
radicals (∙OH), superoxide anions (∙O2
−), and hydrogen peroxide
(H2O2)—that drive oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling
in the disease microenvironment [7, 47, 48]. We therefore system-
atically evaluated the multi-enzymatic antioxidant profile of our
engineered NCs, beginning with an assessment of their overall
radical-scavenging capacity (Figure 3a).
The total antioxidant capacity, measured via the 2,2’-Azino-bis(3-
ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS+) assay kit, revealed
a clear composition-dependent trend across the AgxAu25−x library.
While all bimetallic NCs reduced ABTS+ radical formation com-
pared to the PBS control, the Ag14–20Au5–11 composition demon-
strated the most pronounced effect, indicating the strongest
intrinsic antioxidant activity within the series (Figure 3b).
Quantification against a Trolox standard further confirmed that
Ag14–20Au5–11 NCs possessed an activity 1.51 times higher than that
of Trolox, the highest among all AgxAu25−x variants (Figure 3c,
Figure S9). This superior performance justified its selection
as the optimal core for subsequent functionalization. Notably,
grafting FeTP onto this core (Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP) led to a further
enhancement in ABTS+ scavenging, suggesting an emergent
synergistic effect between the alloyed core and the surface
metalloporphyrin.
We then deconvoluted this overall capacity into specific enzy-
matic activities. First, the potent ⋅OH scavenging ability was
probed by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy

The experimental results of ⋅OH and ∙O2

relationship: moderate Au doping optimizes ⋅OH scavenging
via localized electronic effects, while higher Ag content maxi-

The Ag14–20Au5–11 composition thus represents a strategically
balanced platform that concurrently addresses both critical path-
ways, which explains why Ag14–20Au5–11 NCs demonstrate the
best overall antioxidant performance.
Finally, we evaluated the CAT-like activity, that is, the decom-
position of H2O2 into H2O and O2 (Figure 3a). Pristine Ag14–
20Au5–11 NCs exhibited negligible catalytic activity toward H2O2
decomposition. In contrast, the Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP composite
displayed a dramatic 44-fold enhancement in activity (Figure 3i,j),
confirming that FeTP serves as the primary contributor to the
CAT-like performance. Quantitative analysis based on the CAT-
like activities of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP and free FeTP (Figure S12)
revealed that FeTP accounts for approximately 73.09% of the
total activity, unambiguously demonstrating a synergistic effect
between the alloyed core and the surface FeTP. This synergy is
driven by the enhanced electron transfer from the Ag/Au core to
the Fe center, as evidenced by XPS (Figure 2g,h), which optimizes
the redox cycling crucial for H2O2 decomposition.
The grafting of FeTP reshapes the ROS‑scavenging profile of
Ag14–20Au5–11 through functional redistribution. FeTP acts as a
potent molecular catalyst that directly enhances CAT‑like and
⋅OH‑scavenging activities, while further synergizing with the
alloy core via electron transfer to amplify overall antioxidant
performance. Conversely, this electron transfer mildly attenu-
ates the electron‑donating capacity of the alloy core, to which
SOD‑like activity is highly sensitive, resulting in a slight decrease
in that function. This trade‑off underscores that the “ternary
synergy” achieved is not a simple additive effect, but rather
the outcome of precise interfacial electronic engineering that
optimally allocates catalytic roles: the Ag/Au core specializes in
electron donation and O2
−⋅disproportionation, while the FeTP
sites excel in H2O2 decomposition and ∙OH scavenging. Together,
they enable broad‑spectrum, efficient clearance of complex ROS
networks.
Thus, the Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP nanozyme integrates superior
∙OH scavenging, considerable SOD‑like, and exceptionally
potent CAT‑like activities into a single agent. This compre-
hensive ROS‑elimination capacity originates from our precise
“core‑doping–ligand‑engineering” strategy, which independently
optimizes electronic properties at the atomic (via Au doping) and
molecular (via FeTP grafting) levels to synergistically target differ-
ent ROS species. This work establishes a foundational blueprint
for the rational design of high‑performance, multifunctional
antioxidant nanomaterials.
2.3
In Vitro Antioxidant and Anti-Inflammatory
Evaluation
To translate the promising enzymatic activities into a biologically
relevant context, we systematically evaluated the antioxidant and
anti-inflammatory functions of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP in psoriasi-
form cell models (Figure 4a). A prerequisite for any therapeutic
nanomaterial is biocompatibility. Cytotoxicity assays revealed
that Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP exhibited excellent safety profiles, main-
taining cell viability above 80% for both human keratinocyte
(HaCaT) and normal human epidermal keratinocyte (NHEK)
cells after 24-hour incubation at concentrations up to 1 mM
(Figure S13).
We next established an inflammatory microenvironment by
stimulating cells with the pro-psoriatic “M5” cytokine cock-
tail (IL-22, IL-17A, IL-1α, TNF-α, and oncostatin M), which is
known to induce ROS overproduction. Using the fluorescent
probe 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein (DCFH) and flow cytom-
etry, we quantified intracellular ROS levels. As anticipated,
M5 stimulation caused a marked increase in ROS. Treatment
with Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP, however, effectively mitigated this
oxidative burst, reducing intracellular ROS by 41.6% in HaCaT
cells and 35.7% in NHEK cells compared to the M5-only group
(Figures 4b,c, Figures S14,S15).
Excessive ROS drives the pathological hyperproliferation of ker-
atinocytes and the secretion of inflammatory mediators [49]. We
therefore investigated whether ROS reduction by our nanozyme
could normalize these disease phenotypes. Confocal laser scan-
ning microscopy (CLSM) and direct cell counting confirmed that
M5 stimulation induced significant cell hyperproliferation, which
was dramatically suppressed by Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP treatment
(cell counts reduced by 39.2% in HaCaT and 67.3% in NHEK cells)
(Figure 4d, Figure S16).
To elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying this pheno-
typic rescue, we analyzed the expression of key pro-inflammatory
cytokines via RT-qPCR. Consistent with the observed functional
improvements, Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP treatment led to a significant
downregulation of M5-induced inflammatory gene expression
(Figure 4e,f). In both cell types, mRNA levels of TNF-α, IL-6, IL-
8, and IL-1β were substantially reduced, with decreases ranging
from approximately 48% to 72%. This coordinated suppression
of a broad inflammatory signature underscores the potent anti-
inflammatory action of the nanozyme at the transcriptional
level.
Moreover, other NCs in the series also demonstrated varying
degrees of ROS scavenging (Figure S17) and anti-inflammatory
activities in cellular models (Figure S18), with their efficacy trends
remaining consistent with the overall antioxidant capacities
observed in aqueous solution. These collective cellular studies
validate Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP as a biocompatible and highly effec-
tive agent capable of mitigating the core pathological features of
psoriasis in vitro, including oxidative stress, aberrant keratinocyte
proliferation, and pro-inflammatory signaling. Together, these
findings establish a robust cellular foundation to support its
further evaluation as a therapeutic candidate in vivo.

sequently, these results demonstrate that Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP
is not only capable of penetrating the skin but does so with
enhanced efficiency under psoriatic conditions—a critical feature
enabled by its rationally engineered surface chemistry. Fur-
thermore, compared with previously reported nanozymes, the
Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP described in this work exhibits significant
advantages in key parameters relevant to psoriasis treatment (as
summarized in Table S1), including an ultrasmall size that facili-
tates effective skin penetration, broad‑spectrum ROS‑scavenging
capability, and excellent biocompatibility, highlighting its promis-
ing potential for psoriasis therapy.
2.5
Preventive Efficacy and Immunomodulatory
Mechanism in a Psoriasis Mouse Model
Given the high recurrence rate of psoriasis up to 90%, effective
prevention is a crucial therapeutic goal. We therefore first evalu-
ated the preventive potential of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP in an IMQ-
induced mouse model (Figure 5a). Mice were divided into three
groups: Healthy, IMQ (model), and IMQ + Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP
(prevention). Over six days of IMQ challenge, the dorsal skin of
model mice progressively developed hallmark psoriatic features:
pronounced erythema, silvery scaling, and visible thickening. In
stark contrast, mice co-treated with Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP exhibited
minimal symptoms, maintaining skin that was thin, smooth,
and visually comparable to the Healthy group (Figure 5b). This
dramatic phenotypic improvement was quantified using the Pso-
riasis Area and Severity Index (PASI). The Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP
group showed significantly lower total PASI scores (Figure 5c),
with substantial reductions in all sub-scores—erythema, scaling,
and induration—confirming effective prevention of disease onset
(Figure 5d–f).
Histological analysis of skin tissues harvested on day 7 provided
deeper insight. Compared to the thin, well-organized epidermis
of healthy mice (∼23 µm), IMQ-treated mice exhibited severe
psoriasiform pathology: massive epidermal hyperplasia (up to
88.4 µm), an irregular dermal-epidermal junction, and marked
inflammatory cell infiltration (Figure 5g, Figure S21). In contrast,
preventive treatment with Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP nearly normalized
the skin architecture, reducing epidermal thickness to ∼31 µm,
restoring a clear dermal-epidermal structure, and suppressing
inflammatory infiltration.
Psoriasis pathogenesis is driven by aberrant Th cell responses
[51–53]. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) revealed that IMQ induc-
tion led to substantial dermal infiltration of CD4+ T cells and
upregulated expression of the Th1 cytokine IFN-γ and phos-
phorylated NF-κB p65 (a central inflammatory transcription
factor) (Figure 5g, Figures S22–S24). Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP treat-
ment effectively reversed these changes. To delineate the specific
Th subsets involved, we analyzed chemokine receptor profiles,
which serve as homing markers for different T-cell lineages.
Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP administration significantly reduced the
expression of CCR5 (Th1-associated) and CCR6 (Th17-associated),
while upregulating CCR3 (Th2-associated), indicating a shift in
the immune landscape from a pro-inflammatory Th1/Th17 bias
toward a more regulatory Th2 phenotype (Figure 5h).
Consistent with this cellular shift, we next examined the expres-
sion of corresponding cytokines (Figure 5i). Compared with the
normal group, IMQ-treated mice showed pronounced upregula-
tion of Th1-related cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β) and Th17-related
cytokines (IL-17 and IL-23). Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP intervention sig-
nificantly reversed these increases. Conversely, levels of the Th2
anti-inflammatory cytokines IL-4 and IL-10 were elevated, con-
firming the functional outcome of the altered T-cell landscape.
In summary, Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP restores immune homeostasis
in psoriatic lesions by dually suppressing pro-inflammatory
Th1/Th17 responses (TNF-α, IL-17, IL-23) and promoting anti-
inflammatory Th2 responses (IL-4, IL-10), thereby alleviating
both immune dysregulation and oxidative stress.

3
Conclusion
In summary, this work demonstrates the rational design and
therapeutic efficacy of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP, a ternary synergistic
nanozyme engineered for psoriasis management via complemen-
tary atomic-scale Au doping and molecular-scale Fe-porphyrin
grafting. This “precision synergy” strategy optimizes the elec-
tronic structure and catalytic interface, endowing the nanocluster
with superior, broad-spectrum ROS-scavenging activity that
mimics multiple antioxidant enzymes. The nanozyme exhibits
excellent skin penetration and achieves both preventive and
therapeutic outcomes in an IMQ-induced psoriasis model, with
efficacy rivaling a first-line clinical drug. Integrated transcrip-
tomic and mechanistic studies reveal that its therapeutic action
stems from a dual-pathway immunomodulation: suppressing
the pro-inflammatory IL-23/Th17/IL-17 and NF-κB axes while
enhancing the anti-inflammatory Treg/Th2 pathways, thereby
normalizing keratinocyte hyperproliferation and restoring skin
barrier integrity. Importantly, Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP shows an
excellent safety profile with no observable systemic toxicity.
Beyond presenting a potent and safe nanotherapeutic candi-
date, this study establishes a generalizable design paradigm
for developing high-performance nanozymes against oxidative
stress-driven inflammatory diseases.

Chemicals and Materials

4.2
Instrumentation
The spectral absorption properties of different samples were
investigated using a Shimadzu UV-1800 spectrophotometer. The
sizes of various samples were examined via transmission electron

(XPS) analysis for elemental valence states was conducted on a
Kratos AXIS SUPRA spectrometer using Al Kα radiation, with

carried out on a NETZSCH TG 209 F1 instrument. Electron
paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy for analyzing the
scavenging of superoxide anion (⋅O2
−) and hydroxyl radical (⋅OH)

4.3
Synthesis of AgxAu25–x(MHA)18 NCs
Taking Ag25(MHA)18 NCs as an example: First, 2.35 mL of
ultrapure water was added to a 20 mL flask. Aqueous solutions
of MHA (5 mM, 2 mL) and AgNO3 (20 mM, 0.25 mL) were
sequentially added to the flask, resulting in the formation of
a white precipitate of Ag(I)-MHA complexes. Subsequently, an
aqueous NaOH solution (1 M, 0.3 mL) was added, causing
the white precipitate to gradually dissolve. Aqueous NaBH4
solution (112 mM, 0.1 mL) was then added dropwise. The mixture
was transferred to an oil bath and stirred at 50◦C for 4 h.
The Ag25(MHA)18 NCs solution was collected and purified via
ultrafiltration using a membrane with a molecular weight cut-
off (MWCO) of 3 kDa. The ultrafiltration purification step was
repeated three times. When the Ag+/Au3+ molar ratios were 24/1,
17/8, 8/17, and 1/24, the synthesized AgxAu25–x(MHA)18 NCs had
x values of approximately 24, 14–20, 6–10, and 0–2, respectively.
The synthesis conditions differed slightly: Ag14–20Au5–11 (MHA)18
NCs required stirring at 50◦C for 1 h, while Ag6-10Au15-19(MHA)18
NCs and Ag0-2Au23-25(MHA)18 NCs were synthesized by stirring
at room temperature for 3 h. The aforementioned ultrafiltra-
tion purification steps were repeated for all samples, followed
by reconstitution in an appropriate volume for later use. (All
synthesis conditions were optimized.)
4.4
Synthesis of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP
The ultrafiltration-purified Ag14–20Au5–11 NCs (5 mM, 10 mL) were
added to a flask. The pH of the NCs’ solution was adjusted to
10 using HCl solution (1 M). Subsequently, 34.4 mg of EDC solid
and 12.4 mg of NHS solid were added, and the pH was readjusted
to 7. The mixture was stirred at room temperature for 10 min
to activate the carboxyl groups. Then, 33 mg of FeTP dissolved
in DMF was slowly added to the flask. The reaction proceeded
under stirring at room temperature for 5 h. The resulting product
solution was collected and dialyzed for 24 h using a dialysis bag
with an MWCO of 3 kDa, followed by reconstitution for later use.

∙O2

4.9
CAT-Like Activity Assay
This assay is based on the characteristic absorption of H2O2
at 240 nm. The decomposition rate of H2O2 was quantified by
monitoring the absorbance change at this wavelength. A H2O2
solution of known concentration was added to the reaction
system along with different NCs as catalysts, and the reaction
proceeded at room temperature. Absorbance at 240 nm was
measured at regular time intervals. The concentration (c) of H2O2
was calculated using the Beer-Lambert law (A = ε⋅c⋅l, where A is
absorbance, ε is the molar attenuation coefficient, and l is the path
length). The CAT-like activity of each NC was calculated based on
the consumption of H2O2 per unit time.
4.10
Cytotoxicity Assay (MTT Assay)
The cytotoxicity of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP was evaluated using
the 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide
(MTT) assay on human immortalized keratinocytes (HaCaT) and
normal human epidermal keratinocytes (NHEK). Cells in the
exponential growth phase were seeded in 96-well plates at a
density of 1 × 104 cells per well in 100 µL of culture medium
and incubated for 24 h at 37◦C with 5% CO2 to allow adherence.
The medium was then aspirated. For the experimental groups,
the NCs were tested at final concentrations of 0.25, 0.5, 0.75,
and 1 mM, with three replicate wells per concentration. A
negative control group (untreated cells) and a positive control

concentration 0.5 mg/mL) were added to each well. The plate was
incubated for 4 h at 37◦C with 5% CO2, allowing mitochondrial
dehydrogenases to reduce MTT to purple formazan crystals. The
medium was then aspirated, and 100 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide
(DMSO) was added to each well to dissolve the crystals, followed
by gentle shaking for 10 min. The absorbance of each well was
measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader, with a reference
wavelength of 630 nm. Cytotoxicity was calculated based on the
results.
4.11
Intracellular ROS Scavenging
To simulate psoriatic conditions, HaCaT and NHEK cells were
used. HaCaT cells were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supple-
mented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin,
and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37◦C with 5% CO2. NHEK cells
were cultured in CM8-1 medium (90% EMEM + 10% FBS). Cells
were then co-cultured with an “M5” cytokine cocktail for 48 h
to induce inflammation and increase intracellular ROS levels.
The M5 cocktail consisted of IL-22, IL-17A, IL-1α, TNF-α, and
oncostatin M, each at a final concentration of 10 ng/mL. For
the experimental group, cells were co-treated with the NCs
(2.5 µg/mL) for the final 24 h of the 48-h M5 stimulation period.
A blank control (no M5 induction) and an M5-only control (no
NCs) were included. DCFH-DA was diluted to 15 µM in serum-
free medium. After the treatments, the cell culture medium was
removed, and cells were gently washed twice with pre-warmed
PBS. The DCFH-DA working solution was added to cover the
cells, followed by incubation at 37◦C in the dark for 30 min.
Cells were then harvested (adherent cells were trypsinized and
centrifuged at 1200 rpm for 5 min; suspension cells were directly
centrifuged) and resuspended in PBS. Flow cytometry was per-
formed with excitation at 488 nm and emission at 530 nm.
DCFH-DA, a non-fluorescent probe, crosses the cell membrane
and is hydrolyzed by intracellular esterases to DCFH (non-
fluorescent). DCFH is oxidized by ROS (e.g., H2O2, ⋅OH, ⋅O2
−)
to fluorescent DCF, whose intensity correlates with ROS levels.
Thus, the ability of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP to scavenge intracellular
ROS was quantified. At least 10,000 events were collected per
sample, and data were analyzed using FlowJo software.

4.14
Psoriasis Mouse Model Induction
All animal experiments were conducted in strict accordance with
the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals
(NIH Publication No. 85-23, revised 1985) and were approved
by the Biomedical Ethics Committees of Qingdao University
of Science and Technology and Anhui University. Eight-Toten-
week-old female C57BL/6 mice were used for the experiments.
The mice were housed under standard specific pathogen-free
(SPF) conditions at a constant temperature (22 ± 2◦C) and
humidity (50 ± 10%) with a 12-hour light/dark cycle. Six-week-
old female Balb/c mice were used. Psoriasis-like dermatitis was
induced using a commercially available imiquimod (IMQ) cream.
4.15
Psoriasis Prevention Experiment
On day 0, the dorsal hair of the mice was shaved. Mice were
randomly divided into three groups: Healthy control, IMQ group,
and Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP group. The Healthy group received no
treatment. The IMQ group received a daily topical application of
62.5 mg of IMQ cream on the shaved dorsal area (3 cm × 3 cm).
The Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP group received the same IMQ applica-
tion, followed by topical application of Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP 2 h
later for prevention. The development of psoriatic symptoms was
observed over one week.
4.16
Psoriasis Treatment Experiment
On day 0, mice were shaved and randomly divided into four
groups: Healthy control, IMQ group, Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP treat-
ment group, and BC (positive control) group. Starting from day
0, all mice except the Healthy group received a daily topical
application of 62.5 mg of IMQ cream on the shaved dorsal area
(3 cm × 3 cm) for six consecutive days. By day 7, successful model
induction was confirmed by the appearance of symptoms like
skin thickening, erythema, and scaling. Treatment commenced
on day 7.
4.17
Psoriasis Assessment (PASI Scoring)
In both prevention and treatment experiments, the therapeutic
effect was evaluated daily by assessing the PASI and recording
body weight. The PASI score evaluates three aspects: skin thick-
ness, erythema size, and scaling. Each is scored from 0 to 4:0 = no
symptoms; 0–1 = mild; 1–2 = moderate; 2–3 = severe; 3–4 = very
severe.
4.18
Histological Analysis
Skin tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, washed
under running water to remove residual fixative, and dehydrated
through a graded ethanol series (70% →80% →95% →absolute
ethanol). Tissues were cleared in xylene and embedded in paraf-
fin blocks for sectioning and microscopic analysis. Major organs
(heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney) collected post-euthanasia were
also processed for H&E staining. For staining, sections were
deparaffinized in xylene (I, II, III; 10 min each), rehydrated,
stained with hematoxylin, differentiated, rinsed, counterstained
with eosin, dehydrated, cleared in xylene, and mounted for
examination.
For IHC staining, three different markers (CD4, IFN-γ, NF-
κB p65) were used. Briefly, antigen retrieval was performed by
heating slides in a microwave at 95–100◦C for 3 min, followed
by 20 min at lower power. After cooling, slides were washed
in PBS for 5 min, blocked with 2.5% normal horse serum
(Vector Laboratories) at room temperature for 2 h, and incubated
overnight at 4◦C with primary antibodies diluted in blocking
serum (2.5 µg/mL for CD4, IFN-γ, NF-κB p65). After PBS washes,
slides were treated with 3% H2O2 for 10 min, washed again,
and incubated with appropriate secondary antibodies for 30 min.
Color development was performed using 3,3’-diaminobenzidine
(DAB) substrate for 4 min, followed by counterstaining with
hematoxylin for 2 min, dehydration, clearing in xylene, and
mounting. (Note: All antibodies were used at a 1:200 dilution.)

ELISA for Cytokine Measurement
ELISA kits were used to quantify the expression of cytokines
IL-6, IL-17, TNF-α, IL-23, and IL-1β. Briefly, all reagents were

4.21
Skin Uptake Experiment
Healthy Balb/c mice were shaved on day 0 and randomly divided
into Healthy and IMQ groups. The IMQ group received daily
topical application of IMQ cream on the shaved back for one
week to establish the psoriasis model. On day 7, all mice were
reshaved to ensure identical dorsal skin conditions. Gauze pads
(2.5 cm × 2.5 cm) soaked with 200 µL of 500 ppm solutions of
either Ag14–20Au5–11 NCs or Ag14–20Au5–11@FeTP were applied to
the backs of healthy and model mice, respectively. The gauze was
secured with medical adhesive tape and remained in place for
24 h. After removal, the skin was gently wiped with saline-soaked
cotton. Mice were euthanized, and skin tissue of equal size (2 cm
× 2 cm) was collected. The tissue was digested with sufficient aqua
regia (HCl: HNO3 = 3:1 by volume), followed by dilution with a
matrix solution (2% HCl and 2% HNO3). An internal standard of
10 ppb Indium (In) was added. The digestate was filtered through
a 0.22 µm syringe filter and analyzed by inductively coupled
plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS).
4.22
Statistical Analysis
Prior to statistical analysis, no data transformation was applied
and no outliers were excluded. All data were expressed as mean
± standard deviation (SD). The sample size for each group was
n = 3, as indicated in the corresponding figure captions. Differ-
ences among multiple groups were analyzed using an ordinary
one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), while comparisons
between two groups were performed using a two-tailed Student’s
t-test using OriginPro 2025 Learning Edition software. A p
value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical
significance was denoted as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001,
and ****p < 0.0001.
