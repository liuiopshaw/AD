Abstract
Dendrimers have proven to be effective for drug delivery and their biodisposition varies with change on their surface, generation and core. In an
effort to understand the role of critical nanoscale design parameters, we developed a novel hybrid dendrimer approach to harness unique features of
individual dendrimers and create a nano-assembly. We report an easy in situ method of creating hybrid dendrimer nano-assembly by mixing G4.0
PAMAM (–NH2) and G3.5 PAMAM (–COONa) dendrimers with a chemotherapeutic drug docetaxel (DTX). Zeta potential, HR-TEM, 1H-NMR
proved the formation of nano-assembly. In vitro dissolution, release studies revealed pH dependent dissolution and sustained drug release. Cellular
uptake, cytotoxicity, and flow cytometric analysis in human/mouse glioblastoma cells indicated the effectiveness of hybrid dendrimers. The oral
administration of the hybrid dendrimers showed pharmacokinetic equivalence to intravenous injection of commercially available Taxotere®.
Hybrid dendrimer concept provides much needed fine-tuning to create multistage next-generation dendritic platform in nanomedicine.

Dendritic polymers are the fourth class of polymers.1,2
Dendrimers (a form of dendritic polymer) have proven to be
effective as a drug delivery system. Dendrimer-based systems
have gained significant attention due to their unique nanoscale
properties and specific multifunctional ability to enhance
solubility, stability, oral bioavailability and drug targeting of
various drugs.3–8 In the last two decades, researchers (including
our group) actively reported various new applications of
dendrimers in different pharmaceutical, in vitro diagnostic,
agrochemical and cosmetic fields.3–7,9–14 There were also
concerns raised on the safety, excretion and biocompatibility
of dendrimers.15
To understand the behavior of dendrimer family, we are
investigating the role of critical nanoscale design parameters
(CNDP's) on the performance of dendrimers for drug
delivery.9,16 In this process; we have developed a novel
hybrid dendrimer approach, as a next generation dendritic
platform. Hybrid dendrimers were prepared by in situ mixing
of two different types of dendrimers. The purpose is to
harness unique features of individual dendrimers and create a
nano-assembly. Earlier, we filed a patent on a novel concept
of hybrid dendrimers comprising a mixture of two dendritic
polymers.11 We have demonstrated that dissolution and
release profile of drugs can be controlled by tuning dendrimer
to dendrimer ratio in an assembly. As a next step, we now
report a novel in situ method of creating dendrimer nano-
assembly with simultaneous incorporation of DTX. We
demonstrated the in-vivo potential of the dendrimer nano-
assembly for oral chemotherapeutic delivery of DTX.
DTX, a semi-synthetic analog of Paclitaxel (PTX) from
taxane family, is one of the most potent chemotherapeutic
agent,17–20 and is effective against a wide range of cancers,
including brain, breast, gastric, pancreatic, lung, head and
neck cancers.21 DTX binds and stabilizes microtubule, arresting
the cell cycle at the G2/M phase, leading to apoptosis and
cytotoxicity.22–25 DTX, belongs to Class II of Biopharmaceutics
Classification System, having low aqueous solubility (b5 μg/mL),
high permeability,26,27 and hence formulated commercially as
micellar solutions of polysorbate 80 and dehydrated alcohol
(Taxotere®) in the ratio of 50/50 (v/v).28
Despite its high potency, Taxotere® exhibits serious adverse
effects such as hypersensitivity reactions, anemia, fluid retention,
skin toxicity, anorexia, neutropenic fever and peripheral
neuropathy, which are attributed to formulating vehicles.29–32
Moreover, the intravenous administration has several drawbacks,
including particulate matter in infusion preparations, morbidity
related to the intravenous access site, the risk of catheter-related
infection, potential thrombosis and extravasations.33,34
To minimize such toxicities and low oral bioavailability
associated with the formulation, alternative drug delivery systems
have been developed, including solid dispersion,35 emulsions,36–39
liposome, 40–42 nanoparticles, 43–48 complexation with
cyclodextrins,49 and polymeric micelle.50 These nanocarrier
based systems showed improved solubility and toxicity but, lacked
controlled drug release, due to premature leakage of the drug.
Hence, the major obstacles of aqueous solubility, toxicity and
pharmacokinetic (PK) profile associated with DTX are yet to be
resolved. Oral chemotherapy has advantages over current paren-
teral chemotherapy regimen which can improve patient compliance
and resolve both the solubility and absorption issues.51–53
The primary objective of this investigation is to develop a
next generation hybrid-dendrimer for oral administration with
improved solubility, in vitro drug dissolution, release profile and
oral bioavailability. Hybrid dendrimer concept was developed by
our group (Chauhan et al.)11,12 and we now report an easy in situ
method of creating hybrid dendrimer nano-assembly by mixing –
NH2 and –COONa dendrimers with a chemotherapeutic drug
DTX, in situ. Hybrid dendrimers were characterized by zeta
potential, HR-TEM monographs with SAED pattern and 1H-
NMR and their performance was evaluated in in vitro dissolution
media and cell-lines. The oral administration of the hybrid
dendrimer formulation showed PK equivalence to intravenous
injection of commercially available docetaxel (Taxotere®).
Hybrid dendrimer concept provides much needed fine-tuning of
dendrimer nanotechnology to create multipurpose, and multistage
advanced dendritic nano-assemblies for better therapeutic appli-
cation in nanomedicine.
Methods (detailed method in the supplementary material section)
Strategy for development of formulations containing DTX
and dendrimers
Dendrimer-DTX formulations were prepared by three
methods (Method-I, Method-II, and Method-III) to make
following complexes as:
A-DTX: Docetaxel-amine dendrimer complex.
C-DTX: Docetaxel-sodium carboxylate dendrimer complex.
AC-DTX (Premix): Docetaxel + Amine-carboxylate hybrid
dendrimer complex.
AC-DTX (in situ): Docetaxel + amine dendrimer + carboxylate
dendrimer complex.
In Method-I, physical entrapment of DTX in –NH2 and
–COONa dendrimers was carried out by the method reported in
our previous work to form A-DTX and C-DTX.4,6,13
In Method-II, –NH2 and –COONa dendrimers were physi-
cally pre-mixed at an equivalent volume ratio to form hybrid
dendrimers and then entrapment of DTX was carried out to form
AC-DTX (Premix).
In Method-III, simultaneous mixing of DTX and both
dendrimers –NH2 and –COONa at equivalent volume ratio was
carried out by overnight stirring/mixing to form another hybrid
formulation of DTX to be known as AC-DTX (in situ).
Results
Solubility studies (drug entrapment) and formulation development
Solubility enhancement of DTX was studied with –NH2 and
–COONa dendrimers in water. A-DTX and C-DTX formula-
tions were prepared by method-I with 0.025% (w/v), 0.05% (w/v),
0.1% (w/v) and 0.2% (w/v) of dendrimer concentrations. The
solubility of pure DTX in deionized water (DW) was only 6.85 ±
2.19 μg/mL. Solubility enhancement of DTX was observed in
presence of dendrimers. At0.2% (w/v) of dendrimer concentration,
DTX solubility was found to be 56.69 ± 3.61 μg/mL for A-DTX

and 70.74 ± 4.02 μg/mL for C-DTX. The solubility profiles of
A-DTX and C-DTX showed a non-linear Higuchi AN-type

At pH 10.0, surface charges of –NH2 and –COONa dendrimers

showed net negative charge and it indicates that carboxylate

HR-TEM and SAED
HR-TEM images revealed globular particles having diameters of
4.44 ± 0.38 nm for –NH2 and 5.66 ± 0.34 nm for –COONa
dendrimers. DTX loaded formulations showed increments in particle
diameter (7.63 ± 0.69 nm for A-DTX and 6.39 ± 0.92 nm for
C-DTX) than blank dendrimers. AC-Premix displayed increased
particle size with a diameter of 10.15 ± 1.60 nm due to electrostatic
interactions between –NH2 and –COONa dendrimers resulting in
summation of diameter of both dendrimers respectively. While
AC-DTX (Premix) showed increased particle diameters 12.04 ±
1.54 nm owing to drug entrapment (Figure 2, A–F).
Interestingly, larger particles with distorted sphericity of
42.01 ± 13.01 nm, were observed in AC-DTX (in situ),
suggesting the successful formation of a nano-assembly of
DTX with –NH2 and –COONa hybrid dendrimers (Figure 2, G).
Additional, magnification of AC-DTX (in situ) showed in situ
nanostructures (Figure 2, H–I) with diameter 47.41 ± 10.49 nm
and 43.33 ± 0.09 nm, respectively. Figure 2, J–N, illustrates the
corresponding SAED pattern, displaying the diffraction spots.
NMR studies
The solubilization and complexation of DTX by –NH2 and
–COONa dendrimers was examined in more detail with 1H-NMR
spectroscopy. The 700 MHz NMR helped to acquire the spectra
at lower concentrations. The concentration of DTX was fixed at
100 μg, and equivalent amount of lyophilized A-DTX, C-DTX,
AC-DTX (Premix) and AC-DTX (in situ) formulations were
dissolved in deuterated chloroform (CDCl3) to observe the
chemical shifts. 1H-NMR (Figure 3) of DTX in CDCl3 revealed
the presence of a resolved aromatic region between 7.0 ppm and
8.1 ppm. Protons close to oxygen appeared above 3.5 ppm due to
electronegative effect of oxygen. The intense signal at 1.54 ppm
corresponds to tertiary butyl group of BOC connected to amide
nitrogen. The other methyl groups were also clearly visible at
1.76 ppm, 1.24 ppm and 1.14 ppm.

In vitro drug dissolution studies
The in vitro drug dissolution of DTX, A-DTX, C-DTX and
AC-DTX (in situ) were carried by modified dissolution method
in deionized water (DW) (pH 7.0) and simulated gastric fluid
(SGF) pH 1.2 dissolution mediums. The dendrimer-complexes
showed enhanced in vitro dissolution performance as compared
to the pure drug in both the media.
DTX-dendrimer complexes dissolved far more rapidly than
DTX alone in DW and SGF. In DW, DTX showed negligible
dissolution (1.70%). Dissolution of DTX was increased (from
1.70%) to 99.91% with A-DTX, to 96.94% with C-DTX and to
97.35% with AC-DTX (in situ) in 15 minutes (Figure 4, A).
In SGF, only 1.25% of DTX was dissolved in 15 minutes
from DTX control. The dissolution of DTX was 25.12%, 30.33%
and 29.33% with A-DTX, C-DTX and AC-DTX (in situ),
respectively at 15 minutes. At 180 minutes, 91.11%, 94.38% and
90.49% dissolution was observed with A-DTX, C-DTX and AC-
DTX (in situ), respectively compared to 1.25% for DTX control
(Figure 4, B).
Further, the dissolution of DTX was also evaluated with most
commonly used surfactant Tween 80 (0.5% w/v) at pH 7.0 and
pH 1.2. Tween 80 performed poorly compared to dendrimer
formulations (Figure 4, A and B).
In vitro drug release studies
Dialysis method was used for determination of the pH-
responsive drug release profiles, in DW (pH 7.0), phosphate
buffered saline (PBS) pH 7.4 and sodium acetate buffer (SAB)
pH 5.0 for Taxotere®, DTX, A-DTX, C-DTX and AC-DTX
(in situ).
Almost 90% of drug (DTX) was released from Taxotere® in
2 hours in DW, whereas it was found to be 45.60% and 48.63%
from –NH2 and –COONa dendrimer complexes even after
12 hours. Interestingly, in situ hybrid dendrimer showed
multiphase drug release profile. The initial drug release profile
(6 hours) of AC-DTX (in situ) was similar to A-DTX, C-DTX
but drastic increase in drug release was observed between
8 hours and 12 hours (85.41%) followed by a slower rate in
comparison to A-DTX and C-DTX (Figure S2, A).
When we examined the release profile of AC-DTX (in situ)
in SAB (pH 5.0), the release of DTX was 87.58% in 12 hours
and incessantly increasing to 92.41% till 24 hours. However, at
physiological condition in PBS (pH 7.4), the release of DTX was
slower (86.45%) than the slightly acidic pH 5.0. over 12 hours.

Biocompatibility (hemolytic) studies
Taxotere® (Docetaxel) showed 11.80 ± 0.55% hemolysis
(Table S2). AC-DTX (in situ) nano-assembly showed lower
hemolysis than individual drug dendrimer complex and
Taxotere®. At 0.2% w/v dendrimer concentration, A-DTX and
C-DTX dendrimers caused 4.32 ± 0.17% and 2.35 ± 0.25%
hemolysis, compared with AC-DTX (in situ) which caused only
1.97 ± 0.58% hemolysis.
Cell culture
Cell viability/anti-proliferation assay
Human glioblastoma cells (U87 MG), Mouse glioma cells
(GL-261), Chinese hamster ovary cells (CHO) and Human
embryonic kidney cells (HEK-293) were used to evaluate the
anticancer potential of DTX formulations. Figure S3, A–D
depicts the viability of U87MG, GL-261, CHO and HEK-293
cells after being incubated with various concentrations of DTX
and DTX-dendrimer complexes for 48 hours. The cell viability
of b50% in U87 MG and GL-261 cells was considered toxic to
cells, while cell viability N70% in CHO and HEK-293 cells was
considered non-toxic to cells. Dendrimers were shown to be non-
toxic at the tested concentrations, whereas all the drug-based
formulations showed similar cell viability pattern.

Cellular uptake studies
In vitro internalization studies of Fluorescein isothiocyanate
isomer I (FITC), FITC-amine dendrimer complex (A-FITC),
FITC-sodium carboxylate dendrimer complex (C-FITC) and FITC
+ amine dendrimer + carboxylate dendrimer complex as AC-FITC
(in situ) were carried out in U87 MG and GL-261 cells and uptake
was confirmed by confocal laser scanning microscopy. At 4 hours
of incubation, AC-FITC (in situ) showed highest fluorescence
intensity than A-FITC and C-FITC, respectively (Figure 5, A–E).
Apoptosis and cell cycle analysis
The cell death pathway (apoptosis and necrosis) of DTX-
dendrimer complexes in U87 MG and GL-261 cells was
analyzed by flow cytometer using Annexin V-FITC/PI method.
The elevated percentage of live U87 MG cells (89.73) was
observed in untreated cells. The percentage of live cells decreased
to 74.10 and 65.08 with DTX treatment followed by 84.90 and
68.02 after A-DTX treatment, 80.68 and 70.73 after C-DTX
treatment, 73.64 and 43.17 after AC-DTX (in situ) treatment with
a total percentage of 25.70 and 53.17 apoptotic cells at 25 and
50 μg/mL. On the other hand the percentage of 81.00 live GL-261
cells was observed in untreated cells. The percentage of live
cells decreased to 73.57 and 57.27 with DTX treatment followed
by 77.46 and 57.81 after A-DTX treatment, 76.81 and 55.36 after
C-DTX treatment. 73.14 and 51.20 after AC-DTX (in situ)
treatment with the total percentage of 26.53 and 44.91 apoptotic
cells at 25 and 50 μg/mL (Figure S4 and Table S3).

cycle phases using flow cytometry. DTX inhibited the cell cycle
progression at the G2/M phase in a U87 MG and GL-261 cells. We
calculated the area under the peaks to determine the phase within the
cell cycle, using FACS analyzer, (BD Accuri™with C6 software,

To evaluate the intestinal uptake of FITC labeled dendrimers,
the quantification of fluorescent signal was performed. FITC
alone did not show any intestinal permeation. Interestingly, at

The oral bioavailability (in-vivo) of DTX can effectively be

Single dose administration (10 mg/kg) of Taxotere® and
AC-DTX (in situ) in SD rats was compared with the control
group for acute toxicity by monitoring change in body weight,

Significant decrease (P b 0.05) in hematocrit parameters was

(P N 0.05) observed in AC-DTX (in situ) and Taxotere® treated

Discussion
The available drug delivery systems come with inherent
advantages and disadvantages and they do not solve all the
problems in-vivo. For example, a single drug delivery system
cannot easily achieve a complex dissolution and release profile.
Thus, individual delivery systems can either show burst release or
sustained release; however, achieving complete dissolution with
sustained release from the same system is a complex task.
Combining two delivery systems will cause problem due to their
inherent engineering mechanisms and complex chemistry. Hence,
if delivery systems with characteristic features can be combined to
create a new assembly, it may open new avenues in the area of drug
delivery and targeting.
Dendrimers have three main parts: core, generations and
surfaces. We have demonstrated that dendrimers with different
core, surfaces and generations show different in vitro and
biodisposition pattern.5 In that sense, combination of each
generation, surface, and core dendrimer is characteristic, and each
combination is different from one another. For example, G4-NH2
PAMAM dendrimer showed slow release of indomethacin,
whereas fast drug release was observed with G3.5-COOH
PAMAM dendrimer. Cationic dendrimers are shown to be toxic
at higher concentrations, whereas anionic and neutral dendrimers
are safe. Two or more different dendrimers can be combined to
work in tandem in a system to achieve a therapeutically desired
release profile.
Dendrimers are unique compared to other drug delivery
systems. Drugs are entrapped in dendrimers, post dendrimer
synthesis; whereas they are encapsulated during the synthesis
with liposomes and nanoparticles. Hence drugs in dendrimers are
in constant equilibrium with the outside medium with branched
dendritic architecture. Dendrimers are engineerable due to their
branched architecture to create advanced systems compared to
other systems.
Our group is working to understand the nanoscale pattern of
dendrimers and other nanostructures.54 Previously, we have de-
monstrated assembly of dendrimers with albumin nanoparticles.10
In this dendrimer-albumin nanoparticle nano-assembly, PTX was
entrapped in dendrimers; and targeting ligands, imaging molecules
were attached on albumin nanoparticles. This dendrimer-
nanoparticle assembly was shown to be effective for inhibiting
the growth of cancer cells compared to plain PTX and albumin
nanocarrier.
Currently, we are corroborating CNDP's of dendrimers and its
effect on drug delivery.9 In this process; we have developed a
novel hybrid dendrimer approach, as a next generation dendritic
platform. Hybrid dendrimers were prepared by in situ mixing of
two different types of dendrimers. The purpose is to harness unique
features of individual dendrimers and create a nano-assembly. In a
simple sense it is an assembly of two dendrimers without any
chemical reactions. Tomalia et al., have prepared megamers using
different dendrimers but it involved complex covalent chemical
synthesis, whereas proposed hybrid dendrimers are easy to make in
one step procedure with non-covalent approach.55 Hybrid
dendrimer concept now adds new features of liposomes and

increase its aqueous solubility for in-vivo applications. The

Both AC-Premix and AC-DTX (in situ) dendrimers can be
broadly called hybrid dendrimers. Hybrid dendrimer formulations
were prepared with two approaches: i) amine dendrimers and
carboxylate dendrimers were mixed to create hybrid dendrimer
(Premix) and then DTX was entrapped, ii) amine dendrimer,
carboxylate dendrimers and DTX were all added at the same time
leading to hybrid dendrimer (in situ) formation along with drug
loading. AC-DTX (Premix) hybrid dendrimer is formed by surface
interactions of –NH2 and –COONa dendrimer hence fewer surface
groups are available to interact with drug molecule for solubility
enhancement. This was evidenced by decreased drug loading
observed for AC-DTX (Premix) compared to individual dendri-
mers. Surprisingly, AC-DTX (in situ) showed high entrapment of
DTX compared to all other dendrimer formulations. With in situ
hybrid dendrimer formulation, formation of hybrid dendrimer and
entrapment of DTX takes place simultaneously and that may
explain the higher drug loading compared to the AC-DTX
(Premix) hybrid dendrimer formulations. Drug would be entrapped
in dendrimers as discussed before and encapsulated between
dendrimer nano-assembly.
Solubility of DTX from AC-DTX (in situ) was not significantly
(P N 0.05) altered with change in pH due to unavailability of
ionization groups in their structure, which interact with functional
groups at the periphery of dendrimer. This observation also
correlates with our previous work,8 where drug entrapment in
dendrimer is mainly governed by hydrogen bonding and,
molecular encapsulation excluding electrostatic interactions.
Thus, arithmetical calculation of the total loading efficiency of
hybrid-dendrimer based in situ system (ISS) composed of
dendrimer A and C could be ISS N A + C.
Zeta potential study demonstrated that AC-DTX (in situ)
has either negative charge or less positive charge compared to
AC-DTX (Premix) and cationic dendrimers. The changes in
surface charge might be due to the ionization effect of dendrimer
and non-covalent interaction of dendrimers and DTX. Thus, we
utilized this (in situ) system in our further studies attributing to
its highest solubility profile with highly negative surface charge
for better biocompatibility.
HR-TEM images confirmed the formation of hybrid
dendrimers (Figure 2.A-I). We have used common 20 nm scale
to compare the size of individual dendrimers and hybrid
dendrimers. To confirm the homogeneity of the hybrid
dendrimers, a 100 nm scale HR-TEM is provided in Fig. S1.
The particle size observed was 10.05 ± 1.64 nm (Mean ± SD;
n = 30). Multi-directional non-covalent bond formation between
dendrimers and DTX was the driving force for the dendrimer-
induced formation of nano-assembly. More importantly, the
presence of diffraction spots in SAED pattern clearly indicated
the bright field spots with diffused rings suggesting its poly
nanocrystalline nature for complexation of DTX in the dendritic
matrix. The nature of this orientation order reveals itself in the
TEM images (Figure 2.G-I), where we observe the formation of
large aggregates probably resulted due to the presence of strong
inter-cluster non-covalent interactions in the dendrimer matrix,
However, such SAED pattern is absent in an aqueous dendrimer
solution and minimum diffraction spot in SAED pattern of
AC-DTX (Premix) may not have enough capability for higher
drug entrapment. The distorted spherical assembly and negative
surface charge of AC-DTX (in situ) induces electrostatic repulsion
and hence, may ensure physiochemical stability during storage.57
1H-NMR data provides valuable information on direct
interactions between dendrimer and guest molecule. The
chemical shifts and change in signal intensity of DTX in
dendrimer complex was examined and it was observed that there
are no peak shifts but reduction in signal intensity of DTX
protons when it interacts with dendrimers. This complexation of
DTX with dendrimer might have affected the signal intensity of
NMR spectra. Among the dendrimer complexes of DTX, DTX in
complex with carboxyl dendrimer and in situ complex showed
more signal intensity when compared with DTX complex of amine
dendrimer and premix complex. The variation in proton intensity
in the peak positions of DTX further confirmed the successful
establishment of complexation of DTX with dendrimers following
non-covalent interactions. Presumably, the increased number of
hydroxyl groups contributes to hydrogen bonds with amide, ester
and hydroxyl group of DTX.
Enhanced solubility, stability and dissolution of dendrimer
complexes of DTX are promising and preliminary indicators of
improved oral bioavailability of DTX. Dissolution pattern for all
DTX-dendrimer complexes was compared in different dissolution
media. Insignificant (P N 0.05) difference was observed in
dissolution pattern between individual dendrimer-drug complexes
(A-DTX and C-DTX) and hybrid-dendrimer-drug complex AC-
DTX (in situ).
Dissolution study helps to find pattern of drug-dendrimer
complexes dissolving in the medium, whereas in vitro release
study gives the profile of drug release from the dissolved drug-
dendrimer complex. Hybrid dendrimer displayed multiphase
drug release. The initial drug release from AC-DTX (in situ)
was from the entrapped drug inside dendrimers (like A-DTX,
C-DTX) and later on drug encapsulated within dendrimer
nano-assembly released leading to higher drug release.
Hemolytic studies suggested that AC-DTX (in situ) complexes
are more biocompatible than blank dendrimers with reduced
hemolysis. The lower toxicity of AC-DTX (in situ) can be
explained by presence of negative (−36.63 mV) charge on their
surfaces. Whereas –NH2 dendrimer complex (A-DTX) caused
hemolysis because of their positive surface charge. The hemolytic
toxicity for Taxotere® was found to be 11.80% due to use of
polysorbate 80 and dehydrated alcohol as formulating vehicles.
Hybrid dendrimer in situ nano system will not interact with anionic
membrane of red blood cells leading to higher biocompatibility.
The higher uptake of AC-FITC (in situ) clearly demonstrates
the internalization potential of dendrimer system in U87 MG and
GL-261 cells (Figure 5, A–E). The combined cell morphology
observation, MTT assay of cell viability, flow cytometric
analysis of cell cycles and apoptosis suggested that the AC-
DTX (in situ) have significantly higher accumulation in both the
cells and cytotoxic in the given concentration and should be
amenable for anticancer applications.
Ex-vivo intestinal permeation results revealed that FITC
labeled dendrimers are being taken up by intestine due to their

nano size and availability of larger surface area in the small
intestine. Hybrid dendrimer showed better permeability com-
pared to A-FITC and C- FITC. FITC of its own did not show
any permeability.
The PK profile clearly demonstrated that DTX-dendrimer
complexes were rapidly absorbed into intestinal cells by endocy-
tosis and reached into blood, and then prolonged the DTX plasma
levels. However, in case of oral administration of pure DTX, the
plasma concentration was very low, below the quantification limit
of HPLC technique (0.2 μg/mL). In-vivo performance of all
dendrimer complexes (oral administration) can be ranked as
follows: AC-DTX (in situ) ≈Taxotere® (IV) N A-DTX N C-
DTX and this indicated that oral AC-DTX (in situ) showed similar
PK profile as that of intravenous Taxotere® (commercially
available docetaxel) with better mean residence time (MRT).
Histopathological observation of organs indicated no systemic
toxicity in AC-DTX (in situ) treated animals when compared with
control and Taxotere® treated animals. We observed that decrease
in hematocrit parameters such as red blood cell count (RBC),
hemoglobin (HB), mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration
(MCHC) and white blood cell count (WBC) in Taxotere® group
indicated that these animals might have become anemic which was
supported with a decrease in body weight, feed consumption and
pathological changes in spleen and kidneys. However, such
changes were not observed in AC-DTX (in situ) treated animals.
In summary, we report a next generation dendritic platform; a
hybrid dendrimer-based nano-assembly. We have demonstrated its
application by increasing the oral bioavailability of docetaxel.
Hybrid dendrimers loaded with docetaxel achieved increase in
solubility, high drug loading, enhanced dissolution cum release,
increased physicochemical stability and equivalent pharmacokinetic
properties in comparison to commercially available intravenous
Taxotere®. Our strategy serves as a platform to be utilized via oral
and parenteral modes for various therapies that require multiple and
long-term treatments likes cancer, leukemia and tuberculosis. Hybrid
dendrimer concept will help to fine- tune dendrimer architecture to
prepare an advanced nanodevice for therapeutic applications.
