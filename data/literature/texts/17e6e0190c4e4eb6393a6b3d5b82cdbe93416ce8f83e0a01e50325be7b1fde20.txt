ABSTRACT: The presence of abnormal levels of reactive oxygen
species (ROS) is a recognized pathological feature of inflammatory
bowel disease (IBD). Therefore, the development of orally
administered antioxidants with high antioxidative capacity and
gastric acid tolerance for the treatment of IBD is of great
significance. Here, we present the design and synthesis of a
bimetallic ruthenium−cobalt (RuCo) nanosheet for the treatment
of IBD. The Ru−Co atoms within the nanosheet structure exhibit
significant electron transfer properties owing to their electro-
negativity feature. Density functional theory calculations indicate
that the RuCo nanosheets have higher d-band centers than the
corresponding Ru and Co metal monoliths, which increases the
catalytic activity. Such RuCo nanosheets exhibit superoxide
dismutase and catalase-like cascade enzyme activities and show robust stability in gastric fluid over a 4 h period when exposed
to simulated gastric fluid, ensuring desirable retention of antioxidative activity. Cellular and animal studies show that RuCo
nanosheets are capable of effectively reducing oxidative stress, preventing inflammatory responses triggered by an abnormal increase
in ROS at intestinal sites, and thus protecting cells from inflammatory damages. This research presents a gastric-acid-stabilized
antioxidative nanocatalytic platform for the efficient treatment of inflammatory diseases of the digestive system.
1. INTRODUCTION
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a prevalent, chronic
inflammatory disease that is incurable and characterized by a
high recurrence rate, which significantly affects human
health.1,2 IBD primarily affects young to middle-aged
individuals. Despite the low mortality rate, symptoms such as
diarrhea, bloody stools, and weight loss severely impede
patients’ quality of life.3−5 As a result, accurate diagnosis and
effective treatment for IBD remain crucial, yet unmet needs.
Commonly prescribed drugs for IBD include salicylate,
glucocorticoid, and immunosuppressants (e.g., infliximab,
vedolizumab, risankizumab, and upadacitinib). However,
these drugs exhibit limited efficacy, are susceptible to resistance
development with prolonged use, and carry the risk of
infection due to immunosuppression.6−10 Therefore, there is
still a considerable need for improved IBD therapies. Emerging
research suggests that excessive formation of reactive oxygen
species (ROS) at the lesion site primarily drives and promotes
the malignant development of IBD through complex
interactions involving multiple variables.11,12 For example,
excessive ROS activates the NF-κB signaling pathway,
subsequently triggering the release of additional proinflamma-
tory cytokines that further amplify the inflammatory
response.13,14 Additionally, excess ROS also induces intestinal
dysbiosis and damages the intestinal barrier formed by
intestinal epithelial cells and the immune system.15−18
The strategic administration of antioxidative enzymes such
as superoxide dismutase (SOD),19−21 glutathione peroxidase
(GPx),22 and catalase (CAT)23,24 to the sites of inflammation
effectively eliminates abnormal ROS. This method represents a
promising strategy for the treatment of various inflammatory
diseases. For example, Agrawal et al. developed a recombinant
SV40-derived vector that releases SOD and GPx to protect
neuronal cells from damage.25 Petelin et al. employed
liposomes to deliver the SOD and CAT enzymes in the
treatment of periodontitis.26 However, the inherent limitations
of natural enzymes, such as high preparation costs, singular
functionality, and subpar stability, restrict their further
biological application.27,28 As a result, nanocatalysts with
enzymatic activities akin to those of natural enzymes, such as
those found in ceria,29 Prussian blue,30 MXene,31 and metal−
organic frameworks (MOF),32 are emerging as optimal
alternatives for inflammatory disease treatment due to their
cost-effectiveness and robust stability. Typically, ceria nano-

These properties make them highly promising for the
treatment of various inflammatory diseases, including sepsis,
hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury, acute kidney injury, and
Alzheimer’s disease.33 In recent years, noble metal nano-
catalysts have garnered significant attention due to their
superior catalytic activity.34−36 Further alloying of these
catalysts has demonstrated an enhancement in performance
and an expansion in applications. The dual enzyme properties
exhibited by the AuPd nanoalloy underscore this potential,
primarily driven by the disparities in electron configuration and
electronegativity between the Au and Pd atoms.37
Oral delivery is a prevalent method for in vivo admin-
istration, offering benefits such as high patient compliance,
cost-effectiveness, noninvasiveness, and operational simplic-
ity.38−40 However, the gastrointestinal tract presents numerous
biological barriers, including the harsh acidic environment of
the stomach and gastric digestive enzymes, necessitating higher
drug dosages and the development of sophisticated drug
delivery platforms.41−44 Given the inherent catalytic stability of
the noble metal ruthenium under acidic conditions,45,46 we
propose the development of ruthenium-based catalytic
antioxidants for the oral treatment of IBD. However, the
catalytic antioxidative activity of ruthenium often falls short of
expectations due to insufficient electron-donating capacity and
low electron transfer efficiency.47,48 To overcome this
limitation, recent studies have demonstrated that the
introduction of a secondary metal can effectively modulate
the electronic structure of the catalytic center, thereby
enhancing both the stability and catalytic efficiency of the
system.49,50 Among various candidates, cobalt is particularly
promising due to its essential role as a trace element in the
human body and its presence in vitamin B12, which is known
to contribute to physiological redox processes.51 Moreover,
cobalt-based nanomaterials have demonstrated significant
therapeutic potential in the treatment of inflammatory
diseases.52 Importantly, the electronegativity difference be-
tween ruthenium and cobalt facilitates the electronic structure
modulation of the alloy, enabling the construction of
nanocatalysts with optimized catalytic and antioxidative
properties.
Here, we synthesized bimetallic ruthenium−cobalt (RuCo)
nanosheets using a solvothermal method, whose distinct crystal
facets exhibited SOD- and CAT-like enzyme activities that can
effectively alleviate intestinal inflammation (Scheme 1).
Notably, after 4 h of exposure to simulated gastric fluid
(SGF), RuCo nanosheets maintained stable SOD-CAT-like
cascade catalytic activity. The density functional theory (DFT)
calculations indicate that the catalytic performance of the
ruthenium−cobalt bimetallic composition surpasses that of its
single component counterpart, revealing SOD-like and CAT-
like catalytic reaction processes on different crystal facets.
Furthermore, RuCo nanosheets exhibited significant antiox-
idative and therapeutic effects in cellular experiments and the
mice IBD model. These results provide valuable insights for
the design and development of 2D nanomaterials that can
overcome gastric fluid barriers and exhibit enzyme-like activity,
facilitating the construction of therapeutic systems for effective
treatment of IBD.

2.2. Elemental Valence and Microstructure Analysis.
The valence state of RuCo nanosheets was meticulously
examined by using X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS).
The Ru 3p spectrum indicates a predominantly metallic state,
accompanied by an oxidized state due to surface partial
oxidation (Figure 2a). Similarly, the Co 2p spectrum presents a
major metallic state as well as a partially oxidized state (Figure
2b). We then examine the Ru 3d spectrum, which
demonstrates that the Ru(0) position of RuCo nanosheets is
shifted toward lower binding energy compared to that of
metallic ruthenium (Figure 2c). This observation validates the
electron transfer from cobalt to ruthenium, aligning with the
pronounced electronegativity of ruthenium over cobalt.
To elucidate the bonding and coordination environments of
ruthenium and cobalt atoms within the RuCo nanosheets, we
carried out X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) analysis.
The Ru K-edge XANES curves indicate that the pre-edge curve
of RuCo nanosheets is quite close to that of Ru foil, suggesting
the zero valency of elemental Ru (Figure 2d). Correspond-
ingly, the elemental Co in RuCo nanosheets presents an
intermediate valence state of Coδ+ (0 < δ < 2), attributable to
the pronounced electron transfer between cobalt and
ruthenium (Figure 2g). Next, the local coordination environ-
ment of the Ru and Co sites was acquired by using EXAFS
analysis and related fitting curves.
The corresponding Fourier-transformed EXAFS (FT-
EXAFS) spectra of the Ru K-edge reveal two dominant
peaks at 2.0 and 2.2 Å, which could be designated as the Ru−
Co and Ru−Ru shells, respectively (Figure 2e). Meanwhile, a
weak peak at 1.5 Å is observed in RuCo nanosheets, which can
be ascribed to the Ru−O shell. The oxygen element could
derive from the atmospheric oxygen in the experiment. In FT-
EXAFS curves of the Co K-edge (Figure 2h), Co−O

2.3. Evaluation of Multiple Antioxidative Properties.
O2
·−and H2O2 are typical ROS that proliferate malignantly
during inflammatory responses. Their effective elimination is
crucial for mitigating inflammation and facilitating disease
recovery. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) can specifically
catalyze the highly oxidizing O2
·−to produce H2O2 and O2
through a dismutation reaction.54,55 In this study, we employed
an SOD assay kit to assess the SOD-like activity of the RuCo
nanosheets. The chromogenic agent (water-soluble tetrazolium
salt, WST-1) interacts with O2
·−to form a yellow complex,
which exhibits strong light absorption at 450 nm. Furthermore,
the introduction of materials with SOD-like activity effectively
reduces the concentration of substrate O2
·−, consequently
diminishing the formation of the yellow complex and leading
to a decrease in absorbance intensity at 450 nm (Figure 3a). As
depicted in Figure 3b, RuCo nanosheets demonstrate a
concentration-dependent O2
·−scavenging capability, achieving
up to a 90% removal rate at a concentration of 20 μg/mL. We
also monitored changes in absorbance over time for varying
concentrations of material groups and the control group,
revealing that the RuCo nanosheets possess commendable
SOD-like activity (Figure 3c). To further mitigate oxidative
stress at the lesion site, it is imperative to eliminate both H2O2
produced by SOD mimetics and H2O2 present at the lesion
site. Consequently, we then evaluated the CAT-like activity of
the RuCo nanosheets, which can transform H2O2 into H2O
and O2. Here, ammonium molybdate was selected as the
chromogenic agent for substrate H2O2, and the CAT-like
activity of the RuCo nanosheets was determined by the
reduction of the absorbance at 405 nm (Figure 3a). The RuCo
nanosheets demonstrate a H2O2 removal rate of up to 90% at a
concentration of 20 μg/mL, exhibiting a concentration-
dependent characteristic (Figure 3d).

nanosheets, we initially measured alterations in the UV
absorbance for H2O2 at 240 nm. As shown in Figure 3e,f,
the addition of RuCo nanosheets markedly diminishes the UV

2.4. Theoretical Evaluation of Antioxidative Activities
of RuCo Nanosheets. Previous research has demonstrated
that the ensemble effect, resulting from alterations in catalyst
composition, significantly influences surface properties and
subsequently modifies catalytic activity.56,57 Based on this, we
chose metal ruthenium, metal cobalt, and RuCo nanosheets as
research objects and employed DFT calculations to examine
the impact of compositional differences on the structure of
catalysts and the process of catalytic reactions. The d-band
center theory suggests that the position of the d-band center in
various metals is a critical factor in evaluating the trend of a
specific adsorbent’s adsorption energy, which has been
extensively employed to interpret the relative reactivity of
metal surfaces.58,59 According to the XRD results and HRTEM
analysis, it can be determined that the primary exposed crystal
facets of RuCo nanosheets are 002 and 011. We initially
analyzed the (011) crystal facets, and the results revealed that
the order of d-band centers was RuCo(011) > Ru(011) >
Co(011), confirming that RuCo nanosheets exhibit excellent
catalytic activity (Figure 4a). A similar trend was observed in
the d-band center of the (002) facets (Figure 4h).
Next, the DFT calculation simulation results provide insights
into the enzyme-catalyzed reaction pathways and correspond-
ing energy diagrams on the surfaces of Ru, Co, and RuCo
nanosheets. When the (011) crystal facets are selected for
analysis, the SOD-like processes are absent on the surfaces of
Ru, Co, and RuCo nanosheets due to the unstabilized
adsorption of OOH* but exhibit possible CAT-like catalytic
processes. For the RuCo(011) facet, H2O2 first decomposes to
form surface-adsorbed OH*. These surface-adsorbed OH*
subsequently interacted with one another, producing surface-
adsorbed O2* and generating H2O molecules that overflowed
onto the surface. Ultimately, the surface-adsorbed O2* desorbs
from the surface to form the O2(g), while RuCo nanosheets
revert to their initial state (Figure 4b). The computed free
energy curves reveal that the reaction pathway transitioning

enhanced catalytic activity (Figure 4f).
In addition, we also conducted computational analysis of the
enzymatic catalytic reaction process of Ru, Co, and RuCo
nanosheets with a (002) crystal facet (Figures 4i and S18).
Compared to Co, there is a possible SOD-CAT cascade
enzymatic catalytic process on the (002) facet of Ru and RuCo
nanosheets. The free energy profiles show that the calculated
SOD-like catalytic pathway revealed an energy barrier of 0.2 eV
in the O2* to the OOH* process occurring on the Ru(002)
facet, while no energy barrier was observed in the SOD-like
process on the RuCo(002) facet. Next, the calculated CAT-like
catalytic process demonstrated that the energy barrier for
adsorption of the O* to the O2(g) on the (002) facet of RuCo
nanosheets is 0.05 eV lower than that on the Ru(002) facet.
Consequently, the (002) crystal facet of RuCo nanosheets
exhibits superior SOD-CAT-like cascade enzymatic catalytic
performance compared to the (002) crystal facets of Ru and
Co.
DFT results indicate that the alloy structure of RuCo
nanosheets induces ensemble effects, thereby enhancing the
catalyst’s performance. Furthermore, distinct catalytic proper-
ties are exhibited by different crystal facets, providing valuable
guidance for the design of catalysts for optimal enzyme-
catalyzed reactions.
2.5. In Vitro Cellular Antioxidative Protection by
RuCo Nanosheets. The robust electron transfer between Ru
and Co atoms endows RuCo nanosheets with enzymatic
activity, facilitating their effective scavenging of a range of
oxygen radicals. Based on the above results, we assessed the
efficacy of RuCo nanosheets in attenuating oxidative stress
within physiological environments and investigated the
potential for subsequent bioapplications. The evaluation of
biocompatibility is the first criterion that must be determined
before implementing biological applications. Lactate dehydro-
genase (LDH) is a cytoplasmic enzyme that is released when
the cell membrane is damaged. Consequently, we quantified
both dead and damaged cells by measuring the concentration
of LDH released into the supernatant. As shown in Figure 5b,
the RuCo nanosheets exhibit good biosafety, with cell viability
remaining essentially unchanged even at concentrations as high
as 100 μg/mL.
We further validated the efficacy of RuCo nanosheets in
effectively neutralizing abnormally elevated levels of ROS
within the cells. Initially, H2O2 was used to stimulate an
increase in ROS levels within cells and subsequently labeled
with a ROS probe known as DCFH-DA. This probe could
freely permeate the cells and was transformed by intracellular
esterases into DCFH, which is unable to penetrate the cell
membrane. It was then oxidized by intracellular ROS into the
fluorescent compound DCF. As depicted in Figure 5a, H2O2
stimulation triggered significant cellular ROS signals. However,
the addition of RuCo nanosheets (L-RC: 5 μg/mL RuCo
nanosheets; H-RC: 20 μg/mL RuCo nanosheets) can
effectively alleviate H2O2-induced cellular oxidative stress,
with a significant decrease in ROS signals. Furthermore, we
also recorded the intensities of ROS signals across different
groups for comparative analysis, thereby confirming the cellular
antioxidative protective effect of RuCo nanosheets (Figure 5c).
Cells from each group were collected for flow cytometry
quantitative analysis, and the number of cells with ROS signals
higher than a specific threshold was recorded (Figure 5d,e).
The results showed that H2O2 stimulation led to oxidative
stress in nearly 90% of cells, while the addition of RuCo
nanosheets effectively restored this proportion close to that of
the control group. Furthermore, the cellular antioxidative
protection effect of RuCo nanosheets was further evaluated
using two additional cell lines: HUVECs and CECs. As
illustrated in Figure S19, the introduction of RuCo nanosheets
effectively mitigated the H2O2-induced surge in ROS levels,
which underscores the broad applicability of RuCo nanosheets
for antioxidative protection across various cell lines.
2.6. Colitis Treatment Evaluation. The high efficiency in
vitro antioxidative performance, excellent cytocompatibility,
and antioxidative cell protection of RuCo nanosheets
prompted an exploration of their potential for treating diseases
related to abnormal ROS metabolism. Here, we investigate the
feasibility of RuCo nanosheets for colitis treatment through
oral administration. Oral administration faces challenges due to
the complex gastrointestinal environment, which poses
significant challenges to the retention of drug efficacy.
Consequently, we centrifuged the RuCo nanosheets and
resuspended them in SGF and simulated intestinal fluid
(SIF). After 4 h of incubation, we assessed the morphology,
particle size, and zeta potential of RuCo nanosheets (Figure
S20). The results showed that SGF and SIF treatments did not
induce changes in the structure and surface electrical
properties of the RuCo nanosheets. The morphology of the
RuCo nanosheets still maintains a two-dimensional structure
with lateral dimensions less than 200 nm. Considering the
potential impact of the highly acidic gastric environment on
the catalytic performance, we further measured the properties
of RuCo nanosheets after treatment with SGF. First, we treated
the RuCo nanosheets with SGF and measured the release of
ruthenium and cobalt ions in the supernatant at different time
points, using Ru NPs and Co NPs as controls (Figures S21 and
S22). The results revealed that Ru NPs exhibited moderate
gastric fluid stability with a slow ion release profile, while Co
NPs demonstrated rapid ion-release behavior. In contrast, the
RuCo nanosheets displayed the highest gastric fluid stability,
with a negligible release of ruthenium ions. The initial rapid
release of cobalt ions may be attributed to cobalt atoms or
clusters that were not incorporated into the lattice during the
synthesis process. Subsequently, the antioxidative properties of
SGF-treated RuCo nanosheets were tested to evaluate the
feasibility of oral delivery for antioxidative treatment at
intestinal inflammation sites. Initially, we tested the total
antioxidative capacity of pristine RuCo nanosheets and SGF-

Based on the preceding discussion, we selected C57BL/6
mice to construct a dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced IBD
model and further evaluated the in vivo therapeutic effects of
RuCo nanosheets. After the adaptive feeding period was
concluded, drinking water was supplemented with DSS for the
other two groups, excluding the control group, and they were
fed continuously for 9 days. From day 1 to day 8, the control
group and the DSS group received oral administration of sterile
water, while the DSS + RC group was administered a
dispersion of RuCo nanosheets orally (Figure 6a). The DSS
group exhibited a decrease in mouse weight beginning on day
6, while the DSS + RC group showed a reduction in weight
starting on day 8 (Figure 6b). Similarly, the Disease Activity
Index (DAI) values of mice in the DSS + RC group were
significantly lower than those in the DSS group, underscoring
the mitigating effects of RuCo nanosheets on IBD (Figure
S27).
On day 9, all mice were sacrificed, and blood, spleen, and
colon were collected for further analysis. Photographs of the
colon and its length measurements validated that RuCo
nanosheets effectively mitigated symptoms of DSS-induced
disease (Figure 6c,d). Furthermore, DSS-treated mice without
therapeutic intervention exhibited increased spleen weights
(Figure 6e). Histopathological examination using H&E
staining revealed that DSS treatment induced conspicuous
large ulcers in intestinal tissue, loss of mucosal epithelium and
intestinal gland structure (indicated by blue arrows), extensive
connective tissue hyperplasia, accompanied by a multitude of
scattered lymphocytes and granulocyte infiltration (indicated
by red arrows) (Figure 6f). Intriguingly, these pathological
characteristics were markedly improved in the DSS + RC
group, with the positive results of histological pathology scores
further validating this improvement (Figure 6g).
To corroborate the observed surge in the ROS at lesion sites
in vivo, we performed DHE staining analysis on colon tissues.
The pronounced superoxide signals in the DSS group validated
the abnormal ROS increase. However, RuCo nanosheet
treatment significantly diminished these signals, indicating
their pivotal role in mitigating colitis (Figure 6i). Additionally,
the downregulation of proinflammatory cytokines (IL-6) and a
reduction in monocyte counts (neutrophils and lymphocytes)
in blood further substantiated the therapeutic benefits of RuCo
nanosheets in treating colitis (Figure 6h,j). Immunohistochem-
ical staining for TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 at colonic sites further
corroborated that RuCo nanosheets aid in reducing inflam-
mation at lesion sites (Figures 6k,l and S28). We also
conducted a safety assessment on mice; H&E staining of major
organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney) post 8 days of
oral administration of RuCo nanosheets revealed no significant
pathological changes, thereby confirming their safety for oral
delivery (Figure S29). Furthermore, we investigated the in vivo
metabolic fate of RuCo nanosheets following oral admin-
istration in mice (Figure S30). The results indicated no
significant accumulation of the ruthenium element in major
organs. To further elucidate their metabolic behavior, we
quantitatively analyzed the ruthenium concentrations in the
colons and feces of DSS-induced colitis mice at different time
points post-administration using inductively coupled plasma
mass spectrometry (ICP−MS). The data revealed a notable
increase in ruthenium levels in the colons and feces in 3 and 6
h, which returned to baseline levels in 12 h. These findings
demonstrate that the RuCo nanosheets can selectively
accumulate at inflammatory sites in the colons and are safely
and effectively excreted via feces, highlighting their favorable in
vivo biosafety profile and potential for biomedical applications.
3. CONCLUSIONS
In this study, we successfully synthesized ruthenium−cobalt
(RuCo) bimetallic nanosheets, leveraging the difference in
electronegativity between Ru and Co atoms to enhance
electron transfer and catalytic efficiency. Such RuCo nano-
sheets exhibit both superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like and
catalase (CAT)-like activities, thereby achieving a superior
antioxidative performance. Mechanistic exploration reveals
facet-dependent catalytic behavior. Specifically, the
RuCo(011) facet exhibits pronounced CAT-like activity,
while the RuCo(002) facet demonstrates a synergistic SOD-
CAT cascade catalytic mechanism. These findings underscore
the pivotal role of structural and electronic modulation in
optimizing the catalytic properties of nanomaterials for
biomedical applications. Furthermore, the RuCo nanosheets
display remarkable stability in gastric fluid, highlighting their
feasibility as an orally deliverable antioxidative therapeutic for
IBD. This study establishes a novel approach for treating ROS-
related inflammatory diseases by engineering nanomaterials
with tailored electronic structures and catalytic functionalities,
facilitating future translational research in nanomedicine.
