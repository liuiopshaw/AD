A B S T R A C T   
The effective healing of diabetic wounds presents significant challenges, wherein antibacterial activity, oxygen 
(O2) supplement, and glucose consumption emerge as pivotal factors. Herein, we devise ultrafine Ru nanoclusters 
supported on an amorphous NiB alloy (Ru-NiB) as difunctional a nanozyme for bacterial-infected diabetic wound 
healing. The obtained Ru-NiB exhibited strong peroxidase (POD)- and catalase (CAT)-mimicking activities. When 
combined with the natural glucose oxidase (GOx), it drives a glucose-activated cascaded catalytic reaction for 
sustainable reactive oxygen species (ROS) and O2 generation, glucose consumption. The results demonstrate that 
the cascade system exhibits a potent antibacterial effect both in vitro and in vivo due to the sustainable ROS 
generation. Moreover, the cascade system has the potential to ameliorate hyperglycemia and hypoxic micro­
environment in diabetic wounds through oxygen generation and glucose consumption, leading to a reduction in 
inflammation and enhancement of angiogenesis. The aforementioned effect synergistically enhances the healing 
process of diabetic wounds. This study successfully demonstrates the synergistic effect of ultralow noble metal 
loading and precise regulation of GOx dosage on the healing of diabetic wounds infected with bacteria, providing 
an effective strategy for treating diabetic wounds.   
1. Introduction 
The prevalence of diabetes is rapidly rising, and its complications 
have emerged as a significant threat to human health [1,2]. Diabetic 
ulcers are considered one of the most severe chronic complications, 
characterized by non-healing chronic wounds [3]. The wound area in 
diabetic ulcers creates an environment with hyperglycemia and low pH 
levels, thereby facilitating bacterial proliferation and rendering these 
ulcers highly susceptible to bacterial infections [4–6]. Consequently, 
this leads to the development of robust bacterial biofilm, which can 
significantly hinder the therapeutic effectiveness of antibiotics utilized 
in clinical practice [7–9]. In addition, the hyperglycemic and hypoxic 
microenvironments impede the proliferation and migration of key cells 
involved in wound, such as fibroblasts and vascular endothelial cells 
[10–16]. Therefore, antibacterial, oxygen (O2) generation and glucose 
consumption are the most crucial factors for diabetic wound healing. 
Nanozymes, referring to nanomaterials with enzyme-like activity, 
have garnered significant attention in the field of antibacterial and 
wound healing [17–21]. Noble metal nanozymes demonstrate remark­
able and multiple enzyme-like activities for antimicrobial applications 
[22,23]. In diabetic models, the natural glucose oxidase (GOx) is often 
loaded on noble metal nanozymes to function cascaded catalytic re­
actions [24,25]. For example, Wang et al. constructed a cascaded cata­
lytic nanozyme through the chemical coupling of GOx onto Au/Pt NCs 
(Au/Pt NCs@GOx). In this system, GOx effectively consumes glucose to 
generate hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), while Au/Pt simultaneously 
mimics peroxidase (POD) to convert H2O2 into highly cytotoxic hy­
droxyl radicals (•OH), thereby achieving satisfactory antibacterial and 
antibiofilm effects [25]. However, O2 generation is ignored in this ma­
terial system, which significantly impede the glucose oxidation process,

The potential of Ru metal in the field of biomedicine, particularly for 
antimicrobial applications, has been significantly demonstrated due to 
its POD- and catalase (CAT)-like activities [26–28]. Its CAT-like activity 
can convert H2O2 to generate O2, which can supply O2 for boosting the 
oxidation process of glucose and alleviating hypoxic microenvironments 
of diabetic wounds. To achieve high POD- and CAT-like activities, the 
particle size of Ru nanozymes is crucial [29–35]. It is commonly 
observed that Ru metal, whether in the form of individual particles or 
loaded onto a support, typically exhibits sizes ranging from 2 to 20 nm 
[28,36–38]. However, the challenge lies in achieving a balance between 
the atomic efficiency of noble metals and their catalytic activities, 
particularly within this range of Ru particle sizes. 
Herein, we devise a difunctional nanozyme with ultrafine Ru nano­
clusters supported on an amorphous NiB alloy (Ru-NiB). The obtained 
Ru-NiB nanozyme has strong POD- and CAT-like activities to convert 
H2O2 into •OH and O2, respectively. After integrated with GOx, it 
potently drives an efficient cascaded catalytic reaction for glucose 
depletion, reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation and O2 generation, 
ultimately enhancing the healing of diabetic wounds (Scheme 1). The 
combination of Ru-NiB and GOx demonstrated a synergistic effect in 
both in vitro and in vivo experiments, effectively inhibiting bacterial 
growth, preventing biofilm formation, and promoting diabetic wound 
healing by reducing inflammation and enhancing angiogenesis. 
2. Results and discussion 
2.1. Synthesis and characterization of Ru-NiB 
Ultrafine Ru clusters loaded onto NiB amorphous alloy was synthe­
sized using a facile one-step reduction method with NaBH4. As shown in 
Fig. 1A, Ru-NiB nanozymes exhibited a network of interconnected 
nanoparticles with an average diameter of 29.9 nm. The presence of the 
Ru element was observed as ultrafine clusters with an average size of 
0.42 nm on amorphous NiB (Fig. 1B), thereby generating abundant 
active sites. The enzyme-like activity is significantly influenced by the 
particle size, with smaller particle sizes resulting in higher levels of 
activity [39,40]. Its Ru loading was determined to be 11.9 wt%. 
Furthermore, the B element in NiB substrate could modify the electron 
charge distribution around Ru to improve its catalytic activity [41]. The 
crystal structure of Ru-NiB nanozymes was analyzed using powder X-ray 
diffraction (XRD) pattern, which revealed a single broadened diffraction 
peak at ~ 44◦(Fig. 1C), indicating an amorphous crystal structure [34]. 
No diffraction peaks associated with Ru-related composites were 
observed, further confirming the ultrafine structure of Ru clusters [42]. 
The elemental distribution of Ru-NiB nanozymes was characterized via 
scanning TEM energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (STEM-EDS). As 
shown in Fig. 1D, the Ru element exhibited uniform distribution 
throughout the amorphous NiB. The surface chemical composition and 
valence states of amorphous Ru-NiB nanozymes were further examined 
using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The XPS survey spectrum 
of Ru-NiB nanozymes revealed the presence of Ni, Ru, B and O elements 
(Fig. 1E). As show in Fig. 1F and S1, the Ni 2p spectra of Ru-NiB and NiB

2.2. Enzymatic activities 
The POD-like activity of the as-synthesized Ru-NiB was evaluated 
using 3,3́,5,5́-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) colorimetry. As shown in 
Fig. 2A, the Ru-NiB group showed a distinct absorption peak at 652 nm 
corresponding to the oxidized form of TMB (ox-TMB), whereas a weak 
absorption was observed at this wavelength in the NiB group. Moreover, 
a gradual increase in POD-like activity was observed within the con­
centration range of 0 to 20 μg/mL (Fig. S2). These findings strongly 
indicate that Ru serves as an active component for POD-like activity. As 
shown in Fig. 2B, the absorbance of ox-TMB significantly increased with 
the increase of H2O2 concentrations, indicating that higher concentra­
tion of H2O2 resulted in higher •OH levels. The steady-state kinetics was 
explored under different H2O2 concentrations and the Michaelis −
Menten curve was shown in Fig. S3. According to the corresponding 
Lineweaver −
Burk double reciprocal curve (Fig. 2C), the Michaelis 
constant (Km) and maximal velocity (Vmax) values were determined to be 
0.47 mM and 3.81 × 10-7 Ms−1, respectively. The Km value of Ru-NiB 
was lower than that of most reported nanozymes, while the Vmax 
value was higher than that of most reported nanozymes (Table S1). 
These findings suggest a strong affinity between Ru-NiB and H2O2, as 
well as a rapid kinetics for the catalytic reaction mimicking POD. In

2.4. Antibacterial effect in vivo 
The in vivo antibacterial efficacy of Ru-NiB was evaluated using an 
animal model of PA-infected wounds (Fig. 4A). Blab/c mice were 
randomly divided into four groups: i) Control; ii) H2O2; iii) Ru-NiB; iv) 
Ru-NiB + H2O2. After various treatments, daily photographs of wounds 
were taken, and changes in wound size as well as mouse weight were 
recorded. As shown in Fig. 4B, the average body weight of mice 
remained relatively stable throughout the treatment period. Meanwhile, 
the hemolysis test was conducted to evaluate the hemocompatibility of 
Ru-NiB nanozymes. The hemolysis rate remained below 1.5 % even 
when the concentration of nanozymes reached up to 100 μg/mL 
(Fig. S10). Furthermore, hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining assays 
of major organs revealed no apparent tissue damage in the treatment 
groups involving Ru-NiB (Fig. 4C and S11). These results collectively 
demonstrated good biocompatibility and biosafety of the Ru-NiB. 
As shown in Fig. 4D and E, the Ru-NiB + H2O2 group demonstrated a 
significantly reduced wound area compared to the other groups. By the 
10th day, remarkable therapeutic efficacy for wound healing was 
observed in the Ru-NiB + H2O2 group, with almost complete healing of 
the wound. After completion of the treatment, the wound skin samples 
from the mice were extracted for H&E and Masson staining to further 
evaluate the efficacy of wound healing. As shown in Fig. 4F, the wound 
in the control group still showed signs of inflammation and skin 
epidermal disorder, while both the H2O2 and Ru-NiB groups showed 
mild inflammation in the wound. More importantly, the Ru-NiB + H2O2 
group exhibited no signs of inflammation and formed a complete 
epidermal layer with newly generated hair follicles, indicating complete 
healing of the wound. As revealed from Masson staining, the control 
group exhibited a small amount of disorganized collagen formation, 
while both the H2O2 and Ru-NiB groups showed some collagen forma­
tion but with inadequate density and organization (Fig. 4F and S12). In 
contrast, the Ru-NiB + H2O2 group demonstrated the highest level of 
collagen formation, which was characterized by a neat and dense 
arrangement. Collectively, these results provide compelling evidence 
that combining Ru-NiB with H2O2 can effectively enhance bactericidal 
effects through POD-like catalytic treatment, thereby boosting wound 
healing. 
2.5. Antibacterial effect of diabetic in vitro 
Inspired by the potent antibacterial performance of Ru-NiB in the 
presence of H2O2, we propose that the combination of Ru-NiB with GOx 
can drive a highly efficient cascaded catalytic reaction in high glucose 
environments (Fig. 5A), in which Ru-NiB harnesses the generated H2O2 
from the glucose oxidation by GOx to sustainably generate •OH for 
antibacterial purposes. Additionally, the CAT-like activity of Ru-NiB can 
supply O2 for the glucose oxidation. The consumption of glucose further 
prevents bacterial proliferation and infection. The added amount of GOx 
was determined based on considerations of both biological safety and 
treatment effects. Various concentrations of GOx were individually 
assessed in the high glucose bacterial medium (Fig. S13), and a con­
centration of 0.4 ng/mL was chosen due to the absence of any observed 
antibacterial effects. As shown in Fig. 5B, the addition of GOx and 
glucose can significantly enhance the absorbance of ox-TMB, indicating 
that glucose oxidation promotes •OH generation. Furthermore, the 
absorbance of ox-TMB was enhanced in the O2-saturated solution and 
suppressed in the argon (Ar)-saturated solution (Fig. 5C), due to the 
involvement of O2 as a reactant in glucose oxidation. Thus, a higher 
concentration of O2 promotes H2O2 generation through glucose oxida­
tion, while simultaneously improving •OH generation in the presence of 
Ru-NiB. As shown in Fig. 5D, the coexistence of Ru-NiB and GOx 
demonstrated a more pronounced reduction in glucose concentration 
compared to GOx alone. Upon the addition of H2O2, the combination of 
Ru-NiB and GOx exhibited the highest glucose consumption, thereby 
highlighting the indispensability and viability of CAT-like activity of Ru- 
NiB for glucose consumption. To further validate this hypothesis, the DO 
concentration of the reaction solution was investigated under various 
reaction conditions. As illustrated in Fig. 5E, DO concentration 
dramatically decreased after adding GOx. However, when supplemented 
with Ru-NiB, a partial restoration of DO concentration was observed. 
Notably, an incremental increase in DO concentration was observed 
with increasing H2O2 concentration. Hence, it could be concluded that 
in high glucose environment, the synergistic effect between bifunctional 
Ru-NiB and GOx enables efficient cascade reactions to sustainably 
generate highly cytotoxic •OH. 
To evaluate the antibacterial effectiveness of Ru-NiB combined with 
GOx in the hyperglycemic microenvironment, a bacterial model was 
established in the TSB medium with glucose concentration of 1 mg/mL. 
As shown in Fig. 5F and S14, the combination of Ru-NiB nanozyme and 
GOx demonstrated a remarkable bactericidal rate (95 %) against PA, 
surpassing the effectiveness observed with either Ru-NiB or GOx alone. 
An intense red fluorescence of PI was clearly observed in PA bacteria 
after treatment with Ru-NiB and GOx (Fig. 5G and S15), indicating a 
significant reduction in bacterial viability. Next, the glucose concen­
tration in the bacterial suspension were measured using a blood glucose 
monitor after various treatments. It was found that the glucose con­
centration significantly decreased after the combined treatment of Ru- 
NiB and GOx, compared to individual treatment with Ru-NiB or GOx 
alone (Fig. 5H). Additionally, Staphylococcus aureus is a prevalent 
bacterium found in diabetic wounds, thus the antibacterial efficacy 
against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) was also 
evaluated. As shown in Fig. S16-17, the combination of Ru-NiB and GOx 
demonstrated remarkable effectiveness in eradicating MRSA. These 
findings suggest that the combination of Ru-NiB nanozyme and GOx can 
effectively achieve antibacterial efficacy in the hyperglycemic micro­
environment due to the occurrence of an efficient cascaded catalytic 
reaction for glucose depletion and ROS generation. 
The hyperglycemic environment facilitates the formation of biofilms. 
But it seriously hinders the effectiveness of antibacterial drugs against 
bacteria, resulting in persistent bacterial infection. Therefore, it is 
crucial to inhibit biofilm formation for effective diabetic wound healing.

biofilm formation, a crystal violet staining assay was conducted. The 
combined treatment of Ru-NiB and GOx demonstrated superior

3. Conclusions 
In summary, a difunctional nanozyme with ultrafine Ru nanoclusters 
supported on an amorphous NiB alloy (Ru-NiB) has been successfully 
synthesized. The as-prepared Ru-NiB nanozymes demonstrate remark­
able POD- and CAT-like activities. Combined with GOx, it drives
