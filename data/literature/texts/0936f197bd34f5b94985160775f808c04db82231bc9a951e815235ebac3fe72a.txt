A B S T R A C T   
Designing long-lasting yet high-efficiency antimicrobial and deodorant agents is an everlasting goal for envi­
ronmental and public health. Here we present the design of AIE-featured Au nanoclusters (NCs) for visible-light- 
driven antibacterial and deodorant applications. Owing to the intriguing AIE traits, the good harvest of visible- 
light, and rich surface chemistry, the AIE-featured Au NCs unprecedentedly exhibit excellent visible-light-driven 
antibacterial activities against gram-positive (≥98.5%) and gram-negative bacteria (≥99.94%), which is resulted 
from their photodynamic producibility of abundant reactive oxygen species including O2
•-, •OH and H2O2 via O2 
reduction and subsequent H2O2 oxidation. In addition, the Au NCs are demonstrated to be biocompatible, and 
easy to be deployed for downstream antibacterial and deodorant applications. For example, the Au NCs-modified 
domestic materials (e.g., latex, ceramic glaze, organic fiber, and clothings) achieve long-lasting antibacterial 
efficiency of 99% and deodorant efficiency of >97.9% under visible-light irradiation. This work may shed light 
on designing novel AIE-featured metal NCs with photodynamic antibacterial and deodorant functions, enabling 
metal NCs and corresponding downstream materials to step into the photodynamic antibacterial and deodorant 
era.   
1. Introduction 
Ultrasmall metal nanoclusters (NCs, ≤3 nm in core size) with 
aggregation-induced emission (AIE), have attracted tremendous 
research attention due to their intriguing attributes such as strong 
photoluminescence (PL) [1–4], long-lived PL lifetimes (in μs level) [5,6], 
rich yet engineerable surface chemistry [7], atomically precise size [8, 
9], tailorable composition [10,11], and benign biocompatibility [12, 
13]. Such attributes of metal NCs enable them to be a novel class of 
functional materials in various fields including environmental protec­
tion [14], energy conversion [15], catalysis [16], photoelectronics [17, 
18], and biomedicine [19–21]. For example, AIE-featured highly lumi­
nescent Au NCs have been widely applied for bioimaging [22,23], bio­
sensing [7,24–26], disease diagnosis and therapy [27–29], intracellular 
fuel production [30], and antibacterial application in biomedical field 
[31,32]. Benefiting from the engineerable trait of metal NCs in both 
their surface shell and metallic core, it provides innumerable opportu­
nities in designing AIE-featured metal NCs with specific functionality, 
which is helpful for addressing the diversified challenging issues of 
many fields. 
In recent years, metal NCs (e.g., Au, Ag and Cu) have been demon­
strated to be effective in killing wide-spectrum bacteria without 
inducing drug resistance of bacteria, which has achieved rapid progress 
in both fundamental and applied studies [33–39]. However, the anti­
bacterial mechanism of those metal NCs are generally based on the 
self-consumption (e.g., metal ion release or cell internalization) that 
induces the production of destructive reactive oxygen species (ROS) for 
bacterial killing, which fates their relatively short antibacterial efficacy. 
This demerit of metal NCs curtails their usage in long-lasting antibac­
terial scenarios, e.g., acting as antibacterial additives to render enduring

long-lasting bactericidal property, such application scenarios usually 
also require deodorant function to eliminate hazardous yet embarrass­

developing novel metal NCs-based functional materials that not only 
inherit intrinsic merits of metal NCs (e.g., broad-spectrum antibacterial 
attribute, good biocompatibility, and none of drug resistance for bac­
teria.) but also give rise to long-lasting antibacterial and deodorant 
functions. Indeed, AIE-featured metal NCs may accommodate this need 
in a different manner. 
Very recently, AIE-featured metal NCs as efficient photosensitizers 
have shown their nascent potential in photocatalytic water splitting and 
intracellular fuel production [30,40–43], which inspires us to explore 
their photodynamic producibility of ROS for long-lasting antibacterial 
and deodorant applications. The persistent photodynamic production of 
ROS by AIE-featured metal NCs could bypass the conventional ROS 
production pathway of metal NCs relying on self-consumption, ensuring 
the long-lasting bacterial inactivation and odors elimination through 
advanced oxidation. To achieve excellent photodynamic performance, 
metal NCs should possess sufficient visible-light harvest, excellent gen­
eration/separation of charge carriers (e.g., excited electrons and holes), 
and abundant surface active sites based on photocatalytic principles 
[44]. However, traditional metal NCs are unserviceable to be good 
photodynamic molecules because of their poor generation/separation of 
charge carriers caused by the weak PL and short PL lifetimes (in ps or ns 
level) [45], although their molecule-like properties (e.g., discrete elec­
tronic bands, tunable HOMO–LUMO transitions) and rich surface 
chemistry are favorable for visible-light harvest and surface photo­
catalytic reactions. By contrast, AIE-featured metal NCs with strong PL 
and long-lived PL lifetime (in μs level) may remedy such limitations of 
traditional analogs by promoting the generation/separation of charge 
carriers for ROS production, achieving the long-lasting photodynamic 
sterilization and deodorization. 
Herein we report the design of a long-lasting yet high-efficiency 
visible-light-driven photosensitizer – AIE-featured Au NCs protected 
by tri-peptide glutathione (GSH), for photodynamic antibacterial and 
deodorant application. Arming with appropriate HOMO-LUMO posi­
tion, strong PL and long-lived charge carrier lifetime, the AIE-featured 
Au NCs are able to photodynamically produce abundant ROS, and 
thus unprecedentedly exhibit excellent visible-light-driven antibacterial 
activities against gram-positive (≥99.8%) and gram-negative bacteria 
(≥96.1%). Moreover, the Au NCs can be easily deployed to functionalize 
diversified domestic materials (e.g., latex, ceramic glaze, fiber, and 
clothings) for superior photodynamic antibacterial (99%) and 
deodorant properties (>97.9%). In addition, the Au NCs also show 
benign biocompatibility, which will increase their acceptance as novel 
photodynamic antibacterial molecules for long-lasting sterilization and 
deodorization in real life. To the best of our knowledge, this is probably 
the first successful attempt in leveraging pristine AIE-featured metal NCs 
for visible-light-driven antibacterial and deodorant applications. Pre­
sented below are the details of this work. 
2. Experimental section 
2.1. Synthesis of AIE-featured Au NCs 
AIE-featured Au NCs were prepared according to a previously re­
ported “mild reduction” procedure [46]. In brief, the freshly prepared 
aqueous solutions of GSH (20 mM, 7.5 mL), and HAuCl4 (20 mM, 5 mL) 
were mixed with 37.5 mL water under gentle stirring (600 rpm). After 
that, the solution was heated at 85 ◦C for 3 h to obtain orange-emitting 
Au NCs. Finally, the obtained Au NCs were purified using desalting 
column with a molecular weight cut-off of 5 kDa. 
2.2. Photodynamic antibacterial experiment 
The gram-negative Escherichia coli ATCC 25922 (E. coli) and Pseu­
domonas aeruginosa ATCC 27853 (P. aeruginosa), and gram-positive 
Staphyloccocus aureus ATCC 6538 (S. aureus) and Bacillus subtilis 
ATCC6633 (B. subtilis) were chosen as bacteria models to evaluate the 
photodynamic bactericidal performance of the AIE-featured Au NCs and 
those Au NCs-functionalized materials under visible-light irradiation. 
All the glassware, medium solution, and reagents used were sterilized in 
an autoclave at 121 ◦C and 103 kPa of pressure for 30 min before 
antibacterial tests. The bacteria on solid LB agar plates were transferred 
to 10 mL liquid Luria-Bertani (LB) culture medium and grown at 37 ◦C 
for 18 h under stirring condition (150 rpm). Subsequently, the bacterial 
culture was centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 8 min to remove the metabo­
lites, and then diluted with 0.9% NaCl solution to prepare the bacterial 
suspension with the OD600 of 0.1 (equivalent to the bacteria concen­
tration of ~106 CFU mL−1; here CFU denotes colony forming units). 
Afterwards, 1.2 mL Au NCs solution (2 mM) was mixed with 3.6 mL of 
bacterial suspension, followed by O2 bubbling and illumination by a 
300 W Xenon lamp (λ ≥400 nm) with a light intensity of 100 mW/cm2 
100 μL of mixed suspension was pipetted at given irradiation time in­
tervals (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, and 30 min), which was spread on the 
freshly prepared LB agar plates and further incubated for at 37 ◦C for 24 
h. Finally, the number of colonies growing on the plate was counted 
through visual inspection. In addition, a series of control experiments (e. 
g., bacteria treated with or without antibacterial materials under visible- 
light irradiation or dark condition in presence or absence of O2) were 
performed under the same conditions. 
The photodynamic antibacterial activity of the Au NCs- 
functionalized materials (i.e., latex, ceramic glaze, organic fiber and 
clothings) was assessed using E. coli as the bacterium model. 2 mL 
bacterial suspension (OD600 = 0.1, here OD600 refers to the optical 
density of bacterial suspension at 600 nm) was uniformly introduced on 
the surface of these Au NCs-functionalized materials (3 × 3 cm). After 
30 min of visible-light illumination, the number of bacterial colonies 
was analyzed by the plate counting method. 
2.3. Photodynamic deodorant experiment 
The photodynamic deodorization performance of Au NCs- 
functionalized clothings was evaluated using ammonia (NH3), and 
acetic acid (HAc) as the odor models. In brief, a piece of the Au NCs- 
functionalized clothings (10 cm × 10 cm) was placed in a gas 
container (5 L) containing NH3 and HAc, followed by 120 min visible- 
light irradiation at room temperature. The concentrations of NH3, 
HAc, and degraded products (e.g., NO, NO2, and CO2) were analyzed 
using corresponding gas detection tubes. In addition, the container was 
cleaned with water to recover the nitrate ions (NO3
−), and the concen­
tration of NO3
−was detected by ion chromatography. 
The materials, instrumentation, and other experimental details, 
including the functionalization of latex, ceramic glaze, organic fiber and 
clothing with the Au NCs, ROS assay, electrochemical measurement, and 
cytotoxicity are available in Supporting Information. 
3. Results and discussion 
3.1. Synthesis and characterization of the AIE-featured Au NCs 
In this study, orange-emitting GSH-protected Au NCs with AIE were 
designed as the molecular photodynamic antibacterial and deodorant 
agent, and synthesized according to our previously reported “mild 
reduction” method with minor modification (Fig. 1a, and see detailed 
procedure in Experimental Section) [46]. As shown in Fig. 1b, the 
as-obtained Au NCs solution is yellowish (item 1 of the inset), and shows 
a shoulder absorption band at ~400 nm with the absorption onset of 
510 nm in the UV–visible absorption spectrum (black curve), which

suggests the formation of ultrasmall Au NCs instead of large-size Au 
nanoparticles with a typical surface plasmon resonance absorption at 
~520 nm [47,48]. In this aspect, transmission electron microscopy 
(TEM) image provides direct evidence that the as-synthesized Au NCs 
are below 2 nm in size with an average size of ~1.5 nm (Fig. 1c). 
More interestingly, the as-synthesized Au NCs emit intense orange PL 
(item 2 in the inset of Fig. 1b) with an emission peak at 615 nm (red 
curve, excited at 410 nm), revealing a large Stokes shift exceeding 200 
nm. Moreover, the PL quantum yield (PLQY) of the Au NCs was deter­
mined to be 12.4% in solid, which is several orders of magnitude higher 
than that of AIE-free metal NCs (typically 0.001–0.1%). Meanwhile, 
increasing the solution viscosity by introducing polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) 
constrains the nonradiative relaxation of AIE-featured Au NCs, thus 
enhancing the PL intensity of AIE-featured Au NCs (Fig. S1). In addition, 
as shown in Fig. 1d, the PL decay profile shows that the Au NCs contain 
two PL emitters with long-lived PL lifetimes of 9.13 μs (13.66%) and 
1.74 μs (86.34%), which are also higher than that of AIE-free metal NCs 
(i.e., in ps or ns level). Such μs-level PL lifetimes of the Au NCs may be 
derived from ligand-to-metal or ligand-to-metal-metal charge transfer 
and the consequent radiative relaxation [11,46]. Furthermore, X-ray 
photoelectron spectrum (XPS) result manifests that the dominant Au 
species in the Au NCs is Au(I) with a ratio of ~68.7% (Fig. 1e), 
concurrently suggesting a high GSH-to-Au ratio in the Au NCs. It should 
be mentioned that both the strong PL, large Stokes shift, long-lived PL 
lifetime, and high metal(I) proportion are typical characteristics of 
AIE-featured metal NCs, corroborating the successful synthesis of 
AIE-featured Au NCs. Of note, the AIE traits of the Au NCs may be 
beneficial to their photodynamic activity based on the following aspects: 
1) the high PLQY means the easy generation of photoinduced charge 
carriers; 2) the large Stokes shift (>200 nm) and long-lived PL lifetime 
favor the separation/migration of charge carriers; 3) the high GSH-to-Au 
ratio indicates abundant surface active sites, excellent stability, and 
benign biocompatibility. Taken together, such AIE traits and the good 
harvest of visible-light of the Au NCs may ensure their superior photo­
dynamic antibacterial and deodorant performance.

the Au NCs after 3 h visible-light irradiation (Fig. S6). Such results 
highlight the irreplaceability of light illumination and O2, underlining 
the occurrence of O2-involved photocatalytic ROS production, making 
possible the application of AIE-featured Au NCs as photodynamic anti­
bacterials in daily life with the assistance of sunlight and air. Aside from 
antibacterial activity, the AIE-featured Au NCs could also kill fungus (e. 
g., Monilia albicans) with an excellent antifungal efficacy of 96.3% 
(Fig. S7), which further broadens the application scope of the AIE- 
featured Au NCs. 
3.3. The antibacterial mechanism of AIE-featured Au NCs 
Why AIE-featured Au NCs could have such excellent photodynamic 
antibacterial activities? Tackling this question is critically important 
since it not only can deepen fundamental understandings on the 
photodynamic antibacterial mechanism, but also provides guidelines in 
the design of high-performance photodynamic antibacterial metal NCs 
as well as in the downstream antibacterial applications. To answer this 
question, we concentrated our focus on the following aspects: 1) deci­
phering the HOMO-LUMO transitions of AIE-featured Au NCs to sort out 
the plausible photodynamic reactions for ROS production; 2) assessing 
the specific contributions of different ROS species to bacterial killing. 
Firstly, the DPV analysis was employed to ascertain the HOMO-LUMO 
positions of the Au NCs. As shown in Fig. 3a, the LUMO energy level 
of the Au NCs is deduced to be −0.62 V (vs. Reversible Hydrogen Elec­
trode (RHE), pH 2.78), which is lower than the potentials for oxygen 
reduction (O2/O2
•-, −0.21 V vs. RHE, pH 2.78, and O2/H2O2, 0.53 V vs. 
RHE, pH 2.78), satisfying the thermodynamic requirements of O2
•- and 
H2O2 production via O2 reduction. This speculation is evidenced by the 
time-resolved transient PL decay profiles of the Au NCs in O2 atmo­
sphere (Fig. 3b), where the Au NCs show shorter PL lifetime in O2 at­
mosphere (τave = 4.97 μs) than that in Ar atmosphere (τave = 5.09 μs, 
Fig. 1d) due to the preferential electron transfer from excited Au NCs to 
O2 molecules. In addition, the HOMO level was determined to be 1.33 V 
(vs. RHE, pH 2.78; Fig. 3a), which is higher than the potentials of water 
oxidation (H2O/O2, 1.06 V vs. RHE, pH 2.78) and H2O2 oxidation 
(H2O2/•OH, 0.97 V vs. RHE, pH 2.78), indicating the photodynamic 
production of O2 via H2O oxidation and •OH via H2O2 oxidation by the 
leaving holes (h+) in the HOMO position of the Au NCs. Taken together, 
AIE-featured Au NCs with decent HOMO-LUMO transition are able to 
photodynamically produce various ROS including O2
•-, H2O2, and •OH, 
which is the mainspring for visible-light-driven bacterial killing and 
deodorization. Moreover, one may notice that O2 could be simulta­
neously produced via H2O oxidation by leaving h+ in the HOMO posi­
tion of the Au NCs, which could facilitate the O2-involved photodynamic

After identifying the photodynamic production pathways of ROS (i. 
e., O2
•-, H2O2 and •OH) of the Au NCs, we further evaluated the contri­
butions of different ROS species to bacterial killing by using E. coli strain 
as the bacterium model and the luminescent 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescein 
(DCF) as a ROS-sensitive probe which correlates to the ROS concentra­
tion. As displayed in Fig. 3c, the PL intensity of DCF in the sample 
treated with the Au NCs in the presence of visible-light and O2, is 
approximately a 2–3 fold higher than that of other control groups. This 
result indicates that both the light illumination and O2 facilitated Au 
NCs to achieve the highest ROS level among these photocatalytic sys­
tems, which is also consistent with the photodynamic antibacterial 
performance shown in Fig. 2. Meanwhile, benefiting from the ultrasmall 
size, ultrahigh surface-to-volume, excellent light-harvesting ability, high 
PLQY and long-lived PL lifetimes, AIE-featured Au NCs exhibit higher 
ROS generation efficiency than commercial photosensitizers (e.g., TiO2, 
and C3N4) under the same condition (Fig. S8), which further highlights 
their promising potential in downstream antibacterial applications. In 
addition, two typical ROS scavengers (i.e., 4-hydroxy-2,2,6,6-tetra­
methyl-piperidinooxy (TEMPOL) as the scavenger of O2
•-, and 2-propanol 
(IPA) as the scavenger of •OH) were separately introduced into the 
bacteria suspension to distinguish the specific contributions of different 
ROS (e.g., O2
•-, H2O2 and •OH) to bacterial killing. As shown in Fig. 3d 
and Fig. S9, while the gross ROS could achieve 99.94% of antibacterial 
efficacy, O2
•-, •OH and H2O2 contribute to 25.02%, 51.48% and 23.44% 
of antibacterial efficacy, respectively. Moreover, the •OH is identified as 
the main killer for bacteria (Fig. 3d), which may be due to its stronger 
oxidation ability than O2
•- and H2O2 [49]. Additionally, the control ex­
periments suggest that the introduction of scavengers has negligible 
influence on bacterial growth (Fig. S10). 
Upon uncovering the photodynamic antibacterial mechanism of AIE- 
featured Au NCs, we realized that the AIE traits of metal NCs are so 
important for their photodynamic antibacterial activity that the same 
application can be extended to other AIE-featured metal NCs. For 
instance, another AIE-featured Au22 (GSH)18 NCs with intense red PL 
could also display remarkable photodynamic antibacterial performance 
(Figs. S11–S12). This inference was further supported by an control 
experiment where the same ligand-protected but AIE-free Au25 (GSH)18 
NCs were employed as photodynamic antibacterial, and no visible 
photodynamic antibacterial efficacy could be detected (Figs. S13–S14). 
More importantly, with the unambiguous photodynamic antibacterial 
mechanism in our mind, we are able to further optimize the AIE-featured 
metal NCs for better antibacterial performance. For example, by doping 
Ag species into the AIE-featured Au NCs, the PL intensity could be 
enhanced to 3.3 times (Fig. S15), which further elevated the photody­
namic antibacterial activity from 99.94% to 99.997% (i.e., 4.57 orders 
of magnitude enhancement in reducing CFU; Fig. S16). On the basis, we 
unprecedentedly demonstrated that AIE-featured metal NCs have 
became novel photodynamic antibacterial molecules, which enables 
metal NCs-based molecular antibacterials to step into the photodynamic 
antibacterial era. 
3.4. Cytotoxicity assay 
Owing to the biofriendly attribute of GSH ligand with antioxidation 
as well as the bioinert nature of Au species, AIE-featured Au NCs show 
excellent biocompatibility, which is evidenced by the cytotoxicity assay 
of the Au NCs on HeLa cells. Firstly, we performed LIVE/DEAD viability 
assay to evaluate the cytotoxicity of the Au NCs, in which the alive cells 
appear green PL while those dead cells are red ones. As shown in Fig. 4a, 
the cells treated with the Au NCs present strong green PL of living cells 
and almost invisible red PL of dead cells, which is comparable with the 
control group treated with ultrapure water, suggesting the excellent 
biocompatibility of the Au NCs. Additionally, the cell proliferation kit 
(MTT) test reveals that the cell viability in the presence of the Au NCs is 
up to 98% (Fig. 4b), which is close to that of the positive control group 
(ultrapure water) but significantly higher than that of the negative 
control group (Tween 20, 24.8% cell viability), corroborating the low 
cytotoxicity of the Au NCs.

3.5. Photodynamic antibacterial and deodorant applications of Au NCs- 
functionalized materials 
One salient merit of the AIE-featured Au NCs is their easy deploy­
ment in downstream applications partially due to their ultrasmall size as 
well as rich surface chemistry. For demonstration purpose, we selected 
three representative domestic materials as substrates to incorporate with 
the Au NCs towards long-lasting photodynamic antibacterial applica­
tions, i.e., latex (raw material of rubber products such as gloves), 
ceramic glaze (raw material of ceramic products), and organic fiber (raw 
material of textiles). As shown in Fig. 5a–c, introducing AIE-featured Au 
NCs (2–4 wt%) into these matrix materials give rise to excellent anti­
bacterial activity against E. coli under visible-light illumination, killing 
more than 99% of bacteria (Fig. S17). It should be emphasized that the 
antibacterial test of such Au NCs-functionalized materials was carried 
out under visible-light illumination but without O2 bubbling. The 
exemption of requiring O2 bubbling for those materials, on one hand, is 
ascribed to the ultrasmall size and ultrahigh surface-to-volume ratio of 
Au NCs that made sufficient O2 absorption possible on the surface of the 
Au NCs; on the other hand, allows them more convenient for practical 
antibacterial applications. 
To further investigate the antibacterial behavior of these hybrid 
materials, the Au NCs-functionalized fiber fabricated by electrospinning 
(see details in experimental section) was chosen in response to this issue. 
As shown in Fig. 5d–h, the TEM and EDS mapping results of the Au NCs- 
functionalized fiber disclose that the Au NCs are evenly dispersed in the 
fiber without deforming the fiber structure, which thanks to the ul­
trasmall size of the Au NCs. In addition, upon the treatment of Au NCs- 
functionalized fiber, the cell morphology of S. aureus changes from 
plump and smooth (Fig. 5i) to atrophic and wrinkled (Fig. 5j), which 
suggests the destruction of the cell membrane by ROS, causing the 
apoptosis of bacteria. Additionally, the peptidoglycan membranes of 
gram-negative bacteria are thinner than that of gram-positive ones, and 
thus the gram-negative bacteria could be more easily inactivated. This 
may be the reason for the better antibacterial efficacy of Au NCs against 
gram-negative bacteria than gram-positive counterparts (Fig. 2a and 
Figs. S2–S5). Taken together, these results illustrated that the intro­
duction of AIE-featured Au NCs could grant such matrix materials 
excellent photodynamic antibacterial property, showing great promise 
in the downstream antibacterial applications.

Indeed, the above success in designing Au NCs-functionalized anti­
bacterial materials encouraged us to assess the possibility of using AIE- 
featured Au NCs for more downstream antibacterial and deodorant ap­
plications. Therefore, we aimed to introduce novel photodynamic anti­
bacterial and deodorant attributes to clothings such as outdoor sport 
suits via simply modifying the clothing surface by the Au NCs. The 
reason for endowing clothing with antibacterial and deodorant func­
tions is that the clothings are commonly considered as a breeding 
ground for bacteria. Here AIE-featured Au NCs were chemically grafted 
on commercial clothing via a simple 1-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-3-eth­
ylcarbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC)/N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) 
crosslinking strategy. As shown in Fig. 6a, the Au NCs-modified clothing 
shows excellent antibacterial activity against E. coli under visible-light 
irradiation with a bactericidal efficiency of >99%, while pristine 
clothing exhibits negligible antibacterial performance under the same 
conditions. More surprisingly, the Au NCs-modified clothing also shows 
outstanding deodorant activity by using NH3 and HAc as odor models 
(Note: They are the main components causing embarrassing odors and 
could be produced by bacterial metabolism). As shown in Fig. 6b, the 
pristine clothing merely removes less than 10% of NH3 and HAc under 
120 min visible-light irradiation, whereas the Au NCs-modified clothing 
could achieve deodorization efficiencies of >99.8% for NH3 and 97.9% 
for HAc. Furthermore, corresponding gas assay reveals that the NH3 
could be photodecomposed into nitrogen oxides (e.g., NO, and NO2; 
Fig. 6c), and further oxidized into non-toxic NO3
−(Fig. 6d); the gas assay 
for HAc discloses that the HAc was photocatalytically converted to CO2 
(Fig. 6e). In this way, the Au NCs-modified clothing is confirmed to be 
efficient in both photodynamic bacterial killing and deodorization 
(Fig. 6f), which may be a preview of upcoming commercial clothings 
with antibacterial and deodorant functions. Additionally, the chemical 
states of Au species in the Au NCs-modified clothing do not show marked 
change after 3 months of storage at room temperature (Fig. S18), sug­
gesting the excellent stability of Au NCs-modified clothing. More 
importantly, the bacterial killing and deodorization of these materials 
are based on their photodynamic production of ROS instead of self- 
consumption-induced ROS, guaranteeing their long-lasting down­
stream antibacterial and deodorant applications. 
4. Conclusion 
In summary, we have developed a long-lasting yet high-efficiency 
visible-light-driven photosensitizer – AIE-featured Au NCs, for photo­
dynamic antibacterial and deodorant application. Owing to the 
intriguing AIE traits (i.e., strong PL, long-lived PL lifetime, and large 
Stockes shift), the good harvest of visible-light, and rich surface chem­
istry, the AIE-featured Au NCs could photodynamically produce abun­
dant ROS including O2
•-, •OH and H2O2 via O2 reduction and subsequent 
H2O2 oxidation, made possible visible-light-driven bacterial killing 
against 
gram-positive 
(≥98.5%) 
and 
gram-negative 
bacteria 
(≥99.94%). In addition, the Au NCs also exhibited benign biocompati­
bility. The ultrasmall size and rich surface chemistry allowed their easy 
deployment in downstram antibacterial and deodorant applications, and 
the Au NCs-modified domestic materials (e.g., latex, ceramic glaze, 
organic fiber, and clothings) achieved long-lasting antibacterial (99%) 
and deodorant (97.9%) activities under visible-light irradiation. This 
study is interesting because it not only provides an unprecedented 
paradigm in designing novel AIE-featured metal NCs with photody­
namic antibacterial and deodorant functions, but also demonstrates the 
great promise of metal NCs in downstream antibacterial and deodorant 
applications.
