Abstract 
Pathogenic bacterial infection, especially in the wound, may threaten human 
health. Developing new antibacterial materials for wound healing is still urgent. Metal 
nanoclusters have been explored as a novel antibacterial agent. Herein, biomolecule 
gelatin was chosen as a substrate and functionalized with gold/silver clusters for 
bacterial killing. Through a simple amidation reaction, gold/silver clusters were 
successfully conjugated in gelatin substrate to obtain Au/Ag@gelatin sponge. The 
presence of gold/silver clusters modified the porous structure of gelatin. Thus, the 
water absorption and water retention of Au/Ag@gelatin sponge were enhanced. More 
importantly, the gold/silver clusters show aggregation-enhanced emission and strong 
reactive oxygen generation, that endow Au/Ag@gelatin sponge good antibacterial 
property. The good physical performance and favorable bactericidal activity of 
Au/Ag@gelatin sponge suggest its potential for applying as a wound dressing. 
 
Keywords: metal nanocluster, gelatin, biotechnology, reactive oxygen species, 
antibacterial

the exterior environment to prevent infection of foreign pathogens.[1] If the skin is 
injured, infection on open wounds may lead to serious tissue necrosis and even the 
life-threatening complications.[2] Therefore, the treatment of wounds to prevent the 
invasion of pathogens is particularly important. During wound treatment, the strategy

discrete energy levels.[26] Thus, they exhibit strong absorption in the visible regions.

The absorbed energy by metal nanoclusters is transferred to ground state oxygen 
molecules to produce ROS.[27] Furthermore, noble metal nanomaterials have been 
wildly used in biological filed owing to their biocompatibility.[28-30] Thus, 
considering the biocompatibility, thiol-containing amino acids, peptides, and proteins 
have developed as functional ligands for preparing metal nanoclusters.[31-33] As a 
protein, gelatin molecule consists of a variety of amino acids[34]. Thus, carboxyl 
groups and amino-groups in both gelatin and the functional ligands of metal 
nanoclusters can provide “active sites” for adhesive bonds formation[35], which 
enables metal nanoclusters to be easily modified on gelatin substrate. 
In this work, we designed and prepared gelatin sponge functionalized with 
gold/silver clusters for bacterial killing. As shown in Figure 1, gold nanoclusters (Au 
NCs) were synthesized by a simple one-pot method, in which glutathione (GSH) 
acted as a reducing agent and a thiol-ligand. The addition of silver ions induced the 
Au NCs to aggregate and form gold/silver clusters (Au/Ag clusters). By the amidation 
reaction between gelatin and GSH ligand of Au/Ag clusters, Au/Ag clusters were 
loaded in gelatin substrate to obtain Au/Ag@gelatin sponge in a facile and green 
method. Take advantage of the photosensitive properties of Au/Ag clusters, the white 
lights can activate clusters to yield ROS, thus realizing the antibacterial purpose. The 
prepared Au/Ag@gelatin sponge contains biocompatible constituents, possesses good 
water absorbency and water retention, and allows the efficient bacterial killing. These 
features suggest its potential for applying as a wound dressing.

2.2 Preparation of Au/Ag@gelatin sponge 
The Au NCs were synthesized following the published method.[36] HAuCl4 (20 
mM, 500 μL) and GSH (100 mM, 150 μL) were mixed and stirred with 4.35 mL of 
ultrapure water at 500 rpm. The temperature was set from 25 ℃ to 70 ℃. After 24 
hours of stirring, the solution turned a pale yellow color to produce Au NCs. Then, the 
solution was dialyzed and stored at 4 ℃. Au/Ag clusters were obtained by adding 
AgNO3 (2 mM, 0.5 mL) to Au NCs (0.8 mM, 5 mL) and stirring rapidly (1000 rpm) for 
15 min. Based on the amidation reaction, 1-mL Au/Ag clusters solution was added 
into the gelatin aqueous solution (4%, 3 mL). Then, EDC (1 M) and NHS (0.4 M) 
were injected to the mixed solution at a ratio of 1:40, and gently stirred (300 rpm) at 
37 °C for 10 min to fully crosslink to the gelled state. The gel was frozen at -20 °C for 
12 h and then placed in a freeze dryer for lyophilization for 12 h to obtain 
Au/Ag@gelatin sponge.

2.4 ROS production of Au/Ag clusters  
The production of ROS can be demonstrated by detecting the emission intensity 
of fluorescent 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescein (DCF).[15] Firstly, a NaOH solution (10 mM, 
2 mL) was added to a DCFH-DA ethanol solution (1 mM, 500 μL). After activation at 
25 °C for 30 min, the solution was diluted with 10 mL of PBS to obtain 
2',7'-dichlorofluorescin solution, which was then stored at -20 °C under dark 
condition. In order to compare the ability to generate ROS, 100 μL of deionized 
aqueous, Au NCs and Au/Ag cluster were separately added to 1 mL of 
2',7'-dichlorofluorescin solution, and irradiated under a white light source with an 
optical density of 1 mW/cm2. The fluorescence intensity of the samples was measured 
every minute and each experiment repeated three times.

crystals.[39]. After adding silver ions, the TEM image in Figure 2b shows the forming 
of aggregates (Au/Ag clusters). The aggregates have a spherical shape with an

Furthermore, we measured the absorption spectrum of Au/Ag clusters. The 
absorption peak of Au/Ag clusters located at about 400 nm (Figure 3a). Compared 
with the absorption spectrum of Au NCs, the peak at 400 nm was attributed to the Au 
NCs. The absorption peak does not change after adding silver ions. Moreover, there is 
no observed absorption of Ag2S[42] in the infrared region. It further indicates the 
formation of Au/Ag clusters through Ag(I)–thiolate bond or metallophilic Ag(I)–Au(I) 
bond. The emission intensity of Au/Ag clusters was enhanced 5.6-fold as that of Au 
NCs (Figure 3b). And a red-shift of 21 nm in emission peak was observed for Au/Ag 
clusters. Considering the effect ligand-to metal charge transfer or ligand-to

led to the aggregation of Au NCs and formation of large Au(I)/Ag(I)–thiolate motifs 
with aggregation-induced emission effect.[41] Thus, the change in ligand-to metal 
charge transfer resulted in an enhanced emission with red-shift emission for Au/Ag 
clusters.

3.2 Preparation and Characterization of Au/Ag@gelatin  
As both gelatin and Au/Ag clusters contain free carboxylic and amine groups, 
EDC/NHS participates in their reaction to form amide bonds and obtain cross-linked 
Au/Ag@gelatin. As shown in Figure 4, the absorption band of amide I is mostly 
because of C=O stretching vibrations, and amide II absorption is a result of N-H 
bending vibration coupled with C-N stretching vibrations, and amide III is due to the 
N-H bending vibration.[44] Comparing to the mixture of Au/Ag clusters and gelatin, 
the characteristic bands of amide II and III shift to lower wavenumbers for 
Au/Ag@gelatin with a reduced intensity ratio of amide I to amide II. The 
phenomenon may due to the new C-N bonds formation[45] and the crosslinking 
process was conducted. Moreover, compared with crosslinked gelatin, a blue-shift of 
amide II and III and a lower intensity ratio of amide I to amide II for Au/Ag@gelatin 
may indicate the Au/Ag clusters were involved in the crosslinking process.

3.3 Antibacterial Assays of Au/Ag@gelatin 
Based on the good physical performance of Au/Ag@gelatin, the antibacterial 
property was then examined. As GSH protected Au NCs can act as photosensitizers, 
they transfer absorbed light energy to surrounding oxygen molecules to produce 
singlet oxygen (1O2)[27]. Thus, we detected the ROS generation of Au/Ag clusters by 
using 2ʹ,7ʹ-dichlorofluorescin (DCFH) probe. The generated ROS oxidize DCFH to 
produce fluorescent DCF. As shown in Figure 8, we monitor the emission intensity of 
DCH at 522 nm. In the presence of Au NCs or Au/Ag clusters, the intensity of DCH 
gradually increases with the irradiation time. Both of them are higher than the blank 
group. The results confirmed the ROS generation from Au NCs and Au/Ag clusters. 
However, the DCF emission for the Au/Ag clusters increases more rapidly than that of 
Au NCs. This may likely because of the aggregation-enhanced emission for Au/Ag

4. Conclusions 
In this work, gelatin sponge functionalized with gold/silver clusters was prepared 
for antibacterial application. The presence of silver ions induced aggregation of GSH 
protected Au NCs to form Au/Ag clusters along with aggregation-enhanced emission. 
The Au/Ag clusters were conjugated with gelatin by a typical amidation reaction, 
thereby preparing Au/Ag@gelatin in a green and facile way. Especially, the 
conjugated Au/Ag clusters modified the porous structure of gelatin, and further 
improved their water absorption and water retention. Beyond that, the photosensitive 
property of Au/Ag@gelatin enables Au/Ag@gelatin to produce ROS with white light 
irradiation. With the ROS generation, the Au/Ag@gelatin exhibited strong
