Recovery and in-situ reduction of precious metals
by graphdiyne and graphdiyne oxide for antibacterial use

Abstract Based on the in-situ self-reduction and chemical
stability,
graphdiyne
(GDY)
and
graphdiyne
oxide
(GDYO) are used as trapping agents to investigate the
ability for recovering Au3?, Ag?, and Pd2? under different
pH values and interfering ions. Under strong acidity at
pH = 1, these two agents demonstrate high select recovery
towards the three precious metal ions, which could be in-
situ reduced to nanoparticles (NPs). In addition, super-
paramagnetic Fe3O4 NPs are deposited on the surfaces of
GDY and GDYO. The magnetic responses enable GDY-
Fe3O4 to recover precious metals conveniently and efﬁ-
ciently by the aid of an external magnetic ﬁeld. This study
also conﬁrms the antibacterial activity of the as-recovered
NPs deposited on GDY and GDYO against Escherichia
coli and Staphylococcus aureus (1 9 105 colony-forming
unit (CFU)ml-1), and the antibacterial rates are 100%.
This strategy of recovering precious metals and subse-
quently reusing to combat pathogens will be of great sig-
niﬁcance for environmental remediation and biomedical
applications.

Precious metals; Antibacterial
1 Introduction
Precious metals (PMs), such as gold (Au3?), silver (Ag?),
and platinum (Pd2?), are important ingredients of metallic
materials [1]. Moreover, PMs are widely used in elec-
tronics [2], catalysts [3, 4], sensors [5] and medical mate-
rials [6]. But PMs are so rare and expensive that
researchers are committed to extracting PMs from waste
and exploring new strategies for efﬁcient recovery of PMs
[7, 8]. However, due to the co-existence of interfering ions,
such as copper (Cu2?), nickel (Ni2?) and zinc (Zn2?),
selective recovery of PMs from waste is a major challenge.
At present, the recovery methods for PMs include elec-
trolytic recovery, solvent extraction, ion exchange, and
reduction exchange [1, 9–11]. These above traditional
methods for recovering PMs from waste solution involve
the repeated use of various low-efﬁciency or low-selec-
tivity reagents, which inevitably generate a large amount of

Adsorption is a potential method for recovering PMs
with the advantages of clean working conditions, reusable
adsorbents, excellent adsorption efﬁciency and no sec-
ondary waste [11–13]. Many adsorbents have been devel-
oped to adsorb and recover metal ions. Among the various
adsorbents that effectively capture metals, two-dimensional
nanomaterials are becoming the most popular adsorbents
and are capable of adsorbing metal ions easier than other
adsorbent because of their smaller size and larger surface
area
[14–16]. Graphdiyne
(GDY)
and
its derivative
(GDYO) are an emerging class of two-dimensional carbon
nanomaterial, which is cheap and friendly to the environ-
ment [17–19]. In the following years, GDY and GDYO
have been applied to catalysis, storage and electronic
products [19–26]. Most importantly, due to their electron-
rich structure, GDY and GDYO exhibit a speciﬁc reduced
afﬁnity for metal ions in-situ [27–30]. GDY and GDYO are
similar to the carbon allotrope graphene, which have the
large surface area and more adsorption sites toward metal
ions [31, 32]. Especially, the abundant surface functional
groups of GDYO, such as epoxy, hydroxyl groups and
carbonyl groups, endow them high binding capability with
metal ions, as a result showing high adsorption efﬁciency
toward these ions [31]. In addition, large triangular holes of
GDY and GDYO allow them to easily adsorb the metal
ions [33]. Hence, PMs ions (Au3?, Ag?, and Pd2?) can be
adsorbed and reduced to lower valance and metallic states
on the surface of GDY and GDYO. Meanwhile, GDY and
GDYO exhibit high chemical stability under strong acid
and alkali conditions, and can recover precious metal ions
selectively in highly acidic or alkali environments and
organic solvents [20, 34].
As depicted in Fig. 1a, b, this study utilized the reduc-
tion ability of GDY and GDYO to in-situ recover metal
ions, including Au3?, Ag?, Pd2?, Ca2?, Mg2?, Zn2?,
Cu2?, Co2? and Ni2?, from aqueous solution with high
selectivity. Then, the superparamagnetic Fe3O4 nanoparti-
cles (NPs) can be deposited on the surfaces of GDY and
GDYO by hydrothermal synthesis. With an applied mag-
netic ﬁeld, GDY-Fe3O4 and GDYO-Fe3O4 make the
recovery of PMs more efﬁcient. Furthermore, considering
the strong antibacterial ability of the PM NPs, this study
veriﬁed that GDY and GDYO deposited with PM (GDY-
PM and GDYO-PM) can be against pathogenic bacteria
E. coli [35–39] (Fig. 1c).

2.2 Characterization
The elemental content and surface chemistry of GDY-PM
and GDYO-PM were analyzed by transmission electron
microscopy (TEM, Tecnai G2 20, FEIE) and X-ray pho-
toelectron spectrometer (XPS, ESCALAB 250Xi, Thermo
Scientiﬁc). The morphology and chemical composition of
GDY-PM and GDYO-PM were observed by energy dis-
persive X-ray spectrometer (EDX). Atomic force micro-
scopy (AFM) was also used to conduct the thickness of
GDY-PM and GDYO-PM. The content of metal recovered
by GDY-PM and GDYO-PM was determined by atomic
absorption spectrophotometer (AAS).
2.3 Synthesis of GDY and GDYO
The preparation of GDY powder was carried out according
to the previous work reported by Li et al. [20]. 2.0 mmol
TBAF, 0.40 mmol Pd(PPh3)4, and 20 mmol (trimethylsi-
lyl)ethynylzinc chloride were added sequentially to 40 ml
of THF for 72 h under nitrogen atmosphere. After
1.0 molL-1 HCl was added, the crude product was
extracted with ethyl acetate. The organic layer was washed
with brine and anhydrous MgSO4, and the solvent was
evaporated and puriﬁed by column chromatography. The
monomer of hexakis[(trimethylsilyl)ethynyl]benzene was
synthesized.
0.066 mmol
hexakis[(trimethylsi-
lyl)ethynyl]benzene and 0.40 ml TBAF were added to
15 ml THF at 8 C for 10 min, then diluted with ethyl
acetate, washed with brine and anhydrous Na2SO4. The
solvent was removed under vacuum, diluted with 25 ml of
pyridine, and placed in copper foil, and 50 ml pyridine was
added slowly at 60 C for 24 h. The mixture was stirred
under nitrogen atmosphere for 48 h, and the copper foil
was washed with acetone and N,N-dimethylformamide
(DMF) in turn to form a black ﬁlm on the copper foil,
which is the product of GDY.
About 20 mg of GDY product was ground in an agate
mortar for 10 min to make a homogeneous powder. This
powder was dispersed in 3.0 ml ultrapure water and soni-
cated in an ice bath for 10 h. The above GDY suspension
was centrifuged and freeze-dried for 24 h. Then 2.0 ml
H2O2 (30%) and 6.0 ml concentrated H2SO4 (98%) were
added to 20 mg of sonicated GDY powder, and incubated
for 5 h in an ice bath. The product GDYO was obtained by
dialysis and freeze-dried for 24 h.
2.4 Synthesis of GDY-Fe3O4 and GDYO-Fe3O4
About 15 mg of GDY (or GDYO) was dissolved in 6.0 ml
ethylene glycol by ultrasonication and a homogeneous
solution was obtained; then, 30 mg FeCl36H2O, 0.50 ml
of NH3H2O, and 18.33 mg PVP were added into the
solution under ultrasonication and stirring. The resulting
solution was then transferred to a polytetraﬂuoroethylene
reaction kettle, which was sealed and heated at 180 C for
20 h. After that, the precipitate was collected and washed
with ultrapure water three times, and dried in a vacuum
drying oven at 50 C for 12 h. The product GDY-Fe3O4 (or
GDYO-Fe3O4) was obtained.

3.3 Recovery efﬁciency of PM ions
The recovery of metal ions by GDY and GDYO was
investigated under different pH conditions. Firstly, the
extraction of Au3?, Ag? and Pd2? from aqueous solution
onto GDY and GDYO was performed at pH = 1, 6 and 12
(Fig. 5a, b). It can be observed that GDY and GDYO
worked effectively over a wide range of pH, and it pre-
sented the highest recovery rate (95% and 86%) for Au
NPs at pH = 1. At pH = 6 and 12, the recovery rate of
GDY-Au and GDYO-Au were 65% and 20%, respectively.
The recovery rates of Ag? by GDY and GDYO were both
above 95% at pH = 1, 6, and 12. The recovery rate of Pd2?
by GDY and GDYO was 30% and 40% at pH = 1, and
more than 95% at pH = 6 and 12, respectively.
Additionally, GDY and GDYO were investigated for the
recovery of other ions, including Ca2?, Ni2?, Pb2?, Zn2?,
Mg2?, Cu2? and Co2. Figure 5c, d shows that GDY and
GDYO had basically no effect on the recovery rate of the
above ions at pH = 1. The recovery rates of the above ions

4 Conclusion
In summary, we developed GDY and GDYO as effective
reduction agents based on their adsorption capability. To
further demonstrate the selective reduction of GDY and
GDYO, the non-precious metal ions were selected as
impurity ions. The results indicate that GDY and GDYO
can recover PMs efﬁciently and have no effect on the
recovery of other interfering metal ions in acidic condi-
tions. After loading Fe3O4 onto GDY and GDYO, the
