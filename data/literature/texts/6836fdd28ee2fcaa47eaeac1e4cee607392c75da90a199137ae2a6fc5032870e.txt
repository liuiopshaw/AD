CONSPECTUS: Total synthesis, where desired organic- and/or biomolecules could be produced from simple precursors
at atomic precision and with known step-by-step reactions, has prompted centuries-lasting bloom of organic chemistry since its
conceptualization in 1828 (Wöhler synthesis of urea). Such expressive science is also highly desirable in nanoscience, since it
represents a decisive step toward atom-by-atom customization of nanomaterials for basic and applied research. Although total
synthesis chemistry is less established in nanoscience, recent years have witnessed seminal advances and increasing research
eﬀorts devoted into this ﬁeld. In this Account, we discuss recent progress on introducing and developing total synthesis
routes and mechanisms for atomically precise metal nanoclusters (NCs). Due to their molecular-like formula and properties
(e.g., HOMO−LUMO transition, strong luminescence and stereochemical activity), atomically precise metal NCs could be
regarded as “molecular metals”, holding potential applications in various practical sectors such as biomedicine, energy, catalysis,
and many others. More importantly, the molecular-like properties of metal NCs are sensitively dictated by their size and
composition, suggesting total synthesis of them as an indispensable basis for reliably realizing their practical applications.
Atomically precise thiolate-protected Au, Ag and their alloy NCs are employed as model NCs to exemplify design strategies and
governing principles in total synthesis of inorganic nanoparticles. This Account starts with a brief summary of total synthesis
methodologies of atomically precise metal NCs. Following the methodological summary is a detailed discussion on the
mechanisms governing these synthetic strategies, which is the main focus of this Account. Based on unprecedented precision
(at atomic resolution) and ease (ensured by size-dependent properties) of tracking clusters’ size/structure changes, mechanisms
driving growth (e.g., reduction growth and seeded growth) and functionalization (e.g., alloying reaction and ligand exchange) of
metal NCs have been explored at molecular level. With definitive step-by-step reaction routes, two-electron (2 e−) reduction
(driving the growth reactions) and surface motif exchange (SME, prompting alloying and ligand exchange reactions) are
discussed in depth and details. In addition to those sub- and/or individual-cluster level understandings, the self-assembly
chemistry delivering high orderliness and enhanced materials performance in NC assemblies/supercrystals is also deciphered.
This Account is then concluded with our perspectives toward potential development of cluster chemistry. Advances in total
synthesis chemistry of metal NCs could not only serve as guidelines for future synthetic practice of NCs, but also provide
molecular-level clues for many pending fundamental puzzles in nanochemistry, including nucleation growth, alloying chemistry,
surface engineering and evolution of metamaterials.

1. INTRODUCTION
Total synthesis is a milestone discovery in continuous eﬀorts of
mankind toward demystiﬁcation of nature. It refers to an ideal
synthetic practice that generates desired organic- and/or bio-
molecules at atomic precision from simple and easily accessible
precursors. More importantly, it features known chemistry of step-
by-step reaction along the synthetic route of target molecules.
Since the conceptualization of total synthesis in 1820s,1 it has
prompted centuries-lasting bloom of organic chemistry, and
shaped the latter into not only an expressive science but also
a ﬁne art. Such delicate reaction routes are also highly desirable
in nanoscience. Given that almost all useful physicochemical
properties of nanomaterials are dictated by their composition,
size, and morphology, atom-by-atom customization of these
structural attributes has become one of the most ambitious
dreams of materials scientists.2 Such dream should root in total
synthesis (i.e., synthesis at atomic precision and with known step-
by-step reaction route) exploration of nanoparticles (NPs).
Nevertheless, due to inherent diﬃculties in synthesis and charac-
terization of conventional NPs at atomic precision, total syn-
thesis exploration of NPs has long tramped in synthetic method-
ology development phase, leaving the governing mechanisms
especially those at molecular level (i.e., step-by-step reaction
route) largely unrevealed.
The discovery of thiolate-protected atomically precise metal
nanoclusters (NCs) in the 1990s opens a new avenue for advancing
total synthesis research of NPs.3 Atomically precise metal NCs
are a family of ultrasmall particles with <3 nm core.3,4 The past
decades have witnessed remarkable development of their syn-
thetic and analytic chemistry, which allows production and char-
acterization of many metal NCs at Å resolution.4−6 Advanced
mass spectrometry and X-ray crystallography analyses reveal
that the NCs could be described by a deﬁnitive formula, i.e.,
[Mn(SR)m]q, where n, m and q are numbers of metal atom (M),
thiolate ligand (SR), and net charge in individual cluster,
respectively. Arising from these molecular-like formula and
structure are unique molecular-like properties of metal NCs,
such as HOMO−LOMO transition, quantized charging, strong
luminescence, and intrinsic chirality, which exhibit strong size-/
structure-dependence.3,4,7−10 Such size-/structure-dependence
not only provides a good means to diversify cluster properties for
applications in many practical sectors (e.g., biomedicine,11
energy,12 and catalysis13,14), but also represents a facile means
to monitor the size and structure change of NCs. Therefore,
taking the molecular-like structure and property together, metal
NCs could be regarded as “molecular metals”. Moreover, the
atomic precision in synthesis and ease of tracking the size
and structure evolution suggest these metal NCs as an ideal
model platform for pursuing total synthesis chemistry of inor-
ganic NPs.
In this Account, we discuss recent achievements on intro-
ducing total synthesis routes into Au, Ag, and AuAg alloy NCs, as
a prototype attempt toward establishing total synthesis chemistry
for inorganic NPs. The discussion starts with a brief summary
of state-of-the-art development of synthetic strategies that
deliver atomically precise modulability over size and structure
of metal NCs. Following is a concise and in-depth discussion on
the molecular-level mechanisms governing synthetic strategies
(Figure 1), including reduction growth (a →c), seeded growth
(c →d), alloying reaction (c →f) and ligand-exchange process
(c →g). Based on the as-revealed knowledge on the formation
and functionalization fundamentals, promising utilization of high
quality metal NCs as functional building blocks for construction
of novel metamaterials is also explored (Figure 1c →h).

broadened size distribution of raw NCs in the Brust−Schiﬀrin-

the kinetics of reduction and core-etching reactions.18 By facilely
introducing a proper amount of NaOH in the NaBH4 reduction
of Au(I)-SR complexes, we were able to simultaneously passivate
the reducing power of NaBH4 and enhance the etching ability
of SR ligands. A balanced kinetics of reduction and core etching
made possible by this way could thus produce high quality
[Au25(SR)18]−NCs (Figure 2b and c) in a fast (<3 h) and scal-
able (a preparative scale of gram) manner (Figure 2d). It should
be noted that the critical factor dictating the monodispersity of
product NCs is the relative time scale of the reduction and core-
etching kinetics rather than the absolute one. In a follow-up work,
we showed that the production of [Au25(SR)18]−could be signif-
icantly accelerated (<30 min) by elevating the reaction temper-
ature, when the kinetics of reduction and core-etching were
brought into similar time scale.19 Of note, monodisperse metal NCs
could also be achieved by solely controlling either the reduction
(e.g., kinetic size-trapping approaches) or core-etching (e.g., thermo-
dynamic size-focusing approaches) processes, which have already
been extensively reviewed.3,4 In addition to chemical synthesis,
monodisperse metal NCs could also be isolated from NC mixtures
by high-resolution separation techniques, such as high performance
liquid chromatography20 and gel electrophoresis.21
In addition to monodispersity, the tailorability over size and
composition is another important pursuit in total synthesis devel-
opment of NCs. In the Brust-Schiﬀrin-like synthesis, the size and
composition of metal NCs could be eﬃciently controlled by a
proper choice of metal salts and thiolate ligands. For example,
simply by changing the feeding ligands in the aforementioned
NaOH-mediated NaBH4-reduction method, mono-, bi- and tri-
thiolate protected Au25 NCs could be produced with adjustable
type (inset of Figure 2b) and ratio of thiolate ligands.18 Besides
precursor engineering in the reduction growth of NCs, size and
composition of NCs could also be tailored by post-treatment on
preformed parent NCs (Figure 1c →d, c →f, and c →g). For
example, we reported a seeded-growth approach for synthesis of
[Au44(SR)26]2−NCs, where Au(I)-SR complexes were reduced
to spur further growth of preformed [Au25(SR)18]−.22 In a separate
work, we reacted parent [Ag44(SR)30]4−NCs with Au(I)-SR com-
plexes and precisely allocated Au heteroatoms in clusters’ protecting
shell, yielding [Ag32@Au12(SR)30]4−NCs via a surface motif
exchange (SME) mechanism (vide infra).23 In addition to seeded
growth and SME methods, size and composition control of metal
NCs can also be accomplished via metal exchange,24 ligand
exchange,25 and intercluster reactions26 upon preformed parent
NCs.

3. MECHANISMS GOVERNING TOTAL SYNTHESIS OF
THIOLATE-PROTECTED METAL NCs
Aside from synthetic methodology development, another equally
important aspect of total synthesis is to reveal formation mech-
anism (i.e., step-by-step reactions) at molecular level. Owing to
fascinating advances in high-resolution analytic technologies, an
increasing number of decisive intermediate species in the
formation process of target metal NCs have become capturable
and traceable, shedding essential light on the evolution routes of
several mono- and bimetal NCs.
3.1. Fundamentals of Reduction-Growth of Metal NCs
It should be reminded that the Brust-Schiﬀrin-like synthesis
involves fast reduction of M(I)-SR complexes and slow core-
etching (or size-focusing) of mix-sized raw NCs. Due to the fast
kinetics of reduction process (especially when NaBH4 is used),
the pioneer mechanistic study focused on chemical identity of
M(I)-SR complexes27−30 and size-focusing pathway of raw
NCs.17 Taking synthesis of Au NCs as an example, it has long
been accepted that mixing thiols (H-SR) with Au(III) salt (e.g.,
[AuX4]−where X = halogen ligand) or phase-transferred Au(III)
salt (e.g., [TOA][AuX4] where TOA = tetraoctylammonium
cation) could lead to extensive formation of oligomeric SR-
[Au(I)-SR]x complexes via Scheme 1.16
Recent revisiting of such formation process by nuclear
magnetic resonance (NMR) and Raman spectroscopy, however,
suggests that the chemical identity of as-formed Au(I) species is
highly dependent on the thiol-to-Au ratio (RSR‑to‑Au) and the
deprotonation readiness of thiol.27−29 As illustrated in Figure 3a,
a high RSR‑to‑Au and medium environment conducive for deproton-
ation of H-SR (e.g., presence of water and alkaline species) could
facilitate the formation of SR-[Au(I)-SR]x species, while the
opposite conditions would give rise to [Au(I)X2]−species.27,28
A similar conclusion was also made theoretically by Aikens and
co-workers (Figure 3b).30 Another center of mechanistic eﬀorts is
the size-focusing fundamentals of metal NCs, which has already
been well reviewed elsewhere.3,17

size-focusing pathway, the mechanistic exploration on the reduc-
tion growth of NCs is a recent development, primarily due to the
retarded traceability by its fast kinetics. Our group has managed
to tackle this technical challenge by implementing a mild gaseous
reducing agent, carbon monoxide (CO), in the synthesis of metal
NCs. The mild and readily quenchable reaction kinetics made
possible by CO enabled us to monitor the reduction process by
optical absorption spectroscopy (Figure 4b) and electrospray
ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS, Figure 4c), where in total

noted that the metal exchange reaction at this sub-3 nm size
regime does not necessarily follow the galvanic reduction mech-
anism, which is ubiquitously adopted by metal exchange reaction

dimeric transition state of [Au25Ag25(SR)36]2−.26 Such debate
requires future experimental clariﬁcation.
3.4. Fundamentals of Ligand-Exchange Process of Metal
NCs
As can be seen in Figure 7, the SME mechanism could also drive
ligand-exchange reaction, as long as the metal atoms in the
incoming M(I)-SR′ complex are identical to those in parent NCs.
However, it is unclear by now whether all ligand-exchange
reactions would go through such SME mechanism, which is a
recent discovery and thus unsophisticatedly explored. Prior to
SME mechanism, the fundamental investigations focused on
revealing the exchange site, extent and kinetics by experimental
(e.g., chromatography and X-ray crystallography) and theoretical
means.37−41 As the most important ﬁnding, the ligand-exchange
reaction would occur preferentially on the thiolate ligands anchored
on the solvent-most-accessible metal atoms of the M(0) core.39,40
Another rising center of fundamental interest locates on ligand-
exchange induced size transition (LEIST), where the cluster size
is customizable via designing the bulkiness and intermolecular
interactions of incoming ligands. Beyond size and composition,
recent advances in X-ray crystallography analysis also suggest
that the packing symmetry of metal core of metal NCs could be
tailored among icosahedron-based, face-centered-cubic (FCC),
hexagonal-close-packing (HCP), and body-centered-cubic
(BCC) arrangements, by simply varying the bulkiness of thiolate
ligands.42 The fundamentals and technical details of LEIST have
been discussed in several recent reviews,3,42,43 to which interested
readers may refer.
4. THIOLATE-PROTECTED METAL NCs AS
FUNCTIONAL BUILDING BLOCKS
Due to well-deﬁned structure and thus good property mod-
ulabiliy ensured by the above fundamental knowledge, metal
NCs have become promising functional building blocks of meta-
materials for various practical applications. It has been revealed
that the arrangement of functional building blocks into ordered
hierarchy architectures is an alternative means to strengthen (and
broaden) their materials properties by synergetic eﬀects. For
example, many groups (including us) demonstrated that the
photoluminescence intensity could be signiﬁcantly enhanced in
assembled or crystallized metal NCs by an aggregation-induced
emission (AIE) mechanism.46−48 In addition to property
engineering, self-assembly of metal NCs is also of fundamental
interest, as assembling NCs into three-dimensional ordered super-
crystals is a decisive step for their structure determination by X-ray
crystallography.
Although self-assembly of metal NCs is a recent discovery, its
development has been largely accelerated by the monodisperse
size and rich surface chemistry of metal NCs. For example,

5. CONCLUDING REMARKS
In summary, total synthesis of metal NCs is a rising topic in
cluster chemistry. The worldwide eﬀorts devoted into total syn-
thesis chemistry in the last two decades have yielded a diversity of
metal NCs with tunable structural attributes at atomic precision.
Such unprecedented precision in synthesis and characterization
also suggests NCs as an ideal platform to shed fundamental light
on the growth and functionalization processes of inorganic NPs.
By extensively tracking the reaction intermediates by advanced
analytic tools (e.g., X-ray crystallography, ESI-MS, and MS/MS),
mechanisms of reduction growth, seeded growth, alloying reac-
tion, and ligand-exchange process have been revealed at the
molecular or atomic level, giving rise to a step-by-step constituent
reaction map, which was previously only aﬀordable by organic
synthesis. High quality NCs produced based on these method-
ology and mechanism developments have been employed as
functional building blocks for construction of high hierarchy
architectures, exhibiting enhanced materials performance.
Although total synthesis exploration holds the potential for
prompting the continuous bloom of metal chemistry as what it
has been done in organic chemistry, its development is still in its
infant age, requiring more concerted eﬀorts from the research
communities. The coming research eﬀorts may ﬁrst be devoted
to growth chemistry of NCs bridging the molecular state (metal
NCs < 3 nm) and metallic state (metal NPs > 3 nm) of metal
materials. By monitoring the growth pathway of [Au44(SR)26]2−

composition engineering (made possible by alloying and ligand-

current alloy and/or heteroligand-protected NCs are mostly pro-

size-focusing process could eliminate the size heterogeneity and

process has been rarely explored. Such metal- and ligand-focusing
exploration of NCs could lead to crucial implications to the alloying
chemistry and surface engineering which are widely used but not
well-understood in modulation of functional NPs.
In addition to total synthesis chemistry at the sub- or individual-
cluster level, self-assembly chemistry at the intercluster level also
deserves intense attention. Future eﬀorts in this ﬁeld may be
placed on the shape- and crystallinity-controlled crystallization of
metal NCs, which is a rising topic in metamaterials construc-

devoted to the aforementioned aspects of total synthesis study
could fuel rapid growth of cluster chemistry, spurring crucial
implications to many pending fundamental challenges (e.g., nucle-
ation growth, alloying chemistry, surface engineering) that have
puzzled the nanoscience community for decades.
