ABSTRACT: Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is the most prevalent cause of dementia worldwide, and breakthroughs in effective
intervention strategies are urgently needed. Here, we report that chiral spindle-like Fe7Se8 nanorods (NRs) promote α-ketoglutarate
(AKG) biosynthesis, providing a new potential route for AD intervention through modulation of neuroimmune homeostasis. Oral
administration of L-NRs significantly restored intestinal microbiota homeostasis in 3 × Tg AD model mice, markedly enriched
Lactobacillus johnsonii, and enhanced biosynthesis of the metabolite AKG, which reversed cognitive impairment and neuronal
degeneration in 5 × FAD mice. Moreover, AKG levels in the clinical serum and cerebrospinal fluids were found to be significantly
lower in patients with AD than in healthy controls. Mechanistic studies revealed that L-NRs efficiently promoted AKG biosynthesis
through scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) to restore the activities of three enzymes in the biosynthesis pathway. Crucially,
these NRs are broken down by gastric juice into smaller nanoparticles and subsequently into ions in the intestines. Further studies
explored that AKG crossed the blood-brain barrier via cooperatively mediated transport proteins, targeted microglial phenotypic
switching, reprogrammed the neuroinflammatory microenvironment, and ultimately ameliorated cognitive deficits and neuronal
pathological alterations. Our findings suggest that AKG might serve as a therapeutic drug for the precise treatment of
neurodegenerative diseases.
1. INTRODUCTION
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder
mediated by multiple factors including aging, genetics, and
environmental influences.1 Clinically, AD manifests primarily
as impaired behavioral abilities, cognitive impairment, and
memory decline, which severely affect the lives of tens of
millions of patients and their families worldwide, pose a major
burden, and result in severe challenges regarding social and
healthcare resources.2 Because of the highly heterogeneous and
complex pathology of AD, current conventional therapies have
limited efficacy.3,4 For example, cholinesterase inhibitors and
NMDA receptor antagonists5 provide only short-term
symptom relief and are accompanied by adverse effects.6
Despite the development of forward-looking therapeutic
strategies, including antiamyloid therapy,7 stem cell therapy,8
and multisensory γ stimulation9,10 in recent years,11−13 these
approaches have shown high failure rates during the clinical
translation stage.14 Because the global population of patients
with AD is projected to increase to 152 million by 2050,15 the
development of safer and more effective treatment strategies is
an urgent research priority.16

nanomaterials’ chirality-based precision mechanism and
superior biocompatibility and targeting specificity33,34 enable
precise modulation of biological behaviors at the molecular,
bacterial, and cellular levels.35 For instance, D-Fe3O4 super-

This study elucidated the interventional effects of chiral

positive correlation was observed between changes in
Lactobacillus johnsonii (L. johnsonii) and the gut microbiota

the central nervous system via blood-brain barrier (BBB)

2. RESULTS
2.1. Synthesis and Characterization of Chiral Spindle-like
NRs
Chiral penicillamine (Pen) served as both the chiral ligand and
the structure-directing agent. Sodium selenite and ferrous
chloride reacted and self-assembled under the reduction effect
of ethylenediamine, thus resulting in the successful con-
struction of spindle-like chiral Fe7Se8 NRs. The schematic of
chiral NRs synthesis is shown in Figure S1A. Transmission
electron microscopy (TEM) analysis revealed that, under the
induction of chiral ligands, the nanomaterials initially self-
assembled nanoflowers with a diameter of 85 ± 2.2 nm, which
increased to 110.6 ± 3.7 nm by 3 h. With prolonged reaction
times, the nanoflowers gradually dissociated, underwent
secondary self-assembly, and ultimately formed chiral NRs
(Figure S1B). Scanning electron microscopy analysis demon-
strated that the chiral NRs exhibited excellent dispersion and
morphological uniformity at a large scale. Individual chiral NRs
displayed a typical spindle-shaped morphology with regularly
distributed surface protrusions with an average length of 1.21
± 0.58 μm and a maximum width of 0.33 ± 0.09 μm (Figure
1A). CD spectra of the chiral NRs exhibited three mirror-
symmetric signals at 356, 514, and 572 nm, and the strongest
CD response was observed at 572 nm, at which the ellipticity
values reached 54.3 ± 1.1 mdeg for L-NRs and 51.9 ± 0.6
mdeg for D-NRs, corresponding to a g-factor of 0.002, whereas
DL-NRs showed negligible chiroptical activity (Figure 1B).
Ultraviolet−visible spectroscopy spectra displayed a character-
istic peak at 572 nm, in agreement with the CD trends (Figure
S1C). High-resolution TEM analysis of representative chiral
NRs revealed a lattice spacing of 0.234 nm (Figure 1C).
To investigate the elemental composition and structural
characteristics of chiral NRs, we conducted a systematic
analysis by using multidimensional characterization techniques.
Energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy mapping revealed a
homogeneous distribution of Fe and Se elements in the chiral
NRs (Figure 1D). X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS)
analysis of the Fe 2p spectrum revealed prominent binding
energy peaks at 711.8, 725.3, 714.1, and 727.4 eV,
corresponding to Fe2+ 2p3/2, Fe2+ 2p1/2, Fe3+ 2p3/2, and Fe3+
2p1/2, respectively, thereby indicating the coexistence of Fe2+
and Fe3+, with Fe2+ present in a higher proportion (Figure 1E).
The Se 3d spectrum exhibited two distinct peaks at 54.7 and
55.5 eV (Figure 1F). X-ray diffraction analysis revealed that the
diffraction peaks of the chiral NRs closely matched the
standard pattern according to the Fe7Se8 standard card
(JCPDS 72-1355) (Figure 1G). Fourier transform infrared
spectra revealed the disappearance of S−H stretching vibration
peaks of Pen at 2510 and 2590 cm−1, whereas a broad peak
emerged near 3300 cm−1 and was attributed to N−H
stretching vibrations of Pen molecules anchored on the NR
surface, thereby confirming successful chiral Pen modification
(Figure S1D). This finding was further validated by Raman
spectra of the chiral NRs, which indicated the disappearance of
the S−H stretching vibration peak of Pen in the 2510−2560
cm−1 range (Figure 1H).
2.2. Chiral NRs Mitigate the Cognitive Function and
Reduce Pathological Features in 3 × Tg AD Mice
To investigate whether chiral NRs might mitigate AD
pathological hallmarks and behavioral deficits, we administered
daily oral gavage of L-NRs, D-NRs, or DL-NRs at a safe dosage
(80 mg/kg bw/day) to 12-month-old 3 × Tg AD mice for 90
days. Wild-type (WT) and AD model mice were administered
equivalent volumes of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) daily
for 90 days, and behavioral monitoring was performed monthly
(Figure 2A). After 90 days of treatment, the Morris water maze
(MWM) test demonstrated that the L-NRs group exhibited
escape latencies comparable to those of the WT group,
whereas the D-NRs and DL-NRs groups showed latencies
significantly longer than those of the L-NRs group. Swimming
trajectory analysis further confirmed marked improvements in
spatial learning and memory in the L-NRs group (Figures 2B
and S2A). In the novel object recognition (NOR) test, the L-
NRs group displayed a significantly enhanced novelty
preference, and activity trajectory diagrams revealed superior
exploratory behavior and novelty discrimination (Figures 2C
and S2B).
To evaluate the intervention effects of chiral NRs on AD
pathological hallmarks, we first measured soluble β-amyloid
(Aβ) and phosphorylated tau (p-tau) levels in cerebrospinal
fluid (CSF) in mice in each group. The L-NRs group showed
72.9 and 73.2% lower in CSF soluble Aβ (7.76 ± 2.92 ng/mL)

Notably, we systematically assessed the long-term biosafety
of chiral NRs and the acute toxicity of L-NRs at various
concentrations. No significant pathological damage was
observed in the major organs in the mice across all
experimental groups, and the serum biochemical parameters

biosafety of oral chiral NRs. Control experiments demon-

was dependent on enantiomer specificity.
2.3. Chiral NRs Reshape Gut Microbial Homeostasis in 3 ×
Tg AD Mice
To investigate the regulatory effects of L-NRs on the
composition and distribution characteristics of gut microbiota
in AD mice, we performed 16S rRNA (rRNA) sequencing on
fecal samples from the L-NRs intervention in 90-day mice and
control mice. Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed
that L-NRs intervention significantly reshaped the gut
microbiota structure in AD mice, by shifting the distribution
away from that in the AD control group and toward that in the
WT group (Figure 3A). At the phylum level, the L-NRs
intervention markedly decreased the relative abundance of
Campylobacterota and Bacteroidota, and increased the propor-
tions of Firmicutes, Actinobacteriota, and Verrucomicrobiota
(Figure 3B). The genus-level heat map visually demonstrated
that L-NRs reshaped the gut microbiota in AD mice by
decreasing the abundance of harmful bacteria such as
Enterobacter and Oscillibacter, and significantly increasing the
abundance of beneficial bacteria associated with neurological
health, including Lactobacillus, Akkermansia, and Limosilacto-
bacillus, to levels exceeding those in the WT group. Moreover,
Lactobacillus exhibited the most pronounced elevation (Figure
3C). At the species level, L-NRs treatment significantly
decreased the abundance of potential pathogens associated
with neurological disorders, such as Helicobacter typhlonius,
Oscillibacter valericigenes, and Helicobacter ganmani, and
markedly increased the abundance of L. johnsonii, a species
within the genus Lactobacillus (Figures 3D and S8). L. johnsonii
has been demonstrated to exhibit immunomodulatory effects,
improve intestinal barrier integrity, and suppress inflamma-
tion.41,42
Gut microbiota homeostasis is critical for metabolic
regulation, immune balance, and the treatment of neuro-
degenerative diseases (NDDs).43−45 To determine whether L-
NRs might ameliorate AD pathology via gut microbiota
remodeling, we performed fecal microbiota transplantation
(FMT) experiments. Gut microbiota from AD mice, WT mice,
or AD mice treated with L-NRs for 90 days were transplanted
into antibiotic-pretreated AD or WT recipient mice. Feces
from L-NRs-treated mice were heat-inactivated and trans-
planted into AD model mice (HT of L-NRs→AD group) as
controls, to exclude interference from nonviable micro-
organisms. Behavioral changes were monitored after 7
consecutive days of treatment (Figure 3E). By day 75 post-
FMT, the L-NRs→AD group exhibited escape latency
shortened to levels comparable to those in the WT→WT
group (Figure 3F) and showed swimming trajectories
approaching a nearly linear path from the start to the platform
(Figure 3G). NOR tests further validated the significantly
prolonged exploration time and frequency toward new objects
in L-NRs→AD mice (Figure 3H), whose movement
trajectories reflected enhanced recognition, spatial perception,

2.4. Chiral NRs Modulate the Microbiota Metabolism to
Enhance AKG Production in 3 × Tg AD Mice
Gut microbial metabolites are key mediators of host-micro-
biota interactions that critically regulate neurological function,
immune homeostasis, intestinal barrier integrity, and systemic
host health.46−48 To investigate the effects of L-NRs on gut
microbiota metabolites, we performed untargeted metabolomic
analysis on feces from AD, WT, and L-NRs-treated AD mice.
PCA revealed that the gut metabolite profile in AD mice after
90 days of L-NRs intervention significantly shifted toward that
in the WT group, in contrast to AD controls, in agreement
with changes in microbial composition (Figure 4A). Kyoto
Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway
analysis demonstrated that differentially abundant metabolites
in L-NRs-treated AD mice were enriched primarily in amino
acid biosynthesis; protein digestion and absorption; and
glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism pathway functions.
Notably, amino acid biosynthesis pathways exhibited stronger
significance and the highest metabolite enrichment, indicating
L-NRs enhance dietary protein utilization and amino acid
synthesis in AD mice (Figure 4B). Heatmap analysis visualized
distinct metabolites within the amino acid biosynthesis
pathway across groups (Figure 4C). Among the metabolites
associated with this pathway, serine (Ser) and AKG exhibited
the most pronounced upregulation in the L-NRs group, with
levels increased by 13.64- and 3.32-fold, respectively,
compared to the AD model group, and exceeded WT levels
(Figures 4D−F and S10). Serum analysis confirmed that L-
NRs-treated AD mice exhibited 2.53- and 3.65-fold higher Ser
and AKG levels than AD controls, and significantly surpassed
WT levels, whereas WT mice also showed elevated levels with
respect to AD controls, in agreement with the fecal
metabolomic trends (Figures 4G and S11A). These findings
suggested that AKG might be the core effector metabolite
through which L-NRs ameliorate AD symptoms and that Ser is
a precursor promoting AKG production. AKG, a metabolite
downstream of Ser in the amino acid biosynthesis pathway, is a
highly promising antiaging molecule49 with key roles in
inducing myelin regeneration,50 promoting trophectoderm
induction and maturation,51 reprogramming macrophage M2
polarization,52 and suppressing tumor progression.53
2.5. Intervening Effects by AKG Verified in 3 × Tg AD Mice
and 5 × FAD Mice
To validate the pathological therapeutic effect of AKG in AD
mice, we used AKG-calcium salt (AKG-Ca, purchased from
Macklin) at a dose of 300 mg/kg bw/day for daily gavage in
AD mice in the experimental group (AKG group), whereas
WT and AD mice received equal volumes of PBS and served as
controls. Therapeutic effects were evaluated via behavioral
assays (Figure 4H). In Morris water maze experiments, the
significantly shortened escape latencies in the AKG group over
60 days indicated a significant improvement in spatial learning
ability (Figures 4I and S11B). NOR assays further showed
significantly enhanced recognition and spatial navigation in the
AKG group (Figures 4J and S11C). These findings established
that exogenous AKG supplementation effectively restored
spatial perception, memory, and recognition functions in AD
models. Pathological indices of mice in each group were
subsequently measured. The 60-day AKG intervention
significantly decreased Aβ and p-tau levels in the CSF in AD
mice, by 70.9 and 71.6%, respectively, to values that did not
statistically significantly differ from those in WT controls
(Figure 4K,L). IHC and IF staining further demonstrated that

significantly improved behavioral cognitive performance,
pathological markers, and neuronal integrity after 60 days of
intervention in 5 × FAD model mice (Figures S12 and S13).

2.6. Targeted Scavenging of Reactive Oxygen Species by
Chiral NRs in 3 × Tg AD Mice
To determine the biodistribution and excretion of L-NRs, we
conducted inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry
(ICP-MS) quantification. The data demonstrated that L-NRs
accumulated primarily in the small intestine and liver and that
the feces were the primary excretion route. Excretion peaked at
8 h postgavage, then gradually decreased, with almost complete
elimination by 48 h (Figures 5A and S15), verified with
photoacoustic imaging (Figure 5B). We speculated that redox
reactions of L-NRs occurred with prolonged intestinal
residence and led to changes in the CD signal (Figure 5C).
In vitro validation under H2O2-simulated oxidative conditions
recapitulated a 508 nm CD signature identical to the 24 h fecal
spectra (Figure 5D). These data confirmed that L-NRs
gradually underwent redox reactions in the intestines in AD
mice with excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS),54 and
eventually biodegraded into an ionic state over time (Figure
S16). L-NRs (100 μg/mL) achieved 85.4% H2O2 depletion,
thereby significantly surpassing D-NRs and DL-NRs, and
revealing notable chirality dependence (Figure S17). XPS
valence state analysis revealed that, after incubation with H2O2,
the Fe3+/Fe2+ ratio in L-NRs increased from 0.361 to 1.430%,
and the Se4+/Se2−ratio reached 0.388% (Figure 5E,F). In
contrast, the Fe3+/Fe2+ ratios of D-NRs and DL-NRs increased
to 0.761 and 0.658%, respectively, and the Se4+/Se2−ratios
were only 0.109 and 0.054%, respectively (Figures S18 and
S19, Table S1).

we conducted an electron spin resonance analysis. The L-NRs

2.7. Chiral NRs Promote α-Ketoglutarate Biosynthesis
To elucidate the biosynthesis mechanism of AKG with Ser as a
precursor in the intestines in 3 × Tg AD mice and the
regulatory role of chiral NRs, we conducted isotopic tracing by
orally administering 13C3−Ser (200 mg/kg bw) or 13C3−Ser
(200 mg/kg bw) + L-NRs (80 mg/kg bw) to 3 × Tg AD mice.
Serum was collected 10 h postadministration, and metabolites
in the Ser-to-AKG pathway were analyzed via liquid
chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS)
(Figure 6A). Levels of
13C2-pyruvic acid,
13C3-oxaloacetic
acid, 13C3-citric acid, and 13C2-AKG were detectable in 3 × Tg
AD mice serum (Figures S21 and S22), and were significantly
higher in the 13C3−Ser + L-NRs group than the 13C3−Ser
group, particularly for 13C2-AKG (Figures 6B−E and S23).
Furthermore, we conducted in vitro experiments using H2O2 as
an oxidative stress model of L. johnsonii (L. johnsonii + H2O2),
with 13C3−Ser as the substrate (Figure S24). The data showed
the same trends as those observed in the in vivo experiments
(Figure S25). Together, our findings indicated that H2O2
suppressed
13C2-AKG synthesis by 31.9%, whereas L-NRs
reversed this effect (Figures 6F and S26).
AKG biosynthesis from Ser in vivo is governed by a
multienzyme catalytic network.56 To investigate the mecha-

measured the activity of key enzymes and AKG production in
L. johnsonii under various conditions. In the H2O2-induced
oxidative stress model, pyruvate carboxylase (PC), aconitase
(ACO), and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH) activity de-
creased by 51.4, 72.3, and 77.5%, respectively, compared with
that of the control group. Crucially, chiral NRs significantly
restored the activity of all three enzymes and increased AKG
production. L-NRs exhibited superior efficacy by increasing the
AKG level to 2.16-fold that in the H2O2-treated group (Figures
6G−I and S27A−C). Notably, the substrate Ser did not

ACO protein synthesis and function.57 Concurrently, H2O2
oxidizes the Cys residue at IDH’s active site, and consequently
induces conformational changes and decreased enzymatic

2.8. AKG Crosses the Blood-Brain Barrier and Suppresses
Neuroinflammation in 3 × Tg AD Mice
We next explored the mechanism through which exogenous
AKG supplementation ameliorated pathological phenotypes in
AD mice and investigated its role in regulating the central
nervous system homeostasis. After 60 days of oral AKG
intervention in AD mice, the levels of proinflammatory
cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α) in the CSF decreased
by 65.2, 48.9, and 51.2%, respectively, to WT levels. However,
a marked increase in anti-inflammatory factors (IL-10 and IL-
13) indicated that AKG effectively reshaped the neuro-
inflammatory microenvironment in AD mice (Figures 7A−C
and S28). Further LC-MS/MS quantification demonstrated
that the serum AKG concentrations were elevated by 3.23-fold
in AD mice, significantly exceeding the WT levels. Con-
currently, the AKG concentration in CSF was substantially
increased to WT levels, thereby suggesting that AKG crossed
the BBB and entered the central nervous system (Figure
7D,E). Crucially, clinical analysis of serum and CSF samples
from both patients with AD (50−90 years old) and age-
matched healthy controls revealed significantly downregulated
AKG levels in biofluids of AD patients (Figure 7F,G). This
finding, in agreement with results from the mouse model,
underscored the critical regulatory role of AKG in AD
progression and identified the intestinal-derived metabolite
AKG as a promising clinical target.
To clarify whether AKG traversed the BBB, after oral
administration of 13C3−Ser to AD mice, we detected 13C2-
AKG in the CSF, with significantly elevated levels in mice
coadministered 13C3−Ser and L-NRs, thus indicating that AKG
penetrated the BBB (Figures 7H and S29). Exogenous delivery
of 13C5-AKG (200 mg/kg bw) revealed significantly higher
CSF levels in AD mice than WT mice (Figures 7I and S30).
Fluorescence labeling experiments further verified that, after
oral administration of Cy5.5-conjugated AKG (AKG-Cy5.5) to
AD mice, AKG accumulated predominantly in the liver and
brain, and peak intracerebral concentrations occurred at 8 h
postadministration (Figures 7J and S31). To delineate the
active transport pathway of AKG across the BBB, we
constructed an in vitro BBB model with hCMEC/D3 cells in
Transwells.60 Functional inhibition assays showed that
individual blockade of Organic Anion Transporter 1
(OAT1/SLC22A6) or Organic Anion Transporter 3
(OAT3/SLC22A8) decreased AKG-Cy5.5 transport efficiency
by 26.9 and 50.6%, respectively, whereas combined inhibition
of both transporters exerted a synergistic suppressive effect that
markedly decreased AKG-Cy5.5 transport (Figures 7K and
S32).61 Molecular docking simulations revealed stable binding
of the AKG molecule within the central pore of the
transmembrane transport channels of both OAT1 and OAT3
proteins.62,63 AKG formed a robust hydrogen bond network
with key residue clusters in OAT1 (Lys431, Thr36, Asn35, and
Tyr230) and OAT3 (Asp366, Tyr341, Lys370, and Ser423)
(Figures 7L,M and S33). These findings demonstrated that
AKG exploited a synergistic transport pathway involving the
transporters of the OAT1 and the OAT3 in mediating its
active transport across the BBB.
2.9. Molecular Mechanisms on AKG-Mediated
Reprogramming of Neuroinflammatory Homeostasis
The microglial state is a central regulatory hub governing
neuroinflammation and AD pathogenesis.64 Redirecting micro-
glia toward the neuroprotective anti-inflammatory phenotype
(M2) is critical for efficient Aβ clearance, inflammasome
suppression, and preservation of neuronal integrity and brain
network homeostasis.65−67 We hypothesized that AKG might
precisely modulate microglial polarization, thereby offering a
metabolic-immunological perspective for AD intervention.
Dicarboxylate transporter 3 (SLC13A3) specifically mediates
transmembrane transport of tricarboxylic acid cycle metabo-
lites,68 and is prominently expressed in kidneys and the central
nervous system (particularly astrocytes).69 However, its
presence in microglia remains undefined. To investigate
whether SLC13A3 might facilitate AKG transport into the
microglia cell line BV2, we used AKG-Cy5.5 in cell
coincubation experiments. Confocal microscopy indicated
that AKG entered the microglia, whereas the fluorescence
intensity was significantly attenuated in microglia with blocked
SLC13A3 receptors (Block-SLC13A3) (Figure 8A). In
contrast, controlled experiments by blocking other potential
transporters did not induce any notable change in fluorescence
intensity (Figure S34).70,71 Molecular docking simulations
demonstrated that AKG is precisely embedded within the
SLC13A3 hydrophobic pocket by forming stable hydrogen
bonds with residues Gly250, Thr145, Asn144, and Asn482
(Figure 8B). Molecular dynamics (MD) simulations further
established conformational stability and indicated that
SLC13A3 achieved dynamic equilibrium after 4 ns (RMSD
fluctuation: 0.085−0.476 Å), whereas AKG binding stabilized
within 40 ns (RMSD fluctuation: 0.204−1.473 Å). These
findings therefore indicated high-affinity SLC13A3-AKG
interactions (Figure S35), demonstrating that SLC13A3 is
the essential transport receptor mediating the entry of AKG
into microglia.
To illustrate the regulatory role of AKG in microglial
polarization, we conducted flow cytometry analysis, which
revealed that AKG intervention after LPS stimulation reversed
the proportion of M2 polarization to 14.27 ± 3.45%. However,
with the SLC13A3 blockade before AKG treatment (LPS
+Block-SLC13A3+AKG), the proportion of M1 polarization
increased to 10.98 ± 2.73%, whereas M2 levels reverted to LPS
baseline levels (Figure 8C). These findings were corroborated
well by confocal microscopy images (Figure 8D). Meanwhile,
in vivo flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that, compared to
the AD group, AKG intervention significantly reduced the

an endogenous metabolite-based targeting strategy for AD
therapy.
3. DISCUSSION
By using the 3 × Tg AD mouse model, we discovered that L-
NRs remodeled intestinal homeostasis, significantly increased
the abundance of L. johnsonii, and elevated AKG levels,
indicating a significant positive correlation with amelioration of
AD pathology. L-NRs preferentially scavenged excess intestinal
ROS, thereby restoring the activity of key enzymes in the
amino acid biosynthesis pathway, enhancing the utilization
efficiency of the substrate serine by gut microbiota, and
ultimately promoting AKG biosynthesis. Our findings further
indicated that AKG was actively transported across the BBB via
the synergistic mediation of OAT1/OAT3 transporters and
delivered to glial cells by the SLC13A3 receptor. AKG was
found to ameliorate cognitive function and pathological
features in AD model mice by precisely inhibiting the TAK1-
MAPK/NF-κB-NLRP3 inflammatory signaling cascade and
reprogramming neuroinflammatory homeostasis.
This study is the first, to our knowledge, to propose a
cascade regulatory mechanism in chiral nanomaterial-gut
microbiota metabolism-neuroinflammatory microenvironment
remodeling, thereby expanding the scope of chiral biological
effects systemically. This thorough study of the specific role
and mechanism of AKG might provide a basis for new
therapeutic targets for AD. We revealed that the structural
variations in chiral nanomaterials, specifically dimensional and
compositional heterogeneity, exerted profound differential
effects on gut microbiota composition and metabolic axes. A
critical advancement is their biodegradable nature that unlike
the nondigestible chiral Au nanoparticles used in our previous
study,39 these chiral NRs are broken down by gastric juice into
smaller nanoparticles and subsequently into ions in the
intestines, which not only serve as essential trace elements
that directly promote the growth and metabolism of beneficial
gut microbiota, significantly enhancing their health and
functional activity, but the biodegradable nature of the material
also effectively avoids the potential long-term accumulation
risks associated with nondegradable nanomaterials, thereby
markedly improving biosafety and enabling precise and
efficient modulation of the intestinal microecology. Our
findings might lay a theoretical foundation for chirality-
targeted nanomaterial intervention strategies to advance AD
treatment toward precision and systematization.
