Abstract Zeolite
imidazole

is

promising option for self-cleaning of building exterior

irradiation. To address the issues, we developed the pho-

8-toluene 2,4-diisocyanate) by modifying ZIF-8 with

T-ZIF-8, while maintaining the crystal structure of T-ZIF-8

infrared region, signiﬁcantly narrowed band gap, improved
photogenerated

separation
efﬁciency,
reduced recombination rate, and excellent photocatalytic
performance. When the concentration of antibacterial agent
is 600 mgL-1, the antibacterial rate of Escherichia coli
(E. coli) reaches 99.99% irradiated by visible light for
30 min, and when the concentration of antibacterial agent
is 200 mgL-1, the antibacterial rate of Staphylococcus
aureus (S. aureus) reaches 99.99% irradiated by visible
light for 25 min. We also analyzed the reasons in detail
from the aspects of bacterial species and antibacterial
mechanism, and proposed the antibacterial mechanism of

suggest that T-ZIF-8-TDI photocatalytic antibacterial agent
has potential for use in self-cleaning of building exterior

1 Introduction
Green and low-carbon technological innovation is a key
driving force for cities to develop in the direction of con-
notative, intensive, and green, especially as we work
toward achieving the ‘‘Double Carbon’’ goal. As a result,
self-cleaning materials have emerged as a popular research
topic within the industry due to their excellent self-steril-
ization performance. These materials aim to create an
ecological ‘‘protective clothing’’ for urban construction
with self-cleaning properties, making them a valuable type
of green functional materials.
Compared to traditional photocatalytic antibacterial
materials such as ZnO and TiO2, metal–organic skeleton
materials (MOFs) become the third generation of new
photocatalytic antibacterial agents owing to the advantages

troscopy. The antibacterial properties of the T-ZIF-8-TDI
photocatalytic agent were evaluated using the tablet colony
counting method, and the photocatalytic antibacterial
mechanism was analyzed.

2.2 Preparation of T-ZIF-8-TDI photocatalytic agent
A mixture of 0.732 g of Zn(NO3)26H2O and 1.62 g of
2-methylimidazole in 50 ml anhydrous methanol was
sonicated to achieve even dispersion. The reaction was
stirred for 1 h, and the resulting product was allowed to let
sit for 10 h. After centrifugation, the white powder was
dried under vacuum at 80 C for 6 h to obtain ZIF-8. A
certain amount of ZIF-8 was weighed and spread on a
porcelain boat, and yellow powder was obtained after heat
treatment at 200 C in an O2 atmosphere for 5 h, which was
marked as T-ZIF-8. 0.1 g of T-ZIF-8 was weighed and
dissolved in a mixed solvent of 10 ml methylene chloride
and methanol (4:1), to which 1 ml of TDI was added and
stirred in air for 2 h. After standing, the powder was ﬁl-
tered and dried under vacuum at 80 C for 6 h to obtain
yellow powder, denoted as T-ZIF-8-TDI (17.5), with a
mass 11.5 times of T-ZIF-8. Similar to inorganic nano-
powder, T-ZIF-8 contains a large number of hydroxyl
groups on its surface. TDI contains isocyanate group
(molecular structure is shown in Fig. 1a). Hydroxyl groups
react with isocyanate group to form ammonia ester bond,
and its speciﬁc synthesis route is proposed in Fig. 1b.

3 Results and discussion
3.1 IR spectra
Infrared (IR) spectra of ZIF-8, T-ZIF-8 and T-ZIF-8-TDI
were measured in the range of 4000–400 cm-1 using KBr
disk method, as presented in Fig. 2a. The characteristic
absorption peak at 421 cm-1 is detected in them, which is
assigned to the stretching vibration peak of the Zn-N bond
produced by 2-methylimidazole coordination with Zn2?.
This indicates that heat treatment of ZIF-8 by O2 and TDI
modiﬁcation of T-ZIF-8 do not cause Zn-N fracture, and
photogenerated electrons generated by photoexcitation are
efﬁciently migrated from the ligand to the metal center.
The antisymmetric and symmetric stretching vibration
peaks of –CH3 are detected at 2962 and 2923 cm-1,
respectively, indicating the successful coordination of
2-methylimidazole and Zn2?. In T-ZIF-8, a weak absorp-
tion peak at 2226 cm-1 corresponds to the antisymmetric
stretching peak of –N=C=O group. This is due to the fact
that the aromatic heterocyclic structure of –N=C–CH3
transforms into an open-chain structure of –N=C=O in that
–CH3 in 2-methylimidazole partially oxidizes in O2. This is
in accordance with the results reported in the literature
[17, 18]. The stretching vibration peaks of –OH at 3562,
3473 and 3409 cm-1 are observed in ZIF-8 and T-ZIF-8,
but disappear in T-ZIF-8-TDI. However, the stretching
vibration peaks of –NH–COO– and ester carbonyl group
are detected at 2285 and 1747 cm-1, respectively. The in-
plane
bending
vibration
peak
of
–NH
appeared
at
1690 cm-1, indicating that the –C=N=O group of TDI
react with –OH on the surface of T-ZIF-8 to form an ammo
ester bond, which is bonded on the surface of T-ZIF-8.
3.2 XRD patterns
The XRD patterns of T-ZIF-8 and T-ZIF-8-TDI were
obtained in the range of 5–50 under copper Ka radiation
generated at 30 mA and 36 kV (k = 0.15406 nm), as
illustrated in Fig. 2b. The proﬁle of T-ZIF-8 indicates
obvious peak values resulting from X-ray scattering from
different crystal planes, and diffraction peaks are observed
at 7.39, 10.43, 12.74, 14.74, 16.47 and 18.08, which
correspond to the reported crystal plane diffraction of
(011), (002), (112), (022), (013) and (222) of ZIF-8,
respectively [15, 16]. The result conﬁrms that the crystal
structure of ZIF-8 is not damaged by thermal oxygen
sensitization. Additionally, the diffraction peaks of 24.8
and 25.9 in T-ZIF-8 blueshift to the broadening peaks of
24.5 and 25.6 in TDI-T-ZIF-8, indicating that the crystal
structure of TDI-T-ZIF-8 is similar to that of T-ZIF-8 and
the chemical modiﬁcation maintains the crystal structure of
T-ZIF-8 with only minor changes. This result conﬁrms that
TDI is successfully bonded to T-ZIF-8.

3.5 XPS spectra
To further investigate the elemental composition and
valence state of T-ZIF-8 and T-ZIF-8-TDI, XPS was per-
formed using Al-Ka rays, as shown in Fig. 4. The high-
resolution C 1s spectra of the two samples reveal three
peaks at 284.3, 284.8 and 285.7 eV, which are attributed to
C=C, C–C, and C–N binding energies, respectively. A new
peak at 287.9 eV in T-ZIF-8 corresponds to the C=O
binding energy, supporting the conclusion of partial oxi-
dation of CH3 in IR spectra. In T-ZIF-8-TDI, the intensity
of the C–C peak is signiﬁcantly enhanced compared with
T-ZIF-8 due to the contribution of –CH3 on the benzene
ring of TDI. The C=C peak intensity is higher than that of
the C–N peak, reﬂecting the greater proportion of C=C
bond in TDI. The N 1s spectra reveal energy peaks at 398.4
and 400 eV in T-ZIF-8, corresponding to the binding
energies of C–N and C=N, respectively. The C=O binding
energy appears at 287.9 eV, indicating partial oxidation of
–N=C–CH3 to form the –N=C=O group of an open-chain
structure. In T-ZIF-8-TDI, the C=N peak shifts to 399 eV
due to the C=N bond being connected to the benzene ring
with attracting electrons, reducing the C=N binding energy.
The Zn2? high-resolution spectra in T-ZIF-8 and T-ZIF-8-
TDI reveal peaks at 1022 and 1045 eV, which correspond
to the electron binding energies of Zn2? at 2p3/2 and 2p1/2,
respectively. The result demonstrates that the valence state
of Zn2? in T-ZIF-8 is identical to that of T-ZIF-8-TDI.
3.6 Photocatalytic performance
To compare the photoresponse range of T-ZIF-8 and
T-ZIF-8-TDI, UV–visible diffuse reﬂectance spectra were
measured in the range of 200–800 nm, as illustrated in
Fig. 5a. The results show that T-ZIF-8 exhibits strong
absorption in the range of 200–700 nm, which extends the
light response range to the visible region. T-ZIF-8-TDI
shows even stronger absorption, with the light response
range further expanded to 800 nm, enabling light response
in the ultraviolet, visible and near-infrared regions. Fur-
thermore, the intensity of T-ZIF-8-TDI is higher than that
of T-ZIF-8, indicating improved light utilization. The
chemical structure of T-ZIF-8-TDI, a typical donor–ac-
ceptor p-conjugate structure, explains these ﬁndings. The
p-conjugated surface complex is electron donor, while
Zn2? is electron acceptor due to the lack of electrons in its
4s and 4p orbitals. This donor–acceptor type chemical
structure extends p-conjugation through electron-deﬁcient
orbitals, which reduces the electron transition energy
[19–21]. As a result, electrons in the donor–acceptor p-
conjugated system are readily transferred from the organic

3.7 Photocatalytic antibacterial performance
3.7.1 Antibacterial performance against E. coli
The concentration of antibacterial agent T-ZIF-8-TDI was
200, 400 and 600 mgL-1, respectively. The bacterial
solution was removed every 30 min after visible light
irradiation was uniformly coated on solid medium. In this
experiment, the same mass of TDI in T-ZIF-8-TDI as the
only antibacterial agent (no light) was used as the control
group. The medium was placed in a constant temperature
incubator at 37 C for 24–48 h, and the growth of bacterial
colonies on the plate was photographed and recorded when
the bacteria on the medium were visibly observable.
According to Eq. (1), the antibacterial rate of E. coli was
calculated under different antibacterial agent concentra-
tions and different time, as shown in Fig. 7a.
It can be seen from Fig. 7a that compared with the
control group, the T-ZIF-8-TDI exhibits excellent photo-
catalytic sterilization efﬁciency under light condition,

3.8 Photocatalytic antibacterial mechanism
The antibacterial mechanism of T-ZIF-8-TDI under visible
light excitation can be explained as follows. When irradi-
ated by light, the material generates electron–hole pairs,
and the electrons are transferred to the Zn2? center via a
process known as ligand-to-metal charge transfer (LMCT).
The Zn2? ion gains electrons and is reduced to Zn?, which
in turn undergoes redox reactions with O2 in air or water.
As a result, O2 is converted into O2
-, which is a free
radical with strong oxidation and reduction capabilities.
The generation of O2
- is also veriﬁed by the EPR spectra.
Together with the positive holes (h?) in the valence band,
O2
- attacks bacteria and other microorganisms, damaging
their cell membranes, proteins and genetic materials. The
byproducts are ultimately broken down into harmless CO2
and H2O, leading to a self-cleaning bactericidal effect. A
schematic representation of the photocatalytic antibacterial
mechanism is described in Fig. 8.

4 Conclusion
The T-ZIF-8-TDI antibacterial agent was developed by
modifying T-ZIF-8 with TDI through chemical bonding.
The photocatalytic antibacterial quality of T-ZIF-8-TDI is
11.5 times of T-ZIF-8, improving the yield of T-ZIF-8
photocatalyst. The crystal structure of T-ZIF-8 is not
damaged by chemical bonding. T-ZIF-8-TDI has an
extended optical response range up to 800 nm, a reduced
band gap from 2.27 to 2.17 eV, improved photogenerated
electron–hole separation efﬁciency, and reduced recombi-
nation efﬁciency. As a result, the photocatalytic perfor-
mance of T-ZIF-8-TDI is signiﬁcantly improved. When the
concentration
of

is

when the concentration of T-ZIF-8-TDI antibacterial agent
is 200 mgL-1, the antibacterial rate of S. aureus is
99.99%. Under visible light irradiation, the antibacterial
mechanism of T-ZIF-8-TDI is proposed, in which the main
active species in air or water are identiﬁed as O2
- and h?.
T-ZIF-8-TDI is stable up to 60 C, which is higher than the
exterior wall building’s antibacterial application tempera-
ture. The ﬁndings support that T-ZIF-8-TDI photocatalytic
antibacterial agent has potential application in self-cleaning
of building exterior walls.
