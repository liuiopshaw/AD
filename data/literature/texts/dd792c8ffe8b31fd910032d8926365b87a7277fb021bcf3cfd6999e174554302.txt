Structural Prediction of Thiolate-Protected Au38: A Face-Fused Bi-icosahedral
Au Core
Yong Pei, Yi Gao, and Xiao Cheng Zeng*
Department of Chemistry and Nebraska Center for Materials and Nanoscience, UniVersity of Nebraska-Lincoln,
Lincoln, Nebraska 68588
Received April 22, 2008; E-mail: xczeng@phase2.unl.edu
The synthesis and characterization of thiolate-group (RS-)
protected gold nanoparticles (AuNP) have attracted extensive
interest because of the unique physicochemical properties and
promising applications of AuNPs in catalysis, optical sensors, etc.1,2
The recently revealed novel bonding between RS- and AuNP3,4
provided new insights into the interaction between the gold surface
and the thiolate group.5
The properties of a thiolate-protected AuNP are controlled by
both the ligands and the Au core.1,2 However, determination of
the atomic structure of thiolate-protected AuNP represents a great
challenge for both experiment and theory. Recently, a major
breakthrough was made by Jadzinsky et al., who reported a total
structure determination of thiolate-protected AuNP, Au102(p-MBA)44
(pMBA ) p-mercaptobenzoic acid, SC7O2H5).3 Another major
advance was made with the determination of the atomic structure
of the “magic-number” cluster [Au25(SR)18]- (i.e., a cluster with
highstabilityandselectivity)throughthreeindependentexperimental6a,c
and theoretical studies.6b These previous works have consistently
shown that the Au core within a thiolate-protected AuNP is covered
by certain number of “staple” motifs, including the simple
-RS-Au-RS-[i.e.,Au(SR)2]andextended-RS-Au-RS-Au-RS-
[i.e.. Au2(SR)3]. It is also remarkable that the Au cores resolved
thus far are all highly symmetric (near-Ih and near-D5h symmetry
for Au13 and Au79 in [Au25(SCH2CH2Ph)18]- and Au102(p-MBA)44,
respectively).3,4,6 It is known that, for bare Au clusters with size
<40, few clusters show highly symmetric structures.7 For example,
the icosahedral isomer of an Au13 cluster has been conﬁrmed to be
a relatively high-energy structure from both theoretical8 and
experimental studies.9
In this Communication, we report a theoretical prediction of a
unique bi-isocahedral Au23 core structure for another magic-number
thiolate-protected gold cluster, Au38(SR)24. This study is motivated
by the recent ﬁnding that the Au13 core in [Au25(SR)18]- exhibits
high icosahedral symmetry.6 We note that, despite extensive
experimental studies of the Au38(SR)24 cluster, its atomic structure
is still not fully resolved.10 The structure of Au38(SR)24 reported
in an earlier experimental study10a was later reassigned11 as that
of the magic-number cluster [Au25(SR)18]-. Recently, the structural
composition of Au38(SR)24 was assigned as an Au:SR cluster with
a core mass of 8 kDa.10h,i The latter has a modest optical gap (ca.
0.9 eV)10h and exhibits high stability; it can undergo ligand-
exchange reactions while maintaining its core structure intact.10d
Several structural models for Au38(SR)24 have been proposed
theoretically, with the Au core is either symmetric or disordered.12
A state-of-the-art model was recently suggested by Jiang et al.,
who predicted a disordered and compact Au core, fully protected
by mixed Au(SR)2 and Au2(SR)3 motifs.12d
Our study of the most viable structure for the Au38(SR)24 cluster
is based on the pristine “divide and protect” scheme,12b together
with a set of structural rules derived from recent experimental
studies of thiolate-protected AuNPs.3,6 The basic idea in our
construction of candidate structures for the thiolate-protected AuNP
is
to

run with Is1 as the initial structure for 30 steps). The Monte Carlo
run accounts for some temperature effects, which have been shown
to be particularly important in the dominant structures of metal
clusters.17

Electronic shell models have been invoked to explain the high
stability and selectivity of certain ligand-protected gold clusters,3,6b,18
such as [Au25(SR)18]- and Au102(SR)44, the Au cores of which were
proposed to have 8 and 58 valence electrons, respectively, based
on the assumption that each staple motif can effectively localize
one Au(6s) electron.3,6b In thiolate-protected AuNP, the staple motif
Au2(SR)3 or Au(SR)2 can be viewed as a ligand bonding to the Au
core. However, both the extended motif Au2(SR)3 and the simple
motif Au(SR)2 have odd numbers of total electrons, which tend to
pair with another electron from the Au core to form a closed-shell
structure. As such, each Au2(SR)3 or Au(SR)2 motif may localize
one electron from the Au core, and the localized electrons are no
longer part of the total number of nonlocalized electrons accounted
for by the spherical jellium model.19 The structural composition

The effective total number of valence electrons for the Au core
may be given by the number of core atoms (each Au atom
contributes one 6s electron) minus the number of exterior staple
motifs, i.e., a + a′ - b - c ) m - 2b - 3c ) m - n (considering
eqs 1 and 2). It is known that 8 and 58 are both magic numbers (to
close electronic shells) for compact alkali metal clusters.19 Here,
the net number of valence electrons for Au38(SR)24 is 14, which is
not a magic number according to the spherical jellium model. On
the other hand, the net number of 14 valence electrons suggests
that the Au core of Is1 may be highly nonspherical and, as such,
its electronic shell structure may be explained by the nonspherical
shell model.20 Indeed, it has been found that the alkali-metal cluster

of both oblate and prolate shapes.21
The UV-vis optical absorption spectrum and X-ray diffraction
(XRD) pattern of Is1 are computed (see Supporting Information)
and compared with the experimental ones10h,i (Figure 3). A
comparison of the spectra of Is1, Is2, and the experimental spectrum
is presented in Figure S2. Tsunoyama et al. showed that Au38(SR)24
exhibits ∼0.9 eV optical absorption edge and a major absorption
peak near 2.0 eV.10h As shown in Figure 3a, the computed optical
absorption spectrum of Is1 is in good agreement with the
experimental spectrum. The extrapolated optical band-edge for Is1
is 0.89 eV, in excellent agreement with the measured value (∼0.9
eV).10h Moreover, the ﬂat absorption peak at 1.69 eV (peak c in
Figure 3a) and the major peak at 1.98 eV (peak d in Figure 3a) for
Is1 are also in excellent agreement with the experimental peaks at
1.65 and ∼2.0 eV, respectively.10h In contrast, very small
HOMO-LUMO gaps (<0.1 eV) are found for the cation (q ) 1,
HOMO-LUMOgap)0.06eV)andanion(q)-1,HOMO-LUMO
gap ) 0.05 eV) charge states of Is1, conﬁrming a close match
between the neutral state of Is1 and the experiment.
The computed Kohn-Sham (KS) molecular orbital (MO) energy
levels of Is1 and atomic orbital components in each KS MO are
shown in Figure 3b. We note that Is1 shows distributions of KS
MO bands very similar to those reported for [Au25(SR)18]-.6c As
shown in Figure 3b, the HOMO and a set of unoccupied orbitals
LUMO-LUMO+5, are mainly composed of the Au(6sp) orbital,
denoted as the sp-band. The set of HOMO-6-HOMO-1 are d-band
due to the signiﬁcant contribution of the Au(5d) atomic orbital.
The four major absorption peaks (a-d) in Figure 3a are assigned
to the different excitation modes shown in Figure 3b. We note also
that the HOMO, LUMO, and other orbitals, including HOMO-
1-HOMO-3 and LUMO-1,-2, etc., are almost exclusively com-
posed of atomic orbitals of the Au atoms in the bi-icosahedral core,
suggesting that the absorption peaks a-d are mainly due to the
electronic structure of the bi-icosahedral core. This good agreement
between the theoretical and experimental optical spectra and the
number of major absorption peaks (a-d) and their locations lends
further support to the predicted bi-icosahedral Au core structure in
Au38(SR)24.
The simulated XRD pattern of Is1 is shown in Figure 3c. It can
be seen that major diffraction peaks in the experimental XRD
pattern10i are well reproduced.
In conclusion, a face-fused bi-icosahedral Au23 structure is
predicted as the Au core of the magic-number Au38(SR)24 cluster.
This prediction is supported by good agreement between experi-
mental and simulated UV-vis spectra as well as the XRD pattern.
Given that the icosahedral Au13 core and the vertex-fused bi-
icosahedral Au25 core have been recently conﬁrmed by experiments
on the [Au25(SR)18]- and [Au25(PPh3)10(SCnH2n+1)5Cl2]2+ clusters,
respectively,6,22 we expect that this prediction on the Au core
structure for Au38(SR)24 will stimulate future experimental and
theoretical interests.
