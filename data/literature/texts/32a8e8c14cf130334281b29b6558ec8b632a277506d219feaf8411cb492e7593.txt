A B S T R A C T
Nanofiltration (NF) is crucial for advancing water purification and wastewater reuse technologies. Incorporating 
biocidal nanoparticles (NPs) such as AgNPs and CuNPs is promising for developing antibacterial and anti­
biofouling NF membranes, while their application is limited by NPs aggregation, high cost, and severe ion 
release. In this study, we developed novel NF membranes by integrating bimetallic AgCu nanoalloys via an in- 
situ reduction and coordination method facilitated by a polydopamine/polyethyleneimine (PDA/PEI) interme­
diate layer. The sequential deposition of Cu2+ onto nascent AgNPs formed uniform AgCuNPs with a unique core- 
shell structure. The Cu shell layer can shield the release of Ag+ from the Ag core and chelate with the PDA/PEI 
intermediate layer, thus controlling the release of biocidal ions and prolonging the biocidal properties of the 
membranes. As a result, the AgCuNP-modified membranes exhibited significantly improved membrane water 
permeability, salt rejection, and performance stability, along with reduced release of biocidal ions in the long- 
term operation. Notably, the bimetallic AgCuNP-modified membrane displayed superior antibacterial activity 
and biofouling reversibility compared to the commercial NF and monometallic Ag/Cu-modified membranes, 
achieving the highest sterilization rate (> 99 %), largest flux recovery rate (93 %), and lowest flux decline rate 
(16 %) in both static antibacterial and dynamic biofouling processes. The metal-semiconductor heterostructure of 
the AgCuNPs facilitated the electron transfer from the Ag core to the Cu shell, intensifying the substantial 
generation of reactive oxygen species (H2O2: 71.6 mmol L-1 m-2, •OH: 43.4 mmol L-1 m-2, and O2
•–: 1.3 × 10–4) at 
the membrane-bacteria interface. The synergistic effects of the unique properties of AgCuNPs including micro­
structure, atomic composition, charge transfer, and ROS generation significantly enhanced the antibacterial 
capacity of the AgCuNP-modified membrane. This study presents a facile method for modifying NF membranes 
with bimetallic AgCuNPs to achieve enhanced antibacterial activity and biofouling reversibility, providing 
fundamental insights and promising potential for water treatment applications.
1. Introduction
Nanofiltration (NF) is pivotal for water purification and wastewater 
reuse, offering benefits such as efficient water-solution separation, 
relatively low energy consumption, simple operation, and environ­
mental sustainability (Chung et al., 2012; Long et al., 2021). However, 
biological enrichment and biofilm formation on NF membranes signifi­
cantly reduce membrane permeability and solute rejection, challenge 
their widespread application (Barry et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2022). 
Although bacteria can be preliminarily removed through prefiltration 
and disinfection, biofouling remains an inevitable issue during 
long-term operation due to bacterial proliferation. Surface modification 
with antimicrobial agents (e.g., zeolite, silver, copper, and TiO2) is an 
attractive alternative to enhance the membrane antibacterial capability 
without compromising water treatment performance. Among various 
bactericidal nanoparticles (NPs), AgNPs exhibit excellent antibacterial 
activity and have been extensively studied for preventing membrane 
biofouling (Johnson and Hilal, 2022; Wang et al., 2021). However, the 
leaching and high cost of silver limit the practical application of 
AgNP-modified membranes. CuNP is another biocidal agent with a

lower cost (1 % of AgNPs) that could effectively inhibit the development 
of bacteria and the formation of biofilm, while the bactericidal efficacy 
is generally considered lower than that of AgNPs (Qiu et al., 2011).
Recently, bimetallic AgCu nanoalloys have been synthesized and 
demonstrated superior bactericidal efficacy to monometallic AgNPs and 
CuNPs (Ciacotich et al., 2018). The antibacterial mechanisms of AgNPs 
and CuNPs primarily involve physical disruption, release of metal ions, 
and generation of oxidative stress (Kim et al., 2022a). The enhanced 
antibacterial activity of bimetallic AgCuNPs is likely due to their unique 
chemical compositions and microstructure features (Ciacotich et al., 
2018). The sequential deposition of Cu2+ onto nascent AgNPs can 
facilitate the release and secondary reduction of Ag+, leading to a 
redistribution of NPs with varied Ag/Cu ratios, sizes, and morphologies 
(Tao et al., 2022). Given the growing interest in modifications using Ag 
and Cu for the development of antibiofouling membranes, it is crucial to 
conduct a comparative analysis of the antimicrobial activities and 
mechanisms of AgNPs, CuNPs, and their bimetallic AgCuNPs (Taner 
et al., 2011). Notably, the bactericidal efficacy, biofouling resistance, 
and performance stability of AgCuNPs-modified membranes in water 
treatment applications have not been studied, presenting an opportunity 
for further research.
Bimetallic AgCuNPs can be synthesized through chemical reduction 
with the assistance of microwave irradiation and electrochemical tech­
niques, but they have not been widely applied to enhance the anti­
biofouling properties of membrane materials. Monometallic AgNPs and 
CuNPs can be incorporated into membranes by blending with monomers 
or polymers during the membrane preparation process. However, the 
potential agglomeration of NPs may induce defects and compromise the 
structural integrity of membranes. The uniform distribution of NPs can 
be achieved by in-situ synthesis using strong reducing agents (i.e., 
NaBH4) (Rahaman et al., 2014), while this process should be carefully 
controlled to prevent damage to the membrane materials. Polydopamine 
(PDA), with abundant catechol groups that formed through the 
self-polymerization of dopamine (Lynge et al., 2011; Ye et al., 2011), can 
mildly reduce metal precursors to AgNPs or CuNPs on the membrane 
surface (Yan et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2024). The ad­
hesive and hydrophilic nature of the PDA layer can further enhance the 
nanoparticle stability and membrane hydrophilicity, synergistically 
improving the membrane resistance to biofouling. In addition, poly­
ethyleneimine (PEI), a polyelectrolyte with high charge density, is 
conducive to co-deposit with PDA on the negatively charged substrate 
(e.g., polysulfone and polyethersulfone), forming loose NF membranes 
with high permeability (Wang et al., 2017). The amino groups in PEI are 
beneficial to chelate with Cu2+, immobilizing CuNPs on the membrane 
surface and controlling the release of biocidal ions into solutions (Bisset 
et al., 2003).
In this study, we integrated bimetallic AgCu nanoalloys into NF 
membranes using an in-situ reduction and coordination method facili­
tated by a PDA/PEI intermediate layer to enhance the membrane 
biofouling resistance and performance stability. The physicochemical 
properties, filtration efficiency, bactericidal activity, and anti-biofouling 
capabilities of both bimetallic (AgCu) and monometallic (Ag and Cu) 
NP-modified membranes were comprehensively investigated and 
compared. Additionally, we evaluated the water treatment performance 
stability of membranes and monitored the release rate of metal ions from 
NPs during long-term operation. To elucidate the underlying antibac­
terial mechanisms of the AgCu-modified membrane, we quantified the 
reactive oxygen species (H2O2, •OH, and O2
•–) that potentially gener­
ated at the interface between the membrane and bacteria. This 
comprehensive analysis aims to provide insights into the enhanced 
antibacterial properties endowed by incorporating AgCu nanoalloys into 
NF membranes.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials and chemicals
Polysulfone membranes (PSf, GC-UF0201) with molecular weight 
cut-off of 20 kDa were provided by Guochu Technology (Xiamen, 
China). Tris (hydroxymethyl) aminomethane (Tris, ≥99.9 %), dopa­
mine hydrochloride (DA, ≥98.0 %), and PEI (Mw 600, ≥99.0 %) were 
purchased from Aladdin. Sodium chloride (NaCl), potassium chloride 
(KCl), hydrochloric acid (HCl), copper sulfate (CuSO4⋅5H2O), and so­
dium hypochlorite solution (NaClO) were obtained from Komeo 
Chemical Reagent (Tianjin, China). Silver nitrate (AgNO3) was pur­
chased from Shanghai Test Laboratory Equipment Co., Ltd. Potassium 
dihydrogen phosphate (KH2PO4), disodium hydrogen phosphate 
(Na2HPO4), and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) were purchased from 
Fengchuan Chemical Reagent Technology (Tianjin, China). Beef paste 
was obtained from Jizhi Biochemical Technology (Shanghai, China). 
Peptone (≥99.9 %) and agar powder (≥99.9 %) were obtained from 
Hongrun Baoshun Technology (Beijing, China). Escherichia coli strain 
(E. coli, ATCC25922) was obtained from the China Microbial Strain 
Preservation Center. The LIVE/DEAD BacLight Bacterial Viability Kit 
NucGreen: Ex/Em: 503/530 nm (with DNA) was purchased from Nergy 
Chemical (Anhui, China). The deionized (DI) water was obtained from 
laboratory ultra-pure water equipment.
2.2. Membrane modification
A schematic illustration of the membrane modification process is 
shown in Fig. 1A. Initially, the PSf substrate was mounted in a stainless- 
steel frame with the smooth side facing upwards. DA (2 g L-1) and PEI (2 
g L-1) were dissolved in Tris buffer (pH = 8.5, 50 mM), and the mixture 
was gently poured into the frame to interact with the membrane surface 
on a shaker (80 rpm) at room temperature. The co-deposition of PDA/ 
PEI proceeded for 8 h to obtain a uniform modification layer with 
optimized water permeability and solute rejection (Text S1 and Fig. S1, 
supporting information). The membrane was then thoroughly washed 
using DI water to remove the residual modification solution and denoted 
as the PDA/PEI membrane. Subsequently, the PDA/PEI membranes 
were immersed in the metallic salt solutions for 4 h to obtain the AgNPs 
and CuNPs modified membranes. The membrane modified with bime­
tallic AgCuNPs (PDA/PEI-AgCu) was prepared by sequential metalliza­
tion with AgNO3 and CuSO4. Before the CuSO4 treatment, the membrane 
was thoroughly washed to ensure the complete removal of residual 
AgNO3 from the membrane surface. Table 1 lists the fabrication for­
mulas for the modified membranes.
2.3. Membrane characterization
Attenuated total reflection Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy 
(ATR-FTIR, Nicoletis10) was used to analyze the functional groups in the 
membranes within a wavenumber range of 700–3800 cm-1. The surface 
elemental compositions of the membranes were determined using X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS, K-alpha, Semel Fisher Technology). A 
solid surface zeta potential analyzer (Surpass 3, Anton Paar) was 
employed to measure the streaming potential on the membrane surface 
across a pH range of 3–10. A KCl solution (1 mmol L-1) was prepared as 
the electrolyte solution with the pH value adjusted using 0.1 M NaOH 
and 0.1 M HCl. The water contact angle of the membrane surface was 
measured to evaluate the surface hydrophilicity of the membranes using 
a contact angle tester (CM3250-DS3210, Kruess). Ten measurements at 
different locations on each membrane sample were taken to determine 
the average value. The morphological characteristics of the membrane 
surfaces were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, 
TM3030, Hitachi, Japan) and atomic force microscopy (AFM, Multi­
mode 3, Bruker). Elemental species and distributions on the membrane

2.5. Stability assessment of AgNPs, CuNPs, and AgCu s
The sustained release of ions (i.e., Ag+ and Cu2+) from membranes 
modified with AgNPs, CuNPs, and AgCuNPs was evaluated using both 
static immersion and dynamic filtration methods. In the static immer­
sion test, the PDA/PEI-Cu, PDA/PEI-Ag, and PDA/PEI-AgCu membranes 
with a dimension of 12 × 2.5 cm2 were immersed in 500 mL 5 % HNO3 
solution and agitated at 80 rpm for 10 days. In the dynamic filtration 
test, the membranes were operated at 0.4 MPa in a cross-flow filtration 
equipment using 1000 mL 5 % HNO3 solution as the feed (Fig. S2). The 
permeate solution was continuously returned to the feed tank to main­
tain a consistent feed volume. The concentration of the released Ag+ and 
Cu2+ in the solution was measured daily using inductively coupled 
plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS, Agilent), and the solution was 
refreshed with 5 % HNO3 solution every day.
2.6. Antibacterial capability evaluation
E. coli was used as a model biological contaminant to assess the 
antibacterial efficiency of the membrane samples through diffusion in­
hibition zone test, colony counting method, and fluorescence micro­
scopy characterization. Prior to the experiment, E. coli needs to be 
activated and enriched, and the detailed procedures are described in 
Text S2.

2.6.4. Dynamic biofouling experiments
The biofouling resistance of the commercial and modified NF 
membranes was evaluated through dynamic biofouling experiments 
using E. coli as the model microorganism. Before the experiment, the 
filtration system (Fig. S2) was thoroughly washed using NaClO (0.5 mg 
L−1), EDTA (5 mmol), and ethanol (95 %, v/v). The membrane was pre- 
compacted under 0.5 MPa for 4 h with DI water at 25 ◦C, then a solution 
consisting of 0.85 % NaCl and 0.6 mmol glucose was introduced and 
recirculated at 0.4 MPa until the system reached equilibrium. The initial 
E. coli concentration was ~106 CFU mL−1, and the solution temperature 
was maintained at 25 ± 1 ◦C during the biofouling experiment. The 
permeation flux was measured every 30 min. After 360 min of 
biofouling, the system was washed with DI water for 30 min, and the 
feed solution was refreshed. The flux recovery ratio (FRR) and flux 
decline ratio (FDR) were used to evaluate the biofouling propensity of 
membranes. 
FRR = (Je/J0) × 100%
(4) 
FDR = (J0 −Jt)/J0 × 100%
(5) 
Where Je is the recovered flux after hydro-cleaning, J0 is the initial 
flux, and Jt is the flux after the 360-min biofouling.
2.7. ROS determination
2.7.1. Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)
The concentration of extracellular H2O2 was determined using the N, 
N-diethylop-phenylenediamine (DPD)/horseradish peroxidase (POD) 
method (Li et al., 2022). Firstly, the DPD solution (10 g l-1) and POD 
solution (1 g l-1) were prepared by dissolving 0.10 g of DPD in 100 mL of 
H2SO4 solution (0.05 mol l-1) and dissolving 1 mg of POD in 10 mL of DI 
water, respectively. Then, 200 μL PBS buffer (0.5 M, pH = 6.8) was 
added into 2 mL extracellular fluid, followed by adding 37 μL DPD and 
37 μL POD solutions. Finally, the samples were examined by an ultra­
violet spectrophotometer at a wavelength of 551 nm.
2.7.2. Hydroxyl radical (•OH)
The generation of •OH was assessed by benzoic acid (BA) through 
high-performance liquid chromatography (Zhang et al., 2016). BA is a 
common probe molecule for detecting •OH and was used to capture •OH 
in our study. The wavelength for the measurements was set at 255 nm. 
The •OH concentration can be obtained using the following equation: 
[•OH] = [p −HBA] × 5.87

Where [p-HBA] is the concentration of hydroxybenzoic acid (p-HBA, 
mM) produced from the oxidation of BA by •OH.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Membrane characterization
3.1.1. Membrane morphologies
The morphological characteristics of membrane surfaces before and 
after modification were visualized by SEM and AFM (Fig. 2 and 3). The 
PSf substrate (Fig. 2a) exhibited a uniform and smooth surface. In 
contrast, dispersive agglomerations were formed on the membrane 
surface after PDA deposition (Fig. 2b), which can be attributed to the 
combined effects of oxidation, covalent crosslinking via self- 
polymerization, and non-covalent aggregation of PDA (Li et al., 2021; 
Park et al., 2017). The surface roughness (Fig. 3f) of the PDA-modified 
membrane (Rq = 54.5 nm) was significantly higher than that of the 
PSf substrate (Rq = 12.5 nm), which was consistent with the SEM im­
ages. The amine groups in PEI with flexible polymer chains can chelate 
with PDA (Wang et al., 2023b), thus promoting PDA dispersion and 
preventing the formation of large particles. Consequently, the 
co-deposition of PEI and PDA induced a smoother membrane surface 
(Fig. 2c and Fig. 3b) with reduced roughness (Rq = 19.4 nm).
Scattered granules were observed on the surfaces of the PDA/PEI-Ag 
(Fig. 2d) and PDA/PEI-Cu (Fig. 2e) membranes, indicating the formation 
of aggregated AgNPs and CuNPs through in-situ reduction of Ag+ and 
Cu2+ by chelating with PDA/PEI (the proposed reactions as shown in 
Fig. 1B) (Amantay et al., 2023). The average size of the aggregated 
AgNPs (~260 nm, Fig. 2d) was larger than that of the CuNPs (~140 nm, 
Fig. 2e), resulting in a rougher surface on the PDA/PEI-Ag membrane 
(Rq = 48.7 nm, Fig. 3c) compared to the PDA/PEI-Cu (Rq = 25.9 nm, 
Fig. 3d). Notably, the incorporation of bimetallic AgCuNPs formed 
abundant core-shell structured particles on the PDA/PEI-AgCu mem­
brane surface (Huang et al., 2020; Li et al., 2018) (Fig. 2f) with a Rq of 
31.7 nm (Fig. 3e and f). This morphological feature is likely because the 
nascent AgNPs can catalyze the reduction of Cu2+, which facilitates the 
release and secondary reduction of Ag+, leading to a redistribution of 
NPs with varied AgCuNP morphologies (Tao et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 
2022). Additionally, the nascent AgNPs can react with Cu2+ to form 
Cu2O and AgCuNPs complexes (Fig. 1B) with small sizes (~60 nm, 
Fig. 3f) (Tao et al., 2022; Zhou et al., 2022). EDS images (Fig. 4) further 
confirmed the relatively uniform loading of Ag and/or Cu on the 
PDA/PEI-Ag (Fig. 4e), PDA/PEI-Cu (Fig. 4b), and PDA/PEI-AgCu 
(Fig. 4c and 4f) membrane surfaces.
3.1.2. Surface chemical compositions
The chemical compositions of the membranes were analyzed by 
ATR-FTIR spectra (Fig. 5a). In contrast to the PSf substrate, new broad 
peaks emerged within the wavenumber range of 3108–3743 cm-1 for the 
PDA/PEI, PDA/PEI-Ag, PDA/PEI-Cu, and PDA/PEI-AgCu membranes, 
which were attributed to the stretching vibrations of hydrogen bonds 
associated with O–H/N–H groups from the PDA/PEI deposition (Shi 
et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2023b). C = N bonds at 2230 and 1660 cm-1 
indicated the formation of Schiff base structures through reactions be­
tween the amino groups in PEI and quinone moieties of PDA (Wang 
et al., 2023b). Besides, the bands at 1620 and 1490 cm-1 were assigned 
to the C = O stretching vibrations (Lv et al., 2018). Above all, the FTIR 
spectra demonstrated the successful deposition of PDA and PEI onto the 
PSf substrate. Negligible differences were observed on the PDA/PEI, 
PDA/PEI-Ag, PDA/PEI-Cu, and PDA/PEI-AgCu membranes.
The surface elemental composition of the membranes was analyzed 
before and after modification using XPS spectra (Fig. 5b–f and Table 2). 
The deposition of PDA/PEI induced a notable increase in the atomic 
percentage of nitrogen to 16.2 %, along with significant reductions in 
the carbon percentage (from 73.9 to 70.2 %) and O/N ratio (from 2.0 to 
0.8), which further confirmed the successful grafting of PDA and PEI 
onto the membrane surface (PDA/PEI) (Karan and Livingston, 2015; 
Yang et al., 2020). After incorporating AgNPs and/or CuNPs, discern­
able signals at ~360–380 eV and ~920–980 eV were observed, which

3.2. Membrane performance and stability
The separation performance of the modified membranes, including 
water permeability and rejection for various solutes (Na2SO4, NaCl, 
MgSO4, and MgCl2), is shown in Fig. 7a. The water permeability of the 
PSf substrate reduced dramatically from 214.6 to 30.5 L m-2 h-1 bar-1 
following the deposition of PDA/PEI (Wang et al., 2023b). Incorporating 
AgNPs and CuNPs into the membrane further decreased the water 
permeability while simultaneously enhancing salt rejection rates. 
Compared to the monometallic-modified membranes, i.e., AgNPs 
(PDA/PEI-Ag, 11.5 L m-2 h-1 bar-1) and CuNPs (PDA/PEI-Cu, 10.6 L m-2 
h-1 bar-1), the bimetallic-modified PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane exhibited 
higher water permeability (15.6 L m-2 h-1 bar-1) attributed to the for­
mation of NPs with smaller sizes (Fig. 2f). Notably, the NaCl rejection 
rate of the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane remained unaffected. In contrast, 
the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane exhibited increased rejections for salts 
with larger sizes and higher charges (i.e., Na2SO4, MgCl2, and MgSO4). 
The PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane presented a significantly reduced mo­
lecular weight cut-off (MWCO = 325 Da) compared to the PDA/PEI-Ag 
(MWCO = 448 Da) and PDA/PEI-Cu (MWCO = 408 Da) membranes 
(Fig. S3 and Table S1), indicating that the size exclusion is the major 
rejection mechanism of the modified membranes.
Compared to the commercial NF-270 membrane, the PDA/PEI-AgCu 
membrane presented comparable water permeability. Besides, the 
rejection of MgCl2 for the PDA/PEI-AgCu (89.1 %) was significantly 
higher than that of the NF-270 (42.2 %), which was attributed to the 
surface charge difference of the two membranes. As shown in Fig. 6a, the 
PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane had a significantly higher isoelectric point 
compared to the NF-270 (5.84 vs. 3.3) (Chidambaram et al., 2015). The 
less negatively charged surface of the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane resul­
ted in a stronger electrostatic repulsion for high valence cations (i.e., 
Mg2+) (Karan and Livingston, 2015; Liang et al., 2023), leading to a 
higher selectivity of MgCl2/NaCl by the PDA/PEI-AgCu (5.12) than the 
NF-270 membrane (2.1) in Fig. S4.
The release of Ag+ and Cu2+ from AgNPs and CuNPs incorporated 
into composite membranes can potentially influence the filtration per­
formance and anti-biofouling properties of the membranes (Rousse 
et al., 2016). The filtration performance (water flux and solute rejection) 
of the modified membranes was monitored for 10 days to evaluate 
performance stability (Fig. 7b). All the modified membranes maintained 
their water flux throughout the 10-day operation. However, the rejec­
tion rates of MgCl2 for the PDA/PEI-Ag and PDA/PEI-Cu membranes 
gradually decreased during the initial 5 days, eventually stabilizing at a 
rejection rate of 75–80 %. In contrast, the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane 
exhibited a relatively stable and high MgCl2 rejection rate of about 90 % 
during the 10-day operation. Furthermore, the rejection of Na2SO4, 
NaCl, and MgSO4 showed no significant decline (Fig. S5), indicating that 
the bimetallic nanoparticles enhanced the stability of the membrane 
performance.
The decrease in MgCl2 rejection for the monometallic NP-modified 
membranes was likely associated with the release of Ag+ and Cu2+
ions (Fig. 7c and 7d). The remarkably high release rate of Ag⁺ ions 
during the first five days can be attributed to the rapid leaching of 
loosely bound Ag⁺ ions and Cu2⁺ ions (Jeon et al., 2021; Xu et al., 2020). 
In both static immersion and dynamic filtration tests, the release pattern 
of Ag+ and Cu2+ was found to be consistent with the variation trend of 
MgCl2 rejection in Fig. 7b. Specifically, the release rate of metal ions 
from monometallic NPs was significantly higher than that from the 
bimetallic NPs and reached equilibrium after 4–5 days. Copper can be 
readily oxidized, and the silver nanoparticles can be immobilized by the 
CuOx NPs. In other words, the core-shell structure of the bimetallic 
AgCuNPs can regulate the release of metal ions after the first five days, 
thus promoting the long-term antibacterial property of the membrane 
surface. Besides, the phenolic hydroxyl groups and amino groups within 
the PDA/PEI chains can chelate with metallic NPs and further enhance 
their stability.
The release rate of Cu2+ ions in the NP-modified membranes was 
higher than Ag+ ions, resulting in a higher Cu2+ concentration in the 
permeate solution (Fig. S6). It has been reported that CuNPs are more 
prone to release metal ions than AgNPs at equivalent concentrations 
(Fan et al., 2021; Feng et al., 2021). After a 10-day dynamic crossflow 
filtration, the cumulative release concentrations of Cu2+ and Ag+ in the 
permeate solution were < 4 μg l-1 (Fig. S6b), which were much lower 
than the drinking water standards established by the EPA and WHO 
(1300 and 2000 μg l-1 for Cu2+, and 100 μg l-1 for Ag+, respectively) 
(Istirokhatun et al., 2022)).
3.3. Antimicrobial activity
Inhibition zone assay, colony counting, and fluorescence microscopy 
characterization were utilized to evaluate the antibacterial efficiency of 
the NPs-modified membranes (Fig. 8). Bacteriostatic zones were not 
observed surrounding the PSf substrate (Fig. 8a) or the commercial NF- 
270 membrane (Fig. 8f). The PDA/PEI and PDA/PEI-Cu membrane 
exhibited limited bactericidal effect against E. coli in this study (Fig. 8b 
and 8d), although the phenolic hydroxyl groups, amino groups, and 
Cu2+ in polymer chains and CuNPs have shown some antibacterial 
ability in previous studies (Zhang et al., 2017). The monometallic AgNPs 
demonstrated antimicrobial activity against E. coli, as evidenced by a 
distinct bacteriostatic circle formed around the PDA/PEI-Ag membrane 
(Fig. 8c). Notably, the bimetallic AgCuNPs displayed a significantly 
enhanced antimicrobial activity, with the diameter of the inhibition 
circle expanding from ~1.5 mm (Fig. 8c) to ~3 mm (Fig. 8e), which can 
be attributed to the synergistic antibacterial effects of the AgCu nano­
alloy (Fan et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2019).
The antibacterial activity of the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane was also 
demonstrated by culturing E. coli colonies that detached from the 
membrane surface and visualizing the viable E. coli cells using fluores­
cence microscopy (Fig. 8A-F and Fig. 8I-VI). Following a 2-h exposure to 
the E. coli suspension (107 CFU mL−1), the cells that contacted the 
membrane surface were detached and cultured. As shown in Fig. 9a, the 
sterilization rates of the PDA/PEI and NF-270 membranes were slightly 
higher (20–25 %) than the PSf substrate (12 %), because the relatively 
hydrophilic membrane surface partially hindered bacterial adhesion. 
The bimetallic AgCuNP-modified membrane showed the highest steril­
ization rate (> 99 %) as well as no visualized live cells (Fig. 8V) on the 
membrane surface compared to the monometallic AgNP- and CuNP- 
modified membranes (92 % and 65 %, Fig. 8III and IV), further con­
firming the superior antibacterial activity of the PDA/PEI-AgCu 
membrane.
3.4. Biofouling reversibility
E. coli. was selected as a model microorganism for dynamic 
biofouling experiments to evaluate the biofouling resistance of modified 
membranes. As shown in Fig. 9b, the water flux declined due to the 
formation of biofilm, which resulted from the adsorption and growth of 
bacteria. Compared to the commercial NF-270, the NP-modified mem­
branes (PDA/PEI-Ag, PDA/PEI-Cu, and PDA/PEI-AgCu) showed a 
slower decrease in membrane flux due to the bactericidal effect of AgNPs 
and CuNPs. Notably, the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane maintained the 
highest water flux after the 6-h biofouling.
After a 30-min cleaning using DI water, the NP-modified membranes 
demonstrated significantly improved flux recovery rates (FRR) 
compared to the NF-270 membrane. In addition, the bimetallic AgCuNP- 
modified membrane exhibited the lowest flux decline rate (FDR) (16 %, 
Fig. 9c) compared to the other membranes (PDA/PEI-Ag: 22 %, PDA/ 
PEI-Cu: 25 %, NF-270: 31 %). The modification with bimetallic 
AgCuNPs simultaneously improved the anti-biofouling property and 
flux recovery capability of membranes, demonstrating an intensified 
propensity for biofouling reversibility.

3.5. Antibacterial mechanism analysis
The reactive oxygen species (ROS) potentially generated at the 
membrane and bacteria interface were quantified to elucidate the 
antibacterial mechanism of the nanoparticles incorporated in the 
membranes. As shown in Fig. 9d, the metallic NPs can generate a variety 
of ROS, including H2O2, •OH, and O2
•– (Fan et al., 2021; Lv et al., 2018). 
The incorporation of AgCuNPs resulted in the highest ROS generation 
(H2O2: 71.6 mmol l-1 m-2, •OH: 43.4 mmol l-1 m-2, and O2
•–: 1.3 × 10–4) 
compared to the CuNPs (H2O2: 49.3 mmol l-1 m-2, •OH: 37.0 mmol⋅L-1 
m-2, and ⋅O2
- : 1.2 × 10–4) and AgNPs (H2O2: 59.9 mmol l-1 m-2, •OH: 42.9 
mmol l-1 m-2, and O2
•–: 1.3 × 10–4). Specifically, the concentration of 
H2O2 was ~2 times higher than that of the •OH produced on the surface 
of both monometallic and bimetallic NPs. The AgCuNPs produced a 
significantly higher amount of H2O2 and O2
•– compared to AgNPs and 
CuNPs, suggesting that these two ROS predominantly induced strong 
oxidative stress responses, thus enhancing the antibacterial activity of 
the PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane (Xie et al., 2022).
In previous studies, the antibacterial activity of nanoparticles has 
been attributed to two primary mechanisms, i.e., direct contact and 
ROS-induced oxidative stress (García et al., 2021; Kim et al., 2022a). The 
metallic ions (Cu2+ and Ag+) released from the nanoparticles can 
directly interact with lipopolysaccharides and biological proteins on the 
bacterial cell membrane, causing deactivation and loss of function (Yang 
et al., 2023). In the meantime, the accumulation of intracellular Cu2+
and Ag+ affects membrane integrity and disrupts osmotic pressure, 
inducing cell disruption and leakage of bacteria contents (Slavin et al., 
2017). ROS, generated from interactions between nanoparticles and 
microorganisms, act as signal molecules associated with cell damage (Fu 
et al., 2022).
Fig. 10 illustrates the antimicrobial mechanisms of the PDA/PEI- 
AgCu membrane. In this study, the AgCuNPs formed a core-shell struc­
ture (Fig. 2f), with AgNPs at the core and CuNPs at the shell, resulting in 
a metal-semiconductor heterostructure. Shielded by the copper oxide 
shell layer, the AgCuNPs-modified membrane exhibited enhanced sta­
bility and prolonged antibacterial properties (Fig. 7b-d). Considering 
that the AgNPs have a lower Fermi energy level than that of copper oxide 
(i.e., Cu2O), electrons are transferred from AgNPs (core layer) to copper 
oxide (shell layer) until reaching a thermodynamic equilibrium (Guleria 
et al., 2021; Shi et al., 2014). The charge transfer within AgCuNPs in­
tensifies the generation of ROS (Fig. 9d) and release of Ag+, contributing 
to a pronounced antimicrobial activity (Fig. 9a). Additionally, the 
reduced nanoparticle sizes played a crucial role in enhancing antimi­
crobial activity. The smaller size of AgCuNPs (Fig. 2f) than AgNPs 
(Fig. 2d) and CuNPs (Fig. 2e) increased the surface area ratio of the 
particles, which correlated with a higher number of active sites and 
lower energy barriers, making them more effective at attacking the 
bacterial cell membranes. Furthermore, the size reduction can be related 
to the increased ROS generation, leading to serious oxidative stress 
damage in the antimicrobial action of AgCuNPs. These synergistic 
mechanisms collectively enhanced the antibacterial efficiency of the 
PDA/PEI-AgCu membrane.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we successfully incorporated bimetallic AgCu nano­
alloys on the NF membrane surfaces in situ by reduction and chelation 
with the PDA/PEI intermediate layer. The obtained AgCu-modified 
membranes demonstrated significant enhancement in water treatment 
performance, antibacterial activity, biofouling resistance, and perfor­
mance stability. Compared to the monometallic Ag- or Cu-modified 
membranes, uniform core-shell structured AgCuNPs with smaller sizes 
were formed on the PDA/PEI layer, leading to improved water perme­
ability, salt rejection, and performance stability, as well as reduced ion 
release in the long-term operation. Additionally, the AgCuNP-modified 
membrane exhibited comparable water permeability while improved 
selectivity for separating MgCl2/NaCl compared to the commercial NF- 
270 membrane (selectivity of 5.2 vs. 2.1), owing to the increased iso­
electric point and stronger electrostatic repulsion for high valence cat­
ions (i.e., Mg2+). The bimetallic AgCuNP-modified membrane also 
displayed the highest sterilization rate (> 99 %) against E. coli compared 
to monometallic AgNP- or CuNP-modified membranes, ascribing to the 
substantial generation of ROS (H2O2: 71.6 mmol l-1 m-2, •OH: 43.4 mmol 
l-1 m-2, and O2
•–: 1.3 × 10–4) at the interface between the NPs and mi­
croorganisms. The synergistic effects of the unique properties of 
AgCuNPs such as structure, atomic composition, charge transfer, and 
ROS generation significantly enhanced the antibacterial efficiency of the 
AgCuNP-modified membrane. This study demonstrated a facile method 
for modifying NF membranes with bimetallic AgCuNPs, resulting in 
membranes with enhanced antibacterial activity and biofouling 
reversibility, offering promising potential for water treatment 
applications.
