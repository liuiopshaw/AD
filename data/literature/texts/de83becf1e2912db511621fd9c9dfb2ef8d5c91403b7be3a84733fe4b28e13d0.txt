1  Introduction

industrial products because of its softness, breathability, and 
biodegradability [1–3]. However, their intrinsic abundance 
of hydroxyl groups leads to strong hydrophilicity, which 
causes capillary hysteresis and moisture retention under

Abstract
Advanced personal moisture management requires textiles that can rapidly regulate humidity, inhibit microbial growth, 
and maintain long‑term surface cleanliness. However, traditional cotton fabrics possess inherent hydrophilicity due to 
their abundant surface hydroxyl groups, leading to capillary hysteresis, moisture accumulation, and uncomfortable skin 
sensations under perspiration. Humid conditions further accelerate microbial proliferation, limiting their use in high-
cleaning or high-comfort environments. Here, we present cotton‑based Metafabrics engineered through in-situ chemistry, 
patterned plasma etching, and hierarchical micro–nano structuring on common cotton to achieve asymmetric wettability. 
2,2,6,6-Tetramethylpiperidoxyl (TEMPO) oxidation and in‑situ growth of copper 1,3,5‑benzenetricarboxylate (CuBTC) 
generate stable anchoring sites and antibacterial functionality, while dodecyltrimethoxysilane (DTMS) modification forms 
a durable superhydrophobic surface. Patterned etching creates hydrophilic microdomains that establish a through-thickness 
wetting gradient and the Laplace pressure difference, enabling rapid anti-gravity one-way moisture transport. The Metafab­
rics exhibit strong self-cleaning performance, nearly complete antibacterial efficiency, and rapid liquid absorption within 
seconds, retaining these properties even after repeated washing and abrasion. This work presents a scalable strategy for 
multifunctional Metafabrics that integrate directional moisture regulation, hygiene protection, and durability to develop 
high-performance functional textiles.

these limitations [27, 38–39]. Their micro-nano porous net­
works and hierarchical surface roughness can stabilize the 
Cassie–Baxter wetting state (a regime in which air pock­

Integration of MOFs into cotton fabrics through spray­
ing, pressing, or in situ growth has been widely explored 
to better balance moisture regulation and mechanical per­
formance [42–43]. However, most MOF-based fabrics still 
lack an asymmetric wettability gradient for rapid moisture 
transport, and many MOFs do not possess intrinsic antibac­

often relies on physical interactions, resulting in weak inter­
facial adhesion and poor laundering durability [44–45]. In 
parallel, alternative surface-chemical approaches, such as 
nature-inspired polyphenol chemistry, can produce robust 
superhydrophobic coatings with strong substrate adhesion 
[46–47]; however, these strategies are typically limited to 
single functions (e.g., oil–water separation or static hydro­
phobicity) and rarely integrate directional wetting control, 
antibacterial performance, and scalable fabrication.
Copper benzene-1,3,5-tricarboxylate MOF (CuBTC, 
also known as HKUST-1), which possesses intrinsic anti­
microbial activity and a well-defined porous structure, is 
an ideal candidate for constructing multifunctional cot­
ton-based materials. Herein, we develop a cotton-based 
Metafabrics—a metamaterial-inspired textile engineered 
with deliberate micro-/nano-hierarchical structures and 
chemical patterning to deliver properties beyond those of 
conventional fabrics, including directional water transport 
and programmable thermo-wet management. The fabrics 
feature radially arranged microchannels and an asymmet­
ric wettability gradient, achieved via a scalable patterned 
plasma etching–assisted multistep modification process. 
2,2,6,6-Tetramethylpiperidoxyl (TEMPO) oxidized cotton 
fabric serves as the structural scaffold for uniform in‑situ 
nucleation and layer‑by‑layer growth of CuBTC, forming 
a micro‑nano hierarchical rough architecture. Subsequent 
dodecyltrimethoxysilane (DTMS) modification induces 
a stable superhydrophobic surface, while Plasma etch­
ing generates hydrophilic micro‑domains that establish a 
through‑thickness wetting gradient. The combination of 
hierarchical roughness and low surface energy stabilizes 
the Cassie–Baxter state, whereas the hydrophilic domains 
and internal channels produce a Laplace pressure difference 
that drives rapid one‑way water transport. Meanwhile, the 
dense CuBTC coating provides nearly complete antibac­
terial activity against S. aureus and E. coli. Overall, this 
study demonstrates a scalable strategy for integrating unidi­
rectional moisture regulation, antibacterial protection, and 
mechanical durability into cotton-based fabrics, offering 
new insights for advancing next-generation high-perfor­
mance smart textiles.
2  Experimental Section
2.1  Materials
Sodium bromide (NaBr, analytical grade AR), 2,2,6,6-Tet­
ramethylpiperidoxyl (TEMPO, AR), sodium hypochlorite 
(NaClO, AR), hydrochloric acid (HCl, AR), anhydrous eth­
anol (EtOH, AR), dodecyltrimethoxysilane (DTMS, AR), 
and copper(II) nitrate trihydrate (Cu(NO3)2·3H2O, AR) were 
purchased from Shanghai Titan Technology Co., Ltd. N, 
N-dimethylacetamide (DMA, AR), 1,3,5-benzenetricarbox­
ylic acid (AR), N, N-dimethylformamide (DMF, AR), and 
Rhodamine B (AR) were purchased from China National 
Pharmaceutical Group Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Cotton 
fabric was provided by Liangliang Textile Co., Ltd. Staphy­
lococcus aureus and Escherichia coli were obtained from 
the American Type Culture Collection. Deionized water was 
used throughout the experimental procedures. Unless other­
wise specified, all reagents were used as received without 
further purification.
2.2  Preparation of T-cotton
0.125 g of TEMPO and 3.2 g of NaBr were dissolved in 500 
mL of deionized water, and 10 g of cotton fabric was fully 
immersed. A 5 mM NaClO solution was added, and 1 M 
NaOH was dripped in to maintain a pH of approximately 10. 
After the reaction, 3 mL of ethanol was added to terminate 
the reaction, and the fabric was neutralized with 4 M HCl. 
The fabric was then removed, washed with water, and dried 
at 80 °C to obtain T-cotton.
2.3  Preparation of CuBTC@fabric
A layer-by-layer assembly method was used to load CuBTC 
onto the surface of T-cotton. 30 mL of DMA, 30 mL of 
EtOH, and 30 mL of deionized water were mixed evenly 
to obtain a mixed solvent. 3.8  g of Cu(NO3)2·3H2O was 
dissolved in 48 mL of this solvent to form Solution A, and 
1.35 g of 1,3,5-benzenetricarboxylic acid was dissolved in 
another 48 mL to form Solution B. Solution B was added 
dropwise to Solution A with stirring for 1 h, followed by 
centrifugation and ethanol washing. The product was then

The product was washed five times with 50 mL of ethanol

CuBTC@fabric while preserving the native inter-fiber 
porosity and imparting inherent antibacterial functionality.
2.4  Preparation of DTMS/CuBTC@fabric
The CuBTC@fabric is then modified with DTMS to form 
a stable low‑surface‑energy coating, producing DTMS/
CuBTC@fabric with robust superhydrophobicity. Specifi­
cally, 10 mL of DTMS was mixed with 3 mL of ethanol and 
sonicated for 30 min. CuBTC@fabric was immersed in this 
solution to allow DTMS to react with the surface and form 
a hydrophobic layer. This process was repeated five times 
to enhance the effect, resulting in superhydrophobic cotton.
2.5  Preparation of cotton‑based metafabrics
Patterned oxygen plasma treatment was used to achieve 
directional moisture transport. Metafabrics were treated 
with a patterned acrylic board with holes (parameters: pat­
tern size 1.5  mm, treatment power 80 W, treatment time 
150 s, treatment vacuum 20 Pa).
3  Results and discussion
Traditional textiles are inherently limited in liquid manage­
ment (Figure 1a). Their disordered fiber networks cannot 
establish effective directional wetting pathways, resulting in 
moisture accumulation, hindered vapor exchange, and accel­
erated microbial growth in sweat-rich environments. These 
shortcomings compromise overall comfort and hygiene, 
underscoring the need for cotton-based Metafabrics that can 
facilitate unidirectional liquid transport, provide long-term 
breathability, and offer reliable antibacterial protection. 
As shown in Fig. 1a, Metafabrics achieve these functions 
through a finely engineered hierarchical structure. A multi­
scale rough surface and an interconnected porous network 
enable the coexistence of superhydrophobicity and high air 
permeability, while a precisely constructed through‑thick­
ness wettability gradient drives rapid directional moisture 
transfer. The hydrophobic outer surface repels incoming 
liquid, and the hydrophilic inner regions continuously draw 
moisture away, keeping the skin dry and maintaining an 
efficient vapor‑exchange pathway. At the same time, the 
Metafabrics exhibit strong antibacterial properties, ensuring 
a clean, comfortable interface during prolonged wear. 
The fabrication of the Metafabrics begins with hydro­
philic common cotton (Fig. 1b), which is first subjected to 
TEMPO oxidation to selectively convert surface hydroxyl 
groups into carboxyl groups, thereby increasing surface 
roughness and generating abundant anchoring sites for sub­
sequent CuBTC assembly. Then, a stable low‑surface‑energy 
coating and plasma etching were performed to obtain Meta­
fabrics with a reliable through-thickness wettability gradi­
ent. This asymmetric interface enables rapid unidirectional 
liquid transport while maintaining high breathability and 
antimicrobial performance. The plasma-based patterning 
strategy is compatible with continuous fabrication, enabling 
the production of large-area Metafabrics with excellent flex­
ibility and processability (Fig. 1c).
To reveal how each fabrication step contributes to the 
formation of the wettability‑gradient Metafabrics, we sys­
tematically characterized the chemical structure and micro­
morphological evolution of the cotton substrate throughout 
oxidation, MOF growth, silane modification, and plasma 
patterning. We first examined the changes that occurred dur­
ing the TEMPO oxidation of cotton fibers. As shown in Fig­
ure S1, Supporting Information, the fabric initially became 
slightly whiter and then gradually yellowed as the oxida­
tion time increased, reflecting the successive formation of 
oxygen-containing functional groups and a small number of 
chromophores. FTIR analysis (Figure S2, Supporting Infor­
mation) confirmed this evolution: the O–H stretching peak 
at 3308 cm− 1 became sharper, and a characteristic carboxyl­
ate peak at 1598 cm− 1 emerged, indicating successful gener­
ation of carboxyl groups. X-ray diffraction patterns (Figure 
S3, Supporting Information) retained the typical cellulose 
I peaks at 14.9°, 16.6°, 22.6°, and 34.7°, demonstrating 
that oxidation does not disrupt the crystalline framework. 
Mechanical testing (Figure S4, Supporting Information) 
revealed a moderate decrease in strength, consistent with 
partial cellulose chain cleavage [48]. Meanwhile, SEM 
images (Figure S5, Supporting Information) displayed pro­
gressively roughened fiber surfaces with increased grooves 
and pores, which benefit the subsequent in-situ growth of 
CuBTC.
We then investigated the relationship between oxidation 
time and the density of functional groups. As shown in Figure 
S6, Supporting Information, the carboxyl content increased 
with oxidation time and plateaued at approximately 20 min, 
whereas longer treatment caused unnecessary mechani­
cal weakening. Based on the combined chemical, struc­
tural, and mechanical results, cotton oxidized for 20  min 
was selected as the optimized substrate for Metafabrics

The Metafabrics also demonstrate prominent self-cleaning

liquid adhesion and facilitates contamination removal. This 
is enabled by a “functional zoning” design: micrometer-

spatially isolated within a continuous superhydrophobic 
matrix, thereby preserving the overall liquid-repellent and 
self-cleaning properties.

4  Conclusions
In summary, we developed a scalable strategy to construct 
asymmetric wettability on cotton fabrics by integrating 
TEMPO oxidation, in situ CuBTC growth, DTMS hydro­
phobic modification, and patterned plasma etching. This 
process forms a robust micro–nano hierarchical architecture 
and a stable through‑thickness wetting gradient. The engi­
neered Metafabrics achieve rapid anti‑gravity unidirectional 
the patterned treatment. These significant changes indicate 
that the constructed micro-nano composite structure pro­
vides continuous channels for rapid water exchange and 
transport throughout the Metafabrics.
The mechanical durability of the wettability contrast was 
examined using an abrasion test. As shown in Fig. 3e, even 
after 100 sandpaper abrasion cycles, the hydrophobic region 
maintained a contact angle above 135.6°, confirming strong 
resistance to surface wear. SEM images (Fig.  3g) reveal 
that the hierarchical rough structure remained largely intact, 
suggesting that the preserved micro-texture is responsible 
for maintaining hydrophobicity. Functionally, the direc­
tional transport capability also remained effective—after 20 
abrasion cycles, a droplet could still travel from the hydro­
phobic to the hydrophilic side in approximately 3 s (Figure 
S12, Supporting Information), demonstrating the robustness 
of the asymmetric wetting channels.
Wash durability was further evaluated. After 25 stan­
dard washing cycles (according to the AATCC Test Method 
61-2013 (No 2 A)), the hydrophobic side maintained a con­
tact angle of 141.1° (Fig. 3f), indicating strong adhesion of 
the hydrophobic coating. SEM images (Fig. 3h) show that 
the micro-rough structure and CuBTC particles were well 
preserved, providing stable structural support for the coat­
ing’s performance. Even after 25 washing cycles, unidirec­
tional water transport remained highly efficient—a droplet 
could pass through the Metafabrics from the hydrophobic 
side to the hydrophilic side within approximately 3.8 s (Fig­
ure S13, Supporting Information). This stability is attributed 
to the robust interface bonding formed by the PP Etching 
strategy and to the protective effect of the DTMS layer in 
anchoring CuBTC particles during repeated washing. In 
addition, we further evaluated the comprehensive perfor­
mance of the Metafabrics to demonstrate its multifunction­
ality, including antibacterial activity, breathability, moisture 
regulation, and self-cleaning capability. As shown in Fig. 4a, 
the Metafabrics exhibit pronounced antibacterial efficacy 
against both S. aureus and E. coli. This effect is primarily 
attributed to the CuBTC component, whose metal–organic 
framework structure can disrupt bacterial membranes and 
inhibit growth. Furthermore, the inhibition-zone results in 
Figure S14 also confirm the outstanding antibacterial per­
formance of the Metafabrics and the durability of their anti­
bacterial activity [53].

cal factors affecting textile durability, we systematically 
evaluated the breathability and moisture-permeability per­
formance after surface modification. The pristine T-cot­
ton exhibits a breathability of approximately 270 mm s− 1 
(Figure S15, Supporting Information, according to ISO 
9237:1995). Following CuBTC loading and DTMS hydro­
