RESEARCH PAPER
Norfloxacin detection based on the peroxidase-like activity
enhancement of gold nanoclusters
Yuying Song1,2 & Juan Qiao2,3 & Wei Liu1 & Li Qi2,3
Received: 17 September 2020 /Revised: 20 October 2020 /Accepted: 9 November 2020
# Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2020
Abstract
The use of nanomaterials as mimic enzymes provides a promising way to implement bio-molecule detection in living systems.
However, to achieve highly efficient catalytic processes with gold nanocluster-based nanozymes is still challenging. In this study,
a facile reduction method was utilized to synthesize gold nanoclusters with 1-methyl-D-tryptophan as the reducing and capping
agent. The obtained gold nanoclusters exhibited a peroxidase-mimicking property in the redox reaction of 3,3′,5,5′-
tetramethylbenzidine to blue oxidized 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine in the presence of H2O2. The addition of norfloxacin
endowed the nanozymes with a 10-fold enhancement in catalytic efficiency due to the surface charge-controlled electron transfer
modulation. The colorimetric sensing system presented a high selectivity toward norfloxacin. The good linear relationship of
norfloxacin monitoring was gained in the range of 1.25~8.0 μM (R2 = 0.996), with a detection limit of 0.2 μM. The practical
application of the proposed protocol for the measurement of norfloxacin in capsules was realized. This demonstrates that on
account of their significant catalytic efficiency enhancement, the gold nanocluster-based nanozymes hold great promise in
realizing the selective detection of drugs.
Keywords Gold nanoclusters . Peroxidase-like activity . Norfloxacin . 3,3′,5,5′-Tetramethylbenzidine
Introduction
Norfloxacin (NOR) is a type of fluoroquinolone broad-
spectrum antimicrobial drug that is widely used in clinical
treatments and has a positive effect on various inflammation.
However, patients may have to suffer from the side and toxic
effects of NOR, which are caused by overdose, such as fetal
malformation and renal function damage [1, 2]. Moreover, the
presence of antibacterials (including NOR) in the environment
can lead to the development of antibacterial resistance in or-
ganisms [3–5]. Therefore, it is reasonable to consider the tox-
icity and resistance of antibacterials. NOR monitoring in clin-
ical therapy is of great importance for human health and the
quality control of drugs.
In recent years, various techniques for analysis of NOR
have been utilized, including high-performance liquid chro-
matography [6], enzyme-linked immunoassays [7], the elec-
trochemical method [8, 9], and chemiluminescence assays
[10, 11]. Although these protocols perform well and have high
sensitivity, their shortcomings including harmful organic sol-
vents, complicated and time-consuming operation, and expen-
sive and sophisticated instrumentation limit their applications.
Therefore, it is desirable and a great need to construct a sim-
ple, sensitive, and environmentally friendly technique to de-
tect the presence of NOR.
A simple and colorimetric sensing strategy that can be im-
plemented in real time can help overcome the above-
mentioned drawbacks [12–15]. In particular, the protocol
based on the enzymatic transformation of chromogenic sub-
strates, such as 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) to blue
oxidized 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (oxTMB) in the pres-
ence of H2O2, has attracted significant research interests

[16–20]. The newly developed gold nanoclusters (AuNCs)
[12, 17] have been used in the detecting of various bio-mole-
cules, such as glucose [20, 21], glutathione [22], dopamine
[23], and cancel cells [24, 25] because of their peroxidase
activity. Nevertheless, the catalytic efficiency of some
nanozymes is lower than that of natural enzymes. It is an
aspect that still needs to be improved to meet the requirements
for their practical application for sensing antibacterials.
Generally, the ligands capping thickness [21], surface in-
teraction [26], aggregation, and free radicals [27] of the
nanozymes are critical factors for their catalytic efficiency
enhancement. For instance, the peroxidase-like activity of
magnetic iron oxide nanoparticles could be improved by
changing their ligand-coating thicknesses [21]. A significant
enhancement of the peroxidase activity of DNA protected iron
oxide nanoparticles for TMB oxidation has been realized,
which was attributed to both electrostatic interactions and ar-
omatic stacking with TMB [26]. Melamine has been found to
induce the aggregation of the gold nanoparticles, which was
triggered to generate more reactive oxygen species (ROS) and
improved the catalytic efficiency of gold nanoparticle-based
nanozymes in the TMB-H2O2 system [27]. Although exten-
sive efforts have been made to improve the catalytic efficiency
of nanomaterial-based nanozymes, studies on the modulating
surface charges of AuNCs-based nanozymes to enhance their
catalytic efficiency for sensing antibacterials are relatively
few. Therefore, it is of great scientific interest to create new
AuNCs-based nanozymes that are capable of tunable surface
charge-controlled electron transfer with improved peroxidase-
like activity for NOR detection.
In this work, 1-methyl-D-tryptophan-capped gold
nanoclusters (1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs) were synthesized by a
facile reduction method using 1-Me-D-Trp as the reducing
and capping agent. The prepared 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs ex-
hibited peroxidase-like activity. NOR had the ability to signif-
icantly enhance the catalytic efficiency of the nanozymes by
modulating the surface charge-controlled electron transfer,
which promoted the oxidation process of TMB in the presence
of H2O2 and produced blue oxTMB. The method was further
applied for measuring the NOR in capsules. It demonstrated a
new perspective for improving nanozymes catalytic efficiency
for monitoring drugs.
Results
Characterization of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs
The fluorescence spectra of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs exhibited
an excitation and emission wavelength maximum at 380 and
470 nm (Electronic Supplementary Material (ESM) Fig. S1A)
[28], respectively. 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs solution was pale
yellow under white light (ESM Fig. S1A-a) while emitting a
strong blue fluorescence under the UV light at 365 nm (ESM
Fig. S1A-b). Notably, the characteristic surface plasmon ab-
sorption of gold nanoparticles was not found in the UV-vis
spectra of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs solution (ESM Fig. S1B).
ESM Fig. S2 displays the Fourier transform infrared (FT-
IR) spectra of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs. The absorption peak at
3265 cm−1 was attributed to the stretching vibration of the N–
H group of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs [28]. The absorption peak
at 1745 cm−1, 1620 cm−1, and 1406 cm−1 were ascribed to the
stretching vibration of the C=O, –COOH, and phenyl groups
of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs, respectively.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) shows that the
diameter of the spherical-shaped 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs was
about 2.3 nm, with excellent size uniformity (ESM Fig. S3).
All of the above results indicated that 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs
was successfully synthesized.
Peroxidase-like activity of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs
The peroxidase-like activity of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs with
and without NOR was investigated. Figure 1b displays that
the nanozymes had negligible catalytic activity in the absence
of NOR. In contrast, after the addition of NOR, the catalytic
efficiency of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs significantly increased
(Fig. 1c) in the TMB-H2O2 system and the solution turned
deep blue (inset photo in Fig. 1).
The steady-state kinetics of the peroxidase-like activity of
1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs in the presence and absence of NOR
were also investigated (Fig. 2). The kinetics information is
shown in ESM Table S1. In general, a higher Michaelis-
Menten maximum reaction rate constant (Vmax) indicates that
the catalytic reaction step is favored. In contrast, a lower
Michaelis-Menten constant (Km) implies a higher affinity be-
tween the enzymes and the substrates. The Km value of 1-Me-
D-Trp@AuNCs in the presence of NOR was higher than that
that without NOR, showing that a higher TMB concentration
was required for maximum catalytic efficiency. With H2O2 as

Optimization of NOR determination conditions
To realize improved nanoezmys catalytic efficiency, the opti-
mum conditions for preparing 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs, includ-
ing the concentration of 1-Me-D-Trp (5.0–15.0 mM) and
HAuCl4 (3.0–15.0 mM), the synthesis duration (0–2.0 h),
and temperature (80–120 °C), were investigated. As shown
in ESM Fig. S4A and S4B, when 10.0 mM 1-Me-D-Trp and
5.0 mM HAuCl4 were used for the synthesis of 1-Me-D-
Trp@AuNCs, the nanozymes exhibited the best catalytic ef-
ficiency in the presence of NOR. ESM Fig. S4C and S4D
demonstrate that the nanozymes prepared at 100 °C for 1.0 h
could well perform in the TMB-H2O2 system with NOR.
To select the best testing conditions for the detection of
NOR, the catalytic reaction time, pH value of the catalytic
reaction solution, and catalytic reaction temperature were in-
vestigated. ESM Fig. S5 shows that the catalytic efficiency of
the nanozymes increased with reaction times, reaching a max-
imum at 30 min and remaining constant even if the reaction
time was increased to 40 min. Once the pH of the catalytic
reaction solution changed from 2.0 to 6.0, significant changes
in catalytic efficiency were observed, which peaked at pH 3.0,
as shown in ESM Fig. S6A. ESM Fig. S6B indicates that the
catalytic efficiency of the nanozymes increased with the reac-
tion temperature, reaching a peak at 25 °C and then decreasing
from 35 to 55 °C.

However, the number of O2

It should be noted that the surface electrostatic interaction
and surface charge control also played a key role in modulat-
ing substrate adsorption [32]. TMB contains two amine
groups, which have a strong affinity toward the negatively
charged nanozyme surface. A previous work [21] has reported
that a negatively charged Fe3O4 nanoparticle-based nanozyme
had a strong affinity and catalytic efficiency toward TMB. In
this work, the positively charged 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs (ζ =
18.3 mV) had weak affinity and catalytic efficiency toward the
positively charged TMB (Fig. 1b). NOR was negatively
charged (ζ = −1.1 mV) and could be adsorbed onto the sur-
face of the positively charged 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs.
Notably, the 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs-NOR exhibited much
stronger affinity and catalytic efficiency toward TMB (Fig.
1c) than the nanozymes. From this, it could be deduced that
the surface-adsorbed NOR triggered the surface charge mod-
ulation and further improved the catalytic efficiency of the
nanozymes (Fig. 3).
To facilitate a more in-depth understanding of the surface-
charge modulation mechanism of catalytic efficiency en-
hancement, the peroxidase-like activity of 1-Me-D-
Trp@AuNCs with the negatively charged peroxidase sub-
strate 2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulphonic acid)
(ABTS) was studied. ABTS contains two sulfonic acid groups
and exhibits stronger affinity toward positively charged
nanozyme surfaces [19]. In the presence of NOR, the absor-
bance intensity of the reaction solution obviously decreased
(ESM Fig. S9), indicating that 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs had a
strong affinity and catalytic efficiency toward ABTS. This
result further suggested that the electrostatic attraction and
the surface-charge modulation of TMB and the 1-Me-D-
Trp@AuNCs should be the main factor affecting the catalytic
efficiency enhancement.
The zeta potentials of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs before and
after the addition of NOR were measured and were found to
be 18.3 mV and 17.0 mV, respectively (ESM Fig. S10). The
decrease in the zeta potential of the nanozymes-NOR further
proved the negatively charged NOR adsorption onto the sur-
face of the positively charged 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs (Fig. 3).
Moreover, the morphological characteristics of 1-Me-D-
Trp@AuNCs in the absence and presence of NOR were stud-
ied. The average diameter of the 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs esti-
mated with TEM was about 2.3 nm (ESM Fig. S3). After the
addition of NOR, the diameter of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs-
NOR increased to 5.2 nm (Fig. 4), indicating NOR adsorption
onto the surface of the nanozymes.
ESM Fig. S11 describes that Au0 mainly existed in the
reaction system in the absence of NOR. However, after adding
NOR, the peak area changed significantly, especially the peak
position of Au0 moved to a higher binding energy, which was
in the energy region of typical Au3+. The results demonstrated
that NOR could interact with 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs to change
the binding energy of Au and release more Au3+, which could
be used as oxidant to oxidize TMB and further enhance the
catalytic efficiency of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs [32].

acids, glucose, vitamin C, and antibiotic drugs would not in-
terfere with NOR detection once using the proposed protocol.
Consequently, a colorimetric assay for the detection of
NOR was established. Figure 6 shows that the UV-vis absor-
bance intensity of 1-Me-D-Trp@AuNCs-TMB-H2O2 in-
creased with an increase in NOR. A good linear relationship
for monitoring NOR was realized, ranging from 1.25 to
8.0 μM (R2 = 0.996), with a detection limit of 0.2 μM.
Three commercial NOR capsules (containing 100.0 mg of
NOR per capsule) were analyzed using the proposed assay.
The results obtained for mass per capsule were 96.5 ±
1.14 mg, 98.0 ± 1.74 mg, and 102.5 ± 1.31 mg. Furthermore,
to verify the effectiveness of the colorimetric assay, the NOR
in capsules was determined using the standard addition meth-
od. ESM Table S2 shows that the recovery was 94.5–106.8%
and the relative standard deviation (RSD) was less than 4.0%.
These results proved that the colorimetric protocol has
displayed great potential in analysis of real capsules for de-
tecting NOR.
