Nanozymes
Photo-Switchable Peroxidase/Catalase-Like Activity of Carbon
Quantum Dots
Wei Yang, Tianchi Leng, Weicheng Miao, Xiao Cao, Haoran Chen, Feifei Xu, and
Yimin Fang*
Abstract: Nanozymes possess multi-enzyme activities
over the natural enzymes, which produce multi-pathway
synergistic effects for varies of biomedical applications.
Unfortunately, their multi-enzyme activities are in fight-
ing, significantly reducing the synergistic effects. Dy-
namic regulation of their multi-enzyme activities is the
bottleneck for intelligent therapies. Herein, we construct
a novel oxygen-nitrogen functionalized carbon quantum
dots (O/N-CQDs) with peroxidase-like (Reactive oxy-
gen species (ROS) producer) activity. Interestingly, the
peroxidase-like activity can be reversibly converted to
catalase-like (ROS scavenger) activity under visible light
irradiation. It is found that both the peroxidase/catalase-
like activity of O/N-CQDs can be precisely manipulated
by the light intensity. The mechanism of switchable
enzyme activities is attributed to the polarization of
quinoid nitrogen in polyaniline (PANI) precursor re-
tained on O/N-CQDs under visible light, which con-
sumes the ROS to produce O2 and H2O. As a proof-of-
concept demonstration, we are able to non-intrusively
up and down regulate the ROS level in cells successfully
by simply switching off and on the light respectively,
potentially facilitating the precise medicine based on the
development of the disease. Indeed, the photo-switch-
able
peroxidase/catalase-like
activity
of
O/N-CQDs
opens a non-invasive strategy for better manipulations
of the multi-activity of nanozymes, promising their wider
and more intelligent biomedical applications.
Introduction
In recent years, great progress has been made in the design
of artificial enzymes, and a series of nanozymes with similar
activities to natural enzymes have emerged.[1] Nanozyme is a
kind of nanomaterial containing enzyme-like biological
effects, which is the cross-product of nanotechnology and
life science.[1b,2] Compared with natural enzymes, nanozymes
have the advantages of high stability, low cost, and various
activities, which have broad potential applications in the
biomedical field.[2] In fact, nanozymes with multi-enzyme
activities have gradually occupied the historical stage.[3] In
contrast to single-enzyme activities, they are expected to
work synergistically to maintain the metabolic balance of
the organism.[4] For instance, to maintain intracellular redox
balance and protect the body from oxidative damage, a
series of enzymes such as peroxidase, superoxide dismutase
(SOD), catalase, etc. work together to maintain intracellular
ROS metabolism balance.[4b] Nanozymes with multi-enzyme
activities to simulate complex natural living systems will be
of great benefit to their biomedical applications.[5] Unfortu-
nately, the rational design and precise manipulation of
multi-enzyme activities are complicated and elusive.[4a] On
the one hand, nanozymes such as CeO2
[6] with both oxidase
(peroxidase-like) and reductase (catalase-like) activities are
in-fighting,
which
significantly
reduces
the
therapeutic
efficacy. On the other hand, although the catalytic activity of
nanozymes can be regulated by size, doping of different
active sites, surface modification, etc.,[7] most nanozymes are
lack an artificial switch, which can regulate the catalytic
activity in a dynamic way. The manipulations of external
stimuli such as pH,[8] temperature,[9] DNA,[10] or light[11] are
capable of dynamically adjusting their activities,[12] but the
activities of nanozymes are always at a turn-on state (single-
enzyme activity),[13] inhibiting the performance of other
activities for further applications in biological systems. For
instance, peroxidase plays a crucial role in innate immunity
and various physiological processes, including apoptosis and
cell signaling.[14] When using peroxidase-like nanomaterials,
such as Fe3O4, ROS in cells can be effectively increased.[15]
However, if their catalytic activity remains in an open mode,
a large amount of excess ROS produced results in oxidative
damage to cells and tissues, leading to the development of
numerous human diseases, such as cardiovascular disease,
Alzheimer’s disease, and kidney disease.[16] The dynamic
regulation of multi-enzyme activities can avoid these prob-
lems. Therefore, it is highly desirable to develop nanozymes
with switchable multi-enzyme activities that not only can be
precisely regulated, but also switchable to other activities to
avoid the oxidative damages.
In this work, we used the PANI as a precursor to
construct an O/N-CQDs with excellent peroxidase-like
activity. Different from the previous works where only
single-enzyme activity can be regulated,[8–11] the peroxidase-
like activity can be reversibly converted to the catalase-like
activity under visible light irradiation. The conversion of

Results and Discussion
Recently, it has been found that carbon-based nanomaterials
functionalized by specific oxygen groups can be used as
peroxidase activity mimics.[17] Further study suggests that the
carbonyl in carbon nanomaterials is the catalytic site of
peroxidase activity, and the carboxyl group can improve the
binding affinity to the substrate, while the hydroxyl group
inhibits the activity of peroxidase, which is commonly
reported in carbon nanotubes and graphene QDs and
graphene materials.[17b] PANI has a sp2 hybrid arrangement
similar to carbon atoms in graphene. In general, the
preparation of oxygen-functionalized CQDs from carbon
materials requires the use of concentrated acids at high
temperatures[17c,18] due to the high stability of carbon-carbon
bond. In contrast, oxygen-functionalized CQDs prepared
using PANI as a precursor can be operated under mild
conditions or even at room temperature, as PANI is much
more reactive than graphene-based materials.[19] To this end,
O/N-CQDs were prepared by chemical oxidation of PANI
at room temperature, followed by a hydrothermal reaction
to remove the hydroxyl group (Figure 1a). Firstly, PANI
was prepared via chemical oxidation polymerization of
aniline monomer in the presence of (NH4)2S2O8.[20] Immedi-
ately, the as-prepared PANI was oxidized by ·OH produced
by H2O2 using Fe3O4 NPs as the catalyzer to prepare the
oxidized
segments
including
carbonyl,
hydroxyl,
and
carboxyl groups, as well as quinoid nitrogen on the surface.
Finally, the O/N-CQDs were obtained by carbonization
through
a
hydrothermal
reaction
process,
in
which
a
significant number of hydroxyl groups were sheared from
the surface to increase the peroxidase activity due to the

In addition, the switchability of peroxidase/catalase
activity under different color light irradiation were inves-
tigated. As shown in Figure 4a & b, under the same power
density, the enzyme activity of O/N-CQDs regulated by blue
light was significantly stronger than the green, yellow and
red light. The reason was probably due to its higher
absorption of blue light (Figure S22). Besides the wave-
length, the peroxidase/catalase activity could be precisely
regulated by the modulation of light intensity as shown in
Figure 4c, demonstrating that the peroxidase/catalase activ-
ity of O/N-CQDs could be autonomously switched and
precisely regulated with least invasion to the system. It is
known that peroxidase mainly produces ROS, while catalase
mainly clears ROS. Therefore, it is possible to regulate ROS
level positively and negatively using single nanozyme, which
is superior to traditional nanozymes that increased or
removed ROS level unilaterally, facilitating more intelligent
biomedical applications.

activity of O/N-CQDs. As the Fe doping in the O/N-CQDs
is not relevant to the catalase activity, the candidates for the
ROS scavenger should be the aniline moiety rather than
Fe2+. Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the PANI
(aniline moiety) is photoactive, which acts as an electron
donor for further reduction under light irradiation.[31] There-
fore, we believe that the aniline moiety is the key to the
photo-switched catalase-like activity. Thus, the possible
mechanism of visible light-induced switchover of O/N-CQDs
enzyme activity was illustrated in Figure 5g. Light irradiation
promoted the polarization of quinoid nitrogen (1) on the
surface of O/N-CQDs. The polarized nitrogen (2) was
energetic and positively charged, making it easier to react
with anion radicals ·O2
 to produce O2 and a secondary
amino group (4). This is similar to the previously reported
catalase activity as shown in Figure S27[32] in which the Fe

oxidized by the ·OH (previously produced by O/N-CQDs
via its peroxidase-like activity) and returned to the quinoid
nitrogen structure (1) to form H2O. In this catalytic cycle,
·O2

reduced by (4) to produce H2O, significantly reducing the
ROS level. The proposed reaction pathway of the catalase-
like catalytic cycle was also investigated by utilizing density
functional theory (DFT) calculations as shown in Figure 5h.
The positively charged polaron excited by light irradiation
was highly energetic that could react negatively charged
·O2
  to form the complex (3), which decomposed to form
the secondary amine (4) and O2. The secondary amine could
react with ·OH to form a complex (5), which decomposed
back to quinoid nitrogen through an oxidation process. The
change of Gibbs free energy in the whole process is
negative, suggesting that the catalytic loop was a thermody-
namically preferred reaction.
As demonstrated above, peroxidase/catalase activity of
O/N-CQDs could be precisely regulated from a ROS
producer to ROS scavenger by non-invasive light stimula-
tion, which was superior to the natural enzymes, as well as
the present artificial enzymes that increased or removed
ROS level unilaterally, facilitating more intelligent biomed-
ical application. For instance, H2O2 could be catalyzed by
horse radish peroxidase (HRP) to produce
·OH that
increased the absorption of TMB at 650 nm, the catalysis
could be significantly enhanced with the addition of O/N-
CQDs, while it could be largely inhibited (the absorption is
weaker than pure HRP in Figure 6a) under light stimulation,
indicating that O/N-CQDs were indeed ROS scavengers
under light irradiation. The catalase activity of O/N-CQDs
was compared with the SOD under the same concentration
to remove ·O2
  produced by xanthine oxidase. As illustrated
in Figure 6b, under light stimulation O/N-CQDs were found
to be a better ·O2
  scavenger than SOD, highlighting their
promising biological application. Therefore, O/N-CQDs
were further applied to up and down regulation of ROS in
cells by manipulating the peroxidase/catalase activity under
light irradiation. Firstly, the cytotoxicity of O/N-CQDs was
investigated. As shown in Figure S28, the toxic effect on
cells is neglective when the concentration of O/N-CQDs is
below 25 μg/mL, it exhibits a mild toxic effect on cells over
50 μg/mL. Then, intracellular ·OH was analyzed by utilizing
BBoxiProbe® O28 fluorescent probe in breast cancer MDA-
MB-231 cells (Figure 6c and d). In the absence of O/N-
CQDs, red fluorescence levels remained at a low level
regardless of the light stimulation. However, O/N-CQDs
incubated cells exhibited much higher ·OH levels due to
ingested O/N-CQDs that catalyzed the endogenous H2O2,
and decreased remarkably by turning on the light. Statistical
analysis of fluorescence intensity in single cells suggested
that the external light stimulation could quickly reverse the
ROS level to normal value, and some of which were even
lower than the normal values within 5 min light irradiation
(35 mW/cm2) (red dash box in Figure 6e). In this regard, for
the first time, we provided a novel photo-switchable nano-
zyme with a controllable multi-enzyme activity that could
precisely regulate the balance of ROS levels in cells,
facilitating more intelligent treatment based on the develop-
ment of diseases.
In
different

of

precise

treatment of diseases. To verify that the peroxidase/catalase
switchable activities of the O/N-CQDs can be potentially
employed for an intelligent therapy, we first investigated the
antibacterial experimental study of the O/N-CQDs on
Escherichia coli based on its peroxidase activity. As shown
in Figure 7a and b, in the presence of H2O2 treatment
(1 mM), bacterial activity was slightly inhibited compared
with the control group. However, bacterial activity was
largely inhibited in the presence of O/N-CQDs due to the
peroxidase activity of the O/N-CQDs, which generated ROS
to kill the bacteria. Instead, under light stimulation, the
inhibitory effect on bacteria is largely reduced and even
lower than that of H2O2, indicating that the peroxidase
activity of the O/N-CQDs was switched to catalase activity
to clean up the ROS. Therefore, the O/N-CQDs can be used
as good candidates for antibacterial drugs for bacteria-
infected wounds by utilizing the peroxidase activity without
light irradiation. However, the high peroxidase activity of
the O/N-CQDs should be turned off after finishing killing
the bacteria to avoid higher oxidative damage and the
apoptosis of cells, whereas the catalase activity should be
turned on to help the healing of the wounds. To this end,
the catalase activity of O/N-CQDs was also investigated by
studying the cell viability under light irradiation. The cells

Conclusions
In summary, O/N-CQDs with both peroxidase and catalase
activities were fabricated. Interestingly, the multi-enzyme
activities of this nanozyme could be precisely regulated from
a ROS producer to a ROS scavenger by light irradiation.
The
reversible
switchover
of
peroxidase
and
catalase
activities was achieved by photo-regulation of the polar-
ization and depolarization of the quinone structure of
nitrogen on O/N-CQDs. Compared with the previously
reported nanozymes where their activities were regulated
unilaterally (single enzyme activity), this was the first report
on
the
reversible
photo-manipulation
of
multi-enzyme
activities within one nanozyme. As a non-invasive external
stimulus, light irradiation could be well-controlled in space
and time. Together with its precise modulation of multi-
enzyme activities, this system could reversibly regulate the
exogenous and endogenous ROS level in cells, which was
essential for maintaining the signal transduction and meta-
bolic balance. Using such an artificial “light switch”, it was
feasible to autonomously manipulate the ROS level that was
adaptive to the development of diseases, facilitating more
intelligent therapy. Such a ‘smart’ enzyme provides a new
vision for the intelligent regulation of multi-enzyme activ-
ities of nanozymes, and will find its wide applications in
precise medicine.
