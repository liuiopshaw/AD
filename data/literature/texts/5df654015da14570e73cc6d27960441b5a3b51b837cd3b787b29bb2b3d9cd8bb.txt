Design of synthetic structures that possess the similar functions to natural enzymes held great promise
in environmental detection and biomedical application. Herein, a new concept for the fabrication of
solid-supported catalysts as peroxidase mimic have been proposed to realize high-catalytic activity and
stability by utilizing expanded mesoporous silica (EMSN)-encapsulated Pt nanoclusters. Compared with
PtNCs, the introduction of amino group modiﬁed EMSN would enrich H2O2 on the surface of PtNCs and
increase the catalytic sites for H2O2 decomposition, which gave rise to the higher catalytic activity of
EMSN-PtNCs over a broad pH range, especially in weakly acidic and neural solutions. This would facilitate
their applications for real-time monitoring the secretion of H2O2 from living cancer cells stimulated by
various anticancer drugs. Our ﬁndings not only pave the way to use porous matrix as the structural
component for the design of the biomimetic catalysts, but also provide a simple and reliable platform to
monitor H2O2 released from living cells in real time, which holds great potential for elucidating the
biological roles of H2O2 and underlying molecular mechanisms of drug cytotoxicity as well as drug
therapeutic effects.

1. Introduction
Natural enzymes with high catalytic capability and substrate
speciﬁcity have attracted signiﬁcant research interest in biomedical
area due to their intrinsic biocatalytic property involved in all
biological process [1e3]. In spite of these substantial developments,

most natural enzymes are proteins or RNA molecules whose ac-
tivities are sensitive to the environmental conditions. Moreover, the
high cost for extracting and purifying and the low operational
stability limit the extensive use of natural enzymes [4,5]. To address
these problems, considerable efforts have been made to seek arti-
ﬁcial enzyme mimetics with similar functions to natural enzymes.
Recently, numerous functional nanomaterials have been veriﬁed to
exhibit enzyme-like catalytic activities [6e12]. For instance, metal
organic frameworks [6,7], metallic nanocomposites [8,9] as well as
carbon nanomaterials [10e12] have been conﬁrmed to possess
unique enzyme-like catalytic activity and held great promise in
environmental detection and biomedical application.
Hydrogen
peroxide
(H2O2)
as
a
ubiquitous
intracellular
messenger plays a critical role in the genesis of many vascular
diseases [13e15]. Moreover, H2O2 has been reported to function as
a common mediator for apoptosis induced by various anticancer
drugs [16e18]. Rapid and accurate detection of H2O2 in biological
environment, especially its dynamic release process from cancer
cells under the treatment of anticancer drugs is critically important
for elucidating the biological roles of H2O2 and underlying molec-
ular mechanisms of drug cytotoxicity as well as drug therapeutic
effects. Till now, various methods have been employed for moni-
toring H2O2 [19e21]. Among them, peroxidase-mediated oxidation
of chromogenic substrates is the most commonly used strategy
[22e24].
Therefore,
developing
highly-efﬁcient
and
stable
peroxidase-mimicking catalysts is of great interest.
Pt based nanomaterials especially ultrasmall Pt nanoclusters
(PtNCs) with excellent conductivity, unprecedented catalytic per-
formance and good biocompatibility have been widely used in
nanoelectronics, nanodevices, nanocomposites and biosensors
[25e27]. Very recently, PtNCs were reported to possess a peroxi-
dase mimetic activity and could be utilized as a sensitive colori-
metric sensor for H2O2 and glucose detection [28]. Although
promising, owing to the heterogeneous distribution and easy ag-
gregation, the catalytic activity and stability of PtNCs were rela-
tively low, which restricted their potential application as mimetic
enzyme and made them unsuitable for quantitative analysis in cells
or in vivo. Thus, there is an urgent need to develop novel ap-
proaches to obtain nanoclusters with stable and high catalytic
activity.
Nowadays, the progress in the continuing development of solid-
supported catalysts [29e31] inspired us to employ sieving matrices
to improve the catalytic activity and stability of PtNCs-based arti-
ﬁcial enzyme. Based on these discoveries, herein, we combined the
advantages of expanded mesoporous silica nanoparticles (EMSN)
with PtNCs to create a new solid-supported catalysts as a robust
peroxidase mimic for realizing high-catalytic activity and stability
(Scheme 1). Compared with PtNCs, the EMSN supported PtNCs
(EMSN-PtNCs) exhibited signiﬁcantly higher peroxidase-like ac-
tivity over a wide range of pH value. The EMSN played a critical role
in our catalytic platform. Initially, EMSN with high surface area and
controllable pore size can act as a sieving matrix for encapsulation
of PtNCs, leading to a high density of very small and homoge-
neously dispersed PtNCs. Meanwhile, the sieving matrix was able to
prevent irreversible aggregation of PtNCs. More interestingly, the
porous structure provided EMSN with the ability to be used as a
nanoreactor to allow rapid diffusion of reactants and products in
and out of the porous and protect the catalysts against in-
terferences from the external environment. Using mesoporous
silica nanoparticles (MSNs) with an average pore size of 2.3 nm and
solid silica nanoparticles (SSNs) as controls, the critical role of pore
size for enhancing catalytic activity was also clearly demonstrated.
Moreover, the catalytic activity of EMSN-PtNCs was strongly
dependent on the concentration of H2O2 and showed a nanomolar
detection limit, which guaranteed the possibility for real-time
tracking H2O2 released from living tumorigenic cells.

2.4. Preparation of EMSN
The MSN was ﬁrst synthesized according to the literatures
[29,30]. CTAB (0.50 g) was dissolved in 240 mL of D.I. water. Sodium
hydroxide (1.75 mL, 2 M) was added to the CTAB solution, followed
by increasing the reaction temperature to 80 C. TEOS (2.5 mL) was
added dropwise to the reaction solution under continuous stirring.
After stirring for another 3 h, the obtained white precipitates were
ﬁltered and washed with D.I. water and ethanol, and ﬁnally dried in
air. Then, EMSN was synthesized via a swelling agent incorporation
method [29,30]. The obtained MSN (0.50 g) were dispersed in
ethanol (15 mL) and then sonicated for 30 min. After that, a mixture
of D.I. water and 1,3,5-trimethylbenzene (30 mL, 1:1, v/v) was
added to the above solution. The mixed solution was placed in
autoclave and kept at 140 C for 24 h. The white powder was
washed with ethanol and D.I. water. To remove the CTAB, the
product was reﬂuxed for 16 h in a HCl/ethanol solution (1.00 mL of
37% HCl in 50 mL of ethanol). The obtained white product was
washed with D.I. water and methanol and dried under vacuum.
Finally, to modify the surface of EMSN with amine group, the un-
modiﬁed EMSN (200 mg) was suspended in a APTES (1 mmol)
solution in dry toluene (20 mL) and further heating under reﬂux for
24 h. The ﬁnal obtained precipitates were collected by vacuum
ﬁltration, washed with toluene, and dried under vacuum.
2.5. Synthesis of EMSN-PtNCs
The resulting EMSN were dispersed in distilled water (ﬁnal
concentration: 1 mg/mL) by sonication for 10 min, followed by the
addition of BSA (43 mg/mL), H2PtCl6$6H2O (8.2 mM) and NaOH
(86 mM) under vigorous stirring. After stirring for 30 min, a freshly
prepared NaBH4 (8.2 mM) was added into the above aqueous so-
lution. The reaction was kept for 1 h under stirring. The EMSN-
PtNCs were collected and puriﬁed by centrifugation to remove
the unreacted small molecules and unbounded PtNCs. Similarly,
mesoporous and non-porous silica-supported PtNCs (MSN-PtNCs
and SSN-PtNCs) were also prepared. Quantiﬁcation of the density of
PtNCs loaded into these three types of silica nanoparticles was
accomplished by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry
(ICP-MS), which corresponded to about 31.62 mg Pt/mg EMSN-
PtNCs, 8.38 mg Pt/mg EMSN-PtNCs and 9.28 mg Pt/mg SSN-PtNCs,
respectively. While the amount of Pt in PtNCs was determined to
be 78.42 mg Pt/mg PtNCs.
2.6. Steady-state kinetic assays
The kinetic measurements were performed in time course mode
by monitoring the absorbance change at 652 nm. Experiments
were carried out using PtNCs or EMSN-PtNCs (the ﬁnal concen-
tration of Pt was 2 mg/mL) in a reaction volume of 400 mL PBS (pH
5.0) with 0.42 mM TMB or 10 mM H2O2 as substrate. The Michaelis-
Menten constant was calculated using the Lineweaver-Burk plot: 1/
V ¼ (Km/Vmax)/Cþ1/Vmax, where V is the initial velocity, Vmax is the
maximal reaction velocity, and C is the concentration of substrate.
2.7. Detection of H2O2
For the detection of H2O2, 4 mL TMB (42 mM), 20 mL EMSN-PtNCs
(the concentration of Pt was 40 mg/mL) and 4 mL H2O2 with different
concentrations of were successively added into 372 mL PBS (pH 5.0)
solution. The mixture solution was then incubated in a thermostat
at room temperature for 10 min before the spectral measurements.
2.8. Detection of extracellular release of H2O2 from living cancer cell
Human epithelial A549 cells, human hepatoblastoma HepG2
cells, and mouse B16F10 melanoma cells obtained from the Fourth
Afﬁliated Hospital of Hebei Medical University, were chosen as the
model cells. The cells were cultured in RP1640 (Gibco BRL) sup-
plemented with 10% fetal bovine serum in a humidiﬁed 5% CO2
environment at 37
C and subcultured every 2 days. For the
detection of H2O2, the measurement was performed in 400 mL of
PBS (PH 5.0) containing 4 mL TMB (42 mM), 20 mL EMSN-PtNCs (the
concentration of Pt was 40 mg/mL) and 20 mL culture medium from
different cancer cells. For the detection of drug-stimulated H2O2 in
cell secretions, the living cells were pretreated with 100 mM
cisplatin or doxorubicin (DOX).

of approximately 8.4 nm was obtained. The prepared mono-
dispersed EMSNs were spherical in morphology with an average

employed to conﬁrm the successful fabricated EMSN-PtNCs (Fig. 1

3.2. The peroxidase-like activity and catalytic mechanism of EMSN-
PtNCs
Having established the successful fabrication of the EMSN-
PtNCs, we next investigated the peroxidase-like activity of the
novel nanocomposites. The catalytic ability of EMSN-PtNCs toward
peroxidase substrate TMB was ﬁrstly evaluated. As shown in
Fig. 2A, after incubation of EMSN-PtNCs with TMB in the presence
of H2O2 for 10 min at room temperature, the solution exhibited an
obvious blue color change, with a strong absorption at 652 nm.
Furthermore, an increased catalytic activity was observed with the
steady increase of the concentration of EMSN-PtNCs (Fig. S6). All
these results demonstrated that the as-prepared EMSN-PtNCs
exhibited an intrinsic peroxidase-like activity similar to horse-
radish peroxidase (HRP) [6,9]. Critically, compared with PtNCs, the
catalytic activity of EMSN-PtNCs enhanced almost eight times
(Fig. 2). In contract, EMSN-PtNCs in the absence of H2O2 or EMSN
without PtNCs but in the presence of H2O2 could not induce any
color change (Fig. 2A), indicating that the enhanced peroxidase-like
catalytic activity was originated from PtNCs encapsulated in EMSN.
Similar with other nanomaterial-based peroxidase mimics, the
catalytic activity of EMSN-PtNCs was dependent on many sur-
rounding conditions, such as pH, the concentration of TMB and
H2O2. As show in Fig. 2B, PtNCs only showed relatively high cata-
lytic activity in acidic solutions. However, compared with PtNCs
(Fig. 2B) and the natural HRP (Fig. S7), the EMSN-PtNCs exhibited
showed better catalytic efﬁciency over a broad pH range, especially
in weakly acidic and neural solutions, which would facilitate their
applications under physiological conditions or tumor environment.
Considering the further physiological application as well as the
experimental conditions we used, pH value of 5 was chosen as the
optimal pH for our subsequent experiment.
To investigate the role of EMSN in the enhanced catalytic ac-
tivity of EMSN-PtNCs, we used silica nanoparticles with different
pore sizes as the sieving matrix to synthesize different kinds of

may cause pore blocking. As shown in Fig. 3, the catalytic activity of
MCM-41 based PtNCs (MSN-PtNCs) was higher than that of PtNCs
but obviously lower than that of EMSN-PtNCs. Similar with MCM-
41, the BSA could also attach on the surface of amino group
modiﬁed solid silica nanoparticles (Fig. S8B) to form the BSA sta-

shown in Fig. S10, after dispersed in water for 30 days, the ﬂuo-

stability of PtNCs and expand their further application.
3.3. The steady-state kinetic assays
To further investigate the mechanism of the enhanced catalytic
activity of EMSN-PtNCs, we studied the apparent steady-state ki-
netic parameters for the reaction by enzyme kinetics theory and
method [29,31]. As illustrated in Fig. 4, typical Michaelis-Menten
curves were obtained within the suitable range of either H2O2
concentrations or TMB concentrations. Michaelis-Menten constant
(Km) and Maximum initial velocity (Vmax) were calculated with
Lineweaver-Burk plot (Table 1). The Km value indicated the binding
afﬁnity between enzymes and substrates, and the Vmax value
revealed the turnover number of an enzyme. With TMB as the
substrate, there were no obvious differences in the Km values be-
tween PtNCs and EMSN-PtNCs. However, compared with PtNCs, a
signiﬁcant increase in the Vmax value for the EMSN-PtNCs was
observed, suggesting that the introduction of EMSN did not make
any inﬂuence on the afﬁnity between PtNCs and TMB, but increased
the catalytic sites for TMB oxidation. On the other hand, with H2O2
as the substrate, the Km value of EMSN-PtNCs was notably lower
than that of PtNCs, while the Vmax value of EMSN-PtNCs was higher
than that of PtNCs indicating that the large amino group func-
tionalized pores of EMSN played a critical role in enriching H2O2 on
the surface of PtNCs and increasing the catalytic sites for H2O2
decomposition. All these phenomena gave rise to the higher

3.4. Real-time tracking H2O2 secretion in live cells
Due to the high sensitivity of EMSN-PtNCs for H2O2, we next
evaluated whether the EMSN-PtNCs can be used for H2O2 sensing.
As shown in Fig. 5A, as the concentration of H2O2 increased, the
absorbance of the oxidized TMB at 652 nm was intensiﬁed. The
absorbance intensity increased fast at low H2O2 concentrations and
became slower when the concentration was greater than 1 mM. In a
low concentration region (c ¼ 1e50 mM), the binding isotherm
revealed a linear relationship (Fig. 5A, insert). The limit of detection
was calculated to be 0.87 mM, which was comparable to the recently
reported nanozyme-based H2O2 sensors (Table 2) [37e43].
Inspired by the excellent performance of the synthesized EMSN-
PtNCs in sensing H2O2, we extended our work to real-time track the
secretion of H2O2 in different cancer cells and evaluate the role of
H2O2 in the therapeutic mechanism of various anticancer drugs.
Previous studies reported that accumulation of hydrogen peroxide
was one of the critical mechanism in cisplatin-induced apoptosis
[44e46]. For real-time monitoring H2O2 released from living cells
upon stimulated by cisplatin, three types of living cancer cells,
human epithelial A549 cells, human hepatoblastoma HepG2 cells,
and mouse B16F10 melanoma cells, were chosen as the model cells.

cells, the systematic study of their responses under stimulation by

4. Conclusions
In summary, we demonstrated the interesting example that
PtNCs encapsulated in expanded mesoporous silica to create a new
solid-supported catalysts as a robust peroxidase mimic for realizing
high-catalytic activity and stability. Compared with PtNCs, the
EMSN-PtNCs exhibited higher activity over a broad pH range,
especially in weakly acidic and neural solutions, which would
facilitate their applications under physiological conditions or tumor
environment. By measuring the enzyme kinetic parameters of
PtNCs and EMSN-PtNCs and comparing the catalytic activities of
different kinds of silica nanoparticle-supported PtNCs, we specu-
lated that introduction of amino group modiﬁed EMSN might
enrich H2O2 on the surface of PtNCs and increase the catalytic sites
for H2O2 decomposition, which gave rise to the higher catalytic
activity of EMSN-PtNCs. In addition, owing to the excellent perox-
idase mimic activity in weakly acidic environment, our synthesized
EMSN-PtNCs can be successfully applied to real-time track the
secretion of H2O2 in different living cancer cells stimulated by
various anticancer drugs. Our study not only demonstrates a
feasible approach to synthesis nanozymes with high catalytic ac-
tivities, but also provides a simple and reliable platform to monitor
H2O2 released from living cells in real time, which holds great po-
tential for elucidating the biological roles of H2O2 and underlying
molecular mechanisms of drug cytotoxicity as well as drug thera-
peutic effects.
