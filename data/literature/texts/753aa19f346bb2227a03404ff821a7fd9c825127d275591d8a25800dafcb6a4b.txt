Boosting Enzyme-Like Activity Through Controlled
Coordination of Metal Single-Atoms in Rhombic Cavity of
Graphyne
Ruixue Fu, Jian guo, Huan Jin, Zhen Li, Jianing Xia, Ya Tang, Yaping Ding,
Hongbin Zhao,* Daixin Ye,* and Jiaqiang Xu
The coordination number of metal single-atoms, being an important
factor, should be precisely controlled as it aﬀects both activity and speciﬁcity.
Herein, the introduction of nitrogen into the para position of benzene in
graphyne (2N-GY) is proposed as a carrier for anchoring metal single-atoms
onto pyrazine nitrogen. These atoms are then coordinated to the center of the
rhombic cavity, ensuring precisely controlled coordination numbers. Inspired
by natural enzymes, a series of transition metal single-atom nanozymes (SANs)
are constructed utilizing 2N-GY. Both theory calculations and experiments
demonstrated that the incorporation of metal single-atoms of Fe, Mn,
and Mo eﬀectively augmented the enzyme-like activity, with Mo/GY exhibiting
the highest peroxidase -like activity. Based on the diﬀering activities of M/GY
(M = Fe, Mn, Mo) and the varying abilities of sulfhydryl groups in biothiols
to occupy metal active sites, sensing arrays for the high-throughput
identiﬁcation of six biothiols are constructed. After combining the ﬁngerprint
feature responses of biothiols and SANs with machine learning algorithms,
the eﬃcient distinction of the six biothiols is achieved. This study introduces
a novel pathway for designing and synthesizing SANs that mimic natural
enzymes.

ing research trend.[1] To signiﬁcantly enhance catalytic eﬃciency
and selectivity while bridging the disparity between nanozymes
and natural enzymes, a common strategy involves integrating
structural features from natural enzymes into nanozyme de-
sign. This approach entails simulating the active centers of

Single-atom nanozymes (SANs) have
the advantages of high atom utilization,
high reactivity, good selectivity, and sta-
bility, inheriting the advantages of multi-
phase and homogeneous catalysts.[7] More-
over, the SANs have a well-deﬁned and

speciﬁcity.[9] At the same time, metal-carrier interactions can ef-

Among the many carriers, carbon carriers, especially those doped
with N atoms, are considered to be a promising class of car-
riers for SANs due to their good mechanical properties, large
speciﬁc surface area, excellent electrical conductivity, and struc-
tural ﬂexibility.[11] Therefore, 2D carbon-based materials such as
graphene,[12] N-doped graphene,[13] graphene[14] and graphitic
carbon nitride[15] have been explored as single-atom support
materials.
Graphyne (GY) is a porous structure composed of carbon
atoms hybridized in sp and sp2 states, with abundant Π-bonds,
exhibiting exceptional performance across various ﬁelds.[16]
Moreover, the bonding and antibonding p orbits of sp-carbon
atoms in GY can indirectly maintain the metal atom orbits
around the Fermi level, entrusting the stable electron-rich state
of the metal atom. Additionally, introducing nitrogen into the
para position of benzene in GY (2N-GY) modiﬁes the pore size
and chemical environment of the framework, creating a unique
site for anchoring individual metal atoms.[17] Meanwhile, due to

2N-GY can act as both the electron donor and acceptor, endowing

In this work, a series of metal SANs with peroxidase (POD)-
like activity were synthesized by loading metal single-atoms on
2N-GY. The 2N-GY with rhombic cavity provided a reliable coor-
dination and dispersion environment for the metal single-atoms,
and three SANs (Fe/GY, Mn/GY, and Mo/GY) were successfully
synthesized and their intrinsic source of POD-like activity was
investigated. Both experiments and DFT calculations revealed
that the Mo/GY showed the highest catalytic activity. It can be
attributed to the lower energy barrier in the formation of H2O*,
which is a rate-determining step in the hydrogen peroxide de-
composition process. The diﬀerential charge density analysis also
conﬁrmed the electron transfer between Mo active sites and the
H2O* became easier on Mo/GY during the reaction process.
These three SANs were used to construct sensing arrays for the
detection of biological thiols, and six thiols with analogous struc-
tures and chemical properties were successfully identiﬁed. This
work provides an eﬀective method for designing transition metal
SANs with GY as a carbon carrier.
2. Results and Discussion
According to the structure of natural enzymes, we simulated
and designed three kinds of M/GY (M = Fe, Mn, Mo) SANs
(Figure 1). Generally speaking, the metal single-atom acts as the
active center, and the N atom acts as the coordination atom to
stabilize and regulate the charge distribution of the active center
and aﬀect its activity. The clearly deﬁned active centers are favor-
able for theoretical calculations to investigate the underlying cat-
alytic mechanism of POD-like activity. Density functional theory
(DFT) simulations are used to explore the origin for the improved
catalytic activity on M/GYs. Regarding the local coordination en-
vironment of the atomically scattered metal active sites, the GY
and M/GY models with axial nitrogen coordination were estab-
lished (Figures S1 and S2, Supporting Information). Electronic
band structures of these M/GYs demonstrated that incorporating
metal atoms into GY causes a phase change from semiconductor
to metal. Figure 2A shows that the direct bandgap of the origi-
nal GY is 0.89 eV. Due to the electron transfer to GY, the doping
of metal atoms increases the Fermi level in the conduction band
region, resulting in metal properties in all M/GY (Figure 2B–D).
The metallization of M/GYs enhances electron conductivity and
promotes electron transfer during catalytic processes. As shown
in Figure 2A–D, the project electronic density of states (PDOS)
of TM’s 3d orbital was calculated using the established Mo/GY,
Fe/GY, and Mn/GY models. The electronic state hybridization
between the TM atoms and the GY is particularly obvious in the
Fermi level area, showing that the reaction center and the sub-
strate have excellent conductivity. Meanwhile, the d-band cen-
ters of TM for each sample are also presented in Figure 2A–D.
The higher d-band center on Mo/GY (−0.91 eV) means that the
atomic Mo site is more active than that on Fe/GY (−2.19 eV)
and Mn/GY (−3.10 eV), which can be ascribed to the stronger
interaction between the axial ligand and Mo atom. Hence, the re-
action intermediates are expected to have a stronger interaction
with Mo/GY. The electron localization function (ELF) can also be
used to describe the interactions of TM atoms with GY. As shown
in Figure S3 (Supporting Information), electrons accumulate on
N atoms in pyrazine rings due to the higher electronegativity of
nitrogen compared to carbon. A higher ELF value in atomic Mo
suggests that electrons nearby are more localized, which is ben-
eﬁcial for OH* adsorption.

To elucidate the underlying factors contributing to the en-
hanced catalytic performance, PDOS calculations were con-
ducted to investigate the OH* adsorption characteristics on
M/GY. The d-band centers of the TM atoms for each sample, as
illustrated in Figure 3A–C, indicate that the d-band center of Mo
in Mo/GY (−1.62 eV) is higher than that of Fe/GY (−2.38 eV)
and Mn/GY (−2.52 eV), conﬁrming the activation of the Mo
site. Additionally, the projected crystal orbital Hamilton popu-
lation (pCOHP), derived from overlap-population-weighted den-
sities of states, was employed to analyze the bonding and anti-
bonding properties of the M─O bond. Negative COHP values
represent bonding interactions, while positive COHP values de-
note antibonding contributions. The M─O bond strength was
quantiﬁed using the integral of COHP (ICOHP) up to the Fermi
level. The ICOHP value for Mo/GY (−2.38 eV) was more neg-
ative than that of Fe/GY (−1.99 eV) and Mn/GY (−0.78 eV),
indicating a more signiﬁcant electron population in the bond-
ing orbitals and a stronger M−O bond. Consequently, Mo/GY
demonstrates enhanced electron transfer capability, facilitating
stronger bonding between Mo and the adsorbed oxygen inter-
mediate. In Figure 3D, the energy proﬁle depicts the POD-like
catalytic performance. The dissociation of H2O2 (H2O2
*→2OH*,
2OH*→OH*+H2O) on M/GY models is thermodynamically en-
ergy descent. However, in the following reactions, including the
formation of H2O*, and desorption of H2O, the free-energy is rel-
atively upward. In comparison, the Mo/GY model shows a higher
catalytic activity than that of Fe/GY and Mn/GY models due to its
lower free-energy barrier. Diﬀerential charge density analysis in-
dicates charge transfer between substrates and H2O upon inter-
action (Figure 3E). Bader charge calculations (Table S1, Support-
ing Information) show that Mo in the Mo/GY structure delivers
a Bader charge of 1.07 e, lower than that of Fe in Fe/GY (1.11 e)
and Mn in Mn/GY (1.26 e), signifying that the Mo/GY structure
retains more d orbital electrons on Mo atoms, thereby enhancing
H2O adsorption and accelerating oxygen intermediate conver-
sion. The reasonable atomic structures at diﬀerent steps in POD-
like catalytic processes of Mo/GY are illustrated in Figure 3F.

2N-GY was selected as the substrate and mixed with acetylace-
tone (Fe(acca)3, Mn(acca)3, Mo(acca)2) for metal loading. The
2N-GY@M(acca)n (M = Fe, Mn, Mo) precursor loaded with
metal salt was pyrolyzed to prepare single-atom nanozyme
Fe/GY, Mn/GY, Mo/GY, collectively deﬁned as M/GY (M = Fe,
Mn, Mo). As shown in Figure 4A, there was no obvious metal
peak or metal oxide peak in the X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) pattern.
However, they all (2N-GY, Fe/GY, Mn/GY, and Mo/GY) showed
an obvious envelope peak at about 21.5°, which belonged to
carbon. It shows that the metal might present in the form of
single-atoms rather than in the form of metal particles and metal
oxides in the M/GY. The morphology of the metal-loaded M/GY
was characterized by a transmission electron microscope (TEM).
A clear lamellar structure can be seen in the TEM (Figure 4B)
image, which is essentially the same as that of 2N-GY (Figure
S4, Supporting Information). To further detect the molybdenum
atoms on the Mo/GY, aberration-corrected high-angle annular
dark-ﬁeld scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-
STEM) (AC HAADF-STEM) was used to obtain the atomic
phase graph (Figure 4C). The bright spots with red circles in
Figure 4C represent monodispersed molybdenum atoms on
Mo/GY. Energy-dispersive spectroscopy (EDS) mapping spectra
(Figure 4D; S5, Supporting Information) display that the C, N,
and M atoms are distributed homogeneously in M/GY.
Next, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was used to an-
alyze the surface species and the forms of species present in the
SANs. As shown in Figures S6 and S7 (Supporting Information),
characteristic peaks for carbon (C), nitrogen (N), oxygen (O), and
metallic elements can be clearly observed in the XPS spectra. The
presence of the O 1s peak at ≈535 eV is due to the adsorption of
oxygen from the air by the prepared material. The XPS peaks of C
1s can be ﬁtted into three sub-peaks, the deconvoluted peaks cen-
tered at the binding energies of 284.4 eV, ≈286.0, and 288.0 eV
are assigned to the C─C (sp2) and C─C (sp) and pyrazine-C, re-
spectively (Figure 4E; Figure S7B,E, Supporting Information).
The high-resolution N 1s spectrum of M/GY can be deconvo-
luted into three peaks, which were assigned to M─N, pyrazine-N,
graphtic-N, respectively (Figure 4F; Figure S7A,D, Supporting In-
formation). The Mo 3d XPS spectrum (Figure 4G) can be divided
into three main parts, with the peak at 229.13 eV contributed by
Mo4+ 3d5/2, the peak near 232 eV mainly contributed by Mo4+
3d3/2 at 232.55 eV, with a small contribution from Mo6+ 3d3/2 at
232.25 eV, and the peak at 235.67 eV contributed by Mo6+ 3d3/2.
In the Fe 2p XPS spectrum, the Fe species coordinated to N is

The POD-like and oxidase (OXD)-like activities of diﬀerent
SANs were investigated by UV–vis absorption spectroscopy ab-
sorbance values, and it can be seen that the POD-like and OXD-
like activities are dependent on the type of metal active site. As
shown in Figure 5A, where Mo/GY has the highest POD-like ac-
tivity and the lowest OXD-like activity with excellent POD-like se-
lectivity, while Mn/GY has a lower activity, and the selectivity be-
tween POD-like activity and OXD-like activity is not ideal. Fe/GY,
as Fe-based SANs, showed both good POD-like activity and OXD-
like activity. From Figure 5B we can see that the POD-like activity
of the SANs designed and synthesized in this work is still signiﬁ-
cant, with a signiﬁcant increase in absorbance after the introduc-
tion of M/GY (M = Fe, Mn, Mo) SANs for catalysis.
In SANs sensing, factors such as pH, SANs concentration,
TMB concentration, and H2O2 concentration will have a great
inﬂuence on the activity of SANs, so the conditions under which
SANs exert their optimal activity need to be investigated. As
shown in Figure S8A (Supporting Information), the absorbance
showed an increasing and then decreasing trend with the change
of pH, and the activity was highest and the absorbance reached
the maximum value at pH = 4. Therefore, a buﬀer solution with
pH = 4 was selected to provide a stable environment with con-
stant pH = 4 for the system. Meanwhile, TMB and H2O2 oc-
cupy an important position in this system; therefore, the re-
sults of the experiments to explore the TMB concentration and
H2O2 concentration are shown in Figure S8 (Supporting Infor-
mation). As shown in Figure S8B (Supporting Information), the
absorbance increased gradually as the concentration of SANs in-
creased from 0 to 0.05 mg mL−1. From Figure S8D (Support-
ing Information), it can be seen that from 0 to 1 mm, with the
increase of TMB concentration, the absorbance ﬁrst increased
rapidly, and then the growth rate became slower. The same phe-
nomenon occurred with H2O2, as shown in Figure S8C (Sup-
porting Information), the absorbance almost stopped increasing
when exceeding 5 mm, while before 5 mm, the absorbance rapidly
increased with the increase of H2O2 concentration. Therefore,
0.33 mm TMB and 3.33 mm H2O2 were selected for further ex-
periments from the point of view of solution color and economic
saving. The nanozyme concentration is an important factor in de-
termining the catalytic activity. In order to minimize the amount
of SANs, 16.66 μg mL−1 of SANs was chosen for the following
work. In conclusion, M/GY (M = Fe, Mn, Mo) exhibited excellent
POD-like activity under the conditions of 16.66 μg mL−1 SANs,
3.33 mm H2O2, 0.33 mm TMB, and pH = 4.0, and TMB was ox-
idized to oxTMB in the constructed M/GY-TMB- H2O2 system,
with a signiﬁcant absorbance at 652 nm.

In view of the speciﬁc POD-like activity of M/GY (M = Fe, Mn,
Mo), in order to understand the mechanism of its catalytic reac-
tion, the free radicals generated during the reaction were deter-
mined. Hydroxyl radical is a key intermediate homolyzed from
H2O2 by M/GY during POD mimicking. As shown in Figure 5E,
using 5,5-dimethy-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) as the trapping

suggest that the free radicals produced during the catalysis of
M/GY are hydroxyl radicals, which is consistent with the DFT
calculations.
The SCN−toxicity assay was used to further conﬁrm that
the metal SANs is the active site of the reaction, and the ab-
sorbance decreased signiﬁcantly with increasing concentration
of SCN−, indicating a decrease in the catalytic activity of the
nanozyme. This is due to the coordination of SCN−with the
metal active site; the active site is blocked and cannot catalyze
the substrate reaction. The results of Figure 5C show that the
active sites of Fe/GY are Fe SANs, Mn/GY are Mn SANs, and
Mo/GY are Mo SANs, and the metal SANs play an impor-
tant role as catalytic active centers in the catalytic oxidation of
TMB.
Biothiols are abundant in cells and play a variety of roles in hu-
man physiological processes. For example, glutathione is the re-
dox reservoir of the cell and plays an important intermediary role
in many cellular functions, and cysteine is an important amino
acid for the synthesis of glutathione and sulfur-containing pro-
teins in organisms. Abnormal levels of biothiols have been as-
sociated with a variety of clinical diseases, including malignant
tumors, Parkinson’s disease, and Alzheimer’s disease. mercapto-
succinic acid has anti-hypertensive properties, and isooctyl mer-
captoacetate is an important pharmaceutical intermediate. These
biothiols are involved in regulating the physiological functions
of organisms, and changes in intracellular biothiol levels may
be relevant to the development of many diseases.[18] Therefore,
the development of eﬀective and stable methods for the analy-
sis of thiols is particularly important in the early diagnosis of
certain diseases. However, the speciﬁc identiﬁcation and high-
throughput screening of thiols is a great challenge due to the
high similarity of molecular structures and chemical properties
of thiol compounds.[19]

eﬀectively aﬀected.

3. Conclusion
In this work, a set of GY-based SANs with well-controlled coor-
dination numbers were designed and synthesized. The design of
the SANs with a coordination number of two nitrogen atoms was
accomplished by replacing the para-carbon of the benzene ring in
GY, creating a rhombic cavity to facilitate the anchoring of single-
atoms. The proposed design was initially validated through DFT
calculations. The PDOS, ELF, pCOHP, free-energy diagrams,
and CDD, analysis conﬁrmed the superior potential of Mo/GY
for POD-like performance. Subsequently, 2N-GY was selected
as the substrate, and acetylacetonate metal was chosen as the
metal source for synthesizing GY-based SANs with well-deﬁned
binary coordination. The obtained three SANs (Fe/GY, Mn/GY,
Mo/GY) exhibited exceptional POD-like activities, eﬀectively cat-
alyzing the TMB-H2O2 system, with Mo/GY demonstrating the
most outstanding performance. Subsequently, these SANs were
employed to develop colorimetric sensing arrays for the identi-
ﬁcation of six biothiols: MAA, l-Cys, GSH, TGA, DMSA, and
MH. The catalytic activities are obstructed to varying degrees by
the binding strengths of thiols to the metal active sites. Through
the integration of the distinctive feature responses of biothiols
and SANs with machine learning algorithms, it became feasible
to qualitatively distinguish and quantitatively detect various thi-
ols. Furthermore, the sensing array is capable of discerning mix-
tures of thiols, oﬀering potential for clinical thiol detection. This
research not only introduces a novel approach for synthesizing
SANs with speciﬁed binary coordination but also provides a con-
venient method for thiol detection, thus expanding the utility of
SANs with sensing arrays.
