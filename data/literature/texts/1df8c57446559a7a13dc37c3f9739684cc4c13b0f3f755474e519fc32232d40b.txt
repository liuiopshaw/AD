ABSTRACT: Highly luminescent metal nanoclusters (MNCs)
with aggregation-induced emission (AIE) have emerged as a new
class of functional materials, which could be deployed to address
challenging issues in diverse ﬁelds. In view of the wide application
of white light-emitting diodes (WLEDs), which suﬀer from a low
color rendering index (CRI, ≤70) and chromaticity parameter
drift, we report the development of a high-performance WLED
with high luminous eﬃciency (LE), excellent CRI attributes, and
nonchromaticity parameter drift, achieved by employing AIE-
featured orange-emitting bimetallic AuAg NCs as a red phosphor.
The delicately designed AuAg NCs with ultrasmall size (< 2 nm)
have strong emission in the orange-light region (∼610 nm) with a
large Stokes shift and more importantly show a very stable yet high
absolute photoluminescence quantum yield of 43% in the solid state owing to the AIE property, which makes them suitable for
constructing WLEDs for high-end lighting applications. The as-developed AuAg NC-based WLED exhibits an excellent CRI (80.6)
and high LE (91.9 lm/W) without chromaticity parameter drift. This study is of broad interest because it not only demonstrates the
usage of novel luminescent MNCs for WLED applications but also oﬀers a feasible way for the design of high-performance WLEDs
with a high LE, high CRI, and nonchromaticity parameter drift.
KEYWORDS: AuAg nanoclusters, luminescence, aggregation-induced emission, LEDs
■INTRODUCTION
In the past decade, ultrasmall luminescent metal nanoclusters
(MNCs, < 3 nm) composed of several to a few hundred metal
atoms have emerged as a new class of functional materials.1−10
Owing to their unique attributes such as strong lumines-
cence,11−14 rich surface chemistry,15−17 large Stokes shift,18
good biocompatibility,19 and excellent photothermal stabil-
ity,20 MNCs have attracted extensive research interest in the
ﬁelds of catalysis,21−24 energy conversion,25,26 healthcare,27,28
electronics,29 and photonics.30−33 Currently, researchers have
attempted to solve certain challenging issues in diverse ﬁelds
by engineering MNCs with speciﬁc functionalities,34,35 which
has resulted in some interesting achievements and largely
promotes advances in such ﬁelds.
On the other hand, light-emitting diodes (LEDs) have been
widely applied in indoor lighting, backlight display, aviation
navigation, etc. because of their distinctive advantages
including high eﬃciency, energy-saving capacity, long lifetime,
good monochrome, and fast response.36,37 Particularly, white
LEDs (WLEDs) are considered as the next-generation
illumination lamps.38,39 Although such WLEDs have a high
luminous eﬃciency (LE), they do suﬀer from a low color
rendering index (CRI, ≤70) and chromaticity parameter
drift,40,41 which may curtail their broader application in high-
end lightings such as hotels, museums, and exhibition halls.
Currently, the most common design of WLEDs is to utilize the
blue light of InGaN-based LEDs to shine yellow phosphors
(e.g., YAG: Ce3+ yellow phosphors),42 and most of these
WLEDs lack emission in red-light regions.40 This could be the
key reason for the low CRI of these WLEDs, and thus these
WLEDs are unable to meet the requirements of high-end
lightings (CRI >80).41,43 Although additional eﬀorts have been
devoted to improve their CRI by mixing multicolor phosphors,
the intrinsic limitations of the device including narrow spectral
range, and lower uniformity in mixing such phosphors made
success very limited.44 In view of this, we hypothesize that if we
can delicately design a highly luminescent material with strong
luminescence in the red-light region with a large stokes shift
and excellent photostability, we may realize the development of

high-performance WLEDs with a high CRI (> 80), high LE (>
90 lm/W), and nonchromaticity parameter drift for high-end

In fact, luminescent MNCs have been demonstrated to be
promising in improving the CRI of WLEDs because of their
strong luminescence and large Stokes shift.38,45−51 Recently,
Wang et al.48 reported the fabrication of Cu NC-based WLEDs
with a high CRI value of 92. However, the low LE (< 10 lm/
W) of such Cu NC-based WLEDs remains a major obstacle
toward high-end lighting applications. Very recently, a novel
type of highly luminescent MNC featuring aggregation-
induced emission (AIE) has been developed by several groups
including ourselves.52−56 The intriguing AIE property of such
MNCs endows them with stronger luminescence in the solid
state, which may beneﬁt the development of high-performance
WLEDs with a high CRI, high LE, and nonchromaticity
parameter drift for high-end lighting applications.
Herein, we report the design of a high-performance WLED
with a high CRI (80.6), high LE (91.9 lm/W), and
nonchromaticity parameter drift by using AIE-featured
luminescent AuAg bimetallic NCs as a red phosphor. It is to
be noted that most of the luminescent nanomaterials have
weak or quenched luminescence when aggregated or in the
solid state (the most aggregated state) because of the
aggregation-caused-quenching eﬀect,56 which certainly inﬂu-
ences the practical applications of their solid states. By
contrast, the key strategy of this study is the design of AIE-
featured AuAg NCs with strong emission in the red-light
region, which could augment its luminescence in the solid
state, ensuring the high performance of the WLED (Scheme
1). As shown in Scheme 1, sub-2 nm-sized ultrabright orange-
emitting AuAg NCs are combined with commercial green
phosphors for the fabrication of down-conversion WLEDs.
The ultrasmall size of the AuAg NCs guarantees good mixing
with the green phosphors. This NC-based WLED shows an
excellent CRI (80.6) and high LE (91.9 lm/W) without
chromaticity parameter drift. To the best of our knowledge,
this is the highest record of CRI for MNC-based WLEDs with
a high LE, which meets the requirements of WLEDs in high-
end lighting applications. This study is attractive because it not
only demonstrates the powerful functionality of MNCs but
also provides a feasible way for solving the low CRI and
chromaticity parameter drift issues of LEDs using novel
functional materials.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Synthesis of Luminescent AuAg NCs. Luminescent bimetallic
AuAg NCs were synthesized according to our previously reported
strategy with minor modiﬁcations.48 In a typical synthesis of
luminescent AuAg NCs, freshly prepared aqueous solutions of GSH
(100 mM, 4.5 mL), HAuCl4 (50 mM, 5.4 mL), and AgNO3 (20 mM,
1.5 mL) were mixed with 138 mL of ultrapure water followed by the
addition of 0.6 mL of NaOH (1 M). Afterward, the solution was
heated to 85 °C under gentle stirring (600 rpm). The orange-emitting
AuAg NCs with a molar ratio of Au:Ag = 9:1 could be collected after
3 h. The solution of AuAg NCs was concentrated using a vacuum
rotatory evaporator and then freeze-dried into solid powder for LED
fabrication.
Fabrication of Green LEDs. Green Y3(Al,Ga)5O12:Ce3+ (denoted
as Ga-YAG) phosphors with an emission peak of 525 nm were evenly
mixed with silicone resin with a mass ratio of 1:10 and then the
mixture was dropped onto the blue LED chips. After heating at 55 °C
in an oven for 20 min, the green LEDs were fabricated by combining
blue-light LED chips with the Ga-YAG phosphors.
Fabrication of Orange LEDs. The AuAg NCs were uniformly
mixed with silicone resin with a mass ratio of 1:10 and then the
mixture was coated on the blue LED chips. After heating at 55 °C in
an oven for 20 min, the orange LEDs were fabricated.
Fabrication of AuAg NC-Based WLEDs. The AuAg NCs were
uniformly mixed with silicone resin with a mass ratio of 1:10 to form a
mixture, and the mixture of green Ga-YAG phosphors with silicone
resin was prepared in the same way. To fabricate the WLEDs, the
AuAg NC-based mixture was ﬁrst coated on the blue-emitting InGaN-
based LED chips and then a certain amount of Ga-YAG phosphor-
based mixture was also coated on the same LED chips to obtain
trichromatic WLEDs. After heating at 55 °C in an oven for 20 min,
the natural WLED and warm WLED were fabricated with the ratios of
AuAg NCs to Ga-YAG of 3.5:1 and 4:1, respectively.
Fabrication of YAG: Ce3+-Based WLEDs. Commercial yellow
YAG: Ce3+ phosphor powders were uniformly mixed with silicone
resin with a mass ratio of 1:10 and then the mixture was coated on
blue LED chips. After heating at 55 °C in an oven for 20 min, WLEDs
were fabricated.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Characterization of AuAg NCs. Luminescent orange-
emitting AuAg NCs were synthesized according to our
previously developed strategy with minor modiﬁcations (see
detailed experimental procedures in the Supporting Informa-
tion).52 As shown in Figure 1a, the solution of AuAg NCs is
yellowish in color (upper inset, left item) and displays a
shoulder absorption peak at ∼370 nm in the UV−vis
absorption spectrum (solid black curve), which indicates the
successful synthesis of MNCs instead of large AuAg particles
featuring a surface plasmon resonance peak at 520 nm.
Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) reveals that the size
of the as-synthesized AuAg NCs is below 2 nm (Figure 1b),
and polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE) also provides
supportive evidence (inset of Figure 1b).
In addition, the solution of AuAg NCs shows intense orange
luminescence with an emission peak at 610 nm (Figure 1a,
solid red curve; upper panel inset) when excited at 370 nm
(Figure 1a, dashed red curve), which indicates that this type of
AuAg NCs has a large Stokes shift of >200 nm. Of note is that
the feeding Au-to-Ag ratios during NC synthesis were found to
be important in inﬂuencing the luminescence intensities of the
bimetallic NCs, and the AuAg NC sample with 10% of Ag
species shows about four times the luminescence enhancement
compared to that of pure Au NCs (Figure S1). It should be
mentioned that both the orange emission at 610 nm located in
the red-light region and the large Stokes shift are beneﬁcial to

More importantly, owing to the AIE property, the AuAg NC
sample shows stronger luminescence in the solid state (inset of
Figure 1a, bottom panel), which is evidenced by the fact that
the absolute photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) of the
AuAg NCs changes from 9.6% (in solution) to 43% (in
powder). Similar to other metal NCs, the luminescence
intensity of the AuAg NCs has a correlation with the
temperature, and increasing the temperature could cause the
decrease of their luminescence intensity (Figure S2), which
implies that a very high stability of the AuAg NCs is required
to ensure the LED application performance. Fortunately, the
as-synthesized luminescent AuAg NCs are extremely stable
both in solution and in the powder state and no obvious
change can be found in their luminescence intensity after 3
months of storage at room temperature (see Figure S3).
The luminescence lifetime of the orange-emitting AuAg NCs
was also measured to check the AIE attribute. As shown in
Figure 1c, the luminescence lifetime of all AuAg NC species is
in the microsecond magnitude (∼1.60 μs) by ﬁtting the
luminescence decay curve. Particularly, the luminescence decay
analysis conﬁrmed that there are three dominant luminescence
emitters in the NCs with a lifetime of 1.53 μs (∼41.6%), 1.62
μs (∼58.3%), and 10.49 μs (<0.1%). Such a long luminescence
lifetime of AuAg NCs may be attributed to ligand-to-metal
charge transfer or ligand-to-metal−metal charge transfer from
the sulfur atom in the thiolate ligands to the Au atoms and the
subsequent radiative relaxation via a metal-centered triplet
excited state.53 XPS analysis for the AuAg NCs revealed that
the Au 4f7/2 peak at 84.1 eV and the Au 4f5/2 peak at 87.8 eV
are in between those of Au(0) and Au(I) (see Figure 1d). In
particular, by deconvoluting their Au 4f7/2 XPS spectrum into
Au(I) at 84.3 eV and Au(0) components at 83.7 eV, it is found
that the predominant Au species in the luminescent AuAg NCs
is Au(I) with a proportion of ∼62.5%. In addition, a very weak
Ag 3d signal is also observed in the XPS spectrum of the AuAg
NCs (Figure S4). The binding energy of Ag 3d5/2 in the as-
synthesized AuAg NCs is 367.8 eV, which could be
deconvoluted into Ag(I) with a binding energy of 367.9 eV
(dashed blue curve) and Ag(0) with a binding energy of 367.7
eV (dashed magenta curve), suggesting that the predominant
Ag species is Ag(I) (∼66.3%) in the AuAg NCs.57 Of note is
that Ag(I) species may serve as motif linkers to link Au(I)-
GSH motifs for a longer one or a more compact one, which
leads to signiﬁcant luminescence enhancement for the AuAg
NCs via AIE.52 These results of luminescence lifetime and
oxidation states of metals provide supportive evidence on the
successful formation of AIE-featured AuAg NCs, which may be
conducive to the development of high-performance WLEDS.
Performance of AuAg NC-Based WLEDs. The orange-
emitting AuAg NCs as a red-light conversion material was thus
employed to integrate with the commercially available green
Y3(Al,Ga)5O12:Ce3+ phosphors with a maximum emission of
525 nm (denoted as Ga-YAG phosphors) as a green-light
conversion material and a blue-emitting InGaN-based LED
chip with an emission of 455 nm as the blue emitter for the
construction of trichromatic WLEDs. Brieﬂy, the AuAg NCs as
well as Ga-YAG phosphors were thoroughly mixed with
silicone resin with the mass ratio of 1:10. The AuAg NC
mixture and the Ga-YAG phosphor mixture with proper ratios
were successively coated on the blue-emitting InGaN-based
LED chips for fabricating trichromatic WLEDs. The
trichromatic white-light mechanism of the fabricated WLEDs
is that the orange light emitted from the AuAg NCs mixture,
green light emitted from the Ga-YAG phosphors mixture, and
the blue light emitted from the blue chip are mixed into white
light. As a comparison, orange LEDs and green LEDs were
constructed through coating blue InGaN-based LEDs with

As shown in Figure 2a, the emission peak of the blue
InGaN-based LED is located at 455 nm with a full width at half
maxima (FWHM) of 19 nm under the forward current of 100
mA, and this LED luminesces pure blue light (inset in Figure
2a). The emission peaks of the orange AuAg NC-based LED
and the green Ga-YAG-based LED are located at 600 nm (with
FWHM of 125 nm) and 525 nm (with FWHM of 141 nm) at
100 mA, respectively (Figure 2b,c). The lamp beads of the
LEDs constructed by the sole AuAg NCs and sole Ga-YAG
phosphors emitted orange light and green light (insets in
Figure 2b,c), respectively. Obviously, the visual display colors
are not determined by the low-intensity blue emission peak but
dependent on the high-intensity orange or green emission
peaks of the orange AuAg NC-based LED or the green Ga-
YAG-based LED. Comparing the AuAg NCs in solution, the
emission of the AuAg NCs in the powder state shifted from
610 nm to 600 nm (Figure 2b), which may be ascribed to its
tightest aggregation-induced intercluster interaction.
In order to investigate the optical properties of the AuAg
NC-based WLEDs, a high-performance trichromatic WLED
was fabricated by adjusting the ratio of the AuAg NCs:Ga-YAG
phosphors to 3.5:1 (Figure 2d). The key parameters of these
four LEDs such as LE, Commission Internationale de
l’Eclairage (CIE) coordinates, CRI, and correlated color
temperature (CCT) values are summarized in Table 1. As
shown, the as-developed WLED emits bright natural white
light (inset of Figure 2d) with an LE, CRI, and CCT of 91.9
lm/W, 80.6, and 4959 K, respectively. Owing to the powerful
AIE feature with a high luminescence quantum eﬃciency of
the AuAg NCs, the LE of the AuAg NC-based natural WLED
achieves the highest score than that of the reported MNC-
based LEDs (Table S1). For example, the LE of the orange-
emitting Au NC-based WLED is 29.9 (Figure S5; Note: this
type of Au NCs has the same emission peak as the AuAg NCs
but has a lower PLQY of ∼2.4%), which is much lower than
that of the AuAg NC-based WLED, suggesting the major
contribution of the higher PLQY of metal NCs to the
enhancement in the LE of WLEDs. In addition, for comparison
purpose, a YAG: Ce3+-based WLED was fabricated by coating
commercial yellow YAG: Ce3+ phosphor powders on the same
blue InGaN-based chip (Figure S6), which exhibits an LE of
115.5 lm/W, CRI of 71.6, and CCT of 5683 K (Table S2). In
comparison, although the AuAg NC-based WLED shows a
high LE of 91.9 lm/W that is comparable to the highest value
of the reported MNC-based WLEDs but a bit lower than that
of the commercial YAG: Ce3+-based WLED (115.6 lm/W), its
CRI value (80.6) is apparently higher than that of the
commercial WLED (around 70 of CRI). Such a high CRI
value of the AuAg NC-based WLED is beneﬁcially derived
from the broad spectrum (FWHM∼125 nm) and the strong
orange and red luminescence of the AuAg NCs, whereas the
commercial YAG: Ce3+-based WLED has poor emission in the
red-orange range. On this basis, we may conclude that the as-
developed AuAg NC-based WLED with higher CRI values
could satisfy the requirement of high-quality indoor illumina-
tion.

The CIE diagram was recommended by the Commission
Internationale de l’Eclairage in 1931, which maps all the colors
that are obvious to the human eye (“standard observer”) in
terms of hue and saturation.59 The standard white light was
deﬁned as a point (0.33, 0.33) at 5500 K. Therefore, the CIE
color coordinates were applied to evaluate the quality of the as-
constructed AuAg NC-based WLEDs. The CIE color
coordinates of the emitted light from the AuAg NC-based
natural WLED with references of the blue LED, the orange
LED, and the green LED are plotted as shown in Figure 3. As
shown, the CIE coordinate of the orange LED using AuAg
NCs as the red emitter is at (0.48, 0.40), which is located in
the red-orange area of the CIE diagram, suggesting the
successful application of AuAg NCs as a monochromatic
emitting material for orange LEDs. The CIE coordinate of the
AuAg NC-based trichromatic WLED is at (0.34, 0.34), which
is correlated to the natural white light shown in the inset of
Figure 2d and very close to the ideal CIE for standard white
emission (0.33, 0.33). In addition, the CIE coordinates of the
blue LED at (0.15, 0.03) and the green LED at (0.29, 0.46) are
located in the blue and green area of the CIE diagram,
respectively.
The AuAg NC-based WLED exhibits excellent performance
in stable light conversion and a high LE under variable forward
current conditions. The electroluminescence spectra of the
AuAg NC-based natural WLED reveal that its light intensities
steadily increase with increasing forward currents (Figure 3b),
which implies the stable light-conversion and high color output
quality of the natural WLED. The LE of the natural WLED
was found to be 91.9 lm/W at a forward current of 100 mA,
which changed from 101.4 lm/W at 10 mA to 78.8 lm/W at
200 mA (Figure S8 and Table S4). This phenomenon is
probably caused by the intrinsic eﬃciency “droop” eﬀect of the
blue InGaN-based LED under a larger forward current
condition.60 In addition, with increasing the forward current,
the CIE coordinates of the natural WLED have negligible
change (from 81.2 at 10 mA to 80.4 at 200 mA), as shown in
Figure S9 and Table S4. In particular, their CIE coordinates at
diﬀerent currents are all located in the white light region with a
small variation as shown in Table S4. The stability of the AuAg
NC-based WLED was tested at a working current of 100 mA
under normal atmosphere conditions. As shown in Figure S10,
the luminous intensity of the AuAg NC-based WLED gradually
decreases with increasing lighting time, and about 50% of light
intensity could be maintained after 108 h, suggesting the good
stability of the WLED. However, further improvement of the
luminous stability of the NC-based WLEDs is highly desired in
future research. Taken together, the AIE-featured luminescent
AuAg NCs were demonstrated to be promising as light
convention materials for high-performance WLEDs with high
luminous eﬃcacy and high CRI characteristics.
■CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have developed a high-performance WLED
with both high luminous eﬃcacy and excellent CRI attributes
achieved by deploying AIE-featured orange-emitting bimetallic
AuAg NCs as a red phosphor. The delicately designed AuAg
NCs not only have strong emission in the orange light region
(around 610 nm) with a large Stokes shift and a high PLQY of
43% in the solid state but also have an ultrasmall size (< 2 nm),
which makes them decent in high-end lighting applications
with an excellent CRI (80.6) and high LE (91.9 lm/W) but
without chromaticity parameter drift. This study may shed
light on the design of high-performance WLEDs with high LE,
high CRI, and nonchromaticity parameter drift using other
novel functional materials.
