A Redox Modulatory Mn3O4 Nanozyme with Multi-Enzyme Activity
Provides Efficient Cytoprotection to Human Cells in a ParkinsonQs
Disease Model
Namrata Singh+, Mohammed Azharuddin Savanur+, Shubhi Srivastava, Patrick DQSilva,* and
Govindasamy Mugesh*
Abstract: Nanomaterials with enzyme-like activities (nano-
zymes) attracts significant interest due to their therapeutic
potential for the treatment of various diseases. Herein, we
report that a Mn3O4 nanozyme functionally mimics three
major antioxidant enzymes, that is, superoxide dismutase
(SOD), catalase (CAT) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx)
and the multienzyme activity is size as well as morphology-
dependent. The redox modulatory effect of Mn3O4 plays
a crucial role in protecting the cells from MPP + induced
cytotoxicity in a Parkinson disease (PD)-like cellular model,
indicating that manganese-based nanomaterials having multi-
enzyme activity can robustly rescue the cells from oxidative
damage and thereby possess therapeutic potential to prevent
ROS-mediated neurological disorders.
Generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) such as
superoxide (O2
@), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), hydroxy radical
(COH) is a normal physiological process and is essential for the
survival of aerobic organisms. At basal levels, ROS mediates
various signaling mechanisms that are vital for regulating
proper cellular functions.[1] In a healthy cell, excess ROS is
controlled by antioxidant defense machinery that includes
antioxidant enzymes (superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase
(CAT), glutathione peroxidase (GPx)), and small molecule
antioxidants (vitamins C and E, glutathione, etc.).[2] However,
the excessive production of ROS results in oxidative stress,
causing damage to biomolecules such as DNA, proteins and
lipids,[3] which have been implicated in several pathophysio-
logical conditions such as neurodegeneration, cancer, diabe-
tes, cardiovascular diseases, ageing.[4] Thus, maintenance of
the redox equilibrium to combat oxidative stress is vital for
sustaining the cellular homeostasis and there is a requirement
of artificial enzymes to provide cytoprotection against various
ROS-mediated disease conditions.
In recent years, nanomaterials having enzyme-like activity
(nanozymes) attracted much attention.[5] A few nanomateri-
als have been reported to mimic the activity of heme
peroxidase, CAT, oxidases or SOD enzymes.[5a–i] However, it
is important to develop a nanomaterial that can mimic the
function of all three major cellular antioxidant enzymes
(SOD, CAT and GPx) as these enzymes have differential
affinity towards ROS. While CAT is responsible for removal
of excess H2O2 during oxidative stress, GPx is known to fine-
tune the concentration of H2O2 for cell signaling. Earlier
studies have shown that the simultaneous expression of
multiple antioxidant enzymes is more effective than single or
double expression in combating oxidative stress in plants.[6]
Furthermore, it has been reported that CAT-GPx coopera-
tivity is important for the proper control of H2O2 levels under
pathophysiological conditions.[7]
The present study is aimed at the development of a potent
biocompatible
nanomaterial
that
can
supplement
the
enhanced cellular requirement to scavenge elevated ROS
levels under pathophysiological conditions. We report for the
first time that Mn3O4 nanoparticles with flower-like morphol-
ogy (Mnf) can potentially exhibit the activities of all three
primary antioxidant enzymes namely SOD, CAT and GPx.
Synthesis of Mnf was accomplished according to the pre-
viously described procedure with modifications.[8] The mor-
phology of nanoparticles as analyzed by SEM and TEM
indicate the formation of monodisperse nanoflowers (Fig-
ure 1a, b and Figure S1a, b in the Supporting Information).
HR-TEM and SAED patterns confirm the polycrystalline
nature of Mnf (Figure S1c). The presence of Mn and O in the
material was confirmed by EDX and X-ray mapping (Fig-
ure S1d–g). The diffraction peaks obtained from XRD
analysis could be indexed to the tetragonal hausmannite
structure (JCPDS 24-0734) (Figure 1c).[8,9a] The phase of the
tetragonal Mn3O4 nanoparticles was confirmed using FT-IR
(Figure S2a).[9b] To gain insight into the surface property and
oxidation states of manganese in Mnf, XPS was performed.
The binding energy (BE) values of Mn 2p3/2 and O 1s,
641.67 eV and 529.4 eV, respectively, correspond to Mn3O4
(Figures 1d and S2b). The Mn2p3/2 peak centered at 641.67 eV
and the Mn2p1/2 peak at 653.35 eV, with a spin energy
separation of & 11.7 eV is in good agreement with the
literature values.[9c]

The ability of Mnf to functionally mimic cellular antiox-
idant enzymes was investigated after comprehensive charac-
terization. The CAT-like activity was determined spectropho-
tometrically by monitoring the decrease in H2O2 absorbance
at 240 nm.[5d,10] Strikingly, in the presence of Mnf, there was
a significant decrease in the absorbance of H2O2 as a function
of time, indicating that the nanoflowers mimic the function of
catalase (Figures 2a, and S3a). Furthermore, when the con-
centration of Mnf was varied (0–20 ngmL@1) at fixed concen-
tration of H2O2, a linear dependence of the initial rate was
observed, indicating first-order reaction kinetics (Figure S3b).
The apparent steady-state kinetic parameters determined by
varying the concentration of H2O2 (0–3.0 mm) in the presence
of Mnf (5 ngmL@1) followed typical Michaelis–Menten kinet-
ics (Figure S3c–e).
As discussed previously, CAT acts together with another
enzyme, GPx, in maintaining the cellular H2O2 level. GPx
uses cellular glutathione (GSH) as the reducing agent for
catalyzing the reduction of H2O2 to water along with the
formation of oxidized glutathione (GSSG), which is subse-
quently reduced back to GSH by glutathione reductase (GR)
and NADPH. We studied the GPx-like activity of Mnf using
the classical GR-coupled assay, in which the decrease in the
NADPH concentration was monitored spectrophotometri-
cally at 340 nm.[11] The initial rates determined at various
assay conditions are shown in Figure 2b, which indicate the
GPx mimicking activity of Mnf. The catalytic properties of
Mnf investigated using steady-state kinetics by independently
varying the concentrations of H2O2 (0–1.8 mm) and GSH (0–
5.0 mm) followed Michaelis–Menten kinetics (Figure S4a–e).
A proportional dependence of the initial rate corresponding
to the first-order kinetics was observed (Figure S5a) as the
concentration of Mnf was varied by keeping the concentra-
tion of other reactants constant. Interestingly, Mnf was found
to be highly specific to H2O2 as there was a significant
decrease in the activity when cumene hydroperoxide or t-
butyl hydroperoxide was used as substrates (Figure S5b). The
observed selectivity can be attributed to the availability of
active sites on the surface specific to small molecule H2O2 and
not bulky organic peroxides.
In addition to the CAT and GPx activities, Mnf also
exhibits SOD-like activity by catalyzing the disproportiona-
tion of superoxide (O2
@) to H2O2 and oxygen. Briefly, the
reaction of xanthine with xanthine oxidase (XOD) generates
superoxide, which further reduces WST-1 (2-(4-Iodophenyl)-
3-(4-nitrophenyl)-5-(2,4-disulfophenyl)-2H-tetrazolium)
to
spectrophotometrically detectable formazan at 440 nm.[12]
The percentage of formazan production decreased rapidly
with increasing concentration of Mnf (0–40 ngmL@1), indicat-
ing the SOD-like activity of the nanoparticles under physio-
logically relevant conditions (Figure 2c and Figure S6).
Interestingly, many other metal oxide nanoparticles that
have been studied earlier as antioxidants did not exhibit the
triple-enzyme activity of Mnf, although V2O5 and MnO2
showed significant GPx and SOD activity, respectively (Fig-
ure 2d–f). Further, the commercially available Mn3O4 (97%,
Sigma–Aldrich) exhibited negligible CAT, GPx and SOD
activities (Figure S7a–c), indicating the size and shape of the
material play an important role in the enzyme mimetic
activities.

To understand the effect of different size and morphology
on the multi-enzyme activity of Mn3O4, we synthesized Mn3O4
nanoparticles of different morphology and evaluated their
enzyme mimetic activity. As it can be seen from Figure 3a–c,
the Mnf exhibited the highest activities in all three enzyme
mimetic experiments. The Mn3O4 nanoparticles with flakes-
like morphology (M4) exhibited high SOD-like activity,
which is comparable to that Mnf. The cubes (M1), polyhedron
(M2) and hexagonal plates (M3) also exhibited significant
SOD-like activity, although the activity was almost 50–60%
lower than that of Mnf. However, the CAT and GPx-like
activities are negligible for other morphologies as compared
to that of Mnf. Interestingly, Mnf retained its CAT-like
activity at temperatures ranging from 258C to 608C (Fig-
ure S8a,b), indicating the stability of the material at higher
temperatures and at biologically relevant concentrations of
H2O2. Furthermore, the XRD pattern and SEM images of the
samples obtained after performing CAT, GPx and SOD
activities indicate that the phase and morphology of Mnf is
not altered during the reactions (Figure S9).
To understand the ability of Mnf to mimic three structur-
ally and functionally different enzymes and the difference in
the activity of Mn3O4 nanoparticles in various morphologies,
we studied the size, surface area, pore size and volume of the
nanomaterials (Figure 3d). The SEM analyses indicate that
the size of Mnf (180 nm) is larger than that of cubes (M1),
polyhedron (M2), hexagonal plates (M3) and flakes (M4)
(Figure S10a–d). Similarly, the specific surface area of
97.7 m2g@1, evaluated by BET analysis, for Mnf was also
found to be higher than that of other morphologies. Interest-
ingly, the total volume of the pores and pore diameter in Mnf,
determined by BJH method, were found to be higher than
that of other materials (Figure S11a–e). While the higher
catalytic activity of Mnf can be ascribed to the larger size and
higher surface area, the remarkably larger pore size (8.9 nm)
appears to be responsible for the multi-enzyme activity of
Mnf as these pores can accommodate the substrates H2O2 and
superoxide and cofactor GSH for the reactions.
The in vitro antioxidant enzyme-like activity of the nano-
material prompted us to investigate the cytoprotective
function of Mnf in an experimental model of ParkinsonQs
disease (PD), a major neurodegenerative disorder that affects
millions of people worldwide. The disease is characterized by
oxidative stress mediated loss of dopaminergic neurons.[4a]
SHSY-5Y, a human cell line with neuroblastoma origin, was
utilized as an experimental model of PD phenotype, which
was generated by treating the cells with MPP + (1-methyl-4-
phenylpyridinium), an active metabolite of MPTP (1-methyl-
4-phenyl-1,2,3,6-tetrahydropyridine). The neurotoxin MPP +
, which is specific to dopaminergic neuronal cells, interferes
with oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria by inhibiting
complex I leading to cell death. The cytotoxicity of Mnf in
SHSY-5Y cells was analyzed through MTT (3-(4,5-dime-
thylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium
bromide)
assay,
which confirmed that the nanomaterial is not toxic to
SHSY-5Y cells (Figure 4a). An optimum concentration of

1 mm MPP + was used for further experiments as determined
by ROS enhancement and viability experiments in SHSY-5Y
cells (Figure S12a, b). We investigated the potential of Mnf to
rescue the cells against MPP + induced cellular toxicity.
Briefly, the cells were incubated with increasing concentra-
tion of Mnf followed by treatment of 1 mm MPP + . The
nanoparticles showed robust protection against neurotoxin
induced cell death (Figure 4b). It should be noted that the
neuroprotective effect of Mnf is due to the internalization of
the nanomaterial into the cells, which is confirmed by
Inductively-Coupled Plasma Optical Emission Spectroscopy
(ICP-OES) (Figure S12a). Previous reports suggest that the
cell death caused by MPP + is a result of ROS-mediated
apoptosis.[13] Therefore, we assessed the anti-apoptotic effects
of Mnf in MPP + treated cells by measuring the activation of
caspases-3/7. We observed a significant reduction in MPP +-
induced apoptosis in cells treated with Mnf (Figure 4c). The
antioxidant potential of Mnf was analyzed using ROS-
sensitive fluorescent dye DCFDA-H2. The SHSY-5Y cells
exposed to MPP + showed elevated ROS levels, which were
significantly reduced in the presence of Mnf (Figure 4d). As
the oxidative stress-mediated neuronal death is usually
characterized by loss of neuritis, we analyzed the morpho-
logical changes in SHSY-5Y cells using phase contrast
microscopy. Interestingly, the loss of neurites upon exposure
to MPP + was rescued in the Mnf treated cells (Figure 4e),
confirming the cytoprotective role of the nanoparticles in the
maintenance of cellular health.
We further investigated the role of Mnf on hydroxyl
radical scavenging effect utilizing the spin label DMPO which
forms DMPO/COH adduct having a characteristic spectrum of
1:2:2:1 intensity.[14] The signal intensity was decreased with an
increasing concentration of Mnf (Figure 5a), indicating that
Mnf scavenges hydroxyl radical effectively. To understand the
intriguing multi-enzyme activity of Mnf, we investigated the
possible catalytic mechanisms of reactions. The CAT-like
mechanism of Mnf involves conversion of H2O2 to water and
molecular oxygen, which is similar to that of CAT enzyme.
The gas chromatography experiments confirmed the evolu-
tion of oxygen molecules during the course of reaction
(Figure S13). In biological systems, the interesting redox
chemistry of manganese plays key roles in controlling the
ROS levels.[15] It is known that Mn2+/Mn3+ couple has higher
affinity for H2O2 and O2
@ than other transition metal couples
that are known to possess intrinsic enzyme-like activity. The
XPS data of Mnf confirm the presence of Mn in both 2 + and
3 + oxidation states. As shown earlier for CeO2 nanoparti-
cles,[5c] the rapid redox changes between two oxidation states
plays an important role in the multienzyme-like activity of
Mnf. To understand the relative contributions of two different
oxidation states in the activity, Mnf was treated with oxidizing
(NaIO4) and reducing (NaBH4) agents to obtain the oxidized
(O-Mnf) and reduced (R-Mnf) form, respectively, having
different Mn3+/Mn2+ ratios. The change in the oxidation states
was confirmed by XPS analyses (Figure S14). Interestingly,
the material having higher Mn3+/Mn2+ ratio (O-Mnf) exhib-
ited enhanced CAT and GPx-like activities (Figure 5b,c) as
compared to that of Mnf. In contrast, both O-Mnf and R-Mnf
exhibited slightly higher SOD activity than Mnf (Figure 5d),
indicating that both the oxidation states play crucial roles in
the enzyme mimetic activity.
In summary, we have demonstrated for the first time that
a single nanomaterial, Mn3O4 nanoflowers (Mnf), exhibits the
activity of three major structurally and functionally different
antioxidant enzymes, catalase (CAT), glutathione peroxidase
(GPx) and superoxide dismutase (SOD), which maintain the
redox homeostasis in mammalian cells by controlling the level
of reactive oxygen species (ROS). Mnf also effectively
scavenges hydroxyl radicals, which are known to cause
damage to biomolecules. A mechanistic investigation reveals
that the mixed oxidation states of manganese (Mn2+/Mn+3),
high stability against irreversible oxidation, large surface area
and unusually large pore size are important for the remark-
able multi-enzyme activity of Mnf. Interestingly, Mnf effec-
tively internalizes into human cells and provides efficient
cytoprotection in a ParkinsonQs disease model. This study
suggests that Mnf can be considered as a potential candidate
for therapeutic applications against oxidative stress-induced
neurological disorders.
