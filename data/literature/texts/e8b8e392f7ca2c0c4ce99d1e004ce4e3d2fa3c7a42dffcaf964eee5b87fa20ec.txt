Plasma-treated poly(ethylene oxide) nanoﬁbers containing tea tree oil/beta-
cyclodextrin inclusion complex for antibacterial packaging
Haiying Cui, Mei Bai, Lin Lin⁎
School of Food and Biological Engineering, Jiangsu University, Zhenjiang 212013, China
A R T I C L E I N F O
Keywords:
TTO/β-CD-IC
Electrospun nanoﬁbers

Escherichia coli O157:H7
A B S T R A C T
This work describes the eﬀect of cold nitrogen plasma to enhance the antibacterial activity of poly(ethylene
oxide) (PEO) nanoﬁbers containing antibacterial agent. Beta-cyclodextrin (β-CD) and tea tree oil (TTO) were
used as a host-guest to form water-soluble inclusion complex. The encapsulation eﬃciency of TTO in inclusion
complex could reach 73.23% at 60 °C. As antibacterial agent, the inclusion complex was encapsulated into PEO
matrix by electrospun. After plasma treatment, the release eﬃciency of antibacterial agent from PEO nanoﬁbers
was improved. As a result, the antibacterial activity of PEO nanoﬁbers was enhanced accordingly. The plasma-
treated nanoﬁber membranes achieved the highest antibacterial activity against Escherichia coli O157:H7, which
was tested on the beef for 7 d, with inhibition eﬃciently of 99.99% whether at 4 °C or 12 °C. The plasma-treated
PEO nanoﬁber membranes containing TTO/β-CD inclusion complex (TTO/β-CD-IC) can prolong the shelf-life of
beef, suggesting it has potential application in active food packaging.
1. Introduction
Escherichia coli O157:H7 (E. coli O157:H7) is one of the dominant
spoilage and pathogenic microorganisms (Jiang et al., 2017). E. coli
O157:H7 can cause grave illness such as hemorrhagic colitis and so on
(Campion et al., 2017). Meat, such as beef and their further products
contain high level of moisture and protein. So that, E. coli O157:H7 on
the contaminated meat products would multiply rapidly. In order to
decrease the potential risk of meat and meat products, various anti-
microbial additives were employed for food preservation (Jayasena
et al., 2015). Among them, essential oils (EOs) extracted from natural
plants have attracted a special interest because of the increasing pop-
ular concern about food safety and the potential impact of synthetic
additives on health (Pesavento et al., 2015).
TTO is a natural essential oil extracted from Melaleuca alternifolia.
And it is a complex mixture of various organic compounds including
terpene hydrocarbons, terpene alcohols, terpene phenols, et cetera.
There are numerous articles about the function of TTO, such as its
antimicrobial (Lins, Lustri, Minharro, Alonso, & Neto, 2016) and anti-
cancer (Ireland, Greay, & Hooper, 2012) activities. However, the ap-
plication of TTO is restricted, due to chemical instability when exposed
to air and high temperatures (Catanzano et al., 2015). Microbial con-
tamination often occurs on the food surface. Hence, the application of
eﬀective methods to eliminate foodborne pathogens on the surface of
food products are necessary in the food packaging industry. (Mensitieri
et al., 2011).
The antibacterial packaging material is one of the most promising
active packaging types which aims to prolong shelf-life and sustains the
sensory quality of food. Electrospinning has been gaining the great at-
tention from the perspective of developing multifunctional nanoﬁber
materials in recent years (Akçakoca Kumbasar, Akduman, & Çay, 2014;
Huan, Bai, Cheng, & Han, 2016). Compared with traditional materials,
due to the nanoscale diameter range of electrospun ﬁbers, electrospun
nanoﬁbrous webs have high surface area and highly porous structure
(Aytac, Yildiz, Kayaci-Senirmak, Tekinay, & Uyar, 2017). In addition,
previous study has indicated that electrospinning technique is an easy
process that can lead to the production of porous mats with a tunable
porosity (Valle, Franco, Katsarava, & Puiggalí, 2016). However, TTO
can’t be completely dissolved in PEO solution. And the volatilization of
TTO would increase during the electrospinning process since packaging
ﬁlms are produced by high tension electricity. The volatile nature of
TTO may encounter problems related to quick release during the sto-
rage of the nanoﬁber matrix (Alexandre, Scacchetti, Pinto, & Soares,
2017). Using β-CD to encapsulate TTO was developed and it has anti-
bacterial activity (Shrestha, Ho, & Bhandari, 2017).
β-CD, produced by the enzymatic degradation of starch, is a cyclic
oligosaccharide with a hydrophobic internal cavity and a hydrophilic
outer surface (Cheng et al., 2017). In addition, β-CD is low-cost and
safe. β-CD has a good ability to form a non-covalent inclusion complex
with TTO (Oliveira, Freitas, Cruz, Costa, & Pereira, 2017; Shrestha

cosmetic. The incorporation of β-CD inclusion complexes (β-CD-IC) into
electrospun nanoﬁber membranes matrix is remarkably eﬀective. As a
result, these functional nanoﬁbers have not only large surface area and
nano-porous structure, but also the obvious antibacterial activity of β-

capsulation of TTO/β-CD-IC into electrospun nanoﬁbers. Furthermore,
the TTO/β-CD-IC has not been reported for electrospinning, while
polyethylene oxide (PEO) was identiﬁed as a favorable food packaging
material (Food and Drug Administration, 2009, 2012).
Recently, cold plasma technology was widely applied because of
their capacity of inducing surface modiﬁcation. Plasma is an ionized
gas, which contains an approximately equal amount of positively and
negatively charges, including radicals, electrons, ions, excited mole-
cules and UV photons. Previous studies indicated that plasma can
modify electrospun nanoﬁber membranes (Maslakci, Ulusoy, & Oksuz,
2017; Surucu, Masur, Sasmazel, Woedtke, & Weltmann, 2016). How-
ever, plasma has not been reported for enhancing the release eﬃciency
and antibacterial activity of electrospun nanoﬁbers.
Based on the above-mentioned reasons, the application of TTO/β-
CD-IC in the preparation of packaging material is feasible. In this work,
β-CD was chosen to encapsulate TTO to improve its stability and elec-
trospinning was used to fabricate antibacterial packaging material. It
novelly presented that plasma was utilized to enhance the release ef-
ﬁciency of PEO electrospun nanoﬁbers. Moreover, the PEO electrospun
nanoﬁbers can prolong shelf-life of beef and sustain the sensory quality
of beef. The experimental scheme was shown in Fig. 1.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
TTO was purchased from J.E International (Caussols plateau,
France). β-CD was obtained from the Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co.,
Ltd. (Shanghai, China). PEO (average Wv ∼90,0000) was purchased

Industrial Culture Collection (Beijing, China). The strain was shaking
cultured in nutrient broth (NB, AOBOX, Beijing, China) at 37 °C for
24 h. Fresh beef was purchased from the local supermarket.

2.3. Preparation of TTO/β-CD-IC and characterization of TTO/β-CD-IC
2.3.1. Preparation of TTO/β-CD-IC
The TTO/β-CD-IC was prepared by co-precipitation method. First,
1.458 g ( ± 0.01 g) of β-CD was dissolved in 20 mL of deionized water
and kept at 55 ± 1 °C for 30 min under constant stirring using a
magnetic stirrer. The volume of deionized water used as a solvent was
determined according to the solubility of β-CD in water (7.29 g/
100 mL) at 55 °C (Valle, 2004). TTO (40 mg, 2 mg mL−1) was slowly
added to the warm β-CD solution. The resulting mixture was main-
tained at 50, 60 and 70 ( ± 1) °C for 3 h. After cooling the solution to
room temperature, the ﬁnal solution was refrigerated overnight at 4 °C.
The cold precipitated TTO/β-CD-IC was recovered by vacuum-ﬁltra-
tion. The precipitate was washed twice with 30% ethanol solution and
freeze-drying into power for 12 h. The other starting ratios of TTO
(4 mg mL−1, 6 mg mL−1, 8 mg mL−1) were prepared according to the
above steps. Lastly, the resulting solid TTO/β-CD-IC was crushed in a
mortar to obtain the ﬁne powder. Furthermore, the encapsulation ef-
ﬁciency was analyzed through GC–MS.

2.4. The antimicrobial activity of TTO/β-CD-IC in vitro
The antibacterial activity of TTO/β-CD-IC was evaluated using the
plate count method. Brieﬂy, E. coli O157:H7 was cultured under agi-
tation at 37 °C for 24 h. Diﬀerent concentrations of TTO/β-CD-IC (5, 10,
20, 30 and 40 mg mL−1) were added to the test tubes containing E. coli
O157:H7 (105–106 CFU mL−1). An inoculated culture without TTO/β-
CD-IC was used as a control. The tubes were cultured under shaking at
150 rpm at 37 °C. Then the numbers of residual bacteria were observed
after 24 h of incubation.
2.5. Preparation of the electrospinning solutions
1.0 g of PEO (5% w/v) and TTO/β-CD-IC (30 mg mL−1) were added
respectively to 20 mL deionized water under continuous magnetic
stirring for 6 h to get the electrospinning solution. The homogeneous
solution (PEO & TTO/β-CD-IC) underwent electrospinning (SNAN-01,
MECC Co., Ltd., Fukuoka, Japan) from a 5.0 mL polypropylene syringe
with an injection needle with 0.5 mm inner diameter. The ﬂow rate of
spinning solution was regulated to 0.6 mL h−1 by a syringe pump.
Fibers were gathered on electrical connection of aluminum foil, kraft
paper and plastic wrap respectively, which was placed at a vertical
distance of 15 cm to the needle tip. The impressed voltage was set at
25 kV via a high voltage power supply (Cui, Wu, & Lin, 2017a). For the
sake of comparison, pure PEO solution was prepared as well. The
electrospinning conditions were as described above. The morphology
and nanoﬁbers diameter were characterized by SEM (JSM-7001F,
JEOL, Tokyo, Japan).
2.6. Plasma treatment on TTO/β-CD–IC PEO nanoﬁber membranes
Firstly, TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁber membranes were exposed to
plasma at 300 W for 0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 5 min, respectively. The op-
eration power were 400, 500, 600 W and the samples were added to the
test tubes containing E. coli O157:H7 (105–106 CFU mL−1). The tubes
were cultured under shaking at 150 rpm at 37 °C. Then the numbers of
residual bacteria were observed after 24 h. All the experiments were
performed in triplicate.

2.8. The application of nanoﬁber membranes on beef
Fresh beef was cut into 30 × 25 × 10 mm cuboids. Then, the beef
samples were soaked in a prepared solution of E. coli O157:H7 for 1 min
to obtain an initial concentration of approximately 102–103 CFU mL−1.
After maintaining for 30 min under sterile conditions, the TTO/β-CD-IC
PEO nanoﬁber membranes (aluminum foil, kraft paper and plastic
wrap) was treated with plasma for 4 min and the power was 500 w. The
beef samples were treated with plasma-treated PEO nanoﬁbers. The
beef sample without nanoﬁber membranes was used as control groups.
The samples were stored at 4, 12, 25 and 35 °C for 7 d. Then the
numbers of residual bacteria were observed every day. All the experi-
ments were performed in triplicate.
2.9. Sensory evaluation
Same size and type of beef (control and plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-
IC PEO nanoﬁber membranes treated beef) were used for sensory
analysis by 30 panelists from Jiangsu University. Samples were ran-
domly arranged in a diﬀerent order for each sensory evaluation. Each
sample was presented with three-digit random codes and placed in a
transparent plastic cup. The panelists evaluated each sample by 9-point
hedonic scale or intensity scale with a score of 1 being either dislike
extremely or extremely bland and a score of 9 was like extremely or
extremely ﬂavorful. The samples were evaluated for like/dislike of
appearance, like/dislike of color, like/dislike ﬂavor, like/dislike of
juiciness, and like/dislike of overall acceptability (Kerth, Harbison,
Smith, & Miller, 2015).
2.10. Statistical analysis
The results obtained in this study were analyzed according to SPSS
software (version 22.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). A one-way analysis of
variance (ANOVA) and the Bonferroni statistical test were introduced to
determine signiﬁcant diﬀerences at a signiﬁcance level of P < 0.05.
The data were reported as means ± SD. All the experiments were re-
peated three times.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Analysis of TTO
The chemical compositions of TTO were presented in Fig. 2A. In
general, 12 main components which account for 81.53% of the total
were identiﬁed. The main identiﬁed components were as follows: ter-
pinene-4-ol (31.61%), γ-terpinene (20.47%) and α-Terpinene (9.81%).
This result was in agreement with the previous studies, which reported
that terpinene-4-ol, γ-terpinene and α-terpinene were the major
bioactive constituents of TTO with relative areas of 42.99%, 20.52%
and 9.21%, respectively (Shrestha et al., 2017).
The antibacterial activity of TTO against E. coli O157:H7 was given
in Fig. 2B. After treating with TTO for 4 h, around 99.73%, 99.97%,
99.997% reductions in total account (TC) of E. coli O157:H7 were ob-
served in 1/2 MIC, MIC and 2 MIC of TTO groups, respectively. Pre-
vious articles reported the obvious antibacterial activity of TTO against
Escherichia coli (D'Arrigo, Ginestra, Mandalari, Furneri, & Bisignano,
2010). Moreover, the surface morphology of E. coli O157:H7 cells be-
fore and after TTO (MIC) treatment were observed by TEM. From
Fig. 2C and D, the untreated bacteria had a smooth and intact cell
structure (Fig. 2C), while the treated group by TTO were distorted and
became rough (Fig. 2D). The results indicated that TTO could lead to
irreversible destructions to E. coli O157:H7 cells.
From Fig. 2E, those results imply that TTO might increase the per-
meability of cell membrane, leading to the leakage of the intracellular
substances. The measurement of the quantiﬁcation of DNA, ATP and
protein revealed that the DNA, ATP and protein content in E. coli
O157:H7 was signiﬁcantly reduced by 76.74%, 87.34% and 68.37%, as
compared with the control group. Obviously, the loss of DNA, ATP and
protein were due to the disruption of cell membrane and leaking of
DNA, ATP and protein to the bulk solution (Finger, Wiegand,
Buschmann, & Hipler, 2013). Electrical conductivity and surface Zeta
potential in E. coli O157:H7 were increased compared with the control.
The results indicated that the cell membrane was destroyed and elec-
trolytic leakage led to the increase in electrical conductivity. The results

3.2. Characterization of TTO/β-CD-IC and the antibacterial activity of
TTO/β-CD-IC in vitro
In the process of preparing the TTO/β-CD inclusion complexes, the
entrapment eﬃciency of β-CD could be aﬀected by the stirring tem-
perature. The results were presented in Fig. 3A. From 50 °C to 70 °C, the
entrapment rate climbed up and then decreased. The entrapment eﬃ-
ciency of 6 mg mL−1 TTO in TTO/β-CD inclusion complexes had
reached the maximum value (73.23%) at 60 °C. Other people have used
β-CD to encapsulate cinnamon oil and the eﬃciency of cinnamon oil in
β-CD was 76.2% (Wen et al., 2016). According to the previous studies,
the theoretical maximum loading of essential oil in β-CD powder ranges
from 8 to 12% (w/w) (Shrestha et al., 2017). In our study, the loading
of essential oil in β-CD powder was 6.03%. We used 6 mg mL−1 TTO to
carry out the following study. Moreover, TEM technique was employed
to investigate the morphologies of the TTO/β-CD-IC. The TEM image
(Fig. 3B) indicated that the TTO/β-CD-IC was monodispersed spherical
structure (Zhu, Yan, & Zhao, 2012).
Fig. 3C presented the FT-IR spectra of the TTO, β-CD and TTO/β-
CD-IC. TTO was majority composed of terpinene-4-ol and γ-terpinene,
the presence of −OH was observed with a band that corresponds to
3400 cm−1 due to −OH stretching vibration involving hydrogen
bonding. Aromatic character of terpenes was exhibited by eC]C ex-
hibited at 1514 cm−1, but this peak was not in the spectra of TTO/β-
CD-IC. And, the 1370 cm−1 of TTO appeared in the spectra of TTO/β-
CD-IC, with weak intensity. Fig. 3C indicated that TTO was embedded
into β-CD successfully. By contrast, the spectra of TTO/β-CD-IC showed
similar position and shape of these peaks with β-CD. It further proved

(Fabio,

The antibacterial activity of TTO/β-CD-IC against E. coli O157:H7 in
PBS was investigated at room temperature (Fig. 3D). The results sug-
gested that the population of E. coli O157:H7 was reduced after 24 h of
TTO/β-CD-IC treatment. It was found that the TTO/β-CD-IC at con-
centrations of 30% and 40% achieved good antibacterial activity,
99.94% and 99.96% reductions in population after 24 h, respectively.
The results in vitro indicated that TTO/β-CD-IC could achieve the de-
sired inhibitory eﬀect against E. coli O157:H7. Previous articles re-
ported that β-CD could be used to encapsulate other essential oils
(Cevallos, Buera, & Elizalde, 2010; Wen et al., 2016). In addition, 30%
of the TTO/β-CD-IC was used to carry out the subsequent experiment.
3.3. Characterization of electrospun nanoﬁbers
Fig. 4 showed the representative SEM images and the nanoﬁber
diameter distributions with average diameters of PEO, TTO/β-CD-IC,
plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁbers. The electrospun nanoﬁ-
bers were uniform. As shown in Fig. 4A and B, the surface morphology
of nanoﬁbers were uniform and smooth. In addition, the diameters of
PEO nanoﬁbers were usually ranged from 200 nm to 400 nm. The SEM
image of TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁbers was shown in Fig. 4B. It had
been found that the diameters of nanoﬁbers became larger when TTO/
β-CD-IC was loaded (from 400 nm to 600 nm). The results indicated
that the TTO/β-CD-IC was incorporated into PEO nanoﬁbers success-
fully.

3.4. The antibacterial activity of plasma on electrospun nanoﬁbers
The eﬀects of diﬀerent operation power and exposure time of CNP
on electrospun nanoﬁbers were shown in Fig. 5. The results demon-
strated that the intensity of the plasma was in a good correlation with
the antibacterial eﬀects under higher plasma power. However, there
were no signiﬁcant diﬀerences in the population of E. coli O157:H7 at
power of 300 W and 400 W after the treatment time of 0.5 min and
1 min. It might be because the plasma enhanced the release rate of
nanoﬁbers and plasma-generated reactive ingredients (atoms, photons,
ions, free radicals and free electrons), which closely associated with its
modiﬁed nanoﬁbers (Maslakci et al., 2017; Surucu et al., 2016). We
used 4 min and 500 W of plasma to treat nanoﬁbers in next experiment.
3.5. The release rate of nanoﬁbers
The release rates were determined during 15 d storage at 4 °C,
12 °C, 25 °C and 35 °C (Fig. 6). The encapsulated TTO in nanoﬁbers
underwent diﬀerent degrees of leakage. After 15 d of storage, the cu-
mulative release rate was observed at plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO
nanoﬁbers containing TTO with 58.34%, 69.67%, 79.95% and 86.89%
values at diﬀerent temperature (4 °C, 12 °C, 25 °C, and 35 °C), respec-
tively. It was obvious that the plasma can strongly enhance the release
rate of nanoﬁbers. There was a positive relation between the anti-
bacterial activity and the release rate (Cui, Li, & Lin, 2016).
3.6. The antibacterial activity of plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO
nanoﬁber membranes in beef
The antimicrobial activity of plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO na-
noﬁbers against E. coli O157:H7 in beef at 4 °C, 12 °C, 25 °C and 35 °C
was evaluated immediately after prepared and after storage period for
7 d. The results were shown in Fig. 7. At 4 °C, the population of E. coli
O157:H7 in beef was reduced after the plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC
PEO nanoﬁbers treatment. The counts of the control group from 1 to 7 d
were increased by time. Almost 99.61%, 99.38%, and 99.73% of de-
crease in population were observed on three substrate materials at
cultivation time of 1 d, compared with the control. Furthermore,
around 99.98%, 99.97% and 99.96% reduction in population were
detected after 7 d of the nanoﬁbers treatment (Fig. 7A). Besides, the
growth of E. coli O157:H7 at 4 °C and 12 °C (Fig. 7A and B) were slower
than incubation at 25 °C and 35 °C (Fig. 7C and D). Compared with
control, 99.53%, 98.42%, and 98.71% of decrease in population were
observed on three substrate materials at 35 °C after 7 d, respectively.
The results indicated that plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nano-
ﬁbers had a signiﬁcant eﬀect on the inhibition of E. coli O157:H7 in
beef. The functionalized nanoﬁbers could extend the shelf life of beef.
In conclusion, plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁbers would be a
promising disinfectant in beef products preservation domain.

(P > 0.05). After 7 d treatment, the control group has low grades (Cui,

4. Conclusions
In conclusion, the chemical stability of TTO was signiﬁcantly im-
proved by β-CD and the entrapment eﬃciency of TTO/β-CD-IC was
improved. Correspondingly, the TTO/β-CD-IC could achieve the desired
in vitro inhibitory eﬀect against E. coli O157:H7. Moreover, the TTO
release rate of TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁber membranes could be en-
hanced by plasma treatment, accompanying with the improvement of
antibacterial activity. By considering to the promised practical appli-
cations of TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁbers, the antibacterial assay of
plasma-treated TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁbers exhibited stable anti-
bacterial eﬀect on beef during 7 d storage. Hence, the plasma-treated
TTO/β-CD-IC PEO nanoﬁber membranes have a broad prospect in the
ﬁeld of food preservation.
