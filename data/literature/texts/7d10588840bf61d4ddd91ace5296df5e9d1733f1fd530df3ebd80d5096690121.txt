Silver Doping-Induced Luminescence Enhancement and Red-Shift
of Gold Nanoclusters with Aggregation-Induced Emission
Ziping Wang,[a, b] Zhiling Zhu,[a] Chengkun Zhao,[a] Qiaofeng Yao,[c] Xiyou Li,[d] Heyuan Liu,[d]
Fanglin Du,[a] Xun Yuan,*[a] and Jianping Xie*[c]
Abstract: A deep understanding on the luminescence
property of aggregation-induced emission (AIE) featured
metal nanoclusters (NCs) is highly desired. This paper re-
ports a systematic study on enhancing the luminescence
of AIE-featured Au NCs, which is achieved by Ag doping
to engineer the size/structure and aggregation states of
the AuI-thiolate motifs in the NC shell. Moreover, by pro-
longing the reaction time, the luminescence of the as-syn-
thesized AuAg NCs could be further tailored from orange
to red, which is also due to the variation of the AuI-thio-
late motifs of NCs. This study can facilitate a better under-
standing of this AIE-featured luminescent probe and the
design of other synthetic routes for this rising family of
functional materials.
Luminescent metal nanoclusters (NCs) have emerged as a
novel family of functional materials due to their intriguing at-
tributes such as ultrasmall size, strong luminescence, large
stokes shift, benign biocompatibility, and good photo-stabili-
ty.[1] These advantages make NCs promising in diverse applica-
tion fields including environment, energy conversion, and bio-
medicine.[2] To realize these applications, tremendous efforts
were devoted to the design of efficient synthetic strategies for
highly luminescent metal NCs,[3] and some exciting progresses
have been achieved.[3a,b,d,f] For example, our group recently de-
voloped a novel synthetic strategy for highly luminescent
Au0@AuI-thiolate
NCs
with
aggregation
induced
emission
(AIE).[3d,e,4] The key of this strategy is to immobilize abundant
AuI-thiolate complexes with AIE feature on the surface of Au0
cores at elevated temperature (708C) to form Au0@AuI-thiolate
NCs. The luminescence intensity of this Au NCs is strongly de-
pendent on the aggregation degree of the surface AuI-thiolate
complexes. However, how to tailor the luminescence of this
AIE-featured luminescent Au NCs is still a major challenge. We
hypothesize that if we could engineer the surface AuI-thiolate
complexes of NCs in terms of size/structures and aggregation
states, we may be able to tailor the luminescence property of
the Au0@AuI-thiolate NCs.
Herein we report our study that using silver doping to tailor
the AIE-featured luminescence property of the Au0@AuI-thio-
late NCs based on engineering the size/structure and aggrega-
tion states of the surface AuI-thiolate complexes. Particularly,
we delicately introduce a small amount of AgI ions to link AuI-
thiolate complexes, which renders resultant AuAg NCs with a
more compact shell of long MI-thiolate motifs (M denotes
metal) after 2 h of reaction at elevated temperature (858C).
The as-synthesized AuAg NCs have similar size to its mono-
metallic Au analogue, but exhibit 3.9 times of luminescence
enhancement comparing to that of the Au NCs. Moreover, the
luminescence emission of the AuAg alloy NCs can be tailored
from orange to red by adjusting the structure and thickness of
MI-thiolate motifs in the NC shell, which could be simply ac-
complished via prolonging the reaction time (up to 16 h). This
study demonstrates that engineering the MI-thiolate motifs in
NC shells is an efficient way for tailoring the luminescence
property of metal NCs, which may pave an alternative avenue
for producing highly luminescent metal NCs.
In this study, the luminescent mono-metallic Au NCs is syn-
thesized according to our previously developed strategy with
minor modifications.[3d] Briefly, aqueous solutions of gluta-
thione (GSH) and HAuCl4 are mixed in ultrapure water with a
feeding molar ratio of 1.5:1 under stirring condition, and
highly luminescent Au0@AuI-GSH NCs with AIE feature could
be formed after stirring for 2 h at 858C. Using the same strat-
egy, the Ag-doped AuAg NCs with luminescent enhancement
can be synthesized by introducing 0–20 mol% of AgNO3 to-
gether with HAuCl4 as metal precursors while keeping other
conditions unchanged (See detailed synthetic procedures in
Experimental Section).
Compared to Au NCs, the as-synthesized Ag-doped AuAg
NCs have similar optical absorption and comparable cluster
size, but displays almost 4 times of luminescent enhancement.

As shown in Figure 1a, mono-metallic Au NCs show a shoulder
absorption at &400 nm (solid black curve) with a yellowish
color (Figure 1b, item 1), which suggests the successful synthe-
sis of sub-2 nm sized Au NCs instead of larger sized Au nano-
crystals featuring a surface plasmon resonance (SPR) peak at
520 nm;[1a] by contrast, the Ag-doped AuAg NCs exhibit very
similar absorption (solid red curve) as well as the same color
(Figure 1b, item 2) to mono-metallic Au NCs. Moreover, the
polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (PAGE, 30%)[5] analysis of
both Au NCs and AuAg NCs reveals similar single broad band
with intense orange emission (Figure 1b, right panel), indicat-
ing that the AuAg NCs (item 2’) have similar size to their Au
analogue (item 1’). The luminescence spectra of the Au NCs
and AuAg NCs show dual excitation peaks at 320 nm and
400 nm (Figure 1a, dashed curves on left side), and a dominant
emission peak at 590 nm (Figure 1a, dashed curves on right
side). However, the luminescence intensities of Au NCs and
AuAg NCs are apparently different, and up to 3.9 times of lumi-
nescent enhancement is observed for the AuAg NCs doped
with 10% of Ag (Figure 1a, dashed red curve) comparing to
Au NCs (Figure 1a, dashed black curve). It should be men-
tioned that luminescent enhancement is also observed for
AuAg NCs doped with different ratios of Ag (Figure S1). In ad-
dition, the as-synthesized luminescent AuAg NCs are extremely
stable, and no obvious change is observed in their UV-vis ab-
sorption and luminescence spectra after one month of storage
at room temperature (Figure S2).
To better understand the mechanism of the Ag doping in-
duced luminescent enhancement of the Au NCs, we conducted
luminescence lifetime measurement for these Au and AuAg
NCs. As shown in Figure 1c and Table S1, the luminescence
lifetime of both the Au NCs and AuAg NCs are in microsecond
magnitude. Particularly, the mono-metallic Au NCs have three
dominant luminescence
emitters
with
lifetime
of
1.61 ms
(65.8%), 4.83 ms (34.0%), and 63.66 ms (0.2%), respectively,
while the bi-metallic AuAg NCs possess longer luminescence
lifetime: 2.03 ms (65.7%), 5.16 ms (34.2%), and 52.2 ms (0.1%).
These results confirm that the luminescence of both Au NCs
and AuAg NCs are originated from the AIE of surface MI-GSH
complexes.[3d] In addition, the longer luminescence lifetime of
AuAg NCs may suggest that the doped Ag species could cross-
link the AuI-GSH motifs to make MI-GSH motifs longer and
more compact, which increases the aggregation degree of the
MI-GSH motifs in the NC shell, thus enhancing the lumines-
cence of the NCs.
In order to prove this assumption, X-ray photoelectron spec-
troscopy (XPS) was employed to check the oxidation states of
the Au 4f in Au NCs and Ag-doped AuAg NCs. Theoretically,
the increase of the aggregation degree of AuI-GSH complex by
silver doping will not markedly change the oxidation states of
the Au in the NCs. As shown in Figure 1d, the Au 4f spectra of
the Au NCs and the AuAg NCs show almost the same binding
energy centered at &84 eV for Au 4f7/2 and &86 eV for Au 4f5/
2. Their Au 4f7/2 XPS spectra are deconvoluted into AuI and Au0
components with binding energies of 84.3 eV and 83.7 eV, re-
spectively.[3] On the basis of above XPS analyses, the predomi-
nant Au species in the Au NCs is identified to AuI (&65.4%),
which is comparable to that of AuI in the AuAg NCs (&62.1%).
In addition, the XPS result reveals a very weak Ag signal in
the AuAg NCs (Figure S3), which indicates that the Ag species
are not deposited on the surface of the Au NCs, but locate at
the joint of the AuI-GSH motifs in the same layer (to link two

Ag species in Figure S4a) or at the interlayer of the AuI-GSH
motifs (to make different layers of AuI-GSH motifs more com-
pact, see the linking mode II of Ag species in Figure S4b). This
is different from previously reported AIE-featured luminescent
Au@Ag NCs where Ag atoms are located on the surface of Au
NCs to link surface AuI-thiolate motifs for “turning on” the lu-
minescence.[4b] The high content of AuI in both NCs further
supports that the luminescence is originated from the AIE of
AuI complexes in the NC shell. The comparable amount of AuI
species and weak Ag signal shown in the XPS results of AuAg
NCs imply that the doped Ag atoms may link small AuI thiolate
motifs for a more compact MI-GSH motif shell surrounding the
Au core, leading to stronger luminescence of the AuAg NCs
via AIE.
The doping position of Ag species in the AuAg NCs is also
studied, and we perform a control experiment by introducing
cysteine (Cys) molecules to etch AuAg NCs. It is documented
that Cys molecules could easily etch surface AgI species off
from the NCs via the strong AgI-Cys interaction.[6] If AgI species
are located on the NC surface as linkers between surface AuI-
GSH motifs of the AuAg NCs, the Cys could etch surface Ag
atoms off and thus break the Ag-linked AuI-GSH motifs, which
will cause obvious decrease in the luminescence intensity of
NCs. However, the Cys addition does not result in the decrease
in the luminescence intensity of the AuAg NCs (Figure S5),
which further validates that the Ag species are more likely
doped inside the AuI-GSH shell rather than on the outside sur-
face of AuI-GSH shell. In addition, it is known that AgI is more
difficult to be reduced compared to AuI as the redox potential
of AuCl4
@/Au (&1.0 V) is higher than Ag+/Ag (&0.8 V).[7] There-
fore, the AgI species may not be co-reduced with an amount
of AuI to contribute to the formation of bi-metallic metal
cores, but probably just locate inside the NC shell as motif link-
ers, which is supported by the fact that the metal core of this
AIE-type NCs has a little contribution to its luminescence. On
this basis, the doped Ag species are supposed inside the shell
of AuI-GSH motifs either to link AuI-GSH motifs to be a longer
one or to link different layers of AuI-GSH motifs for a more
compact structure, which leads to large luminescent enhance-
ment for the AuAg NCs.
Another interesting phenomenon is that the luminescence
emission of the AuAg NCs could change if prolonging the re-
action time from 2 to 16 h at 858C. As shown in Figure 2,
while the AuAg NCs display an orange emission (inset, left
item) with a maximum intensity at 590 nm after 2 h of reaction
at 858C (red curve), its luminescence emission gradually shifts
to red color (inset, right item) at 620 nm with a concurrent in-
tensity decrease after prolonging the reaction up to 16 h (ma-
genta curve). The solution color of the AuAg NCs also changes
from yellowish for 2 h of reaction (Figure 2, inset, left top item)
to yellow-brown for 16 h of reaction under visible light
(Figure 2, inset, right top item), which may imply their size var-
iation. Therefore, we applied PAGE (30%) analysis to character-
ize the size of the luminescent AuAg NCs. As shown in the
inset of Figure 2 (bottom panel, right item), after 16 h of reac-
tion the AuAg NC sample shows two packed luminescent
bands: red-emitting NC band with larger cluster size, and
orange-emitting NC band with unchanged cluster size.
Such two species of the luminescent AuAg NCs are compre-
hensively characterized to reveal the correlation between the
luminescence emission and reaction time of AuAg NCs. The
two bands in the PAGE gel of the AuAg NCs were individually
cut and soaked with 5 mL ultrapure water for 12 h at 48C to
allow the NCs to diffuse out of the PAGE gel. The two liquid
supernatants acquired from band 1 and 2 of PAGE gel (Fig-
ure 3a, labelled as band 1 NCs and band 2 NCs hereafter) are
firstly analyzed by UV-vis absorption and luminescence spec-
troscopies. Both NC species from two bands have no obvious
absorption peak in their UV-vis absorption spectra (Figure 3b,
red and black solid curves). No shift in the luminescence exci-
tation spectra of the band 1 and band 2 NCs is observed, but
red luminescence is emitted at 650 nm for larger sized band 1
NCs (Figure 3b, dashed black curve) and intense orange lumi-
nescence is emitted at 610 nm for smaller sized band 2 NCs
(Figure 3b, dashed red curve).
We rationalize that the red shift in the luminescence emis-
sion of the band 1 AuAg NCs could be due to the variation of
the MI-GSH motifs in the NC shell after 16 h of reaction. On
one hand, a long reaction at elevated temperature (858C) in-
creases the reduction degree of the AuAg NCs, which means
some MI-GSH complexes initially in the NC shell could be grad-
ually reduced to M0 and become a part of metal core. This var-
iation in the metal core and MI-GSH shell influences the size
and structure of the NCs, which could explain why the band 1
AuAg NCs run slower than the AuAg NCs acquired after 2 h of
reaction during PAGE analysis (Figure 2, bottom inset). The de-
creased amount of MI-GSH complexes (i.e., thin MI-GSH shell),
which indeed serve as luminescence emitters in the AuAg NCs,
directly lead to weakened emission intensity. Similarly, the red
shift of luminescence should also be attributed to such struc-
ture adjustment made possible by prolonged incubation.
The decrease of the MI-GSH motifs in the NC shell could the-
oretically lead to the short luminescence lifetime via the short-
er path of ligand-to-metal-metal charge transfer (LMMCT).[3d,8]
Hence, the luminescence lifetime of bands 1 and 2 AuAg NC
species are further examined (Figure 3c and Table S2). As ex-

The reduction degree of the two band AuAg NCs is also ex-
amined by XPS. As shown in Figure 3d, the Au 4f spectra
reveal that the predominant species in the red-emitting band
1 NCs become Au0 (&60.0%) instead of AuI, while no much
change is observed for the binding energy of Au 4f in orange-
emitting band 2 NCs (&59.5% of AuI). The result suggests pro-
longing reaction time could partially reduce AuI to Au0 in the
resultant AuAg NCs, inducing the red shift of their lumines-
cence emission. Inductively coupled plasma-mass spectrometry
(ICP-MS) is applied to evaluate the composition of the band 1
and band 2 NCs, and the molar radio of Au:Ag is 1:0.09 for
band 1 NCs and 1:0.08 for band 2 NCs, which matches very
well with the feeding molar ratios of Au:Ag, suggesting negli-
gible loss of Ag species after 16 h of reaction and hence sup-
porting the proposed structure adjustment mechanism.
Different from Ag-doped AuAg NCs, mono-metallic Au NCs
basically could rarely change luminescence emission (only
10 nm of red shift) by prolonging reaction time (Figure S6), in-
dicating doped Ag atoms play an important role in affecting
the luminescence emission, which will be systematically stud-
ied in our future research. The reaction temperature is also cru-
cial to the luminescence shift of the AuAg NCs. Upon reaction
at 708C up to 24 h, the Ag doped AuAg NCs does not emit
red luminescence; however, red luminescence could be ob-
served for AuAg NCs reacted at 958C for 12 h (Figure S7).
Therefore, higher temperature (+858C) could help MI-GSH
motifs in the NC shell to overcome the energy barrier, which
enables the partial reduction of MI in the AuAg NCs to M0, re-
sulting in the variation of the MI-GSH motifs in the length/
structures as well as the luminescence shift of the AuAg NCs.
In summary, we have reported the tailoring of the lumines-
cence property of AIE-featured Au NCs by using Ag doping to
engineer the size/structure of the AuI-thiolate motifs in the NC
shell. The doped Ag species could act as motif linkers and in-
crease the aggregation degree of the AuI-thiolate motifs to
form a more compact shell for NCs, which could enhance the
luminescence intensity by up to 3.9 times compared to pure
Au NCs. In addition, by prolonging the reaction time or in-
creasing the temperature, we could further modify the MI-thio-
late motif shell of the AuAg NCs by increasing the reduction
degree of the Au species and decreasing the shell thickness of
MI-thiolate motifs, which causes a shift of the luminescence
emission of NCs from orange to red. This study is interesting
not only because it provides an efficient protocol for synthesiz-
ing AIE-featured highly luminescent bi-metallic NCs but also
because it demonstrates that the luminescence property of
the AIE-featured metal NCs could be tailored through engi-
neering the MI-thiolate motifs in NC shells.
