The emergence and evolution of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) pose a 
significant challenge to the current arsenal to fight infection. Antibiotic 
adjuvants represent an appealing tactic for tackling the AMR of pathogens, 
however, their practical applications are greatly constrained by the harsh 
infectious microenvironment. Herein, it is found that silver nanoclusters (Ag 
NCs) can possess tunable enzymatic activities to modulate infectious micro-
environments. Based on this finding, an enzymatic nanoadjuvant (EnzNA) 
self-assembled from Ag NCs, which is inert under neutral physiological condi-
tions but can readily disassemble into isolated Ag NCs exhibiting biofilm 
destructive oxidase-mimetic activity in the acidic biofilm microenvironment, 
is developed. Once internalized into the neutral cytoplasm of bacteria, Ag 
NCs switch to reveal the thiol oxidase-mimetic activity to suppress ribosomal 
biogenesis for AMR reversal and evolution inhibition of pathogens. Conse-
quently, EnzNAs revitalize various existing antibiotics against methicillin-
resistant Staphylococcus aureus, and potentiate the antibiotic efficacy against 
biofilm-mediated skin infection and lethal lung infection in mice. These 
findings highlight the capability of enzyme-mimetic nanomaterials to modu-
late the infectious microenvironment and potentiate antibiotics, providing a 
paradigm shift for anti-infection therapy.

1. Introduction
Antimicrobial resistance (AMR) has become a significant 
public health concern worldwide especially with the rise of

and the lack of novel antibiotics nowadays, a critical approach is 
to revitalize existing antibiotics using antibiotic adjuvants.
Ideally, antibiotic adjuvants can suppress AMR by disturbing 
antibiotic-inactivating 
enzymes,[21] 
membrane 
stability,[22] 
and/or efflux pumps,[23] to potentiate the effect of antibi-
otics.[24,25] However, the harsh infectious microenvironment 
hinders the practical antibiotics-adjuvant therapy in vivo.[26] 
Indeed, the extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) provides a 
physical barrier to protect bacteria from antibiotics and adju-
vants;[27] and the intracellular ribosomal biogenesis mediated 
bacterial biosynthesis and secretion further support bacterial 
adhesion, biofilm formation as well as the production of AMR-
associated substances (e.g., antibiotic-inactivating enzymes), 
which synergistically form a conducive microenvironment that 
promotes the AMR evolution and nullifies the antibiotics-adju-
vant therapy.[28,29] In light of these findings, we hypothesize that 
the development of infectious microenvironment modulating 
antibiotic adjuvants, combined with conventional antibiotics, 
would effectively overcome AMR and greatly boost antibiotic 
therapy.
In this study, we found that silver nanoclusters (Ag NCs) 
exhibit tunable oxidase-mimetic activities at different pH con-
ditions, holding great potential to modulate infectious micro-
environment. Inspired by the fact that extracellular biofilm 
formation and intracellular ribosomal biogenesis of microbes 
are both sensitive to oxidative stress,[30,31] we further engineer 
a silver-based enzymatic nanoadjuvant (EnzNA) composed of 
self-assembled Ag NCs to dynamically modulate extracellular 
and intracellular infectious microenvironments with a pH 
discrepancy for combating AMR of pathogens. Mechanistic 
studies show that EnzNAs are bioinert in normal tissues, but 
can disassemble into Ag NCs in the acidic biofilm microenvi-
ronment, where the isolated Ag NCs with more exposed (111) 
facets and rich H3O+ exhibit oxidase (OXD)-mimetic activity to 
generate H2O2, facilitating the degradation of EPS matrix for 
biofilm eradication.[32] Besides, the acid-activated surface with 
O-adatoms enables EnzNAs to exert thiol oxidase-mimetic 
activity under neutral cytoplasmic conditions, inducing intra-
cellular ribosomal dysfunction in bacteria (Figure 1). Con-
sequently, these context-dependent enzymatic activities of 
EnzNAs can dramatically disrupt the pathogen-friendly extra-
cellular and intracellular microenvironments, successfully 
revitalizing the antibiotic activity of various existing antibiotics 
against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and 
inhibiting antibiotic resistance evolution of pathogens. Finally, 
EnzNAs are demonstrated to potentiate the antibiotic efficacy of 
ampicillin against MRSA biofilm-mediated skin infection and 
lethal lung infection in mice.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Design and Fabrication of EnzNA with Context-Dependent 
Catalytic Activities
Ultra-small Ag NCs with a size of ≈3.5  nm were synthe-
sized using a modified thermal decomposition method 
(Figure S1, Supporting Information).[33] The pH-responsive 
ligand, octadecyl amine-poly (aminopropyl imidazole-aspar-
tate) (octadecylamine-p(API-Asp)), was synthesized via ring-
opening polymerization and aminolysis of 1-(3-aminopropyl) 
imidazole (Figures S2 and S3, Supporting Information).[34] 
EnzNAs were constructed by the self-assembly of Ag NCs with 
octadecylamine-p(API-Asp) and Pluronic F127 via an emulsion 
solvent evaporation method (Figure 2a). The EnzNAs are highly 
stable in various neutral solutions including phosphate buffer 
solution (PBS), tryptone soybean broth (TSB)-, and fetal bovine 
serum (FBS)-containing media (Figure S4, Supporting Infor-
mation), but can quickly disassemble upon exposure to acidic 
conditions due to the protonation of imidazole groups in octa-
decylamine-p(API-Asp) (Figure 2b–d and Figure S5, Supporting 
Information), as evidenced by transmission electron micro­
scopy (TEM) images, size distributions, zeta potentials, and 
ultraviolet–visible (UV–vis) absorption spectra (Figure 2b–d and 
Figure S5c, Supporting Information). In contrast, such a dis-
assembly behavior is not observed in the silver nanoassembly 
(AgNA) with a pH-insensitive octadecylamine-poly(β-benzil-ι-
aspartate) (octadecylamine-pBla) ligand (Figure S6, Supporting 
Information).
The X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of Ag NCs matches the 
face-centered cubic structure with dominant (111) facets, pro-
viding a structural basis for catalytic activity (Figure S1, Sup-
porting Information).[35] EnzNAs remarkably induce the oxi-
dation of o-phenylenediamine (OPD) or ascorbic acid (AA) at 
pH 5.5 but not 7.4 (Figure 2e,f; Figures S7 and S8, Supporting 
Information), indicating the OXD-mimetic activity of EnzNAs 
exclusively in acidic conditions. In addition, the release of 
ions under acidic (pH 5.5) conditions shows little effect on the 
enzyme-mimetic activity of EnzNAs (Figure S9, Supporting 
Information). The density-functional theory (DFT) calcula-
tion unravels that the H3O+ on the Ag (111) surface occupies 
a higher activity to adsorb O2 under acidic conditions, which 
promotes the hydrogen abstraction by O2 for H2O2 genera-
tion (Figure  2f,i and Figure S10, Supporting Information). 
In contrast, there is much less H3O+ formed on the Ag (111) 
surface under neutral conditions, resulting in a higher energy 
barrier for H2O2 generation, and thus leading to a weak OXD-
mimetic activity. Moreover, the disassembly of EnzNAs under 
acidic conditions can significantly boost the exposure of Ag 
(111) facets for an amplified OXD-mimetic activity. Notably, 
once the surface O-adatoms of Ag NCs are formed under 
acidic conditions, Ag NCs can facilitate the oxidization of sub-
strates even re-exposing to neutral environments (Figure  2i 
and Figure S10, Supporting Information). Consequently, the 
intracellular l-cysteine can readily be adsorbed on the (111) 
surface of Ag NCs with a low free energy (−4.41 eV), and effi-
ciently react with the surface O-adatoms to form sulfonates 
(Figure  2j), indicating a highly potent thiol oxidase-mimetic 
activity of Ag NCs (Figure  2g,h; Figures S11 and S12, Sup-
porting Information). These results collectively demonstrate 
that EnzNAs can disassemble into isolated Ag NCs under 
the acidic biofilm microenvironment to exhibit OXD-mimetic 
activity, and to switch to exhibit thiol oxidase-mimetic activity 
once enter into a neutral environment of bacterial cells, which 
context-dependently interfere with the infectious microenvi-
ronment with pH discrepancy.

2.2. EnzNAs Induce Extracellular EPS Degradation  
and Intracellular Ribosomal Dysfunction for the Infectious  
Microenvironment Modulation
MRSA is the leading pathogen to cause fatal AMR burden 
through biofilm formation and over-production of AMR-related 
proteins.[36,37] Therefore, an MRSA strain extracted from clin-
ical patients was used to investigate the capability of EnzNAs to 
modulate infectious microenvironments for combating AMR. 
First, EnzNAs show good colloidal stability in bio-context, and 
exhibit efficient local accumulation and penetration in the acidic 
biofilm environment (pH 4.5–6.5) via the pH-dependent disas-
sembly behavior (Figure 3a and Figure S13, Supporting Infor-
mation).[38,39] Moreover, EnzNAs can elevate the ROS level in 
MRSA bacterial suspension under an acidic condition but not 
under a neutral condition, which is driven by the acid-activated 
OXD-mimetic activity (Figure 3b,c). The significantly decreased 
biofilm burden after EnzNAs treatment indicates that the oxida-
tive stress can induce biofilm eradication (Figure  3d).[40] This 
activity of EnzNAs can be almost eliminated by catalase (CAT, 
a H2O2-scavenging enzyme) rather than other ROS inhibitors 
including superoxide dismutase (SOD, an O2·−-scavenging 
enzyme), histidine (inhibitor of 1O2), and mannitol (inhibitor 
of ·OH) (Figure S14, Supporting Information), revealing the 
leading role of H2O2 production in biofilm eradication. These 
results suggest that EnzNAs can easily accumulate and exhibit 
OXD-mimetic activity in the acidic biofilm microenvironment 
to generate ROS for biofilm eradication.
The locations of substantial Ag elements are highly coinci-
dent with the images of MRSA cells as shown by the energy 
dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy (Figure S15, Supporting 
Information), and the amount of Ag elements in MRSA cells 
increases over time (Figure  3e). These results indicate that 
EnzNAs can be internalized by MRSA cells in a time-dependent

2.3. EnzNAs Revitalize Conventional Antibiotics by Reversing 
AMR and Inhibiting the Evolution of Pathogens
In light of the inhibitory effect of EnzNAs on biofilm and ribo-
somal biogenesis, two key factors for the formation of path-
ogen-friendly microenvironment,[50–52] the antibacterial effect of 
EnzNAs combined with antibiotics was investigated using the 
MRSA strain, which is eight times more resistant to β-lactam 
antibiotics than the standard S. aureus strain (Figure S18, Sup-
porting Information). First, owing to the biofilm-eradicating 
capability, EnzNAs can effectively facilitate the infiltration of 
ampicillin (Amp, a typical β-lactam antibiotic) into MRSA bio-
film (Figure S19, Supporting Information). As expected, the 
combination of EnzNAs and Amp remarkably kills biofilm-
embedded MRSA (Figure 4a and Figure S20, Supporting Infor-
mation), as well as suppresses the growth of planktonic MRSA 
(Figure 4b). EnzNAs potentiate the efficacy of Amp largely ben-
efiting from intrinsic enzymatic activities rather than the Ag+ 
releasing, as indicated by a lower fractional inhibitory concentra-
tion index (FICI) of the combination of EnzNAs and Amp than 
that of Ag+ and Amp (Figure 4c–e and Figure S21, Supporting 
Information).[53] Moreover, EnzNAs plus Amp treatment can 
significantly inhibit MRSA biofilm formation, contributing to 
the inhibition of AMR (Figure 4f–i). The antimicrobial effects 
of two other antibiotics, including penicillin (Pen, another 
β-lactam antibiotic) and streptomycin (Str, an aminoglycoside 
antibiotic), can also be restored by EnzNAs (Figure  4j and 
Figure S22, Supporting Information). Although the resistance 
to Str emerges mainly by antibiotic target modification,[51] the 
infectious microenvironment modulating EnzNAs can improve 
the antibacterial effect of Str by increasing antibiotic internali-
zation into MRSA (Figure S22i–l, Supporting Information). 
These results demonstrate a potent adjuvant effect of EnzNAs 
to restore the efficacies of conventional antibiotics, which is 
driven by their intrinsic enzymatic activities, ultimately sup-
pressing the growth of MRSA, inhibiting MRSA biofilm forma-
tion, and promoting MRSA biofilm eradication combined with 
antibiotics.
To further unravel the biological mechanism underlying 
EnzNAs to overcome AMR of pathogens, the secretion of 
β-lactamase (β-lactam antibiotic-inactivating enzyme) and EPS, 
two crucial components of infectious microenvironments that 
impair the activity of antibiotics, were studied. Compared to 
the treatment with Ag+, the treatment with EnzNAs can sig-
nificantly reduce β-lactamase activity and EPS secretion level 
(Figure 4k–m), contributing to the AMR reversal. Considering 
the important role of ribosomal biogenesis in AMR develop-
ment, the effect of EnzNAs on AMR evolution was investigated 
using a serial passage assay. At the 20th generation, the median 
MIC50 (minimum inhibitory concentration to inhibit the 
growth of pathogens by 50%) of Amp increases by 16-fold than 
that in the first generation, suggesting the evolution of MRSA. 
In stark contrast, this phenomenon is not observed in MRSA 
treated with Amp in combination with EnzNAs, revealing that 
EnzNAs can suppress the evolution of AMR (Figure  4n,o). 
Altogether, EnzNAs can reverse AMR and inhibit its evolution 
by eradicating biofilm matrix, promoting membrane hyperpo-
larization, and inhibiting AMR-related secretion of MRSA via 
context-dependent enzymatic activities, which restores the ther-
apeutic effect of conventional antibiotics (Figure 4p).
2.4. The Combination of EnzNAs and Antibiotics Overcomes 
AMR for Enhanced Antimicrobial Effects In Vivo

and its evolution in MRSA, we next evaluated whether EnzNAs 
can re-empower the antimicrobial effect of antibiotics against 
MRSA-associated infection in mice. Topical skin infection mice 
models were used to evaluate the therapeutic effect of EnzNAs 
combined with Amp on mature biofilm related bacterial infec-
tions. The reduced colony forming units (CFU) amount in 
wound tissues of EnzNAs plus Amp treated mice demon-
strates the excellent anti-biofilm efficacy in vivo (Figure 5a,b).

promote the wound healing, as evidenced by the shrunken

Furthermore, acute lung infection mice models were utilized

in inflammatory factors of the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid

tion model (Figure 5e–l). The significantly decreased bacterial 
burden in BALF of infected mice as treated by EnzNAs plus 
Amp indicates the significantly potentiated antibacterial effi-
cacy of Amp by EnzNAs (Figure  5e,f). Four of ten mice suc-
cumbed to acute lung infection within 5 days, and the treat-
ment with Amp, EnzNAs, or Ag+ cannot reduce the mortality 
or restore the body weight of infected mice. Strikingly, all of 
the infected mice can survive after treatment with EnzNAs 
plus Amp, with remarkable body weight recovery (Figure 5g,h). 
Moreover, the increased levels of neutrophil and inflammation 
factors (IL-1β, MIP-1, and TNF-α) in BALF of infected mice are 
remarkably reduced by the treatment of EnzNAs plus Amp 
(Figure  5i–l), indicating the effective alleviation of inflamma-
tion response caused by MRSA infection. The hematoxylin

reduced by the EnzNAs plus Amp treatment (Figure S25, Sup-
porting Information). These results clearly reveal that the acute 
lung infection can be prominently resolved by the treatment 
with EnzNAs plus Amp, demonstrating their excellent combi-
national antimicrobial effect. Notably, the β-lactamase activi-
ties are significantly reduced by the EnzNAs treatment in both 
infected skin tissue and BALF of mice, indicating the capability 
of EnzNAs to reverse AMR in vivo (Figure  5m). Additionally, 
the histopathological examination of major organs in BALB/c 
mice and C57BL6 mice demonstrates that EnzNAs are highly 
biocompatible (Figure S26, Supporting Information). The above 
results argue that EnzNA can act as a promising antibiotic 
adjuvant for the tunable enzymatic activities mediated modu-
lation of the infectious microenvironment, which overcomes 
AMR and potentiates the therapeutic efficacy of conventional 
antibiotics against various MRSA-associated infections in vivo 
(Figure 5n).
3. Conclusion
Antimicrobial-resistant infection is a growing threat to public 
health. Thus far, efforts to combat AMR are mainly focused on 
the development of novel antibiotics, whereas current antibiotic 
discovery is at a disadvantage in the arms race against AMR 
evolution, and there is nearly no antibiotic class with new thera-
peutic mechanisms that has been approved during the last few 
decades.[25] Recently, antibiotic adjuvants have been developed 
to resensitize the drug-resistant pathogens to conventional 
antibiotics by suppressing existing resistant mechanisms.[24] 
However, the therapeutic efficacy of antibiotics-adjuvant com-
bination can be significantly restricted by physical and bio-
chemical compositions across intracellular and extracellular 
infectious microenvironments that are conducive to bacterial 
resistance and evolution,[26,27] necessitating the exploration 
of next-generation adjuvants that can modulate infectious 
microenvironments.
Nanomaterials have attracted attention to exert adjuvant-
like activity for promoting the delivery of antibiotics into bac-
teria,[55–57] protecting antibiotics from being attacked by antibi-
otic inactivating enzymes, and so on.[58] Moreover, some nano-
materials have been reported to exhibit antibacterial effects 
without inducing evolution through interfering with lipid 
metabolism, peptidoglycan metabolism, and cell division pro-
cesses in bacteria.[59,60] Nevertheless, the infectious microen-
vironments not only act as physical barriers, but also continu-
ously promote antibiotic resistance by governing the growth 
and metabolic activity of bacteria.[26] In view of this, we aim to 
design an adjuvant that can modulate infectious microenviron-
ments including extracellular biofilm and intracellular ribo-
somal biogenesis to combat AMR. Different from previously 
reported works, this study focuses on the modulation of the 
microenvironment that is conducive to AMR, contributing to 
the revitalization of conventional antibiotics and inhibition of 
the AMR evolution.
Nanozymes, nanomaterial-based artificial enzymes, have 
aroused tremendous interest to mimic natural enzymes in bio-
logical processes, such as the engineered Fe–N doped graphene 
for mimicking NADPH oxidase[61] and 2D SnSe nanosheets for 
mimicking dehydrogenases.[62] Indeed, the complicated bio-
logical microenvironments place great demands for specific 
and tunable activities of nanozymes.[63] For example, the pH 
discrepancy in extracellular and intracellular infectious micro-
environments limits the catalytic activities of nanozymes.[64]

pathogens to conventional antibiotics. The EnzNA-mediated 
modulation of the infectious microenvironment effectively

the bacterial secretion of inactivating enzymes and EPS. In this 
process, we apply nanomaterials to modulate the intracellular 
microenvironment to induce structural damage of ribosomal 
proteins by thiol oxidation,[30] opening up a new approach in 
regulating global protein synthesis. Moreover, the repeated 
Amp treatment along with EnzNAs can inhibit the increase 
in MIC50 of Amp, demonstrating the suppression of the AMR 
evolution. A single injection of EnzNAs along with Amp treat-
ment unprecedentedly decreases the mortality of lethal acute 
lung infection, as well as significantly eliminates the stubborn 
biofilm in skin infection. Overall, our study demonstrates that 
EnzNA, a rationally designed silver-based nanomaterial with 
context-dependent enzymatic activities, can act as an antibiotic 
adjuvant to modulate infectious microenvironments for resen-
sitizing drug-resistant pathogens to conventional antibiotics, 
providing a paradigm shift in combating the emergence and 
evolution of AMR.
