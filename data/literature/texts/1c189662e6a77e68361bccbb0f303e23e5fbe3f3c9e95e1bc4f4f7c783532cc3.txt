Kernel Homology in Gold Nanoclusters
Shengli Zhuang+, Lingwen Liao+, Jinyun Yuan+, Chengming Wang, Yan Zhao, Nan Xia,
Zibao Gan, Wanmiao Gu, Jin Li, Haiteng Deng, Jinlong Yang,* and Zhikun Wu*
Abstract: Homology is well known in organic chemistry;
however, it has not yet been reported in nanochemistry. Herein,
we introduce the concept of kernel homology to describe the
phenomenon of metal nanoclusters sharing the same “func-
tional group” in kernels with some similar properties. To
illustrate this point, we synthesized two novel gold nano-
clusters, Au44(TBBT)26 and Au48(TBBT)28 (TBBTH = 4-tert-
butylbenzenethiol), and solved their total structures by X-ray
crystallography, which reveals that they have the same Au23 bi-
icosahedron capped with a similar bottom cap (Au6 and Au8,
respectively) in the kernels. The two novel gold nanoclusters,
together with the existing Au38(PET)24 nanocluster (PETH =
phenylethanethiol), have the same “functional group”—
Au23—in their kernels and have some similar properties (e.g.,
electrochemical properties); therefore, they are comparable to
the homologues in organic chemistry.
In organic chemistry, compounds that have the same func-
tional group but a different repeating unit (e.g., a methylene
bridge-CH2) are referred to as homologues.[1] This phenom-
ena (the appearance of homologues) is called homology, and
to the best of our knowledge, has never been reported in
nanochemistry. Metal nanoclusters[2] are an emerging type of
materials bridging metal atoms (complexes) and metal nano-
crystals[3] are generally composed of pure-metal kernels and
metal-ligand-mixed staples.[4] Because the kernels always
show high chemical and physical activity, the kernels of the
metal nanoclusters have received relatively more attention
than the staples.[5] It is known that the kernel atoms are
packed in different modes, such as fcc[6] and non-fcc[2a–c,j,7]
modes, and recently, we reported an fcc and non-fcc quasi-
dual-packing structure.[5d] Fundamental issues in these mate-
rials include but are not limited to (1) Wether there is kernel
homology in the metal nanoclusters, and (2) if so, what is the
identity of the shared functional group in the homologues?
These intriguing questions inspire our research. Fortunately,
we synthesized two novel nanoclusters, which, together with
the existing Au38Q nanoclusters (Au38(SCH2CH2Ph)24 with bi-
icosahedral Au23 kernel),[2i,7] share the same functional
group—an
Au23
bi-icosahedron—and
therefore
can
be
regarded as kernel homologues. Below, we will present our
detailed research.
Au44(TBBT)26 and Au48(TBBT)28 (TBBTH = 4-tertbutyl-
benzenethiol) were
synthesized
via the
“acid-induction
method” first introduced by our group.[2t,8] For details, see
the Supporting Information. Briefly, tetraoctylammonium
bromide (TOAB) was dissolved in THF in a three-neck flask,
and then, HAuCl4·4H2O was added into the solution. After
the solution was stirred for 15 min, TBBTH was added. When
the color of the solution changed from deep red to yellow,
a cold-water solution of NaBH4 was immediately added all at
once. Then, nitric acid and water were added. The gold
nanoclusters were allowed to grow for 5 h. Au44(TBBT)26 and
Au48(TBBT)28 nanoclusters were separated from the crude
product
by
preparative
thin-layer
chromatography
(PTLC),[2i,n,9] and were collected and thoroughly washed
with methanol.
To determine the precise composition of and molecular
information about the nanoclusters, electrospray ionization
mass spectrometry (ESI-MS) was applied.[10] Cesium acetate
(CsOAc) was used as an assistant charging reagent and was
added to the nanocluster solution to form cesium adducts for
ESI-MS analysis in cationic mode. As shown in Figure 1a, an
intense peak was observed at m/z 6615.40 corresponding to
the [Au44(TBBT)26Cs2]2+ species (calculated: 6615.14 Da,
deviation: 0.26 Da). In Figure 1c, two peaks were observed
at m/z 4828.12 and 7174.28 corresponding to the [Au48-
(TBBT)28Cs2]3+ (calculated: 4827.73 Da, deviation: 0.39 Da)
and [Au48(TBBT)28Cs2]2+ (calculated: 7174.64 Da, deviation:
0.36 Da) adducts, respectively. The two nanoclusters are
charge neutral since the charge is equal to the number of
adducted Cs+ ions. The neutrality of the nanoclusters was
further supported by the observation that no strong signals
were present in the mass spectra without the addition of

As shown in Figure S1 (Supporting Information), Au44-
(TBBT)26 contains a Au29 kernel consisting of one Au23 unit
and a Au6 bottom cap (see Figure S1a–c). Six Au2(TBBT)3,
three Au(TBBT)2, and two TBBT thiolates serve as exterior

The Au48(TBBT)28 nanocluster consists of a Au31 kernel
and a protective shell, including five Au2(TBBT)3, four
Au(TBBT)2, and one Au3(TBBT)4 staple motifs, as well as
one TBBT thiolate (Figure 2b,d,f,h). The Au31 kernel of
Au48(TBBT)28 is composed of face-fused bi-icosahedral Au23

Figure 2b,c), and one TBBT thiolate (see Figure 2h,i) adsorb
on the Au23 bi-icosahedron, two Au(TBBT)2 (see Fig-
ure 2d,e), and two Au2(TBBT)3 (see Figure 2b,c) staple
motifs bridge the Au23 unit and the Au8 bottom cap, and
one Au3(TBBT)4 staple motif is anchored to the Au8 surface.
It is known that the kernel structure affects the arrangement
of the metal-ligand motifs on its surface,[4c,6h,10j] which is also
reflected in the structures of Au44(TBBT)26 and Au48-
(TBBT)28. The kernel of Au48(TBBT)28 is only two Au
atoms larger than that of Au44(TBBT)26, and they have
structure similarity as mentioned above, which results in the
same motifs (including one dimeric Au2(TBBT)3 and two
Au(TBBT)2 monomers, see Figure S4a–c, 4 f–i). While the
slight difference in the kernel structure leads to distinct
variety in the arrangement of the metal-ligand motifs, see
Figure S4d–e, 4j–k for a comparison: the other five dimeric
Au2(TBBT)3 staples, one Au(TBBT)2 monomer and two
TBBT thiolates (Figure S4d–e) in Au44(TBBT)26 adsorbing
on the Au29 kernel are obviously different from one Au3-
(TBBT)4 staple, four dimeric Au2(TBBT)3 staples, two Au-
(TBBT)2 monomer and one TBBT thiolates (Figure S4j–k) in
Au48(TBBT)28 adsorbing on the Au31 kernel. These structure
similarity and difference between Au48(TBBT)28 and Au44-
(TBBT)26 again illustrate the kernel influence on the staple
motifs.
It is interesting to compare the kernels of Au44(TBBT)26
and Au48(TBBT)28. Both species have one identical Au23 unit
and one close bottom cap: a Au6 cap for Au44(TBBT)26 and
a Au8 cap for Au48(TBBTT)28. It is known that Au38Q
nanoclusters have the same face-fused bi-icosahedral Au23
kernel;[2i,7] therefore, the kernels of Au38Q, Au44(TBBT)26 and
Au48(TBBT)28 nanoclusters have one identical-Au23 and only
differ in their number of capping gold atoms: 0, 6, and 8,
respectively (Figure 3). If the shared Au23 unit is comparable
to the related functional group in organic chemistry, the three
nanoclusters can be regarded as kernel homologues due to
their similar kernel structures. To validate this concept, we
investigated the location of the HOMOs and LUMOs in the
three nanoclusters by DFT calculations (Figure 4). Indeed, it
was found that the HOMOs-LUMOs are majorly distributed
on the Au23 unit for every nanocluster, indicating that the Au23
unit acts as a “functional group”. The electrochemical gap

In summary, in this work, we introduced the concept of
kernel homologues to define those nanoclusters sharing the
same functional group in the kernels with some similar
properties. The importance and significance of this work lie in
the following: (1) Two novel gold nanoclusters were synthe-
sized and structurally resolved; (2) it was revealed that the
two gold nanoclusters exhibit similar HOMO–LUMO distri-
butions and electrochemical properties, which are primarily
determined by the shared Au23 group; and (3) for the first
time, the concepts of homologue, homology and functional
group were introduced into the nanochemistry to help the
understanding of structure-property relationship. Our work
provides new insight into the structures of metal nanoclusters,
has important implications in structure-property correlations
in metal nanoclusters, and is particularly expected to stim-
ulate vigorous research on homology at the nanoscale.
