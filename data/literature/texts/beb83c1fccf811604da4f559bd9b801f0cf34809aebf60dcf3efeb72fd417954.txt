Advancements in NADH Oxidase Nanozymes: Bridging
Nanotechnology and Biomedical Applications
Dan Li, Juyang He, Guanyu Ding, Yan Xin, Fan Feng, Shuaining Ma, Lu Lin,
Erkang Wang, and Jin Wang*
Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) oxidase (NOX) is key in
converting NADH to NAD+, crucial for various biochemical pathways.
However, natural NOXs are costly and unstable. NOX nanozymes oﬀer a
promising alternative with potential applications in bio-sensing, antibacterial
treatments, anti-aging, and anticancer therapies. This review provides a
comprehensive overview of the types, functional mechanisms, biomedical
applications, and future research perspectives of NOX nanozymes. It also
addresses the primary challenges and future directions in the research and
development of NOX nanozymes, underscoring the critical need for continued
investigation in this promising area. These challenges include optimizing the
catalytic eﬃciency, ensuring biocompatibility, and achieving targeted delivery
and controlled activity within biological systems. Additionally, the exploration
of novel materials and hybrid structures holds great potential for enhancing
the functional capabilities of NOX nanozymes. Future research directions can
involve integrating advanced computational modeling with experimental
techniques to better understand the underlying mechanisms and to design
more eﬀective nanozyme candidates. Collaborative eﬀorts across disciplines
such as nanotechnology, biochemistry, and medicine will be essential to
unlock the full potential of NOX nanozymes in future biomedical applications.

Moreover, NOX plays a crucial role in the electron transport
chain (ETC), particularly within mitochondrial complex I, where
it is responsible for transferring electrons to ubiquinone (UQ)
during the oxidation of NADH. UQ then aids in the transport of
electrons through complexes III and IV, which culminates in the
reduction of O2. Subsequently, electron-coupled proton translo-
cation occurs at complexes I, III, and IV, ultimately resulting
in ATP generation (Figure 1b).[6] Consequently, the conversion
between NADH and NAD+ is vital for cellular metabolism,
immune responses, and overall cellular function.[5a,7] Under-
standing the regulatory mechanisms that govern the balance
of NAD+ and NADH can lead to innovative strategies for dis-
ease diagnosis and treatment. Overall, NOXs play a pivotal
role in maintaining cellular redox balance and energy produc-
tion, underscoring their importance across various biological
systems.
Despite their essential roles, natural NOXs face challenges
including low stability and high production costs. Nanozymes,
which are nanomaterials with enzyme-mimicking properties, of-
fer several advantages over natural enzymes, such as enhanced
stability, reduced production costs, and simpliﬁed manufactur-
ing processes.[8] These nanozymes are constructed from a wide
variety of materials, including metals, metal oxides, carbon-based
substances, and hybrid composites.[9] They mimic the catalytic
functions of natural enzymes, which serve as biological catalysts
to accelerate chemical reactions within living organisms.[10]
The development of NOX nanozymes marks a signiﬁcant ad-
vancement in this research ﬁeld. Various materials, including
carbon-based nanomaterials, metal-organic frameworks (MOFs),
and other metal-integrated hybrid nanocomposites, are being ex-
plored for their potential in NADH oxidation. These nanomate-
rials are engineered to exhibit speciﬁc surface properties and ac-
tive sites that mimic the catalytic centers of natural NOXs. The

NOX nanozymes have been employed in various ﬁelds such

catalytic eﬃciency, enhanced stability, and remarkable adaptabil-

performances across a wide range of therapeutic and sensing
applications. With continued research and development, NOX
nanozymes have the potentials to revolutionize healthcare and
biotechnology, oﬀering more eﬀective, durable, and versatile so-

Despite numerous reports on the application of various
nanozymes in biomedical contexts, a comprehensive review
summarizing recent advancements in NOX nanozyme research
is still lacking. Such a review would greatly stimulate the inten-

pand research within this ﬁeld. In this article, we will introduce
the diﬀerent types of NOX nanozymes, provide an overview of
their biomedical applications, and discuss the challenges and fu-
ture perspectives associated with them in detail. This review aims
to be a valuable resource for ongoing innovation and develop-
ment in NOX-like nanozyme studies, thereby broadening their
biomedical applications.

2. Types of NOX Nanozymes
Based on the main composition, NOX nanozymes can be classi-
ﬁed into three types:
2.1. Carbon-Based NOX Nanozyme
Carbon-based nanomaterials have gained prominence in the ﬁeld
of nanozyme research due to their low toxicity and exceptional
chemical properties.[12] Initially recognized for their inherent
POD-like activity, the carbon-based nanomaterials have been ex-
plored for a variety of bio-catalytic functions, such as oxidase
(OXD), CAT and SOD.[13] An innovative approach by Kent et al.
involved the development of poly(ethylene glycol)-functionalized
hydrophilic carbon clusters (PEG-HCCs).[14] These PEG-HCCs
enhance electron transport and catalyze the oxidation of NADH,
as demonstrated in Figure 3a, indicating promising potential for
addressing mitochondrial disorders.
More recently, Wang et al. further advanced the ﬁeld by doping
carbon nanotubes (CNTs) with nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), or
sulfur (S) to construct enzyme analogs capable of emulating the
activities of NOX and cytochrome c (Cyt c) reduction (Figure 3b).
The study revealed that the O2 was reduced to either H2O or
H2O2 by CNTs, mimicking the action of natural NOXs.[15] This
progression underscores the versatility and potential of CNTs
in nanozyme technology, opening new avenues for biomedical
applications and enzyme mimic. Similarly, the deployment of
multi-walled carbon nanotubes anchored by platinum nanopar-
ticles, named as (PtNPs@MWCNTs), has been instrumental in
the development of NOX nanozyme.[16] The application of Pt-
NPs@MWCNTs in the ﬁeld of biosensing will be discussed in
the following section.

In addition to carbon clusters and nanotubes, graphene is also
utilized to synthesize NOX nanozymes. Li et al. demonstrated
that N-doped graphene variants incorporating iron (Fe), cobalt
(Co), or copper (Cu), collectively termed as MNGRs, exhibited
robust NOX-like activity (Figure 3a).[17] In particular, the FeNGR
can be internalized into the cytoplasm, enhancing the ratio of
NAD+/NADH. This enhancement facilitated glucose uptake and
adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production. The FeNGR holds sig-
niﬁcant potential for the treatment of various metabolic disor-
ders, including diabetes, fatty liver disease, and hyperlipidemia.
Single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes) represent an advanced
class of nanoscale enzyme mimics characterized by their cat-
alytic activity derived from individual atoms integrated within a
suitable support matrix.[18] The distinct architecture of SAzymes
enhances the utilization eﬃciency of metal atoms and demon-
strates superior catalytic performance, owing to the high surface-
to-volume ratio and the unique electronic properties of the
isolated atoms.[19] To harness the exceptional properties of
SAzymes, Huang et al. developed a kind of SAzyme that incor-
porates palladium (Pd), graphene oxide (GO), and polypyrrole
(PPy), termed as Pd@PPy/GO (Figure 4b). This nanozyme em-
ulates natural NOXs activity and catalyzes the oxidation of fer-
rous Cyt c to its ferric form in the presence of O2, mimick-
ing the action of Cyt c oxidase (CcO).[20] These ﬁndings pave
the way for further in-depth exploration of innovative NOX
nanozymes. In contrast, other studies have shown that materi-
als like CuxO nanorods,[21] Cu2+-GO,[22] Cu2+-modiﬁed MOFs,[23]
and oxidation-derived nanoparticles from Ti3C2,[24] mainly ex-
hibit NADH POD-like catalytic activity. This activity facilitates the
oxidation of NADH to NAD+ only in the presence of H2O2, dif-
fering from the behavior observed in the NOX nanozyme.
Fu et al. advanced the ﬁeld by developing a novel metal-
doped reduced GO (rGO) as the NOX nanozyme. They em-
ployed a layer-by-layer technique to electrostatically deposit
polyethyleneimine (PEI) and polyacrylic acid (PAA) onto rGO
sheets, forming rGO/PEI/PAA nanoﬁlms (Figure 4c). Sub-
sequently, various metal ions, including Au3+, Pt4+, Cu2+,
and Mg2+ were incorporated to create multimetallic nanopar-
ticles
(rGPn@Au@Pt@Cu@MgO).[25]
These
nanoparticles
achieved remarkable eﬃciency in catalyzing the conversion of
NADH to NAD+, indicating potential beneﬁts for anti-aging
therapies.
Furthermore, Liu et al. synthesized Cu-doped graphene quan-
tum dots (Cu-GQDs), aiming at antimicrobial applications. They
assembled the Cu,N-GQDs@Ru-NO nanoplatform, as depicted
in Figure 4d. This negatively charged nanoplatform targets in-
ﬂammatory infection sites via electrostatic interactions and ex-
hibits signiﬁcant NOX-like activity. Under light irradiation at
808 nm, this nanoplatform photo-oxidizes NADH to NAD+, dis-
rupting the redox balance within bacteria and contributing to
their eradication.[26]
2.2. MOF-Based NOX Nanozyme
MOFs are a class of fascinating crystalline materials synthesized
through the coordination of metal ions or clusters with organic
ligands, resulting in highly ordered structures that can form
1D, 2D, or 3D arrays.[27] Within the MOFs’ family, zeolitic
imidazolate frameworks (ZIFs) typically employ metal ions, like
zinc (Zn) or Co, coordinated with imidazolate ligands to form
structures that resemble the characteristics of zeolites.[28] A sig-
niﬁcant advancement in this ﬁeld has been achieved by Dong’s
group. They developed an innovative ZIF by encapsulating it
with PEI and functionalizing it with ﬂavin mononucleotide
(FMN), resulting in the creation of PEI/ZIF-FMN, as illustrated
in Figure 5a–d.[29] This innovative structure mimics the ar-
chitecture of ﬂavin-dependent enzymes, acting as a NOX-like
nanozyme. Furthermore, it has been eﬀectively paired with
dehydrogenases for sequential enzymatic reactions. Remarkably,
the PEI/ZIF-FMN nanozyme enables the electron transfer from
NADH to Cyt c, demonstrating catalytic activity analogous of Cyt
c reductase. This development not only showcases the versatility
and potential of ZIF-based materials but also opens new avenues
for their application in biochemical processes and enzyme
mimicry.
Additionally, Lei et al. developed Co2+-ZIF-67 MOFs (NMOFs),
which function as nanozymes capable of catalyzing a variety of
reactions, based on the typical POD, OXD, and CAT-like activities
(Figure 5e–g). The OXD-like activity was evidenced by the aerobic
oxidation of dopamine to aminochrome, and NADH to NAD+.[30]
However the NOX-like activity of Co2+-ZIF-67 NMOFs was found
to be lower than their NADH POD-like activity in the presence of
H2O2.
2.3. Additional Metal-Integrated Hybrid Nanocomposites
Metal-integrated hybrid nanocomposites, particularly those in-
corporating elements like Co, molybdenum (Mo), have shown
promise in replicating the activity of natural NOXs. The catalytic
eﬃciency of these nanomaterials can be attributed to their abil-
ity to facilitate surface redox reactions that closely resemble the
processes catalyzed by natural NOXs.
The Co-doped MoS2 nanocomposite (Co-MoS2) has shown
promising NOX activity, achieving its optimized performance at
pH 4.5.[31] This nanozyme eﬀectively catalyzes the oxidation of
NADH to generate O2
•−from dissolved O2, showcasing a no-
tably high recovery yield. Similarly, Liu and colleagues have de-
veloped hierarchical porous MoS2 particles (Figure 6a) exhibiting
enzyme-like activities analogous to sulﬁte oxidase (SuOx), NOX,
and SOD.[32] The catalytic mechanism for the NOX mimics in-
volves the oxidation of NADH with O2, leading to the formation
of the intermediate (O2
.−), which then converts to H2O2. These
MoS2 particles hold promising potential for applications in bio-
analytical ﬁelds.
Additionally, Wang and colleagues discovered that cadmium
sulﬁde (CdS) nanorods can catalyze the formation of O2
•−, ex-
hibiting NOX-like activity due to the inﬂuence of photo-generated
holes in the CdS nanorods (Figure 6b).[33] Furthermore, the CdS
nanorods have been shown to reduce Cyt c, demonstrating Cyt c
reductase-like activity. Given that Cyt c reductase (complex III,
C3) mediates electron transfer from CoQ to Cyt c and plays a
vital role in the cellular ETC (Figure 6c), it is essential for bet-
ter understanding various biological diseases by studying the
NAD+/NADH ratio and Cyt c status. Consequently, these CdS
nanorods hold signiﬁcant potential for therapeutic applications
related to mitochondrial disorders.

Overall, while the number of studies on NOX nanozymes
for bio-sensing applications is relatively limited compared to
the more extensively researched OXD and POD nanozymes, the
emerging data indicates that NOX nanozymes have substantial
potential as powerful biosensor tools. These nanozymes are ca-
pable of providing rapid detection, which is necessary for real-
time monitoring and urgent diagnostic needs. Moreover, their
precise detection capabilities ensure high speciﬁcity and accu-
racy, reducing the likelihood of cross-reactivity and false posi-
tives. Additionally, the reliability of NOX nanozymes in various
testing conditions suggests that they can be trusted for consis-
tent performance, making them highly suitable for both clinical
diagnostics and environmental monitoring. This combination of
speed, accuracy, and reliability positions NOX nanozymes as a
promising area of study, deserving of further research and devel-
opment to fully harness their potential in the ﬁeld of bio-sensing.
3.2. Applications in Antibacterial Therapy
Antibiotics have signiﬁcantly revolutionized the treatment and
management of bacterial infections, establishing themselves as
the cornerstone of modern antimicrobial therapy.[34] They have
been instrumental in reducing morbidity and mortality associ-
ated with bacterial diseases, making surgeries and numerous
medical procedures safer and more successful. However, the
extensive and inappropriate use of these traditional antibiotics
has precipitated a serious global health challenge: the emer-
gence and spread of multidrug-resistant (MDR) bacteria.[35] This
growing resistance undermines the eﬀectiveness of existing
antibiotics, posing a signiﬁcant threat to public health worldwide
and creating a pressing need for new antibacterial agents. These
new agents must possess mechanisms of action that are distinct
from those of current antibiotics to eﬀectively combat resis-
tant strains and ensure continued success in treating bacterial
infections.[36] Addressing this critical issue is of utmost urgency

Nanobactericides, a class of nanoparticles renowned for their
intrinsic antibacterial properties and their ability to enhance the
eﬀectiveness of existing antibiotics, are emerging as a highly
promising solution in the ﬁght against bacterial infections.[37]
These innovative nanoparticles exert their antibiotic eﬀects
through various mechanisms, such as inhibiting key enzyme
activities, disrupting DNA synthesis, inducing reactive oxygen
species (ROS), hydrolyzing glycosidic compounds, and interfer-
ing with essential energy transduction processes.[38]
The application of nanozymes in antimicrobial applications
oﬀers substantial beneﬁts, particularly for infections that are

3.3. Applications in Anti-Aging
Typically, the body maintains a delicate balance between produc-
tion of ROS and antioxidant defenses.[47] However, as individu-
als age, this equilibrium can become disrupted, resulting in ox-
idative damage to cells and tissues. This oxidative damage not
only manifests as visible signs of aging, such as wrinkles and
age spots, but also contributes to the development of various age-
related diseases.[48]
Research indicates aging is also accompanied by a decline in
the NAD+/NADH ratio, a critical factor in numerous cellular
processes. A reduced ratio can lead to a range of age-related is-
sues, including reduced energy production, impaired DNA re-
pair, and compromised regulation of oxidative stress.[49] The de-
crease in NAD+ levels, coupled with the excessive accumulation
of NADH, has been closely linked to the aging process, mani-
festing in impairments in skin regeneration and metabolic dys-
regulation, and other age-associated health problems.[50] Conse-
quently, strategies aimed at enhancing NAD+ levels and main-
taining a higher NAD+/NADH ratio are increasingly recognized
as eﬀective approaches to mitigate the impacts of aging and its
associated health challenges. These strategies may involve sup-
plementation with NAD+ precursors, lifestyle modiﬁcations, or
therapeutic interventions designed to boost NAD+ levels.[3,51] By
focusing on these approaches, it is possible to promote health-
ier aging, improve cellular function, and reduce the risk of age-
related diseases.
NOX nanozymes are of particular interest due to their capac-
ity to modulate oxidative stress and increase the NAD+/NADH
ratio. By maintaining an optimal cellular redox balance, NOX
nanozymes can eﬀectively mitigate the adverse eﬀects of ox-
idative stress, thereby protecting cells from damage and dys-
function. Huang et al. have developed a SAzyme, Pd@PPy/GO,
which functions as artiﬁcial cascade nanoreactor with both NOX
and CcO-like activities.[20] The CcO enzyme is a critical compo-
nent in complex IV in the mitochondrial ETC,[52] playing a vital
role in electron transfer for bioenergy production. Consequently,
Pd@PPy/GO has been found to enhance mitochondrial function
both in the Caenorhabditis elegans (C. elegans) and cellular models.
Furthermore, the activation of the mitochondrial unfolded pro-
tein response (UPRmt) pathway plays a key role in its anti-aging
eﬀects (Figure 11a). This research paves the way for developing
anti-aging therapies through the strategic design of simple and
eﬀective nanozymes with NOX and CcO dual-enzymatic activi-
ties.

3.4. Applications in Anticancer Therapy
In the ETC of mammalian mitochondria, Complex I plays a
pivotal role by catalyzing the oxidation of NADH and facilitating
the transfer of electrons to Complex II, as depicted in Figure 6c.
This electron transfer is crucial for the subsequent movement
of hydrogen ions (H+) from the mitochondrial matrix to the in-
termembrane space. Inhibiting the catalytic activity of Complex
I disrupts this electron transfer process, thereby obstructing
the transport of H+ ions.[54] The resultant blockage causes a de-
crease in the mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm), which
is essential for maintaining the electrochemical gradient that
drives ATP synthesis.[55] The inhibition of H+ transport and the
consequent decline in ΔΨm eﬀectively impede ATP production,
undermining the cell’s energy supply. Additionally, NADH plays
a multifaceted role within cells. As a powerful reducing agent,
NADH maintains the cellular reducing environment, indispens-
able for protecting cells from oxidative damage. It serves as the
primary scavenger of ROS and functions as an indispensable
coenzyme for various antioxidant enzymes, including GPX and
GSH-S-transferases. Therefore, a reduction in NADH levels
can compromise the cell’s antioxidant defenses, leading to
oxidative stress. Interestingly, this induced oxidative stress can
signiﬁcantly augment the eﬃcacy of therapeutic strategies,
particularly in the context of cancer treatment.[56] The dual role
of NADH, as both a critical player in cellular energy production
and a central component of the antioxidant defense system,
highlights its importance in maintaining cellular homeostasis.
By targeting NADH, it is possible to exploit its role in cellular
defense mechanisms against oxidative stress, positioning it as a
potential therapeutic target in cancer treatment.
NOX nanozymes represent an innovative and promising ap-
proach in cancer therapy. These nanozymes are capable of re-
ducing NADH levels, disrupting ΔΨm, impairing ATP synthesis,
and creating redox imbalances within cells. By harnessing these
capabilities, NOX nanozymes can induce a state of metabolic and
oxidative stress that is detrimental to cancer cells, making them
highly eﬀective candidates for cancer therapy. This innovative ap-
proach underscores the potential of NOX nanozymes to serve as
powerful tools in the ongoing battle against cancer, leveraging

3.4.1. Cancer Therapy Primarily via Metabolic Regulation
Metabolism, an intricate and interconnected network of bio-
chemical reactions, plays a pivotal role in both the production
and consumption of energy essential for the survival and func-
tion of living organisms. This dynamic system is responsible
for converting nutrients into energy, building cellular structures,
and regulating various physiological processes. In the context of
cancer, signiﬁcant alterations in metabolic pathways are com-
monly observed.[57] These metabolic changes are instrumental
in facilitating the growth and progression of tumors. Enhanced
metabolic activity allows cancer cells to support rapid cell divi-
sion, providing the necessary energy and biosynthetic materials
to sustain proliferation.[58] Furthermore, these metabolic adapta-
tions enhance the survival capabilities of cancer cells, enabling
them to thrive even under adverse conditions, such as low nu-
trient supply and hypoxia.[55] One notable metabolic characteris-
tic of cancer cells is the elevated ratio of NAD+ to NADH com-
pared to normal cells.[5a] This altered redox balance supports var-
ious cellular processes that are advantageous for tumor progres-
sion, including enhanced glycolysis and biosynthetic activities.
The increased NAD+/NADH ratio also helps cancer cells man-
age oxidative stress, further promoting their survival and growth.
Given these fundamental metabolic diﬀerences between cancer
cells and normal cells, targeting the disrupted metabolic path-
ways within tumors has emerged as a promising strategy for can-
cer treatment.[59] The ongoing exploration of metabolic interven-
tions thus holds signiﬁcant promise for advancing cancer therapy
and achieving better control over tumor growth.
Lin et. al. have developed an amorphous honeycomb-like N-
doped carbon (N/C) nanozyme characterized by NOX and CAT
activities (Figure 13a).[60] This N/C nanozyme was cleverly com-
bined with natural glucose dehydrogenase (GDH) to enhance the
regeneration of NAD+, the coenzyme of GDH, resulting in ef-
fective management of hyperglycemia through consuming glu-
cose. This integration facilitates the transfer of electrons from
glucose to NAD+, and subsequently to O2. Compared to other
cancer-starving therapies,[61] the N/C-GDH nanozyme ensures
sustainable and eﬀective glucose consumption while preventing
the generation of cytotoxic H2O2. Furthermore, the synergistic
combination of the N/C-GDH system with 5-ﬂuorouracil (5-FU)
has been shown to signiﬁcantly inhibit the proliferation of hu-
man colon cancer cells by lowering glucose levels and inducing
apoptosis. This innovative approach not only broadens the appli-
cation of nanozymes in managing blood glucose but also opens
new avenues for employing nonmetallic nanomaterials in the de-
velopment of eﬀective cancer therapies.
Furthermore, the serine/threonine kinase AKT is requisite for
various cellular processes. The AKT signaling is often excessively
active in many cancer types, leading to abnormal cell growth
and transformations.[62] The regulation of the NAD+/NADH ra-
tio is intricately linked to the AKT signaling pathway. Du and
colleagues developed a carbon-coated Co composite (C@Co)
nanozyme, which exhibited signiﬁcant NOX-like activity, as
shown in Figure 13b. The C@Co eﬃciently reduced NADH lev-
els in cancer cells, resulting in increased production of ROS and
a reduction of Δ𝜓m. Simultaneously, the elevated levels of NAD+
suppressed the AKT/GSK3𝛽signaling pathway, thereby promot-
ing apoptosis of cancer cells both in laboratory and animal stud-
ies. These results further elucidate the role of NADH in cancer
therapy, and highlight the potential of NOX nanozyme as a ther-
apeutic strategy in oncology.[56]
Studies have provided evidence that the NAD+/NADH ra-
tio, which is associated with various biochemical processes, can
be restored during the cell repair process.[63] This restoration
is vital for maintaining cellular health and function. However,
when it comes to cancer treatment, a signiﬁcant challenge arises
due to the metabolic ﬂexibility of tumor cells. The cancer cells
can adapt by utilizing various metabolic pathways to restore the
NAD+/NADH ratio, thereby sustaining their growth.[64] As a re-
sult, targeting a single metabolic pathway is often insuﬃcient for
achieving eﬀective therapeutic outcomes in cancer therapy. This
adaptability of tumor cells underscores the urgent need for inno-
vative treatment strategies. Speciﬁcally, there is a pressing need
to develop approaches that can simultaneously disrupt multiple
metabolic pathways. By doing so, it may be possible to eﬀectively
undermine the metabolic resilience of tumor cells, leading to
more successful and lasting therapeutic results. Hence, the de-
velopment of multi-targeted metabolic interventions represents
a signiﬁcant and necessary challenge in advancing cancer treat-
ment eﬃcacy.
In response to the challenge of eﬀectively targeting the
metabolic ﬂexibility of tumor cells, Ge and the colleagues pro-
posed an innovative strategy for tumor therapy that involves
the simultaneous disruption of multiple cellular metabolism
pathways.[65] This triple cellular metabolism disruption strategy
employs the use of Co-doped hollow carbon spheres (Co-HCS),
which exhibit catalytic activities akin to those of three types of
enzymes: NOX, OXD and CAT (Figure 14). The mechanism of
this approach is multifaceted. First, the NOX-mimicking catalytic
activity of Co-HCS eﬀectively disrupts the NAD+/NADH bal-
ance within tumor cells, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction
and a consequent inhibition of ATP production via OXPHOS.
This inhibition of ATP production hampers the energy supply
that tumor cells need for rapid therapy growth and survival. Sec-
ond, the design of Co-HCS incorporates a substrate-cycle mecha-
nism that enhances its catalytic eﬃciency. This mechanism helps
mitigate the issues of declining catalytic activity that can occur
due to substrate depletion, thereby ensuring sustained and ef-
fective therapeutic outcomes against tumors. Third, this strat-
egy includes the induction of 2-Deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) and 6-
aminonicotinamide (6-AN). These compounds play a vital role
by eﬀectively blocking glycolysis and the pentose phosphate path-
way (PPP) in cancer cells. By inhibiting these critical metabolic
pathways, the overall energy production and biosynthetic pro-
cesses of the cancer cells are severely compromised. The newly
developed construct system, named DSPE-Co-HCS/D/A, func-
tions as an NADH-circulating oxidation reactor and is engineered
to simultaneously disrupt multiple cellular metabolic pathways.
This design oﬀers a synergistic approach to cancer therapy. This
research presents an innovative method for enhancing the ef-
ﬁcacy of tumor treatment by coordinating the disruption of
metabolic functions, thereby opening new avenues for cancer
therapy.

3.4.3. Cancer Therapy with Combined and Synergistic Treatment
Strategies
To enhance the precision and eﬃciency of cancer treatments,
thereby maximizing therapeutic eﬀects and reducing mortality,
synergistic strategies have emerged. These combine imaging-
guided technologies, including chemotherapy, radiation ther-
apy, phototherapy, chemodynamic therapy, sonodynamic ther-
apy, immunotherapy, and other therapies, beneﬁting from their
mutually reinforcing therapeutic eﬃcacies.[72] Currently, most
reported nanozymes have limited active sites for enzyme-
mimicking catalytic reactions, and their low atom-utilization eﬃ-
ciency signiﬁcantly restricts their catalytic performance.[73] Con-
sequently, enhancing atom utilization within the catalytic cen-
ters of nanozymes is an eﬀective strategy to improve the eﬃ-
cacy of nanozyme-based catalytic therapies. With advances in
atomic nanotechnology, SAzymes featuring precisely deﬁned co-
ordination structures and optimal atom utilization have attracted
signiﬁcant interest.[74] Their superior catalytic capabilities make
SAzymes a promising avenue for cancer treatment, utilizing cat-
alytic therapy that leverages TME-triggered and integrated cas-
cade reactions.[75]
Yang et al. have developed an innovative Iridium-nitrogen 5
SAzyme (Ir-N5 SA), distinguished by its versatile enzyme-like cat-
alytic properties.[76] Within TME, the Ir-N5 SA eﬃciently gener-
ates a substantial amount of ROS through its OXD- and POD-
like catalytic activities. Additionally, it produces O2 and H2O2 via
CAT- and NOX-like functions, respectively. This enables eﬀec-
tive catalytic therapy through a substrate-cycle mechanism, as
illustrated in Figure 17. Crucially, the Ir-N5 SA disrupts the in-
tracellular NAD+/NADH cycle by mimicking activity of NOX.
This disruption is enhanced by the fatty acid synthase inhibitor
cerulenin (Cer). Consequently, the strategically engineered Ir-N5
SA/Cer nanoagent eﬀectively disrupts both redox and metabolic
balance within tumors through a series of enzyme-mimicking re-
actions. This approach not only addresses the limitations of cur-
rent nanozyme catalytic therapies but also introduces a powerful
new strategy for cancer treatment.

generate ROS storms, eﬀectively triggering pyroptosis, a form of
programmed cell death. Additionally, this SAzyme strategically
targets pyruvate metabolism, thus enhancing the immune TIME
and amplifying the extent of pyroptosis (Figure 18). This dual-
action strategy makes a substantial improvement in antitumor
immunotherapy by leveraging multiple mechanisms. These in-
clude the generation of ROS, consumption of NADH, GSH and
L-cysteine, along with the oxidation of pyruvate, and depletion
of lactic acid (LA) and ATP. Collectively, these processes not only
trigger pyroptosis but also modulate metabolism, oﬀering a com-
prehensive approach to cancer treatment.

4. Conclusions and Future Perspectives
Natural NOX, an enzyme associated to cellular metabolism, pri-
marily functions within the ETC by catalyzing the oxidation of
NADH to NAD+. The eﬃcient conversion of NADH to NAD+
sustains the cell’s metabolic functions and overall vitality. In con-
trast, NOX nanozymes, which are synthetic analogs of natural
NOXs, oﬀer several advantages, including ease of preparation,
lower production costs, and enhanced resistance to extreme con-
ditions such as high temperatures and varying pH levels. The
facility of preparation and cost-eﬀectiveness of NOX nanozymes
opens up possibilities for large-scale production and widespread
use in various sectors, including pharmaceuticals, and environ-
mental engineering. Their ability to maintain functionality under
diverse conditions also makes them attractive for use in diagnos-
tic and therapeutic tools in vivo, where consistent and stable en-
zymatic activity is critical.
Due to these properties, NOX nanozymes present signiﬁcant
potential in a wide range of applications. In the ﬁeld of bio-
sensing, NOX nanozymes can catalyze speciﬁc reactions, en-
abling the sensitive and precise detection of NADH and its as-
sociated biological molecules. The capacity of NOX nanozymes
to maintain a higher NAD+/NADH ratio also positions them
as promising candidates for anti-aging therapies. In these treat-
ments, they can enhance energy production, repair DNA dam-
age, and mitigate oxidative stress. Moreover, NOX nanozymes

nanozymes not only enhance their eﬀectiveness in bio-sensing

clinical ﬁelds. As research and development progress, the full po-
tential of NOX nanozymes is likely to be explored, oﬀering new
transformative solutions or improving current methodologies for
the diagnosis and treatment of metabolic-related diseases.
While NOX nanozymes can mimic the activity of natural
NOXs, achieving comparable eﬃciency and speciﬁcity remains
a formidable challenge. Firstly, NOX nanozymes often exhibit
lower catalytic activities and broader substrate speciﬁcities, po-
tentially leading to undesirable side reactions. Additionally, cer-
tain types of NOX nanozymes can exhibit toxic eﬀects inﬂu-
enced by their composition, size, shape, and surface modiﬁca-
tions. Safely integrating these nanozymes into biological systems
without causing adverse eﬀects poses a signiﬁcant challenge,
particularly in anti-aging applications. Secondly, although NOX
nanozymes are generally more stable than natural NOX, main-
taining their long-term stability under physiological conditions
remains challenging in certain environments. Over time, they
may degrade or aggregate, thereby diminishing their enzymatic
activity. Thirdly, scaling up the production of NOX nanozymes
while maintaining consistent activity and quality poses another
challenge. It is imperative to develop large-scale synthesis meth-
ods that are cost-eﬀective and environmentally friendly. Lastly,
integrating NOX nanozymes into complex biological systems re-
quires an in-depth understanding of these systems and careful
consideration of the interactions between the NOX nanozymes
and native biomolecules.
Future perspectives in the development of NOX nanozymes
should emphasize overcoming existing challenges through pio-
neering research and inventive strategies. This involves explor-
ing biocompatible, and environmentally stable nanomaterials,
as well as reﬁning production processes for eﬃcient, large-scale
synthesis. The development of intelligent NOX nanozymes that
respond to environmental stimuli, such as pH changes, tempera-
ture shifts, or the presence of speciﬁc biomolecules, could signiﬁ-
cantly enhance their functionality in dynamic biological settings.
Furthermore, continued research into the interactions between
NOX nanozymes and biological systems is optimum to reduce
cytotoxic eﬀects and maximize their therapeutic potential.
Given the inherent complexity of biological environments, a
key focus should be on the systematic study of NOX nanozymes’
behavior in vivo. This involves understanding their distribution,
metabolism, and clearance from the body, as well as their interac-
tion with immune systems. Advancements in imaging and track-
ing technologies could provide critical insights, allowing for real-
time monitoring of NOX nanozyme activity within living organ-
isms. To address the potential cytotoxicity, research should prior-
itize the design of NOX nanozymes with tailored surface modiﬁ-
cations that enhance biocompatibility. Strategies such as coating
NOX nanozymes with biopolymers or functionalizing their sur-
faces with targeting ligands could help reduce undesired interac-
tions with healthy tissues while ensuring that they reach speciﬁc
pathological sites more eﬀectively.
Furthermore, interdisciplinary collaboration will be indispens-
able, integrating knowledge from biochemistry, pharmacology,
computer modeling, materials science, and engineering to ex-
pand the capabilities of NOX nanozymes. By fostering a cooper-
ative research environment, it is possible to accelerate the devel-
opment of multifunctional nanozymes that can perform complex
catalytic activities tailored to speciﬁc biomedical applications.
Ultimately, despite signiﬁcant challenges, the potential of
NOX nanozymes across various ﬁelds makes them a promis-
ing focus for ongoing research and development. The aim of the
studies is to fully harness the capabilities of NOX nanozymes,
transforming promising concepts into practical solutions that ef-
fectively address critical issues in biomedical applications. This
may include innovative therapies for inﬂammatory and aging-
associated diseases, precise cancer treatments, and advanced di-
agnostic tools. As the ﬁeld progresses, proactive and strategic col-
laboration among researchers will be essential in translating the
scientiﬁc promise of NOX nanozymes into tangible beneﬁts for
human health and beyond.
