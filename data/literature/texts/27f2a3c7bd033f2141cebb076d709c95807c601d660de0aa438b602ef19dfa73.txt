ABSTRACT: Oxidative stress and neuroinflammation in the aging
brain are correlated with the development of neurodegenerative
diseases, such as Alzheimer’s disease (AD). The blood−brain barrier
(BBB) poses a significant challenge to the effective delivery of
therapeutics for AD. Prior research has demonstrated that menthol
(Men) can augment the permeability of the BBB. Consequently, in the
current study, we modified Men on the surface of liposomes to
construct menthol-modified quercetin liposomes (Men-Qu-Lips),
designed to cross the BBB and enhance quercetin (Qu) concentration
in the brain for improved therapeutic efficacy. The experimental
findings indicate that Men-Qu-Lips exhibited good encapsulation
efficiency and stability, successfully crossed the BBB, improved
oxidative stress and neuroinflammation in the brains of aged mice,
protected neurons, and enhanced their learning and memory abilities.
KEYWORDS: quercetin, menthol, blood−brain barrier, aging, Alzheimer’s disease
1. INTRODUCTION
The brain, as the most complex organ in the human body,
plays a pivotal role in the formation of memory and cognitive
functions. As the brain ages, there is a reduction in size and
weight, resulting in a decline in neuronal count and the
emergence of symptoms akin to Alzheimer’s disease (AD),
including impairments in memory, cognition, and motor
function.1,2 The annual increase in the number of elderly
patients with AD is primarily attributed to population aging.3
The projections indicate that the number of AD cases is
projected to double by 2050,4 while the annual mortality rate
continues to rise. This trend poses a significant burden on both
society and families. Identifying approaches to slow cognitive
decline, improve AD symptoms, and alleviate familial and
societal burdens is crucial.
The disruption in the balance between oxidants and
antioxidants in the brain has been demonstrated to initiate
an oxidative stress response, which is closely associated with
aging and is considered a significant etiological factor in AD.5
Reactive oxygen species (ROS) are indispensable byproducts
necessary for maintaining physiological functions; however,
excessive levels can induce oxidative stress, leading to impaired
mitochondrial function and central nervous system dysfunc-
tion.6 This ultimately results in neuronal damage within the
brain and a decline in learning and memory capacity.7,8 The
inflammatory response is also associated with the activation of
microglia in the aging brain, resulting in a sustained release of
pro-inflammatory factors.9 Astrocytes respond to the inflam-
matory signals produced by microglia and secrete neurotoxic
factors that exacerbate neurodegeneration.10 Brain-derived
neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is essential for learning and
memory, with its level alterations linked to cognitive disorders.
BDNF promotes neural growth and regulates synaptic
plasticity.11 Brain injury affects BDNF expression, with
increased pro-inflammatory cytokines leading to decreased
BDNF levels.12 Thus, reversing oxidative stress and reducing
neuroinflammation are potential therapeutic strategies for
attenuating brain aging and improving cognitive function.
Quercetin (Qu), a flavonoid commonly present in tradi-
tional Chinese herbs as well as everyday fruits and vegetables
like apples, bananas, onions, and leeks, exhibits promising
potential for various applications due to its wide availability in
various foods.13 The antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and
neuroprotective properties of Qu have prompted extensive
research into its therapeutic efficacy in the treatment of

high drug loading capacity, and low toxicity. Chief among
these is their capacity for surface modification, which enhances
the drug delivery capabilities.21 The BBB is a critical protective
mechanism in the central nervous system (CNS),22 composed

cross the BBB. We evaluated learning and memory in mice, as
well as the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuroprotective
effects of Men-Qu-Lips in an aging model. The treatment

2. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
2.1. Characterization of the Liposomes. The particle
size, polydispersity index (PDI), ζ-potential, and encapsulation
efficiency of various liposomes were primarily determined, with
the results presented in Table 1. Men-Qu-Lips exhibited a high
encapsulation efficiency of (91.71 ± 0.17)%, with a particle
size of approximately 97 nm and a ζ-potential close to −2.4
mV. The distribution of the particle size and ζ-potential for
Men-Qu-Lips is illustrated in Figure 2D. Transmission electron
microscopy (TEM) analysis depicted the shape and structure
of Men-Qu-Lips, as shown in Figure 2A, indicating that Men-
Qu-Lips possess an elliptical shape with a uniform distribution
of particle sizes.
To evaluate the stability of the liposomes, different types of
liposomes were stored at 4 °C for a duration of 20 days. The
encapsulation rate and leakage rate were measured on days 0,
10, 15, and 20, as depicted in Table 2. The encapsulation rate
of the Men-Qu-Lips decreased from (91.37 ± 1.02)% to
(84.34 ± 2.14)% but was still above 80%. The particle size and
PDI statistics of Men-Qu-Lips over a 20-day period are
presented in Figure 2B,C, demonstrating that the variations in
liposome particle size and PDI also fall within an acceptable
range. These results indicate that Men-Qu-Lips exhibit
favorable stability over a span of 20 days.
2.2. In Vitro Cellular Uptake. The immortalized mouse
brain endothelial cells (bEnd.3 cells) were utilized as uptake
cells in this study. To visualize the cellular uptake process, we
employed coumarin (Cou) instead of Qu as a fluorescent
probe and analyzed it using fluorescence microscopy and flow
cytometry.29
The microscopy results demonstrated the uptake of bEnd.3
cells at different time points (1, 2, and 4 h) and various
concentrations of Cou (1.5, 3, and 6 μM) (Figure 3A,C). The
fluorescence intensities were analyzed using semiquantitative
methods (Figure 3B,D). The fluorescence absorption intensity
of bEnd.3 was subsequently quantified using flow cytometry, as
shown in Figure 3E−H. These results are consistent with the
microscopic observations, indicating that the fluorescence
intensity increased gradually over time and with increasing
drug concentration. Compared to other groups, the Men-Cou-
Lips group exhibited a significantly higher fluorescence
intensity.
2.3. In Vitro BBB Transport. The previous studies have
demonstrated that the in vitro BBB model of bEnd.3/N2a
fulfills the requirements for establishing an in vitro BBB
model.30 Figure 4A,B presents fluorescence microscope images
of N2a cells and corresponding statistical results of relative
fluorescence intensity. Figure 4C,D displays the quantitative
flow cytometry analysis of relative fluorescence intensity in
N2a cells. Men-Cou-Lips showed greater fluorescence intensity
than other liposome formulations, as demonstrated by the
results. Additionally, BBB permeability experiments indicated
that Men-Qu-Lips facilitated a significantly higher Qu passage
across the BBB compared to Qu-Lips, as shown in Figure 4E.
2.4. Imaging of Liposomes in Aging Mice. In order to
investigate brain targeting in various liposome formulations,
mice were intravenously injected with DiR fluorescent dye
instead of Qu, and real-time in vivo fluorescence was recorded
at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h. The correlation results are presented in
Figure 5. It is evident that Men-DiR-Lips exhibited the highest
fluorescence enrichment, followed by DiR-Lips and free DiR.
Over time, the areas of fluorescence enrichment gradually
decreased in the brain. At 12 h, the fluorescence areas from
free DiR disappeared completely while those from DiR-Lips
became almost invisible; however, the fluorescence areas from
Men-DiR-Lips reduced but remained clearly visible. The
results demonstrate the exceptional brain-targeting capability
of Men-modified liposomes, which facilitate enhanced drug
concentration in the brain.

and memory deficits in the model group; Free Qu and Qu-Lips
exhibited modest improvements in learning and memory
across trials, while Men-Qu-Lips demonstrated the most
significant enhancement (Figure 6C). Furthermore, analysis
of data regarding time spent by mice in the target quadrant and
number of platform crossings also indicated that Free Qu and

2.6. Men-Qu-Lips Can Delay Brain Aging by Reducing
Inflammatory Damage and Oxidative Stress. Microglia
and astrocytes are implicated in the process of neuro-
inflammation. Under conditions of disease, inflammation, or
injury, activation of microglia not only causes synaptic
dysfunction and neuronal death but also triggers the secretion
of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-1beta (IL-
1β) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α).33,34 The
immunohistochemical staining results of microglia marker
Iba-1 and astrocyte marker GFAP are presented in Figure 7A−
B. It is evident from the data that the abundance of Iba-1 and
GFAP in both the hippocampal DG and CA3 was significantly
higher in the model group, whereas their levels were reduced
upon treatment with Men-Qu-Lips. Subsequently, the pro-
inflammatory factors IL-1α, IL-1β, and TNF-α were
quantitatively assessed, and the results are depicted in Figure
7C−E. It is evident that the model group exhibits a heightened
level of inflammation, which subsequently decreases in the
brain following treatment with Men-Qu-Lips.

2.8. In Vivo Safety Evaluation. The safety of the various
formulations in major organs was assessed through histological
examination with HE staining. As shown in Figure 10, no
significant signs of organ damage were observed in mice
treated with the formulations. Therefore, the evidence suggests
that Men-Qu-Lips demonstrates a certain level of safety.
2.9. Discussion. The prevalence of AD increases with age,
making it a social epidemic disease characterized by slow onset
and complex pathogenesis. Unfortunately, there is currently no
effective treatment available for this neurodegenerative
condition.42 In the context of our aging society, finding ways
to alleviate or even treat AD becomes crucial in order to
address the burden it places on global healthcare systems,
society, and individual families. Oxidative stress has been
demonstrated to be an early contributing factor in the
pathogenesis of AD. With age, it leads to an imbalance in
oxidation−reduction reactions within the brain and the
subsequent accumulation of ROS, resulting in neuronal
damage within the brain.43 Neuroinflammation plays a pivotal
role in the development of AD. Following brain injury, there is
a disruption in the equilibrium between pro-inflammatory and
anti-inflammatory factors, leading to a cascade of pro-
inflammatory mediators that further contribute to brain
damage.44

D-gal is an aldehyde sugar, which, under the catalysis of
galactose oxidase at high concentrations, can generate ROS,
leading to oxidative stress and inflammation.45 Numerous
studies have demonstrated that aging models established with
D-gal can significantly reduce the lifespan of animals, increase
aging markers such as aging-associated β-galactosidase, and
cause neurodegenerative diseases like AD.46 Due to its short
modeling time, simple application method, and high animal
survival rate, it has become a popular research tool in the field
of aging.47
In this study, we chose Qu as the therapeutic drug due to its
potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.48How-
However, considering Qu’s poor water solubility and
susceptibility to metabolism in vivo,49 liposomes were chosen
as an effective delivery system. To enhance drug permeability
across the BBB, Men was chosen as a modifying ligand that can
downregulate the expression of P-glycoprotein (P-gp) and
tight junction proteins, such as Zonula occludens 1 (ZO-1).
This modification promotes drug transport across the BBB and
improves targeting.50
During the investigation into the characterization of
liposomes, the encapsulation efficiency of Men-Qu-Lips was
approximately 91%; they possessed a small particle size and an
overall negative charge and demonstrated good stability at 4
°C. Over the 20-day period, the encapsulation rate slightly
decreased and the leakage rate correspondingly increased;
however, both stayed within acceptable limits. The presence of
cholesterol’s rigid structure in liposomes effectively regulates
membrane permeability and enhances stability.51 Furthermore,
the incorporation of DSPE-PEG2000 also enhances the stability
of liposomes. The hydrolysis of phospholipids under specific
temperature and pH conditions leads to the accumulation of
products, disrupting the structural integrity of the bilayer
membrane, and potentially causes an increase in leakage
rates.52 The fluorescence uptake and trans-BBB experiments
showed that Men-modified liposomes had a higher fluo-
rescence intensity than unmodified ones, suggesting that Men
modification enhances BBB targeting and drug delivery.
Additionally, these findings were further validated by BBB
permeability results, showing that Men-modified liposomes
approximately doubled the drug permeation rate compared to
unmodified liposomes over the same time period. This
suggests that Men modification significantly enhances the
ability of drugs to cross the BBB, possibly due to its capacity to
reduce tight junction protein expression in the BBB. In the
flow cytometry results, it can be seen that the blank group also
produces a weak fluorescence signal. This is because, in the
experiment, we are using the blank as a control and adjusting
for scattered light, setting its signal value as close to zero as
possible, to accurately observe the fluorescence signal values
produced by the Cou-Lips and Men-Cou-Lips groups. In vivo
imaging experiments showed that Men-modified liposomes
had prolonged retention in the mouse brain, which can be
attributed to the ability of DSPE-PEG2000 to protect liposomes
from reticuloendothelial system uptake, thereby extending
their circulation time in vivo.53

the MWM and nest-building experiments. Statistical analysis
revealed significant cognitive impairment and reduced learning
and memory capacities in aged mice. Men-Qu-Lips offered a

3. MATERIALS AND METHODS
3.1. Materials and Reagents. Cholesterol was obtained from
Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). Egg
phosphatidylcholine (EPC) was obtained from NOF Corporation.
(Japan). Qu was obtained from Pufei De Biotech Co., Ltd. (purity =
98.96%, Chengdu, China). DSPE-PEG2000-Menthol was obtained
from Ruixi Biological Technology Co., Ltd. (Xi’an, China). 4′,6-
Diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) was obtained from Beijing
Biyuntian Technology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). D-Galactose (D-
gal)(purity ≥98.96%) was obtained from Dalian Meilun Biotechnol-
ogy Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). ELISA kits were purchased from
Shanghai Sinovac Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Oxidative
stress-related kits, including SOD and MDA, were purchased from
Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute (Nanjing, China). β-
galactosidase activity assay kits were purchased from Beijing Solarbio
Biotech Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China). The HE kit was purchased from
Dalian Meilun Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Dalian, China). BDNF,
NeuN, and GFAP antibodies were ordered from Boster Biological
Technology Co., Ltd. (Wuhan, China). Iba-1 antibodies were ordered
from Wako Pure Chemical Industries, Ltd. (Japan). SYP antibodies
were ordered from Shenyang Wanclass Biotechnology Co., Ltd.
(Shenyang, China). Horseradish peroxidase (HRP) biotin-labeled
secondary antibody was purchased from Biosynthesis Biological Co.,
Ltd. (Beijing, China). All other reagents used were of analytical grade.
3.2. Cells and Animals. Mouse brain microvascular endothelial
cells (bEnd.3) and mouse neuroma mother cells (N2a) were obtained
from iCell Bioscience Inc. (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in
DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1%
penicillin−streptomycin solution (100 U/mL penicillin and 100 μg/
mL streptomycin) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 in
air. These reagents were purchased from GIBCO (Billings, MT).
Yunnan Kunming male mice of SPF grade, 8 weeks old, were
purchased from Liaoning Changsheng Biotechnology Co., Ltd.
(Liaoning, China).
3.3. Preparation of Liposomes. The Men-Qu-Lips were
prepared using the thin-film hydration method. Briefly, a mixture of
110 mg EPC, 30 mg cholesterol, 10 mg DSPE-PEG2000, 10 mg DSPE-
PEG2000-Men, and 5 mg Qu was dissolved in 5 mL of methanol. The
solution was then placed in a round-bottomed flask and subjected to
rotary evaporation at 40 °C to remove the organic solvents.
Subsequently, the resulting thin film was hydrated with 5 mL of
PBS and sonicated at an ultrasonic power of 200 W using a JY92-IIN
sonicator (Xinzhi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Ningbo, China). The same
procedure was followed for preparing blank liposomes (Blank-Lips)
and quercetin liposomes (Qu-Lips). For in vivo and in vitro targeting
studies, coumarin or DiR were used as fluorescent probes instead of
drugs.
3.4. Characterization of Liposomes. The particle size, PDI, and
ζ-potential were measured using a Nano Series Zetasizer Nano ZS
(Anton Paar Instruments Ltd., Graz, Austria). The encapsulation
efficiency of Qu was determined by high-performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC) (Dalian Yili Analytical Instrument Co.,
Ltd., Dalian, China). TEM was employed to observe the morphology
of Men-Qu-Lips using a Tecnai G220ST (FEI Company, Tokyo,
Japan).
3.5. Cellular Uptake. Flow cytometry and fluorescence
microscopy were used to evaluate drug uptake by cells. Cou, used
as a fluorescent probe instead of Qu, allowed for the observation of
drug uptake by cells. bEnd.3 cells (5 × 106 cells) were cultured in 6-
well plates with Blank-Lips, Cou-Lips, and Men-Cou-Lips and treated
for different times (1, 2, and 4 h) and different concentrations of Cou
(1.5, 3, and 6 μM). A blank liposome group was used as a control
group. At the end of the culture, the culture medium was removed,
the cells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde,
and the nuclei were stained with DAPI. Finally, the samples were
imaged with a fluorescence microscope (Nikon Eclipse E800, Nikon,
Tokyo). The assay was then performed using a flow cytometer (BD
Biosciences, Franklin Lakes, NJ); the medium was removed, rinsed
with PBS, and centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 5 min. Cells were
resuspended in 400 μL of PBS, and the fluorescence intensity of Cou
in the samples was analyzed by flow cytometry.
3.6. Transport of Liposomes across the BBB Model. The in
vitro BBB model was established by coculturing N2a and bEnd.3 cells.
Specifically, N2a cells (2 × 104 cells) were added to the lower
chamber of a 24-well plate (Corning, NY), while bEnd.3 cells (8 ×
104 cells) were seeded onto the membrane of a separate transwell
insert for the upper chamber of a 24-well plate. The medium was
refreshed every 48 h. After a period of 7 days, the integrity of the BBB
was assessed based on previous experiments. Subsequently, Blank-
Lips, Cou-Lips, and Men-Cou-Lips were introduced into the upper
chamber for further analysis. Following a 4 h incubation, fluorescence
microscopy was employed to record the cellular uptake, and flow
cytometry was utilized to analyze the fluorescence intensity of these
cells. After modeling the BBB, permeability tests were performed. Qu-
Lips with Men-Qu-Lips were added to the top chamber of the BBB
with a Qu content of 30 μg, and PBS solution was used as the delivery
medium. At 1, 2, and 4 h of treatment, a 200 μL sample was taken
from the basolateral compartment and immediately replaced with 200
μL of PBS. The drug content in the basolateral compartment was
determined by HPLC.64
3.7. Establishment and Administration of the D-gal Mice
Model. The 30 mice were randomly divided into five groups, each
consisting of six mice: Blank control, Model, Qu, Qu-Lips, and Men-
Qu-Lips. Following a 4-day acclimation period, the model, Qu, Qu-
Lips, and Men-Qu-Lips groups received subcutaneous injections of D-
gal solution (200 mg/kg) for 12 weeks. Meanwhile, the blank control
group was injected with an equivalent volume of normal saline. After
12 weeks, treatment with Qu, Qu-Lips, and Men-Qu-Lips (10 mg/kg)
commenced, along with continued subcutaneous injections of D-gal.
All procedures were performed according to the guidelines of the
Liaoning University of Traditional Chinese Medicine Institutional
Animal Care and Use Committee, and this research was approved by

3.8. In Vivo Imaging of Aging Mice. The mice were injected
with free DiR, DiR-Lips, and Men-DiR-Lips (2 μg DiR per mouse) via
the tail vein. Subsequently, anesthesia was induced by intraperitoneal
administration of sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg), and images were
acquired using an in vivo fluorescence imaging system, the IVScope
8200 (Clinx Science Instruments Co., Ltd.), at 0, 6, 12, and 24 h.65
3.9. Behavioral Assessment. The MWM test was conducted to
evaluate the learning and memory capabilities of the mice. During the
MWM test, the mice were given 60 s to locate the platform. If they
failed to find it, they were guided to stay on the platform for 10 s.
Following 5 days of training, the platform was removed, and the mice
were placed in any quadrant for 60 s. The number of times they
crossed into the target quadrant was recorded and analyzed using an
automated tracking system (Beijing Zhongshi Di Chuang Technology
Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The nesting experiments were conducted
with each mouse individually housed in a sterile cage and provided
with five 5 cm × 5 cm pieces of cotton. Photographs were then
captured at various time points to evaluate the nesting score of each
mouse based on established research protocols.
3.10. In Vivo Pharmacodynamic Assessment. First, the levels
of SOD and MDA were quantified using oxidation assay kits. The
extent of aging in mice was evaluated using β-galactosidase assay kits.
The expression of IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β inflammatory factors was
measured using ELISA kits. Subsequently, brain nerve damage was
assessed through HE staining and Nissl staining; NeuN immuno-
fluorescence staining was employed; and immunohistochemical
staining was primarily used to observe the expression of inflammatory
markers Iba-1 and GFAP, along with neuroplasticity markers BDNF
and SYP, in the brain.
3.11. Statistical Analysis. The data were analyzed by GraphPad
Prism 9.0 software and presented as a mean ± standard deviation.
Differences between groups were assessed by a one-way analysis of
variance (ANOVA). Post hoc multiple comparisons were performed
with the Student−Newman−Keuls test. Escape latency data were
subjected to a repeated-measures two-way ANOVA followed by
Bonferroni’s post hoc test. P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically
significant.
