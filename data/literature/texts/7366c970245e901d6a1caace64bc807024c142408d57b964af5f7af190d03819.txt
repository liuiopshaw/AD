Synergistic Antimicrobial Titanium Carbide (MXene)
Conjugated with Gold Nanoclusters
Kaiyuan Zheng, Shuo Li, Lin Jing, Po-Yen Chen,* and Jianping Xie*
Bacterial resistance toward antibiotics is a world-wide problem, and one
potential solution to ﬁght against the resistance is to develop
multi-mechanism antimicrobial agents to achieve synergistic performance.
Titanium carbide (MXene) is an emerging 2D nanomaterial with antimicrobial
ability to physically damage bacterial membrane and chemically induce
oxidative stress, and it can be further conjugated with nanomaterials to
improve its antibacterial performance. Herein, a synergistic antimicrobial
agent is developed through conjugation of the ultra-small gold nanoclusters
(AuNCs) on MXene nanosheets. The conjugated AuNCs are eﬀectively
delivered into bacteria after bacterial membrane damage caused by MXene,
generating localized reactive oxygen species (ROS) of high concentration to
eﬀectively oxidize bacterial membrane lipid for enhanced membrane broken,
as well as bacterial DNA for violent fragmentation. Thus, the synergistic
physical (via MXene) and chemical (via MXene and AuNCs) antimicrobial
mechanisms lead to eventual bacterial death of both Gram-positive and
Gram-negative bacteria, with low IC50 values of 11.7 µg mL−1 of MXene and
0.04 µm of AuNCs. Moreover, the crumpled MXene-AuNCs structure is
constructed to inhibit bioﬁlm formation, which hold synergistic antibacterial
ability of MXene-AuNCs conjugation, hydrophobic surface to prevent bacterial
attachment, and large surface area containing higher density of bactericides.
Bacterial resistance towards antibiotics leads to serious clini-
cal infection, which has been one of the worrisome world-wide
threats for years. Therefore, new types of antimicrobial agents
like antimicrobial peptides and metal based bactericides have
been developed recently to combat bacterial infection.[1] How-
ever, the majority of them only hold single antibacterial mode,
resulting in bacterial resistance after generations of evolution.[2]
Thus, a possible solution is to develop antimicrobial agents with
dual bacterial killing modes (physical interruption and chemi-
cal disruption), which could be achieved through combination of
two bactericides for synergistic antimicrobial eﬀects.[3]2D nano-
materials are capable to kill bacteria through physical inserting
into bacterial membrane for bacterial contents leaking, as well

To further improve the antimicrobial performance of MXene
nanosheets, another antimicrobial agent can be introduced to
achieve synergistic eﬀects. We choose the emerging metal-based
antimicrobial agents, gold nanoclusters (AuNCs), as the second
component to conjugate on the surface of MXene nanosheets.
AuNCs are the ultra-small gold nanoparticles with core sizes
less than 2 nm, which hold discrete electronic states as well
as characteristic geometric structures, leading to molecule-like
properties, such as well-deﬁned molecular formulae and struc-
tures, quantized charging, highest occupied molecular orbital–
lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (HOMO-LUMO) transi-
tion, molecular magnetism, molecular chirality, and strong
luminescence.[9] Moreover, the ultra-small AuNCs are biocom-
patible, tumor targeting, and highly renal clearable, making them
as ideal probes in various theranostic ﬁelds that include can-
cer therapy, imaging-guided therapy, drug delivery, bioimaging,
etc.[10] Recently, we found that the ultra-small AuNCs could be
internalized into bacteria to induce ROS accumulation, disturb-
ing the normal metabolism of bacteria for bacterial death.[11]
Thus, the antimicrobial ultra-small AuNCs would be the proper

also expected to be the promising conjugation guests for MXene

combination of physical and chemical mechanisms. Besides, this
is the ﬁrst report to conjugate the ultra-small metal NCs instead
of the bigger sized metal nanoparticles on the surface of MXene
nanosheets.
Beyond antibiotic resistance, stubborn bioﬁlm formation is an-
other nerve-wracking problem for decades. Bioﬁlms developed
on the surface of medical devices and medical implants have
been a serious clinical threat, which are strongly resistant to-
wards commonly used antimicrobial agents, leading to persistent
infection, and the chronic polymer-associated infection.[14] The
promising technologies to target microbial bioﬁlm include sur-
face modiﬁcation to inhibit bacterial adhesion, using nanoparti-
cles to target bioﬁlm matrix, and smart release of bioactive agents
by bacterial and external triggers.[15] Among these methods, one
eﬀective strategy is to establish the crumpled surface to pre-
vent bacterial attachment for following bioﬁlm formation. Crum-
pling of 2D nanomaterials can be easily achieved through various
ways, providing large surface area and porous structure,[16] to ex-
hibit exceptional electronical, mechanical, and chemical proper-
ties for promising applications in energy storage,[17] stretchable
electronics,[18] molecular barriers,[19] catalysis,[20] and sensing.[20]
The crumpled surface is also hydrophobic to prevent the adhe-
sion of bacteria, leading to the inhibition of bioﬁlm formation.[21]
Thus, we will use the MXene nanosheets based crumpled surface
to defend against the bioﬁlm formation.
Herein, we ﬁrst conjugated the ultra-small AuNCs onto the
surface of MXene nanosheets to achieve synergistic antimicro-
bial ability. The sharp MXene nanosheets could physically dam-
age the bacterial membrane, and then the conjugated AuNCs
would be better internalized inside bacteria to generate localized
ROS for bacterial membrane and DNA oxidation, leading to even-
tual bacterial death of both Gram-positive and Gram-negative
bacteria. Moreover, we also achieved bioﬁlm formation inhibi-
tion through constructing of crumpled MXene-AuNCs struc-
tures, which possess synergistic antibacterial ability of MXene-
AuNCs conjugation, hydrophobic surface to prevent bacterial at-
tachment, and larger surface area contained higher density of
bactericides with improved antimicrobial eﬃcacy. This syner-
gistic antimicrobial MXene-AuNCs conjugation with dual-mode
(chemical and physical) antibacterial mechanisms is a promis-
ing candidate with enhanced bacterial killing eﬃcacy, and it is
feasible to be constructed as crumpled surface to prevent bioﬁlm
inhibition.
We ﬁrst conjugated AuNCs onto the surface of MXene
nanosheets to fully use the advantages from both materi-
als to achieve synergistic antimicrobial eﬀects. The MXene
nanosheets we used are the most studied titanium carbide
Ti3C2Tx nanosheets (T represents the surface functional groups,
such as –O, –OH, or –F), and their size is ≈0.9 µm as shown in
the representative transmission electron microscopy (TEM) im-
age (Figure 1a, ii) and the size distribution result estimated from
TEM images (Figure 1b).[22] As for the AuNCs, they are ultra-
small nanoparticles with core sizes less than 2 nm (Figure 1a,i,b).
The AuNCs were synthesized through well-controlled reduction
of Au(I)-MBA complexes (MBA = p-mercaptobenzoic acid) by
mild reducing agent carbon monoxide (CO) under alkaline con-
dition, resulting in highly monodispersed Au25(MBA)18 NCs.[23]
The molecular formula of the as-synthesized AuNCs was charac-
terized by electrospray ionization mass spectra (ESI-MS) at atom-
ically precise level (see detailed analysis as Figure S1, Support-
ing Information). The ﬁnal product only contained one single
species, Au25(MBA)18, suggesting the high purity. The structure
of Au25 NCs has already been reported, which contains a icosahe-
dral kernel with 13 Au(0) atoms, protected by surface motif of 12
Au(I) and 18 MBA ligands (see inset of Figure 1a,i).[24] Therefore,
the only functional group surrounding the surface of AuNCs is
the carboxyl groups (–COOH) from MBA ligand, and we will use
this functional group to conjugate the AuNCs onto the surface of
MXene.

As reported, and similar to other types of 2D nanomateri-
als, with the thickness of ≈1–2 nm, [25] the working mecha-
nism of MXene nanosheets is mainly through the direct in-
teraction with bacteria by their sharp edges, which are able to
cause physical damage towards bacterial membrane, leading to
bacterial content releasing for bacterial killing.[8a,b] This is also
proved by the SYTOX green stain (Figure 2a), which is cell im-
permeable and only can penetrate into bacteria with compro-
mised bacterial membrane. After conjugation of AuNCs onto
MXene nanosheets, the intensity of SYTOX green dye inside
bacterial cells was obviously increased compared to MXene-only
treated group (Figure S6, Supporting Information), indicating
the stronger bacterial membrane damage eﬀects caused by the
MXene-AuNCs conjugation. Thus, beside the physical destroy
towards bacterial membrane caused by MXene nanosheets, the
conjugated AuNCs also powerfully contributed towards the bac-
terial membrane broken.
The antimicrobial mechanism of AuNCs is through the gener-
ation of ROS inside bacteria for bacterial killing, which would
not contribute to the physical interruption of the bacterial
membrane.[11] Besides, the MXene nanosheets can also induce
oxidative stress in bacteria.[8a] Therefore, we further tested the
ROS generation ability of MXene-AuNCs conjugation. As shown
in Figure 2b, MXene only induced ≈1.8-fold of ROS genera-
tion, while AuNCs at such low concentration barely induced ROS
generation (≈1.2-fold). However, the MXene-AuNCs conjugation
reached ROS generation level to ≈3.8-fold. Such conspicuous im-
provement from ≈1.8-fold to ≈3.8-fold could be attributed by
the AuNCs conjugated on the surface of MXene nanosheets.
Compared to the free AuNCs, the MXene nanosheets worked
as vehicles to load these AuNCs on the surface. After the sharp
MXene nanosheets piercing the bacterial membrane, the conju-
gated AuNCs on their surface could be much better internalized
into bacteria than free ones (Figure S7, Supporting Information).
Therefore, the abundant AuNCs internalized into bacteria induce
more ROS generation to kill bacteria.[13] Furthermore, the AuNCs
conjugated on the surface of MXene nanosheets are localized to-
gether, so they could unitedly work to generate localized ROS of
high concentration, which serves as ROS reservoir that is hard
to be consumed quickly.[3b] Thus, the ROS of localized high con-
centration could work eﬀectively for further damage.
In addition, the lipid peroxidation test was conducted to ver-
ify the damage towards bacterial membrane caused by the ROS
generated. Lipid peroxidation refers to the oxidative degrada-
tion of lipids, in which its ﬁnal products are reactive aldehy-
des (e.g., malondialdehyde (MDA) and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-
HNE)) that would react with thiobarbituric acid (TBA) to gen-
erate ﬂuorescent MDA-TBA adducts. As shown in Figure 2d,
the MXene-AuNCs conjugation signiﬁcantly oxidize the bacte-
rial membrane (≈1.9-fold) compared to the MXene (≈1.3-fold)
and AuNCs (≈1.05-fold) treatment. This also proves that the
conjugated AuNCs are easily delivered into bacteria after MX-
ene nanosheets piercing bacterial membrane, and the localized
AuNCs on the surface of MXene nanosheets could generate local-
ized ROS of high concentration for eﬀective lipid peroxidation.
The lipid peroxidation would also contribute to the enhanced
bacterial membrane damage as shown in Figure 2a and Figure
S6, Supporting Information, suggesting the important role of
AuNCs in the synergistic antimicrobial performance.
The synergistic antibacterial performance of MXene-AuNCs
was demonstrated not only on the Gram-positive S. aureus, but
also on a Gram-negative bacterial model, Escherichia coli (E. coli,
as shown in Figure S8, Supporting Information). As for E. coli,
the bacterial killing eﬃcacy of MXene-AuNCs reached >96%, sig-
niﬁcantly increased from MXene-only and AuNCs-only treated
group, which was ≈36% and <5%, respectively. Similarly, the
ROS generation level of MXene-AuNCs was ≈3.6-fold, also sig-
niﬁcantly increased from MXene-only and AuNCs-only treated
group, which was ≈1.6- and ≈1.1-fold, respectively. The eﬃcient
antimicrobial ability on both Gram-positive and Gram-negative
bacteria suggests that the synergistic MXene-AuNCs nanosheets
could work as a board-spectrum antimicrobial agent.
Furthermore, we found the antimicrobial mechanism of
MXene-AuNCs conjugation also included tremendous damage
towards bacterial DNA, which is another very important factor
contributed to the synergistic antimicrobial ability. As shown
in Figure 2e,f, the bacterial DNA fragmentation was quantiﬁed
with terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end la-

through the enzyme terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase (TdT),

red color for better contrast (Figure 2e). Afterwards, the TUNEL

no DNA damage (DNA damage index of 0). The DNA damage

sulting in negligible bacterial DNA damage. As for the MXene
treated group, the DNA damage index was ≈0.1, indicating the
MXene could cause some damage towards bacterial DNA. It has
been reported that MXene could interact with bacterial DNA to
break the DNA.[8b] In comparison, the DNA damage index of

Therefore, with the physical and chemical damage contributed
from MXene, and remarkable chemical killing eﬀects of conju-
gated AuNCs, the multi-functional MXene-AuNCs conjugation
achieved synergistic antibacterial eﬀect. The MXene-AuNCs con-
jugation could ﬁrst disrupt the bacterial membrane through the
physical damage of the sharp MXene nanosheets, and then the
AuNCs loaded on the surface of MXene nanosheets would be
easily internalized to generate ROS inside bacteria. The conju-
gated AuNCs were localized on the surface of MXene nanosheets,
which could eﬀectively generate localized ROS of high concen-
tration for enhanced bacterial membrane damage and bacte-
rial DNA broken, leading to violent eventual bacterial death. Al-
though the Ag@MXene antimicrobial agents have been reported,
the synergistic antimicrobial mechanism of MXene and the sec-
ond metal component is systematically investigated in this study
for the ﬁrst time.[8d]
After conﬁrming the synergistic antimicrobial eﬀects of
MXene-AuNCs conjugation, we further investigated the bioﬁlm
inhibition performance. It has been reported that the MXene
nanosheets worked as eﬀective antimicrobial membranes for wa-
ter puriﬁcation, which could prevent bacteria formation.[8c,d] In
this study, we constructed two types of MXene based structures,
one is planar structure and another one is crumpled structure, to
analyze their bioﬁlm inhibition performance. Compared with the
previously reported Ag@MXene membrane, we have moved one
step forward to construct the crumpled structure to improve their
bioﬁlm inhibition eﬃcacy.[8d] The crumpled structure was re-
ported to be more hydrophobic than the planar one, which would
prohibit the bacterial attachment for bioﬁlm formation.[16c,21b,c]
The crumpled structures are fabricated by transferring the pla-
nar structures onto latex balloon under pre-applied areal strain,
and then undergoing full deﬂation (see Supporting Information
for detailed procedure). As shown in the scanning electron micro-
scope (SEM) images of Figure 3a, the crumpled MXene structure
(c-MXene) and crumpled MXene-AuNCs structure (c-MXene-Au)
were successfully fabricated from planar structures, with average
feature size of ≈500 µm.
Then, the crystal violet bioﬁlm assay was conducted to test
the bioﬁlm formation situation on planar and crumpled struc-
tures, and both Gram-positive S. aureus and Gram-negative E. coli
were used. As shown in Figure 3d,f, the bioﬁlm inhibition trends
were c-MXene-Au > p-MXene-Au > c-MXene > p-MXene, that
the crumpled MXene-AuNCs structure achieved the best bioﬁlm
inhibition result. The relative bioﬁlm formation was ≈0.34, 0.57,
0.62, and 0.82 for S. aureus on c-MXene-Au, p-MXene-Au, c-
MXene, and p-MXene, respectively. As for E. coli, the relative
bioﬁlm formation was ≈0.51, 0.64, 0.66, and 0.79 for c-MXene-
Au, p-MXene-Au, c-MXene, and p-MXene, respectively. This sug-
gests that the crumpled MXene-AuNCs could reduce ≈66% and
≈49% bioﬁlm growth for S. aureus and E. coli, respectively. More-
over, SEM images of each treatment group also conﬁrmed their
bioﬁlm inhibition condition (Figure 3c,e, see more representative
SEM images of four structures in Figures S9 and S10, Supporting
Information). Compared to the abundance and stacking bacteria
on planar MXene structure, only very little S. aureus and E. coli
were attached on crumpled MXene-AuNCs structure. The SEM
images agree well with the quantitative crystal violet assay result,
verifying that the crumpled MXene-AuNCs structures could ef-
fectively inhibit the bioﬁlm formation. Moreover, the crumpled
structures are very stable, with the morphology unchanged after
bioﬁlm formation test of S. aureus and E.coli, suggesting their
feasibility for future applications (Figure S11, Supporting Infor-
mation).
The signiﬁcant bioﬁlm inhibition eﬀect of the crumpled
MXene-AuNCs structure could be caused by following reasons.
On the one hand, the synergistic antimicrobial ability of AuNCs
and MXene nanosheets could work together to combat bacte-
rial growth on their surface. When comparing the bioﬁlm inhi-
bition between p-MXene-Au and p-MXene, as well as c-MXene-
Au and c-MXene, both of the AuNCs contained groups showed
improved bioﬁlm inhibition eﬀects, suggesting the key role in
bioﬁlm inhibition provided by AuNCs. As discussed, the conju-
gated AuNCs on the surface of MXene nanosheets could be bet-
ter internalized in bacteria and generate localized ROS to dam-
age bacterial membrane and DNA, which would better kill the
bacteria attached upon the membranes to prevent formation of
bioﬁlm. On the other hand, the increased hydrophobicity of the
crumpled structure is another important factor to impair bacte-
rial attachment for bioﬁlm formation, for both c-MXene and c-
MXene-Au showed better bacterial bioﬁlm inhibition ability than
p-MXene and p-MXene-Au, respectively. As shown in Figure 3b,
the contact angle of c-MXene and c-MXene-Au are 116° and 125°,
respectively; while the p-MXene and p-MXene-Au are 83° and 81°,
respectively. The higher hydrophobicity of the crumpled struc-
tures will prevent the bacterial attachment as reported,[16c,21b,c]
leading to less bacterial growth on their surface and less bioﬁlm
formation. Moreover, the crumpled structures can provide large
speciﬁc surface area, which is ≈4-fold compared to the planar
structures,[16c,27] thus the densities of MXene and AuNCs (per
unit area) of crumpled structures are higher than those of the pla-
nar structures. The higher density of bactericides would also in-
crease their antibacterial eﬃcacy to kill attached bacteria, leading
to less bioﬁlm formation on the surface of crumpled structures.
With all these advantages, the crumpled MXene-AuNCs conju-
gation structure showed better bioﬁlm inhibition than planar
MXene-AuNCs and crumpled MXene groups, indicating their po-
tential applications in biomedical ﬁelds to prevent bioﬁlm forma-
tion.
In conclusion, we have conjugated the ultra-small AuNCs on
the surface of MXene nanosheets to achieve synergistic antimi-
crobial ability. The sharp MXene nanosheets could pierce the
bacterial membrane, so the conjugated AuNCs on their surface
would be better internalized inside bacteria for ROS generation
to disturb bacterial normal metabolism. Moreover, the gener-
ated ROS are localized status with high concentration to work as
ROS reservoir, providing continuous oxidization ability to oxidize
bacterial membrane lipid for enhanced membrane broken, as
well as bacterial DNA for violent fragmentation, leading to even-
tual bacterial death. This MXene-AuNCs conjugation realized
both physical (via MXene) and chemical (via MXene and AuNCs)

Experimental Section
Synthesis of Gold Nanoclusters:
The synthesis of Synthesis of Gold
Nanoclusters (AuNCs) was according to a reported method.[23] First,
freshly prepared aqueous solutions of HAuCl4 (40 mm, 0.25 mL) and MBA
(50 mm in 0.15 m NaOH aqueous solution, 0.4 mL) were mixed in wa-
ter (9.5 mL) under mild stirring at room temperature, and light-yellow
Au(I)−MBA complexes were formed after 2 h. Next, the pH of the solu-
tion was brought to 11.6 by using 1 m NaOH solution. Afterwards, CO
was bubbled into the solution for 2 min. Then, the bottle was sealed air-
tight, and the product was collected after 72 h. Finally, a stirred ultraﬁltra-
tion cell (Model 8010, Millipore Corporation, USA) with a semipermeable
membrane of 3000 Da molecular weight cutoﬀ(MWCO) was used to pu-
rify and concentrate the sample.
Synthesis of Ti3C2Tx MXene Nanosheets:
Ti3C2Tx MXene nanosheets
were prepared according to the literature.[22] First, LiF (3.0 g) was added
to HCl solution (9.0 m, 40 mL) under vigorous stirring. After the disso-
lution of LiF, Ti3AlC2 MAX powder (1.0 g) was slowly added into the HF-
containing solution. The mixture was kept at 35 °C for 24 h. Afterwards,
the solid residue was washed with DI water for several times until the pH
was about 6.0. Subsequently, the washed residue was added into 70 mL of
DI water, ultrasonicated for 30 min under N2 atmosphere in ice bath, and
centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 20 min. The supernatant was collected as the
ﬁnal suspension of MXene nanosheets.

Functionalized MXene Nanosheets:
In a typical synthesis, 2 mL of
APTES was added step-wise in freshly prepared MXene suspension
(100 mg in 50 mL ethanol) under mild stirring at room temperature for 48
h. To remove the unbound APTES molecules, the obtained product was
washed with water and ethanol (1:1) mixture for three times, and then
dispersed in water for further use.
Conjugation of MXene with AuNCs:
In a typical synthesis, freshly pre-
pared MXene-NH2 dispersion (2 mg mL‒1, 50 mL) and freshly prepared
AuNCs solution (0.04 mm, 12.5mL, which is 1 mm on the basis of Au
atom) were mixed under mild stirring at room temperature, and then
freshly prepared aqueous solution of EDC (50 mm, 0.6 mL) and N-
hydroxysuccinimide (50 mm, 0.5 mL) were added into the mixture. The
ﬁnal product was collected after 4 h, and washed with water for three times
and then dispersed in water for further use. The ﬁnal concentration of MX-
ene in the solution is 1 mg mL‒1, and the concentration of AuNCs is 3.4 µm
(85 µm on the basis of Au atom).
Fabrication of Crumpled MXene and MXene-AuNCs Structures:
To im-
prove the adhesion interaction at the 2DM-latex interface, a 750 nm-thick
GO interlayer (1.5 mL of 0.5 mg mL−1 GO dispersion) with a diameter of
35 mm was ﬁrst ﬁltered through the hydrophobic polyvinylidene ﬂuoride
(PVDF) membrane (0.22 µm pore, Merck Millipore). Next, MXene disper-
sion (1.6 mL of 0.5 mg mL−1) was added into the vacuum ﬁltration system,
forming a top 250 nm-thick MXene layer. Then, the dried MXene mem-
brane was cut into certain dimensions, detached from PVDF membrane
in ethanol, and transferred onto the plasma-treated latex balloon with a
pre-applied areal strain of 100%. After further drying of the MXene mem-
brane, the balloon underwent full deﬂation to obtain the crumpled MXene
structure. Based on the same process, crunpled MXene-AuNCs structure
was prepared as well. The planar MXene and MXene-AuNCs structures
were prepared in the same way without the deﬂation process. All the sam-
ples were prepared with size of 8 × 8 mm2 for the bacterial bioﬁlm test.
Statistical Analysis:
The data were reported as mean ± standard devi-
ation (SD). The statistical analysis was conducted in triplicate (n = 3) un-
less otherwise mentioned. Statistical signiﬁcance of diﬀerences between
two groups was determined using Student’s unpaire t-test comparison.
Results were considered as statistically signiﬁcant with p < 0.05. All statis-
tical analyses were performed using Excel and OriginPro 9.1 software.
