Nanozyme
A Self-Reinforced “Microglia Energy Modulator” for Synergistic
Amyloid-β Clearance in Alzheimer’s Disease Model
Mengmeng Ma, Jing Wang, Kaiming Guo, Wenbin Zhong, Yu Cheng, Li Lin,* and
Yanli Zhao*
In memory of Professor Sir Fraser Stoddart
Abstract: Microglial phagocytosis is a highly energy-
consuming process that plays critical roles in clearing
neurotoxic amyloid-β (Aβ) in Alzheimer’s disease (AD).
However, microglial metabolism is defective overall in
AD, thereby undermining microglial phagocytic func-
tions. Herein, we repurpose the existing antineoplastic
drug lonidamine (LND) conjugated with hollow meso-
porous Prussian blue (HMPB) as a “microglial energy
modulator” (termed LND@HMPB-T7) for safe and
synergistic Aβ clearance. The modified blood–brain
barrier penetrating heptapeptide (T7) enables efficient
transport of LND@HMPB-T7 to the AD brain. LND in
LND@HMPB-T7 could fuel Aβ phagocytosis by stim-
ulating microglial adenosine triphosphate (ATP) pro-
duction, whereas HMPB with catalase and superoxide
dismutase-mimicking activities substantially alleviates
the mitochondrial side effects commonly associated with
LND and thus further enhances ATP production. The
synergism of LND and nanozyme affords a high micro-
glial Aβ clearance efficacy without triggering mitochon-
drial dysfunction. In vivo experiments ascertain that
LND@HMPB-T7 could synergistically promote phago-
cytic clearance of Aβ, relieve neuroinflammation and
ameliorate
cognitive
function
in
AD
mice.
These
findings indicate that LND@HMPB-T7 holds tremen-
dous clinical potential as a repurposed drug for AD
treatment.
Introduction
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is an irreversible neurodegener-
ative disorder characterized by the inability to learn,
progressive behavioral dysfunction and memory loss.[1,2] The
current drugs used clinically (e.g., tacrine, donepezil, riva-
stigmine, galantamine, memantine) only moderately allevi-
ate behavior and cognition in AD patients, but cannot
reverse AD onset and progression.[3–7] Ergo, it is highly
desirable to invent new drug candidates against AD.
However, the development of new chemical entities from
scratch is colossal challenges, mired by substantial costs,
lengthy timelines and high attrition rates. Alternatively,
drug repurposing, well known as “new uses for old drugs”,
represents a
less risky,
cost-effective
and
more rapid
approach for expedited drug development, benefiting from
the historical evidence of clinical tolerability and reliable
safety in humans for these repurposed drugs.[8–13]
Recent studies increasingly highlight that microglial
phagocytosis is an energy-intensive course that exerts an
important role in clearing neurotoxic amyloid-β (Aβ), the
accumulation
of
which
is
a
critical
event
in
AD
pathology.[14–18] Unfortunately, metabolic programming is
impaired in microglia in AD, thereby unsettling microglial
phagocytic functions.[19–22] Therefore, regulation of microglial
metabolism may be a promising therapeutic tactic for AD.
Lonidamine (LND), an indazole 3-carboxylic acid deriva-
tive, is approved in Europe for treating multiple cancers,
with a long history of clinical use.[23–27] This LND interferes
with bioenergetic metabolism in cancerous cells through the
inhibition of mitochondrial hexokinase 2, an enzyme respon-
sible for phosphorylating glucose into glucose-6-phosphate.
This interference impairs aerobic glycolysis and decreases
NADPH levels, thus blocking tumor growth.[23,24,27–29] For
this reason, LND could be repurposed as an energy
modulator to promote microglial phagocytic function in the
fight against AD. However, it is important to consider that
LND also suppresses respiratory complex II (succinate
dehydrogenase) activity, leading to increased reactive oxy-
gen species (ROS) generation, mitochondrial depolarization
and eventual cell damage.[24,30] In addition, inherent limita-
tions of the small molecule LND, including low solubility,
rapid
clearance,
and
poor
blood–brain
barrier
(BBB)
penetration, further restrict the therapeutic efficacy.[25,27,31]
Thus, there are validity and safety concerns of repurposing
LND for AD treatment.
Nanomaterials with enzyme-like activities (termed nano-
zyme), have garnered growing interest over recent years
because of their high operational stability, facile synthesis
and low cost, which have been explored for various disease
therapies.[32–35] Up to now, numerous nanozymes that func-

As illustrated in Scheme 1, the modified T7 peptide enables

ATP and promotes Aβ phagocytosis, whereas HMPB with

systemic toxicity. Last but not least, LND@HMPB-T7 can
also mitigate neuroinflammation in the brains of 3xTg-AD
mice. Overall, this work showcases a promising candidate
for safe and effective AD treatment.
Results and Discussion
As the first step toward synthesizing LND@HMPB-T7, solid
PB nanoparticle was prepared by mixing K3[Fe(CN)6] and
polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) at 80°C. Second, the PB nano-
particle was etched using hydrochloric acid (HCl) to obtain
HMPB. Afterwards, poly(acrylic acid) was absorbed onto
the surface of HMPB via carboxyl coordination, resulting in
HMPB-COOH. Finally, the T7 peptide was grafted onto
HMPB-COOH through an amidation reaction, followed by
loading LND. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)
images demonstrated that the resultant LND@HMPB-T7
was uniformly dispersed with a diameter of approximately

We then evaluated the whole body bioelimination of
LND@HMPB-T7. 3xTg-AD mice were received intravenous
injection of LND@HMPB-T7. They were then sacrificed at
different time points (1, 5 and 10 days), independently
frozen, ground to tissue homogeneity, and weighted. The
entire specimen was analyzed for iron (Fe) ion content using
ICP-AES. As depicted in Figure S18a, the Fe ion content in
the whole bodies of mice decreased significantly over time.
Within 10 days, approximately 94.8% of LND@HMPB-T7
was eliminated. These results suggested that the clearance of
LND@HMPB-T7 was a time-dependent process in vivo. We
also investigated the tissue distribution of LND@HMPB-T7
following intravenous administration. Organs were removed
at different time points (1, 5, and 10 days) and dissolved in
aqua regia. The Fe ions in the resulting liquids were
quantified using the ICP-AES method. As shown in Fig-
ure S18b, LND@HMPB-T7 primarily accumulated in the
liver and spleen, indicating that the liver and spleen played a
major role in LND@HMPB-T7 metabolism. Notably, the Fe
ion content decreased after 10 days of injection. These
findings suggested that LND@HMPB-T7 could be cleared
from the body possibly through a hepatobiliary route, which
is consistent with the in vivo clearance behavior of many
other nanomaterials used in biomedicine.[57–60]

improve memory and cognitive functions in 3xTg-AD mice
by performing Morris water maze (MWM) tests (Fig-
ure 5a).[61,62] In comparison to wild-type (WT) mice, 3xTg-
AD mice exhibited longer escape latency (Figure 5b), less
time took in the target quadrant (Figure 5c), and lower
platform crossover number (Figure 5d), indicating defective
learning and memory functions in 3xTg-AD mice. Impor-
tantly, the swimming speeds of mice in the MWM were
similar among all experimental groups (Figure 5e). In sharp
contrast, LND@HMPB-T7 injection dramatically rescued
the cognitive functions of 3xTg-AD mice, whereas HMPB-
T7, LND, and LND@HMPB only exhibited a mild effect
(Figure 5b–f).
Overall,
these
results
demonstrated
that
LND@HMPB-T7 synergistically improved the cognitive
function of 3xTg-AD mice.
Conclusions
In summary, we developed a microglia energy modulator
LND@HMPB-T7 for safe and synergistic treatment of AD.
