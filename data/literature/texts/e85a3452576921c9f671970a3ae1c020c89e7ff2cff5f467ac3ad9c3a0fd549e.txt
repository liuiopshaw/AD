Eﬀect of subtle changes of isomeric ligands on the
synthesis of atomically precise water-soluble gold
nanoclusters†
Xinlei Zhang,a Ziping Wang,b Shuyu Qian,a Naiwei Liu,a Lina Sui*a and Xun Yuan
*a
The subtle structural change of hydrophilic ligands on the size control of metal nanoclusters (NCs) is
unclear but critically important for fundamental understanding. Herein, we report our ﬁndings that subtle
changes of isomeric ligands lead to a dramatic diﬀerence in the size of water-soluble Au NCs. By using
isomeric para-mercaptobenzoic acid (p-MBA), m-MBA, and o-MBA as model ligands, it was found that
both the steric hindrance and the electronic eﬀect of isomeric ligands signiﬁcantly inﬂuences the size of
Au NCs, resulting in the formation of diﬀerent sized Au44(p-MBA)26 NCs, Au25(m-MBA)18 NCs, and
Au37/43(o-MBA)22/26 NCs. Besides this, by collocating any two of the isomeric MBAs as ligand pairs to
compare their protecting capability for Au NCs, the protecting abilities of such ligands were found to
follow the trend: m-MBA > o-MBA > p-MBA. In addition, the growth process of Au44(p-/o-MBA)26 NCs
from Au(I)-MBA complexes in the NaBH4 reduction system was also monitored by real-time UV-vis
absorption spectroscopy and ESI mass spectrometry, which complies with the 2e−hopping growth prin-
ciple, indicating the universal applicability of this principle in the synthesis of thiolated metal NCs. This
study provides a fundamental understanding of the eﬀect of ligands’ steric hindrance and electronic
factors on the size control of water-soluble metal NCs and sheds light on the formation of metal NCs in
the NaBH4 reduction system.
Introduction
Thiolated metal nanoclusters (NCs) have emerged as a new
class of functional nanomaterials that hold great potential in
various applications, such as catalysis,1–8 optoelectronics,9,10
sensing,11–16 biomedicine,17–23 and energy conversion,24,25
which mainly benefit from their intriguing molecular-like pro-
perties, rich yet tailorable surface chemistry, and sensitive size
eﬀect.26–35 Therefore, tremendous research eﬀorts have been
devoted to engineering the surface chemistry and controlling
the size of metal NCs.36–42 It is documented that the steric hin-
drance of surface ligands has a significant influence on the
size of metal NCs. For example, Chen et al. exemplified that
three hydrophobic magic-sized Au NCs (i.e., Au130, Au104, and
Au40)
could
be
synthesized
by
using
isomeric
methyl-
benzenethiols (p/m/o-MBT) with subtle structural changes as
thiolate ligands via a size-focusing strategy.43 Furthermore, in
the system of water-soluble metal NCs, Zheng et al. employed
diﬀerently structured hydrophilic ligands including flexible
6-mercaptohexanoic acid (MHA), rigid 4-mercaptobenzoic acid
(p-MBA), and bulky glutathione (GSH) to demonstrate the syn-
thesis of diﬀerent sized Ag25, Ag44, and Ag9–15 NCs, respect-
ively.44 However, it is unclear how sensitive the size of water-
soluble metal NCs is to the subtle structural changes of hydro-
philic ligands during NC synthesis in a relatively complicated
aqueous environment, where multiple factors such as solvent
polarity, pH, and the functional groups of ligands have an
impact on metal NCs. Moreover, it is also unknown whether
the size of water-soluble metal NCs has enough tolerance to
bear subtle changes to their surface landscape by planting two
kinds of isomeric ligands on the NC surface. Therefore, trying
to understand such fundamental problems may be an impor-
tant project, which is the driving force of investigating the
influence of the subtle changes of isomeric ligands on the
precise size control of water-soluble metal NCs.
In addition, recent studies revealed that real-time monitor-
ing of the growth of metal NCs by UV-vis absorption and elec-
trospray ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS) has elevated
NC research into the molecular- or even atomic-level.19,45–48

Herein, we report our findings about the eﬀect of the subtle
changes in the position of the carboxyl group of hydrophilic
ligands on the atomically precise size control of water-soluble
Au NCs. By employing three hydrophilic isomeric mercapto-
benzoic acids (i.e., p/m/o-MBA) as protecting ligands, water-
soluble Au44(p-MBA)26, Au25(m-MBA)18, and Au37/43(o-MBA)22/26
NCs with atomic precision could be obtained in a NaBH4
reduction system under similar synthetic conditions. This
result clearly demonstrates that the size of water-soluble Au
NCs could be sensitively influenced not only by subtle changes
to the ligand’s steric hindrance but also the electronic eﬀect of
the ligand’s carboxyl group. Moreover, the size tolerance of Au
NCs against the steric hindrance of bi-ligands was also exam-
ined by planting any two types of isomeric MBA ligands on the
NC surface, which revealed that the size of the Au NCs has a
moderate tolerance to the variation in the ligand’s structures.
In addition, we also monitored the growth of Au44(p/o-MBA)26
NCs in a real-time manner by using UV-vis absorption and
ESI-MS, which was compared with the previously reported
seed-mediated growth process of Au44(p-MBA)26 in the gaseous
CO-reduction system for some fundamental insights.
Results and discussion
In this study, three types of hydrophilic isomeric MBA ligands
were selected as model ligands for synthesizing Au NCs via a
NaOH-mediated NaBH4 strategy under the same synthetic con-
ditions.49 Briefly, HAuCl4 solution as the Au precursor and one
of the isomeric p/m/o-MBA solutions were mixed in 36%
ethanol under stirring conditions, followed by adding a certain
volume of NaOH and NaBH4 solutions. After a 12–32 h reac-
tion, MBA-protected Au NCs were collected for analysis.
These three kinds of Au NCs protected by isomeric MBA
ligands were firstly characterized by UV-vis absorption spec-
troscopy. As shown in Fig. 1, the UV-vis absorption spectra of
the three samples display a step-like feature, which indicates
the successful synthesis of a molecular-like NC species instead
of large sized Au nanoparticles. Specifically, the UV-vis absorp-
tion spectrum of p-MBA-protected Au NCs shows three distinct
absorption peaks at 497, 590, and 794 nm (Fig. 1a), which
resemble the characteristic absorption of previously reported
Au44(p-MBA)26 NCs.47 By contrast, m-MBA-protected Au NCs
exhibit three absorption peaks at 440, 580, and 690 nm in
their UV-vis absorption spectrum (Fig. 1b), which is very
similar to that of Au25(m-MBA)18 NCs;46 while no distinctive
absorption peak is observed in the spectrum of o-MBA-pro-
tected Au NCs (Fig. 1c). Moreover, as shown in the insets in
Fig. 1, both the p-MBA- and m-MBA-protected Au NCs are
brown in solution color, whereas the solution of o-MBA-pro-
tected Au NCs looks dark brown. Taken together, such results
of the diﬀerent UV-vis absorptions and solution colors evi-
dently clarified that the carboxyl group on the benzene ring of
the MBA ligand transforming from para to meta then to the
ortho position could induce the size variation of the Au NCs.
The molecular formulas of the Au NC samples were further
analyzed by ESI-MS (Fig. 1d–f). For p-MBA-protected Au NCs,
only one set of intense peaks at m/z = 4216 is observed in the
range of 2000–5000 in its ESI mass spectrum (Fig. 1d), which
can be explicitly assigned to be [Au44(p-MBA)26]3−(see Fig. S1†
for a detailed ESI-MS assignment). In comparison, the ESI
mass spectrum of the m-MBA protected Au NCs displays two
sets of intense peaks at m/z = 3840 and 2560 in the range of
2000–5000 (Fig. 1e), which could be determined to be doubly
charged
[Au25(m-MBA)18]2−
and
triply
charged
[Au25(m-
MBA)18]3−, respectively (see Fig. S2 and S3† for detailed ESI-MS
assignments). In addition, the ESI mass spectrum of the
o-MBA-protected Au NCs shows two sets of peaks at m/z = 3595
and 4150, which can be identified as [Au37(o-MBA)22]3−, and
[Au43(o-MBA)26]3−, respectively (see Fig. S4 and S5† for detailed
ESI-MS assignments).
Why did subtle changes in the isomeric MBA ligands lead
to a dramatic diﬀerence in the size of Au NCs? We reasoned
that the steric hindrance as well as the electronic eﬀect of the
MBA ligands are responsible for the size diﬀerence of the Au
NCs. Firstly, it is well documented that the steric hindrance of
the protecting ligands could markedly influence the size of
metal NCs,43,44 which is also the major factor aﬀecting the size
control of Au NCs in this study. As is well known, ligands with
a larger steric hindrance usually have a better protecting

Indeed, both the Au44(p-MBA)26 and Au25(m-MBA)18 NCs
comply with the above-mentioned NC’s size trend correlating
to the steric hindrance of ligands, whereas the Au37/43(o-
MBA)22/26 NCs are an exception. This result is not unexpected
if the influence of the functional group’s electronic eﬀect (e.g.,
carboxyl group) of the MBA ligands on the NC’s size control is
taken into account. It is well known that the carboxyl group in
isomeric MBA ligands is an electron-withdrawing group, and
the electron cloud densities of the carboxyl groups in o-MBA
and p-MBA are relatively bigger than that in the m-MBA.
Therefore, the benzene rings of both o-MBA and p-MBA are
positively charged, while that of the m-MBA is negatively
charged. This allows the sulfur in the m-MBA to have a stron-
ger coordinating ability to metals (i.e., stronger protecting
ability) than that in the o-MBA and p-MBA because of the
bigger electron cloud density of the sulfur in the m-MBA,
thereby leading to the formation of the smallest-sized Au25(m-
MBA)18 NCs. On the other hand, while the coordinating abil-
ities of sulfur with metals in o-MBA and p-MBA are compar-
able, o-MBA has a larger steric hindrance than p-MBA, which
naturally results in the o-MBA protected Au NCs being smaller
in size than the p-MBA protected Au NCs.
Another interesting question is which ligand could play a
predominant role in determining the size of Au NCs when two
types of isomeric MBA ligands are planted on the NC surface.
To answer this question, we synthesized bi-MBA protected Au
NCs. Firstly, we chose m-MBA and p-MBA as ligand pairs for
the NC synthesis. As shown in Fig. 2a and d, by fixing the
feeding ratio at m-MBA : p-MBA = 1 : 1, Au25(m-/p-MBA)18 NCs
could be synthesized (see Fig. S6 and S7† for detailed ESI
assignments), sharing the same size with mono-ligand pro-
tected Au25(m-MBA)18 NCs instead of Au44(p-MBA)26 NCs. This
result indicates that m-MBA with a larger steric hindrance has
a better protecting ability than p-MBA, which enables the m-/
p-MBA bi-ligands to have an overwhelming capability of con-
trolling the size of Au NCs compared to the mono-ligand
p-MBA. We rationalized that the co-protection of m-MBA and
p-MBA for Au NCs is always stronger than the mono-protection
of p-MBA because of the larger steric hindrance of m-MBA, and
therefore the size of m-/p-MBA protected Au NCs could be
smaller than the mono-ligand p-MBA protected Au44 NCs. To
confirm this speculation, we adjusted the feeding ratio of
m-MBA : p-MBA from 1 : 1 up to 1 : 2.5, smaller sized Au NCs
such as Au25(m-/p-MBA)18 NCs and Au38(m-/p-MBA)24 NCs still
can be synthesized (Fig. S8†), which further corroborates the
larger protecting ability of m-MBA than p-MBA.
Moreover, the bulkiest o-MBA was also applied to collocate
with p-MBA and m-MBA for the synthesis of bi-MBA protected
Au NCs. It is worth noting that the impact of the functional
group’s electronic factor on the NCs’ size control would com-
plicate the synthetic system, therefore we would like to discuss
the influence of p-MBA and m-MBA on the size control of bi-
MBA protected Au NCs by using Au37/43(o-MBA)22/26 as refer-
ence. As shown in Fig. 2b and e, the size of p-/o-MBA protected
Au NCs is determined to be Au44(p-/o-MBA)26 NCs (see Fig. S9†
for detailed ESI assignment), which shares the same size with
the p-MBA protected Au44 NCs but is larger than the o-MBA
protected Au37/43 NCs. This result obviously suggests that the
protecting ability of p-MBA is relatively lower than that of
o-MBA, otherwise smaller-sized Au NCs (≤Au37/43(MBA)22/26)
would be formed. By contrast, as shown in Fig. 2c and f, the
o-/m-MBA protected Au NCs are identified as Au25–43(o-/
m-MBA)18–24 NCs (see Fig. S10–S14† for detailed ESI assign-
ments),
which
are

MBA)22/26 in size. This result reveals that the protecting ability
of m-MBA is relatively larger than that of o-MBA. Therefore, we
may conclude that the protecting ability of such isomeric MBA
ligands for Au NCs follows the trend: m-MBA > o-MBA >
p-MBA. In addition, it seems that monodisperse Au NCs
cannot be synthesized if protected solely by o-MBA or the
ligand pair of m-/o-MBA, which is most probably ascribed to
the fact that the largest steric hindrance of the o-MBA ligand
made the “ligand etching induced size-focusing”43 impossible
by preventing the permeation of a free o-MBA ligand into the
surface ligand shell of NCs (Scheme S1†).

Conclusions
In summary, we have reported that a subtle change in the posi-
tion of a carboxyl group on MBA ligands could cause a big
diﬀerence in the size of Au NCs. By using isomeric MBA mole-
cules as ligand models (e.g., p-, m-, and o-MBA), it was found
that both the steric hindrance and the electronic eﬀect of the
carboxyl
groups
of
isomeric
MBA
ligands
significantly
impacted the size of the Au NCs. Moreover, we also collocated
any two of the isomeric MBAs into ligand pairs to compare
their protecting capability for Au NCs, and found that their
protecting ability follows the trend: m-MBA > o-MBA > p-MBA.
In addition, by monitoring the growth process of Au44(p-/
o-MBA)26 NCs in a real-time manner, we revealed that the syn-
thesis of Au44(p-/o-MBA)26 NCs in the NaBH4 reduction system
is very similar to the seed-mediated synthesis of Au44(p-MBA)26
NCs in a mild CO reduction system, and both of them followed
a 2e−hopping growth pathway. This study is very interesting
because it not only exemplified the synergistic eﬀect of the
ligand’s steric hindrance and electronic factors on the size
control of water-soluble metal NCs, but it also shed light on
the growth mechanism of metal NCs in the NaBH4 reduction
system.
Experimental section
Chemicals and materials
Ultrapure water (18.2 MΩ) was used throughout the experi-
ments. All glassware and magnetic stir bars were washed with
aqua regia, rinsed with ethanol and ultrapure water, and then
dried in an oven at 90 °C before use. 4-Mercaptobenzoic acid
(p-MBA) and 3-mercaptobenzoic acid (m-MBA) from TCI,
2-mercaptobenzoic acid (o-MBA) and glacial acetic acid (AcOH)
from
Adamas-beta,
hydrogen
tetrachloroaurate(III)
hydrate
(HAuCl4·3H2O) and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) from Sigma-
Aldrich, and sodium borohydride (NaBH4), sodium chloride
(NaCl), absolute ethanol and N,N-dimethylformamide (DMF)
from Sinopharm were all used as received.
Instruments
UV-vis absorption spectra of samples were acquired on a
Shimadzu UV-1800 spectrometer. The molecular formulas of
the Au NCs were analyzed by a Bruker Impact II ESI-MS system
operating in the negative ion mode. The ESI-MS parameters
are as follows: nebulizer of 1.5 bar, capillary voltage of 4 kV,

Synthesis of mono-thiolate-protected Au NCs
Aqueous solutions of HAuCl4 (20 mM) and NaOH (1 M) were
prepared by using ultrapure water as solvent, while p-MBA,
m-MBA and o-MBA were dissolved in ethanol as stock solutions
(20 mM). In addition, NaBH4 solution was prepared by dissol-
ving 43 mg of NaBH4 powder in 10 mL of a 0.2 M NaOH solu-
tion. In a typical synthesis of Au44(p-MBA)26 NCs, an aqueous
solution of HAuCl4 (20 mM, 0.25 mL) and an ethanolic solu-
tion of p-MBA (20 mM, 0.5 mL) were separately added to a
mixed solvent of ethanol/water (3.4 mL, 36 vol% of ethanol) to
form Au(I)-p-MBA complexes under stirring conditions. After
that, NaOH solution (1 M, 0.5 mL) was added to the reaction
system. Finally, fresh NaBH4 solution (0.1 mL) was added
dropwise to reduce the Au(I)-p-MBA complexes. The Au44(p-
MBA)26 were collected after 12 h. By using the same synthetic
conditions, Au25(m-MBA)18, and Au37/43(o-MBA)22/26 can be
synthesized.
