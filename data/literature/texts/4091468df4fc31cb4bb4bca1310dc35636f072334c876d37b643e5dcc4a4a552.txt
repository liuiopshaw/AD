ABSTRACT: Antibacterial composite polymers are promising
substrates for food packaging to reduce or inhibit the growth of
foodborne pathogens. In this study, ion-exchangeable ions of
zeolite were partially or wholly exchanged with silver (Ag+) and
ammonium ions and then subjected to heat treatment to generate
hydrogen ions (AgH1Z) through the heat decomposition of
ammonium ions. AgH1Z was incorporated into LDPE/LLDPE
(70/30 w/w %) and their blends with ethylene vinyl alcohol
copolymer (EVOH) and polyethylene-graft-maleic anhydride
(PEMa) to produce the corresponding antibacterial composite
samples. The loading of the silver ion to the zeolite structure
during the ion-exchange process and its efficiency were inspected
by several analyses. The prepared composites possessing hydrogen-
exchanged zeolite (ΔE* of 0.11 for LL@AgH1Z) presented prolonged color stability relative to those without hydrogen (ΔE* of
13.87 for LL@AgZ). Interestingly, composite films have reliable optical properties (haze and transparency) compared to the
corresponding neat polymer films. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) micrographs illustrate that the addition of PEMa to the
LDPE/LLDPE matrix improves the AgH1Z particle dispersion, while EVOH induces a droplet-matrix morphology. The effect of
AgH1Z and polymer blending on water vapor (WVTR) and the oxygen (OTR) transmission rate and mechanical behavior of
composite samples were investigated. The silver ion release profile demonstrates a higher cumulative release amount for samples
containing EVOH (0.069 mg) and PEMa (0.044 mg) than that for the LL (0.039) sample, after 10 days of incubation in an acidic
solution. The antibacterial activity results reveal remarkable inhibitory activity against Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus
bacteria for all composite samples. Furthermore, accomplished biocide performance shows a remarkable antibacterial response
toward both bacteria with R values higher than 4, in the presence of EVOH and PEMa polymers, which denotes their potentiality for
advanced antibacterial food packaging.
KEYWORDS: zeolite, LDPE/LLDPE, silver cation, antibacterial activity, food packaging
1. INTRODUCTION
Nowadays, active packaging has gained much attention
throughout the current food chain, and consumer demands
have led to an increase in the safety, quality, preservation, and
prolonged shelf life of products.1−3 Antimicrobial packaging is
a type of active packaging that can successfully reduce or
inhibit the growth of microorganisms and foodborne
organisms.4−6 Outstanding characteristics of ionic silver and
silver nanoparticles (AgNPs), such as strong toxicity against a
wide range of microorganisms with minimal human toxicity,
have extended their potential as antimicrobial agents.7−11
Sustained and controlled release of antimicrobial agents like
Ag+ ions from the packaging films to the surface of foodstuffs
and products will obviate the shortcoming associated with
direct incorporation into food formulation.12−15 The inhibitory
mechanisms and antimicrobial activity of silver nanoparticles
against bacteria result from the inactivation of the membrane
proteins and also membrane permeability changes followed by
DNA damage through the interaction of released Ag+ ions and
free radicals with the membrane.16,17
Low weight and cost, flexibility, transparency, high chemical
stability, ease of implementation, and low toxicity of
commodity polymers have extended their application in
construction, clothing, and particularly packaging.18,19 Low-
density polyethylene (LDPE), linear LDPE (LLDPE), high-
density polyethylene, and polypropylene have been widely

materials have remarkable features and greater potential over
the individual components owing to combining different
mechanical, physical, and chemical properties.21−23 However,
undesired problems associated with the incorporation of
hydrophilic or polar organic and inorganic compounds into
the above polymers including aggregation and weak distribu-
tion, as a consequence of immiscibility and incompatibility,
and inability to recycle these materials have limited their
processing conditions and product features.24 Blending with
compatible polymers like ethylene vinyl alcohol copolymer
(EVOH) and polyethylene-graft-maleic anhydride (PEMa) is
suggested to address this issue.25,26 These polymers act as
compatibilizers and provide establishing efficient interactions
between non-polar ethylene-based polymer chains and polar/
hydrophilic fillers. Moreover, the significant inter- and
intramolecular interactions between hydroxyl groups in vinyl
alcohol moieties result in outstanding O2 barrier properties of
EVOH and made it one of the most convenient polymers for
constructing multilayer food packages.27−29
In the case of free antimicrobial agents, their burst release
will limit their long-term antimicrobial behavior for practical
applications.30,31 Therefore, exploiting appropriate carriers
such as zeolite for hosting antimicrobial agents (such as silver)
could protect them and provide a controlled release profile for
long-term applications.32 Silver cation-containing zeolite shows
significant antibacterial activity, although neat zeolites are not
active against microorganisms.33,34 Incorporation of silver-
loaded zeolite into packaging films will improve their potential
through a rational Ag diffusion rate (lower release) than the
neat film containing Ag nanoparticles.35
Clinoptilolite, a common natural zeolite belonging to the
heulandite (HEU) group, has high exchange capacities, unique
nanoscale pores, availability, low cost, nonreactive nature, and
environmental compatibility and has been widely used in
various applications.31,36 The aluminosilicate framework of
HEU is made of tetrahedral SiO4 and AlO4 units with
interconnected parallel 8- (A channels) and 10-member (B
channels) rings perpendicular to a connected 8-membered ring
(C channels). The zeolite cation exchange performances
strongly depend on the chemical composition (Si/Al ratio),
structure, and nature of the exchanged cations.37 Zeolites with
lower Si/Al ratios have higher polarity, hydrophilicity, and ion-
exchange capacity than those with intermediate and higher Si/
Al ratios.38,39 Zeolites possessing Si/Al in the middle range are
the most stable species and have heterogeneous hydrophilic
surfaces in their porous structure.40 The total charge of HEU
frameworks can naturally be balanced by hydrated alkaline or
alkaline-earth metals. In addition, these cations can be easily
exchanged with other cations like ammonium ions and heavy
metals in the solution.41,42 The long-time color stability of
composite polymers containing silver ions is rarely investigated
in the patents. For instance, the introduction of a water-soluble
salt of a nitrogen atom-containing 6-membered heterocyclic
compound,43 acid scavengers or stabilizer (known as hydro-
talcite),44 and ammonium ions (converted into hydrogen ions
by heat treatment)45 to the composite polymers improves their
color stability. Actually, the aforementioned components
deactivate the probable radicals to preserve the Ag+ ion
reduction to Ag (metal) and subsequent film color
darkening.12,46 Therefore, the incorporation of hydrogen
cations into antibacterial zeolites will address the problem of
color change over time for composite polymers containing
antibacterial zeolites.45
Although different research studies concerning the incorpo-
ration of Ag−zeolite into LDPE-based polymer films could be
found,46,47 the addition of a second compatible polymer to
improve the Ag−zeolite dispersion, controlled water and
oxygen permeability, sustained Ag+ release, and antibacterial
activity is still scarce. Furthermore, the color stability of the
prepared composite film is another important parameter that
would be considered for long-term applications. In this study,
silver- and hydrogen-exchanged zeolites were prepared and
incorporated into LDPE/LLDPE (70/30 w/w %) and their
blending with PEMa and EVOH polymers to obtain the
corresponding composite films through melt-blending and
hydraulic hot press methods, respectively. The composition
and efficiency of silver exchange onto the zeolites were
inspected by several analyses. Afterward, the color character-
istics and transparency of the prepared composite films and the
influence of hydrogen ions on their color change were studied.
The contribution of PEMa and EVOH polymers to the
dispersion of zeolites into the LDPE/LLDPE blend with
their mechanical properties, oxygen (OTR) and water vapor
(WVTR) transmission rate, silver cation release, and

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Materials. LLDPE (grade 209AA) and LDPE (grade 2420F)
in pellet form were purchased from Amir Kabir Petrochemical
Industry, Iran. Natural zeolite, clinoptilolite (Si/Al ratio of 5.2), was
supplied from Afrand Toska Co, Semnan, Iran. Polyethylene-graft-
maleic anhydride (PEMa) as a compatibilizer was prepared from
Karangin, Iran. Commercial ethylene vinyl alcohol copolymer
possessing 32% w/w ethylene content (EVOH, EVAL T101B
grade) in white, straw-colored, or transparent pellets was supplied
by Kuraray. Silver nitrate (AgNO3), ammonium nitrate (NH4NO3),
nitric acid (HNO3), and acetic acid were provided by Sigma-Aldrich.
Deionized (DI) water (18.2 MΩ.cm) was prepared by the Desktop-
type α series (Organo Co., Ltd.) and used in all recipes.
2.2. Preparation of Cation (Silver and Ammonium)-
Exchanged Zeolites. The ion-exchange method was utilized to
incorporate silver and silver/ammonium cations in the zeolite
structure (Figure 1).45 For this purpose, zeolite was first passed
through a 90 μm sieve to attain uniform-sized powders. The sieved
zeolite powder was preliminarily dried at 105 °C for 24 h in an oven.
Then, 10 g of the cation-exchanged zeolite powder was added to 13
mL of DI water and magnetically stirred for degassing (200 rpm) at
40 °C for 30 min. The pH of obtained mixture was adjusted between
6 and 7 using nitric acid solution (0.5 N in DI water) and the total
slurry volume reached 18 mL using DI water. Afterward, 30 mL of an
aqueous solution of silver nitrate (0.2 mol/L) and its solution with
ammonium nitrate (2 or 4 mol/L) was added to the zeolite mixture
while maintaining stirring. The ion-exchange treatment was continued
in the dark at a temperature ranging from 60 to 70 °C (320 rpm).
After 24 h, the obtained suspension was centrifuged at 7000 rpm for 7
min, and the supernatants were removed. The precipitated product
was then redispersed in DI water and centrifuged to eliminate
unexchanged ions with three replicates. The ion-exchanged zeolite
was dried by heating at 110 °C for 5 h and kept in air-tight glassware
containers in the dark. It should be noted that owing to the sensitivity
of silver to light, all experimental procedures were done in the dark.45
The amounts of ingredients for the preparation of silver and
ammonium cation-exchanged zeolites together with their loading
efficiency have been summarized in Table 1.
The resulting antibacterial zeolites in which ion-exchangeable ions
were replaced with ammonium were heated up to 250 °C for 3 h to
generate hydrogen ions through the heat decomposition of
ammonium ions.45
2.3. Preparation of Composite Samples. The LDPE/LLDPE
(LL; 70/30, w/w %) and their blending with PEMa and EVOH
composite films containing AgZ and AgHZ (AgH1Z and AgH2Z)
were prepared by the melt-blending method. The amounts of
ingredients for the preparation of different composite films and their
labeling are listed in Table 2. Before melt-blending for composite film
preparation, AgZ, AgHZ, and all polymer granules were dried in a
vacuum oven at 70 °C for 12 h. To investigate the influence of EVOH
and PEMa on the dispersion of AgZ or AgHZ in the polymer matrix,
Ag+ release profile, and antibacterial activity of final composite films, 8
w/w % of each of them was added to the composition of samples
according to Table 2. For melt-mixing, the prepared AgZ or AgHZ
was individually mixed with polymer granules using an (internal
mixer) Brabender plasticorder W50 with a capacity of 60 cm3 (at 165
°C and 70 rpm within 10 min). The obtained compound was cooled
down to room temperature and then cut into pellets. Afterward, the
prepared composites were preheated for 3 min (at 180 °C) and
subsequently pressed at 190 °C in a hydraulic hot press (Toyoseiki
mini test press) at 20 Ton to give homogeneous films with a thickness
of about 100−200 μm. The thickness of composite films was
controlled by manipulating the temperature and applying pressure.
The thickness of composite films was averaged over three measure-
ments at different points of each sample. It is noteworthy that blown
film extrusion is the main method for film manufacturing, and the hot-
pressing technique is just used for the preparation of laboratory
samples according to previous reports.46,48,49

crystal structure of the zeolites was determined using X-ray diffraction

AgH1Z was determined using a Vega Tescan II scanning electron

dispersion state of LL, LL@AgH1Z, LL/PEMa@AgH1Z, and LL/
EVOH@AgH1Z samples, their films were kept in liquid nitrogen for
15 min and then broken. To investigate the miscibility of EVOH with
LL and its effect on the dispersion of AgH1Z, the freeze-fractured
cross section of LL/EVOH@AgH1Z was etched with dimethyl
sulfoxide (DMSO, as the solvent of EVOH) within 5 h at 60 °C.25
After extracting EVOH, the LL/EVOH@AgH1Z sample was dried
and its cross section was monitored. Before scanning, sputter-coating
(EMITECH K450x, England) was used to deposit a layer of gold
under vacuum (flushed with argon) on the cross-sectional surface of
the samples. The presence of silver cations in the zeolite structure was

2.8. Oxygen Transmission Rate (OTR). The oxygen trans-
mission rate (OTR) is known as the quantity of oxygen passing
through the surface area of the film (A) at a steady state over a given
time (t) when subjected to a partial O2 pressure (Dp) across the two
surfaces of the film. OTR was measured based on the ASTM D1434-
82R09E01 standard method (Volume 08.01, ASTM International:
West Conshohocken, PA, 2010) using a GDP-C gas permeability
tester (Coesfeld Meteriatest, Germany). The specimens were
prepared in circular shapes possessing a 12 cm diameter and stored
in a desiccator before testing. The OTR analysis was performed at a
relative humidity (RH) of 50% and a temperature of 25 ± 1 °C.
Three replications were performed for each sample to make the
statistical average.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Composite materials with appropriate antibacterial properties
have remarkable features for food packaging and successfully
reduce or inhibit the growth of microorganisms and foodborne
organisms. The prolonged color stability of the antibacterial
packaging is an important feature of food materials for
consumer acceptability. Interestingly, the co-exchange of
ammonium cations into the zeolites will address the problem
of color change over time. Good dispersion of cation-
exchanged zeolite with polar behavior into non-polar
commodity LDPE/LLDPE polymers has been attained
through blending with compatible polymers such as ethylene
vinyl alcohol copolymer (EVOH) and polyethylene-graft-
maleic anhydride. In addition, the prepared composite films
provide controlled water and oxygen permeability and
sustained Ag+ release and antibacterial activity. The precise
synthesis, composition, and performance of the prepared
LDPE/LLDPE/PEMa and LDPE/LLDPE/EVOH blends con-
taining silver/hydrogen-exchanged zeolites will be discussed
thoroughly in the following.
3.1. Identification and Efficiency of Silver/Hydrogen-
Exchanged Zeolites. The composition of zeolite and the
efficiency of silver exchange onto the zeolites were determined
by BET, ICP-OES, XRF, EDX, and XRD analyses. BET
analysis shows that replacing the ion-exchangeable ions of
zeolite (partially or wholly) with silver (AgZ) and silver/
ammonium ions (AgH1Z and AgH2Z) causes a loss in their
specific area values from 32.36 to 18.83, 15.57, and 13.81 m2/
g, respectively. This refers to the blockage of zeolite pores,
which are impregnated with Ag+ and ammonium ions. The
ICP-OES result reveals the appearance of the Ag element for
AgZ, AgH1Z, and AgH2Z compared to neat zeolite and
confirms the accuracy of the ion-exchanging process (Table S1
in the Supporting Information). The measured Ag content in
AgZ is about 49.5 mg/g, which implies that the exchange of
Ag+ onto the zeolite pores results in a yield of about 76%
(theoretically 65.13 mg/g). The amounts of exchanged Ag+
ions to zeolite for AgH1Z and AgH2Z were 43.2 (65% yield)
and 38.5 (58% yield) mg/g, respectively. A comparison
between AgH1Z and AgH2Z samples and AgZ demonstrates
that by introducing ammonium cations into the zeolite pores,
the extent of exchanged silver cations has decreased. Moreover,
the contents of the Na element for all silver/ammonium-
exchanged zeolites demonstrate about 80% w/w decreases
relative to neat zeolite. This certifies that Ag+ and NH4
+ have
mostly been exchanged (Tables S1 and S2 in the Supporting
Information), with Na+ ions owing to their relatively similar
ionic radius and the same charge.55
According to the XRF results, the observed silver contents
(Ag2O) in AgZ, AgH1Z, and AgH2Z samples were found to be
4.57, 4.23, and 3.81% w/w, respectively (Table S2 in the
Supporting Information). The exchanging Ag+ and NH4
+
cations have mostly been replaced with sodium cations,
which is consistent with ICP-OES results. It is obvious that by
introducing ammonium cations into the zeolite structure, the
content of exchanged silver cations has decreased. Besides, the
results show that the Si/Al molar ratio of the cation-exchanged
zeolites remained constant and the loading of silver and
ammonium cations does not change during the chemical
modification of the zeolites (Table S2 in the Supporting
Information).
In addition, EDX analysis was used to investigate the
composition of zeolite and estimate the efficiency of silver
exchange onto the zeolites. The elemental composition of
zeolite, AgZ, AgH1Z, and AgH2Z was measured by EDX
analysis. According to ICP-OES and XRF results, the
appearance of the Ag element approves the modification of
zeolites with Ag cations. In addition, the EDX results are
consistent with XRF results and show that the Si/Al molar
ratio was kept constant during the Ag cation modification of
zeolite. The measured Ag weight percentages in AgZ, AgH1Z,
and AgH2Z are about 4.65, 3.9, and 3.1% w/w, respectively,
and denote the performance of the cation exchange process,
respectively. The obtained results from ICP-OES, XRF, and
EDX analyses are consistent and show that by the introduction
of ammonium cations into the cation exchange process, the
extent of exchanged silver cations into zeolite has decreased,
which confirms the exchange of ammonium cations with
available metal cations in the zeolite structure (Table 1).
Figure 2 shows the XRD diffractograms of zeolite, AgZ,
AgH1Z, and AgH2Z samples. The modification of ion-
exchangeable ions of zeolite with Ag+ and ammonium ions
has an ignorable effect on the zeolitic structure. Actually, it
could be found that the zeolitic structure maintained its
crystallinity during the ion-exchange process. However, the
observed slight decrease in the relative intensity of peaks may
be assigned to the introduction of silver ions in the zeolite
channels.14,56,57

the silver element.

3.5. Water Vapor (WVTR) and Oxygen (OTR) Trans-
mission Rate. The WVTR or water vapor permeability
(WVP) and OTR are two important factors for storage
stability (shelf life) and preserving the product quality of
pasteurized food. Figure 5 represents WVTR, WVP, and OTR
measurements of neat LL and LL, LL/PEMa, and LL/EVOH
composites containing 2% w/w AgH1Z. The composite films
containing zeolite reveal an enhancing trend in water vapor
permeability and transmission ability relative to their neat
polymeric films because of the increase in absorption media
(Figure 5a,b). The significance of WVTR and WVP for LL/
EVOH@AgH1Z is higher than that for LL/PEMa@AgH1Z and
LL@AgH1Z samples (P < 0.05). This increase in WVTR and
WVP values of LL/EVOH blends compared to LL has two
reasons: (i) increasing the composite polarity and (ii)
improvement of the AgH1Z dispersion. The former return to
the polar hydroxyl functional groups (high moisture
sensitivity) in the EVOH moiety, which is responsible for
the improved WVTR and WVP. The latter implies to the
compatibility of the LL matrix with AgH1Z in the presence of
EVOH, which makes relatively better dispersion and
distribution of AgH1Z. Besides, the higher WVTR and WVP
values of LL/PEMa blends than those of LL are a result of
better dispersion of AgH1Z, which provides more pathways
and water absorption media.61,67,68
As expected, the incorporation of AgH1Z with the high
surface area into the LL matrix makes an enhancement in the
oxygen transmission rate owing to created oxygen gas
pathways (Figure 5c), which is in accordance with Huang et
al. findings.61 On the other hand, LL/PEMa@AgH1Z shows a
higher OTR value than the LL@AgH1Z sample (P < 0.05).
This returns to the blending of LL with PEMa, which provides
uniform distribution and dispersion of AgH1Z in the polymer
and consequently makes a higher specific surface area.
Subsequently, this will provide more pathways for oxygen gas
absorption and transportation through the polymer composite
film and result in higher OTR values. It is known that the
outstanding characteristic of EVOH as an oxygen gas barrier
makes a 35% decrease in OTR quantity compared to LL@
AgH1Z (P < 0.05, Figure 5c). This is ascribed to the high
barrier properties of the EVOH polymer, creating a barrier
against O2 gas permeability.64,69−71
3.6. Evaluation of Silver Cation Release. The maximum
amount of the allowed silver cation migration to the food
surface has been specified as 0.05 mg Ag+/kg of food, and the
maximum amount of silver-exchanged zeolite A (containing ≤
5% silver) in the polymer matrix has been specified as 10% (w/
w), by European Food Safe Authority (EFSA).72 Therefore,
controlling the migration of silver cations on foodstuff is
necessary for efficient antibacterial activity without side effects
at the moment of consumption for rational and practical
antibacterial packaging. The results of silver cations released
for AgH1Z exhibited that about 13 and 20% of the loaded silver
cations in the zeolite structure (all of the percentages in this
test are based on ICP-OES (solution)) were leached to the
surrounding DI water and acidic aqueous media within 60 min,
respectively. However, the sustained release of Ag+ cations and
a prolonged lifetime can be obtained by incorporating AgH1Z
into the polymer matrix.73 Actually, by immobilizing AgH1Z in
polymer chains, the release amount of Ag+ is controlled
(lower) owing to the resistance of the polymeric matrix for Ag+
diffusion from AgH1Z to the surrounding medium. This result
indicates that the integration of AgH1Z with the polymer
matrix will adjust the releasing content of Ag+ cations and
decrease the side effects associated with the burst release.
Furthermore, a comparative study between composite samples
shows that the LL/EVOH@AgH1Z sample (0.069 mg) has a
higher release amount relative to LL/PEMa@AgH1Z (0.044
mg) and LL@AgH1Z (0.039 mg) samples after 10 days of
incubation in acidic solution (Figure 6). This returns to the
more polarity and hydrophilicity of EVOH compared to the
PEMa and LL polymer matrix, which led to a higher silver
cation release amount. In other words, the tendency of the
hydrophilic polymer to absorb water molecules will provide a
suitable pathway for cation diffusion from zeolite to the
surrounding medium. Moreover, in the presence of inter-
changeable cations (H+ in acidic media), the release amount of
silver is more abundant than DI water. This indicates that
acetic acid acts as an extraction medium and improves the
ionic silver leaching from the prepared composite films. A

4. CONCLUSIONS
Herein, the silver- and hydrogen-exchanged zeolite (AgH1Z)
was incorporated into LDPE/LLDPE and their blends with
ethylene vinyl alcohol copolymer (EVOH) and polyethylene-
graft-maleic anhydride (PEMa) to produce the corresponding
composite samples. BET analysis shows that the specific area
value of zeolite decreased from 32.36 to 13.81 m2/g for AgH1Z
during the ion-exchange process. The loading efficiency of
silver to zeolite for AgH1Z was 43.2 mg/g, 4.23, and 3.9 w/w %
according to the ICP-OES, XRF, and EDX analyses results,
respectively. The results of EDX, XRF, and XRD were
consistent and showed that the Si/Al molar ratio and zeolitic
crystallinity were kept constant during the ion-exchange
process. The results of the color analysis indicate prolonged
color stability for composite samples containing hydrogen-
exchanged zeolite (e.g., ΔE* of 0.11 for LL@AgH1Z)
compared to those without hydrogen (e.g., ΔE* of 13.87 for
LL@AgZ), after UV irradiation. It was found that the addition
of PEMa and EVOH polymers to the LDPE/LLDPE matrix
improves the AgH1Z particle dispersion. A comparison
between the mechanical properties reveals that the composite
films have a higher Young’s modulus relative to their neat
polymer films (P < 0.05). Additionally, the incorporation of
AgH1Z particles has a comparable effect on the tensile strength
of LL/PEMa (17.7 MPa) relative to neat LL (13.9 MPa). The
significance of WVTR for LL/EVOH@AgH1Z (9.3 g/day·m2)
is higher than those for LL/PEMa@AgH1Z (6.2 g/day·m2)
and LL@AgH1Z (5.7 g/day·m2) samples (P < 0.05). Besides, it
was found that LL/PEMa@AgH1Z has a higher OTR value than
the LL@AgH1Z sample, whereas introduction of EVOH to the
LL@AgH1Z sample makes a 35% decrease in the OTR value.
The Ag+ release profile demonstrates that composite samples
containing polar and hydrophilic EVOH (0.069 mg) and PEMa
(0.044 mg) have higher release amounts relative to the LL
(0.039) sample after 10 days of incubation in an acidic
solution. Furthermore, the antibacterial activity observations
exhibit remarkable inhibitory activity for all composite samples
against E. coli and S. aureus bacteria, and accomplished biocide
performance shows a remarkable antibacterial response for
composite samples containing PEMa and EVOH.
