Mechanistic insights into the two-phase synthesis
of heteroleptic Au nanoclusters†
Xiangyu Wang,‡a Shanshan Wang,‡a Shuyu Qian,a Naiwei Liu,a Xinyue Doua and
Xun Yuan
*a,b
A mechanistic study on the two-phase synthesis of heteroleptic Au
nanoclusters (NCs) is reported here. First, the eﬀects of binary
ligands on controlling the size of Au NCs were examined: (1) the
binary ligands could exhibit an eclectic eﬀect on the size control
of Au NCs if the binding aﬃnities of such hetero-ligands with Au
are comparable and (2) the binary ligands could exhibit a competi-
tive eﬀect on the size control of Au NCs, and the size of the Au
NCs could be determined by the ligand with stronger binding
aﬃnity to Au. This ﬁnding is interesting and can shed some light
on the design of new functional metal NCs. Secondly, the for-
mation mechanism of the heteroleptic Au NCs that originated from
the
complex
precursors
was
unprecedentedly
studied.
The
complex precursors of the heteroleptic Au NCs were identiﬁed to
be the predominant hybridized ligand#1–Au(I)–ligand#2 species,
which is helpful for understanding the synthetic mechanisms in
depth. Moreover, the growth processes of the heteroleptic Au NCs
were also monitored, and some fundamental perceptions about
the growth pathway and the structures of the Au NCs were
obtained.
Homoleptic (i.e., mono-ligand-protected) and heteroleptic (i.e.,
bi-ligand-protected) metal nanoclusters (NCs) have attracted
considerable
attention
in
the
fields
of
catalysis,1–7
environment,8,9 energy,10–15 and biomedicine16–19 owing to
their intriguing molecule-like properties, such as quantized
charging,20 intrinsic chirality,21–23 and photoluminescence.24–30
For example, various homoleptic metal NCs protected by
diﬀerent ligands (e.g., thiol,15,31,32 selenol,33,34 phosphine,35,36
alkyne,37,38 and carbene29) have achieved great progress in syn-
thesis and application,39–51 and heteroleptic metal NCs with a
precisely controlled proportion of hetero-ligands (e.g., invol-
ving phosphine/alkyne ligands) to metal atoms have also been
reported.52–58
Nevertheless,
compared
to
the
successful
advancement of homoleptic metal NCs, the same cannot be
said of heteroleptic metal NCs due to their relatively compli-
cated synthesis process as well as the possible incompatibility
of binary ligands. In fact, heteroleptic metal NCs may be even
more important in some fundamental and applied fields than
homoleptic metal NCs because of their richer yet modifiable
surface
functionalities
and
improved
physiochemical
properties.55,58–61 There is, therefore, a pressing need to funda-
mentally understand the synthesis of heteroleptic metal NCs,
which can be beneficial to their advancement in method
design, NC synthesis, and applications.
In addition, two-phase based synthetic strategies were very
eﬃcient for NC synthesis with a precise control of size, compo-
sition, etc.34,62–66 This was because the two-phase system
might provide more possible ways to control the synthetic
kinetics of metal NCs (e.g., through interfacial engineering).
Numerous two-phase based strategies have been designed to
synthesize metal NCs in the past two decades.34,52–59 Despite
the progress in the advancement of the two-phase synthesis of
metal NCs, a deep understanding of the two-phase synthesis
of metal NCs, especially heteroleptic metal NCs, was crucial.
Herein, we report a mechanistic study on the two-phase syn-
thesis of heteroleptic Au NCs. First, we aimed to examine the
influence of binary ligands on controlling the size of Au NCs
and identified both eclectic and competitive eﬀects of the
binary ligands on the size control of NCs depending on their
binding aﬃnities with Au. Particularly, if the binary ligands
(i.e., ligand#1 and ligand#2) had a comparable binding aﬃnity
with Au, the as-synthesized heteroleptic Au NCs would have an
average size between those of the homoleptic Au NCs due to
the eclectic eﬀect of the binary ligands. In contrast, if ligand#1
had an apparently stronger binding aﬃnity with Au than
ligand#2, then the size of the heteroleptic Au NCs could be
determined by ligand#1 because of its competitive eﬀect. In
addition, the composition or forms of the complex precursors

study might shed light on the understanding of the two-phase
synthesis of heteroleptic metal NCs, stimulating more studies
on the design and application of metal NCs with engineerable

In this study, heteroleptic Au NCs were synthesized by the
recently developed “miscible-solvent-assisted two-phase syn-
thesis” method.34 This method provided an ideal platform for
us to study the eﬀect of binary ligands on the size control of
heteroleptic metal NCs because of their totally similar syn-
thetic conditions as that of homoleptic metal NCs within the
two-phase system. Briefly, a toluene solution containing two
types of hydrophobic ligands, an aqueous HAuCl4 solution,
and an aqueous NaOH solution were separately added into a
mixed solvent of water/DMF (dimethylformamide) to form
ligand–Au(I)
complexes,
followed
by
the
introduction
of
additional toluene solvent and an aqueous NaBH4 solution to
initiate the reduction of ligand–Au(I) complexes in the two-
phase system, thereby leading to the formation of high-quality
heteroleptic Au NCs in the organic phase within 3 hours.
Detailed synthetic procedures can be found in ESI.†
Binary ligands could govern the size of heteroleptic Au NCs
in an eclectic or competitive manner. As a proof-of-concept,
the eclectic eﬀect of the binary ligands on the size control of
Au NCs was demonstrated by using 1-adamantanethiol (Adm)
and benzeneselenol (PhSe) as the ligand pair. With respect to
the ligand pair selection, the ligands used in this study must
meet the following two requirements: (i) they should have a
functional group with a relatively strong binding aﬃnity to the
hydrophilic metal species for complexation and (ii) they
should have a hydrophobic entity to ensure a phase transfer
from water to organic phase. Moreover, the ligand pairs must
be able to synthesize homoleptic Au NCs with a distinct size
under the same two-phase synthetic condition, which was
essential for the mechanistic study of the two-phase synthesis
of heteroleptic Au NCs. The size control of homoleptic Au NCs
could be achieved by regulating the reduction kinetics of the
ligand–Au(I) complexes. Upon increasing the miscible solvent
in the synthetic system, larger ligand–Au(I) complexes that
were easier to be reduced could be formed, the hydrolysis of
NaBH4 could be promoted, and the encounter probability
between hydrophobic ligand–Au(I) complexes and hydrophilic
NaBH4 could be enhanced.34 On this basis, the introduction of
diﬀerent amounts of the miscible solvent in the two-phase
system could provide size-tunable homoleptic Au NCs.
In this study, Adm_PhSe-protected Au NCs together with
the Adm- and PhSe-protected Au NCs as references were syn-
thesized under the same synthetic conditions. These three Au
NC species were first examined by using UV–vis absorption
spectroscopy. As shown in Fig. 1a, Adm-protected Au NCs
(black curve) exhibit a shoulder absorption peak at 570 nm,
which resembles the characteristic absorption of thiolated
Au23 NCs.67 In contrast, Adm_PhSe-protected Au NCs (red
curve) and PhSe-protected Au NCs (blue curve) displayed
almost featureless absorption peaks, which indicated that two
such NC species were either polydispersed or monodispersed
but with a larger size.68 Subsequently, we performed trans-
mission electron microscopy (TEM) to roughly check the size
of such samples, and the TEM images (Fig. S2a–c†) showed
that the size of the Adm-protected, PhSe-protected Au NCs,
and Adm_PhSe-protected Au NCs were all less than 2 nm,
further corroborating the successful formation of ultrasmall
Au NCs. In addition, Fourier transform infrared spectra (FTIR,
Fig. S3a and ESI I†) of such NCs revealed that the Adm_PhSe-
protected Au NCs combined the characteristics of the Adm-
protected and PhSe-protected Au NCs, suggesting the realiz-
ation of ligand hybridization in Au NCs.

One advantage of this synthetic strategy was that high-
quality size-tunable Au NCs could be obtained by simply
adjusting the proportion of water to DMF in the reaction solu-
tion, which made it a good platform to provide more para-
digms to corroborate the eclectic eﬀect of the binary ligands
on the size control of Au NCs. For example, by changing the
proportion of water to DMF in the abovementioned case,
Au15(Adm)12, Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1, and Au40(PhSe)24 could
be synthesized (Fig. S3b, S4, and S5†).
The size of Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1 NCs was found to be in-
between the size range of Au15(Adm)12 NCs and Au40(PhSe)24
NCs, which further confirmed the eclectic eﬀect of the binary
ligands on controlling the size of the Au core.
Apart from the eclectic eﬀect, some ligand pairs could also
show the competitive eﬀect on controlling the size of the Au
NCs. Herein, cyclohexanethiol (Cyc) was collocated with Adm or
4-methylbenzenethiol (MBT) as a ligand pair for demonstration.
First, the TEM images (Fig. S2d–f†) and the FTIR spectra
(Fig. S3c†) of such samples provided supportive evidences on
the formation of the Au NCs. By using Cyc and Adm as a ligand
pair, Adm-protected Au NCs and Adm_Cyc-protected Au NCs
exhibited similar absorption peaks at 505/570 nm (Fig. 2a, black
and red curves), while Cyc-protected Au NCs exhibited a domi-
nant absorption peak at 570 nm (blue curve), which were the
typical absorption features of thiolated Au15 and Au23 NCs,
respectively.67 As shown in Fig. 2b, a single set of peaks at m/z
of ∼4962 for Adm-protected Au NCs, ∼4753 for Adm_Cyc-pro-
tected Au NCs, and ∼6373 for Cyc-protected Au NCs were
observed in each ESI-MS spectrum, which can be clearly
assigned to Au15(Adm)12, Au15(Adm)6–10(Cyc)6–2, and Au23(Cyc)16
NCs, respectively (see detailed ESI-MS analyses in Fig. S6†).
Diﬀerent from the aforementioned Adm_PhSe-protected Au
NCs (Fig. S4†), Adm_Cyc-protected Au15 NCs had the same core
size as that of Adm-protected Au15 NCs, which manifested that
the size of Adm_Cyc-protected Au NCs was governed by the
Adm ligand instead of the Cyc ligand, revealing the competitive
eﬀect of the binary ligands in determining the size of Au NCs.
The ligand pair of Cyc and MBT also confirmed the com-
petitive eﬀect on the size control of Au NCs. As shown in
Fig. S3d, S7 and S8,† Au23(Cyc)16, Au23(Cyc)14–16(MBT)2–0, and
Au25(MBT)18 can be obtained. The core size of the Cyc_MBT-
protected Au23 NCs was the same as that of the Cyc-protected
Au23 NCs, which implied that Cyc had an overwhelming competi-
tiveness compared to the MBT ligand in controlling the size of
the Cyc_MBT-protected Au NCs, thereby confirming the competi-
tive eﬀect of the binary ligands on the size control of the Au core.
It should be noted that the size of those heteroleptic Au
NCs was unchanged with varying ligand#1-to-ligand#2 ratios.
For instance, when the feeding Adm-to-PhSe ratios were
adjusted from 1 : 7 to 7 : 1 (the total ligand-to-Au ratio was
fixed to 2 : 1) in the synthesis of Adm_PhSe-protected Au41 NCs
(Fig. S9 and S10†), the size of the Au core was unchanged.
Similarly,
the
core

of
Adm_PhSe-protected

Adm_Cyc-protected Au15, and Cyc_MBT-protected Au23 NCs
were all unchanged regardless of the variation in the ligand#1-
to-ligand#2 ratios from 1 : 7 to 7 : 1 (see Fig. S11–S16†).
We proposed that the eclectic or competitive eﬀects of the
binary ligands on the size control of Au NCs have certain corre-
lations with the binding aﬃnity of the ligand to Au. In particu-
lar, if both ligand#1 and ligand#2 had similar aﬃnities with Au,
they might exert an eclectic eﬀect on controlling the size of the
heteroleptic Au NCs, which made the size of the Au NCs to be
compromised compared to their homoleptic Au analogues. On
the other hand, if ligand#1 had a stronger aﬃnity with Au than
ligand#2, then ligand#1 might dominate the size of the hetero-
leptic Au NCs. To prove this assumption, we analyzed the
binding energies between the ligands and Au by using the
density functional theory (DFT)69 at the level of B3LYP/6-311+G
(2d,p) in toluene (see Fig. S17†). The computationally deter-
mined results demonstrated that the binding energies of Adm–
Au(I), PhSe–Au(I), Cyc–Au(I), and MBT–Au(I) were −48.61 kcal
mol−1, −48.19 kcal mol−1, −46.22 kcal mol−1, and −42.23 kcal
mol−1, respectively. As expected, the binding aﬃnity of Adm–Au
(I) was comparable to that of PhSe–Au(I). Owing to the compar-
able aﬃnities, neither Adm ligand nor PhSe ligand could solely
dominate the size of the Adm_PhSe-protected Au NCs, and this
only led to an eclectic result in controlling the size of the Au
NCs, which might be the main reason why the Adm_PhSe pair
had an eclectic eﬀect in this issue. In contrast, the binding
energy of Cyc–Au(I) was weaker than that of Adm–Au(I), but
stronger than that of MBT–Au(I), which made the Adm ligand
competitive in controlling the size of the Adm_Cyc-protected
Au15 NCs and the Cyc ligand competitive in dominating the size
of the Cyc_MBT-protected Au23 NCs.

Another interesting issue is how the heteroleptic Au NCs
formed. It is known that the compositions or forms of the
ligand–Au(I) complexes (e.g., mono ligand#1- or ligand#2–Au(I)
complexes or hybridized ligand#1–Au(I)–ligand#2 complexes)
could dictate the growth pathway of the Au NCs.70 Owing to
the diﬀerences in the complexes, there were several possible
synthetic mechanisms for heteroleptic Au NCs. As shown in
Fig. 3, the first possible mechanism involves the direct
reduction of the hybridized ligand#1–Au(I)–ligand#2 complexes
to the heteroleptic Au NCs; the second possible mechanism
comprises the reduction and incorporation of the ligand#1–Au
(I) and the ligand#2–Au(I) complexes into the heteroleptic Au
NCs; and the third possible mechanism includes two steps: (1)
the reduction of the ligand#1–Au(I) and the ligand#2–Au(I) com-
plexes to the ligand#1- and ligand#2-protected Au NCs, respect-
ively, and (2) the integration of the homo-ligand#1- and
ligand#2-protected Au NCs with the heteroleptic Au NCs.
Considering that the major diﬀerence between the first poss-
ible mechanism and the rest two was the composition of the
complexes of the Au NCs, we analyzed the complexes of those
four bi-ligand-protected Au NCs by using ESI-MS. As shown in
Fig. 4 and S18,† we identified all the species for the complexes
of Au41(Adm)8(PhSe)18 NCs (Fig. 4a), Au15(Adm)6–10(Cyc)6–2
NCs (Fig. 4b), Au23(Adm)5–6(PhSe)11–10 NCs (Fig. S18a†), and
Au23(Cyc)14–16(MBT)2–0 NCs (Fig. S18b†). These species are listed
in Fig. 4c and S18c† (see detailed ESI analyses in Fig. S19–S22†).
Based on the results, it was found that the hybridized ligand#1–
Au(I)–ligand#2 complexes were the dominant species in the com-
plexes of those four Au NCs, suggesting that the synthesis of the
heteroleptic Au NCs might follow the first synthetic mechanism
mentioned above (Fig. 3a).
To further confirm the synthetic mechanism, the investi-
gation of the real-time growth process of the heteroleptic Au
NCs was essential.34,70 The growth processes of these four NC
samples were studied by using the UV–vis absorption spectra
(Fig. S23†) and ESI-MS (Fig. 5). As shown in Fig. S23,† during
the synthesis of the Au41(Adm)8(PhSe)18 NCs, the UV–vis
absorption spectrum dramatically changes immediately after
the addition of NaBH4 (black and red curves), which testifies
the fast reduction of the complex precursors by NaBH4. The

nism of heteroleptic Au NCs.
The growth process of the Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1 NCs was
more straightforward. As shown in Fig. S27,† the UV–vis
absorption spectrum shows a typical feature of the Au15 NCs at
5 min from NaBH4 addition, which is confirmed by ESI-MS
analysis. In particular, the Au15(Adm)10–11(PhSe)2–1 species was
firstly observed at 5 min, and then Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1
species was formed at 40 min. With further extension of the
reaction time, the peak of the Au15(Adm)10–11(PhSe)2–1 species
gradually disappeared in the ESI-MS spectrum, which was evi-
dently diﬀerent from the increasing peak intensity of the
Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1 species. This fact clearly revealed that
the Au23(Adm)13–15(PhSe)3–1 NCs (8e−) was formed by the
growth of the Au15(Adm)10–11(PhSe)2–1 species (4e−).34 Detailed
ESI-MS analysis of the Au15(Adm)10–11(PhSe)2–1 species can be
seen in Fig. S28.†

Conclusions
In summary, we have reported the mechanistic understanding
of the synthesis of heteroleptic Au NCs within the two-phase
reaction system. First, two eﬀects of binary ligands on control-
ling the size of the heteroleptic Au NCs were confirmed based
on ESI-MS analysis and DFT calculations: (1) when the binding
aﬃnities of the binary ligands (i.e., ligand#1 and ligand#2) to
Au were comparable, the binary ligands could exhibit an eclec-
tic eﬀect on the size control of the Au NCs, and the size of the
