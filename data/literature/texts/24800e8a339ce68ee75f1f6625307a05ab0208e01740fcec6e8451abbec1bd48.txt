A B S T R A C T   
Development of novel nanomaterials with enzymatic activity as nanoprobes for cancer cell detection and therapy 
is an essential issue in clinic diagnostics. In this work, due to their low cost and high stability of metal oxides 
nanomaterials we report synthesis of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers via one step and simple hydrothermal 
method as dual-function enzyme with enhanced peroxidase and catalase-like activity. Interaction between 
prepared nanoflowers and H2O2 in aqueous solution studied by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR), X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and UV–vis techniques. Artificial enzymes being increasingly used but little 
has known based on the pH switchable peroxidase-like and catalase-like activities of metals. Here, we report pH- 
switchability for Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers, which at acidic pH (pH < 7) peroxidase catalytic activities 
is dominant in contrast at basic pH (pH > 7) the catalase activities is dominant. Indeed, these nanomaterials 
catalyze H2O2 to hydroxyl radicals at acidic pH and catalyze H2O2 to oxygen at basic condition. It was also found 
that the amount of generated hydroxyl radicals and oxygen was subordinate to the concentration of Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers. Due to peroxidase-like catalytic behavior of proposed nanoflowers a colorimetric 
biosensor for detection of human cervical cancer cells (HeLa cancer cells) at wide detection range from 50 to 5 ×
104 cells/mL with a detection limit as low as 12 cells/mL developed using 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine(TMB) 
as typical peroxidase substrate in the presence of H2O2. Furthermore, the anticancer behavior of Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3 via their intrinsic catalase-like activity and production of O2 supply for photodynamic therapy 
(PDT) is also evaluated. This work paves a way to design the highly active nanozymes not only in development of 
colorimetric biosensors but also in diagnosis and treatment of cancers.   
1. Introduction 
Enzymes are known as the most sophisticated biocatalysts due to 
their high catalytic efficiency and stereo-, chemo-, and regioselectivity. 
The competencies have been applied for the chemical, biological, and 
food industries [1,2]. However, industrial uses and applications of en­
zymes are usually limited because enzymes can be denatured, are sen­
sitive and unstable to environmental factors, and they are expensive to 
produce [3,4]. With the appearance of nanotechnology, mimic enzymes 
nanomaterials with more stability and lower cost have promising 
candidates for artificial enzymes [5,6]. Up to now, various metal nano- 
structured materials such as CeO2 [7,8], Fe3O4 [9], WO3 [10,11], Co3O4 
[12,13], and α -Fe2O3 nanozyme particles [14], have been reported to 
possess intrinsic enzyme mimetic activities similar to those found in 
natural peroxidases or catalases. Some of these metal oxides possess 
intrinsic multiple enzyme-like activity in a different pH condition. For 
instance, Soba´nska and coworkers synthesized ZrO2 Gel with multi- 
enzyme activities [15]. Interaction between a-ZrO2 and H2O2 in 
aqueous solution resulted in formation of various reaction intermediates 
such as superoxide (O2

MFe2O4 (M = Mg, Ni, Cu) magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) were also 
reported to have catalytic activities similar to those of natural enzymes 
such as catalase and peroxidase [16]. To investigate peroxidase activity 
of MFe2O4 (M = Mg, Cu, Ni) MNPs, 2.2′-azino-bis (3-ethylben-

MFe2O4 (M = Mg, Cu,Ni) MNPs could catalyze the oxidation of ABTS by

activity of MFe2O4 (M = Mg, Cu,Ni) MNPs and decomposition of H2O2 
into water and oxygen.

Till now, nanozymes have been established in a wide range of 
practical applications especially in biomolecules detection such as pro­
teins [17], glucose [18], nucleic acids [19], and cancer cell detection 
[20]. Cancer cells detection provides an easy and useful way to monitor 
the progressions of diseases and their relevant biological processes [21]. 
Typical techniques were used for cancer diagnosis including flow 
cytometry [22], magnetic resonance imaging [23], PCR [24] and 
immunohistochemistry [25]. However, most of these methods are high 
cost, time consuming and limited to a few biomarkers. So, it is highly 
desirable to develop a simple, rapid, sensitive and specific methods to 
detection of cancers. Nowadays, the number of cancer survivors is rising 
due to both advances in early detection and treatment [26]. Photody­
namic therapy (PDT) is a biomedical method that employs a photosen­
sitizer, an appropriate excitation light, and oxygen (O2) molecules to 
generate cytotoxic singlet oxygen (1O2) for the treatment of various 
cancers [27–29]. PDT has several advantages over conventional thera­
pies including minimally invasive, low systemic toxicity, the fast healing 
process, portable and relatively low-cost technologies [30,31]. Howev­
er, tumor hypoxia, which is caused by an inadequate oxygen supply, 
decrease the therapeutic efficiency of PDT in clinical application [32]. 
To address this problem, various strategies have been explored for 
enhancing the 1O2 generation, for instance, using perfluorocarbon 
nanoparticles and red blood cells to transport oxygen into tumors [33]. 
As an alternative approach, Liu et al.[34] enhancing the 1O2 generation 
via the catalase-like activity of gold nanoclusters for photodynamic 
therapy against hypoxic cancer cells. PAMAM dendrimer-encapsulated 
gold nanoclusters (AuNCs-NH2) can produce O2 for PDT through their 
intrinsic catalase-like activity. The triggering of catalase-like reaction 
was achieved by protonation of enriched tertiary amines of dendrimers 
in acidic solutions and preadsorption of OH on the metal surface. 
Therefore, by taking advantage of the excellent feature on AuNCs-NH2, a 
simple strategy to self-supply O2 was developed and a great enhance­
ment of the PDT efficacy was resulted. So, development of novel nano­
materials with dual peroxidase and catalase activity is a challenge in 
biosensing, cancer diagnostics and PDT. 
Herein, Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers were prepared using 
Fe3+ and Co2+ via a one-step hydrothermal treatment. The resulting 
nanocomposite were found to be able to exhibit dual enzyme-like ac­
tivity. At alkaline solution Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers act as 
mimic catalase, could catalyze the decomposition reaction of H2O2 into 
water and oxygen. As well as the Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers 
revealed peroxidase-like activity in acidic condition, were tested 
3,3′,5,5-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as the substrate. It should be noted 
that the catalytic activity of prepared hybrid was much higher than Co 
(OH)2 and FeOOH. For further application, cancer cell targeting ligand, 
folic acid (FA), functionalized Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 and FA/Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3 was successfully used for the colorimetric detection of 
cancer cells. The hybrid was successfully employed for sensitive and 
selective detection of cancer cells in the range of 50 to 5 × 104 cells/mL. 
Given the catalase like activity of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3, we employed 
the nanozyme combined with methylene blue (MB) as PDT photosen­
sitizers for photodynamic therapy. We demonstrated the effective light- 
trigger treatments with Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 and the photosensitizer, 
but, negligible toxicity in the dark or without nanozyme. The applica­
tion of mixed metal oxides as effective nanozymes with duel enzyme 
activity by exploiting its pH-tunable for analysis and treatment dis­
cussed. Our research on artificial enzyme for both cancer detection and 
treatment will open a way of nanozymes toward fabrication of micro 
devices for chemical and biological applications.

2.1. Reagents and apparatus

(NH4Fe(SO4)2⋅12H2O), glucose (C6H12O6), urea (C6H12O6), H2O2,

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and Transmission electron

achieved by a spectrophotometer (Varian Cary Eclipse Fluorescence 
Spectrophotometer and a SPECTROD 250-analytikjena spectrophotom­
eter (Germany) respectively. 
2.2. EPR experiments 
Electron paramagnetic resonance measurements were performed 
using a Jeol FA-200 EPR spectrometer operating in the X-band at 9.1 
MHz, and equipped with a cylindrical resonator. The hydroxyl radicals 
generated in the liquid phase were detected by applying the spin trap­
ping technique. 5, 5-Dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) with con­
centration of 20 mM was chosen as a suitable spin trap because of its 
high trapping ability and selectivity toward oxygen-centered radicals. In 
the spin-trapping experiments a pre-determined amount of cerium oxide 
NSs was contacted with a mixture of 1 mL of H2O2/H2O and 10 μL of 
DMPO. 5 µL of liquid sample was inserted into a quartz capillary tube 
with 1.0 mm inner diameter using a micro pipette. The filled capillary 
was then sealed with parafilm and placed in a quartz glass EPR tube of 5 
mm inner diameter (Wilmad LabGlass, 710-SQ-250 M) and inserted in 
the microwave cavity, with all measurements recorded at room tem­
perature unless otherwise stated. 
2.3. Synthesis of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers 
The Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers were synthesized by a facile 
hydrothermal route. Briefly, 0.24 g of CoSO4⋅7H2O was dissolved in 20 
mL of deionized water and then 0.12 g of urea was added into the so­
lution. The mixture was stirred vigorously and then 0.12 g of glucose 
and 700 µL ammonia was added into the solution. The as-obtained 
product was mixed with 10 mL of NH4Fe(SO4)2⋅12H2O (0.04 M) fol­
lowed by addition of 10 mL of phosphotangestic acid solution (0.01 M).

2.5. Peroxidase and catalase activity assay 
The catalytic reaction was performed at 25 ◦C using 0.1 mg/mL Co 
(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 in a reaction volume of 300 µL of PBS buffer (100 
mM, pH = 5.5) with 850 μM 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as 
substrate and 1.24 mM H2O2. The kinetic analysis of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/ 
WO3 with TMB as substrate was performed using Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 
with fixed concentration of H2O2 and varying concentrations of TMB 
and similarly, for H2O2 as the substrate the fixed concentration of TMB 
was used with varying concentration of H2O2. Apparent kinetic pa­
rameters were calculated using Lineweaver-Burk plots of the double 
reciprocal of the Michaelis-Menten equation, 1/V = Km/Vm(1/[S] + 1/ 
Km), where V is the initial velocity, Vm represents the maximal reaction 
velocity, [S] corresponds to the concentration of substrate, and Km is the 
Michaelis constant [35]. 
The catalase-like activity of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 in different pH 
solutions (pH 4.3, pH 7.4, and pH 11.0) was measured in 10 mL buffers 
(0.1 M) with 0.5 mg/mL Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 and 1 M H2O2. Dis­
solved oxygen was monitored after the addition of H2O2 by a Handheld 
meter Oxi 330i/340i (WTW GmbH & Co. KG). To eliminate the intrinsic 
differences between H2O2 decomposition, the same process (control 
experiment) was conducted without the addition of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/ 
WO3 in corresponding buffers. 
2.6. Cell culture, colorimetric detection and cell viability evaluation 
Human cervical cancer cells (HeLa cells) and human embryonic 
kidney cells (HEK 293 normal cells) were grown in Dulbecco’s Modified 
Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) cell culture medium containing 10% fetal 
bovine serum, penicillin (100 U mL−1), and streptomycin (100 mg 
mL−1) under a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37 ◦C. For the 
colorimetric detection, cells were plated into 96-well plate at 37 ◦C for 1 
day. Then, it was washed with PBS (10 mM, pH 7.4), the cells were fixed 
with 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature for 10 min. Next, 
formaldehyde was removed and the cells were again washed with PBS 
and followed by incubation with 30 µL of prepared FA/ Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3 for 2 h. Afterward, each cell well was washed three times 
with phosphate buffer solution (0.1 M, pH = 7.4) to remove unattached 
FA/ Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3. Thereupon, PBS (0.1 M, pH = 5.5, 200 μL) 
containing TMB (0.85 mM) and H2O2 (1.24 mM) was added to each well, 
and incubated for 20 min at room temperature. In final step, the reaction 
was terminated by H2SO4 (0.3 M) and quantitatively measuring were 
performed for color reaction. 
For cell viability evaluation, a total of 3 × 103 HeLa cells were seeded 
in a 96-well plate and then treated with FA/ Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 in 
serum RPMI medium for 20 h at 37 ◦C. For PDT assessment, the cells 
were treated with the photosensitizer of MB for another 4 h. The plates 
were then immediately irradiated by a 1 W LED lamp for 15 min. Next, 
the irradiated cells were incubated for another 24 h, and the cell 
viability was determined by measuring the absorbance intensity at 565 
nm with a micro plate reader.

3.1. Characterization of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers 
Fig. 1 shows the field-emission scanning electron microscopy 
(FESEM) images of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers. It can be 
observed from Fig. 1A and B, the as-prepared products present flower- 
like nanostructures. The nanoflowersare are composed of multilayer 
self-assembled nanosheets with a thickness of 20 nm, indicating its hi­
erarchical structure. In order to further study, the morphology of the as- 
synthesized product, TEM characterization is conducted. Fig. 1C-E re­
veals that the surface of the Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers is 
basically smooth. The EDX (Fig. 1G) and elemental mapping (Fig. 1H-L) 
analysis of typical Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 ternary hybrid sample further 
indicates the presence of cobalt (54.81 wt%), iron (20.69 wt%), tungsten 
(14.29 wt%) and oxygen (10.21 wt%), Moreover, the cobalt, oxygen, 
iron and tungsten elements are homogenously distributed in the selected 
area. 
The X-ray photoelectron spectrum (XPS) survey shown in Fig. 2A 
indicated that the elements Co, Fe, W and O exist in the composite. The 
Co 2p XPS of the composite exhibits two major peaks at 797.1 and 781.1 
eV, respectively, corresponding to the Co 2p1/2 and Co 2p3/2 spin–orbit 
peaks of Co(OH)2 (Fig. 2B) [36]. In Fe 2p XPS spectra (Fig. 2C), two 
peaks are observed with the binding energies of 711.1 and 724.2 eV. 
They are respectively corresponding to Fe 2p3/2 and Fe 2p1/2, suggesting 
that Fe(III) bonds are the main compositions in FeOOH samples [37]. 
The W 4f spectrum revealed the characteristic doublet at binding en­
ergies 35.1 eV, 37.2 eV for the W 4f7/2 and W 4f5/2 of W6+ in WO3, 
respectively (Fig. 2d). The 4f doublet lines are separated by a binding 
energy of 2.1 eV, which is well matched with reported literature [38]. 
Furthermore the peaks observed at 2θ = 25.45◦, 35.93◦, 24.45◦, 
44.06◦, 53.73◦, 56.42◦, 61.16◦and 64.71◦which are consistent with 
FeOOH (JCPDs card number 74-1594) [39].The structures of the as- 
synthesized hybrid was examined by XRD technique. The peaks at 2θ 
= 18.83◦, 30.40◦, 38.34◦and 51.61◦, correspond well with Co(OH)2, 
while the observed peaks at 2θ = 23.57◦, 30.40◦, 24.45◦, 41.04◦, 45.57◦
and 48.35◦in accordance with monoclinic phase of WO3 (JCPDs card 
number 05-0363) [40].

(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers compared to separate components is 
related to combination of H2O2 with metal oxide to producing of OH 
radical through the redox reaction between hydrogen peroxide and 
metal oxide surfaces. Since during the combination of FeOOH with Co 
(OH)2 the surface morphology and active sites of the metal oxide may 
change, so, the affinity of the prepared three components nanoflowers 
toward H2O2 and TMB increased.

could be observed in a concentration dependent manner.It is widely 
accepted that peroxidase mimics catalyze the decomposition of H2O2 to 
produce •OH radicals, Known as the Fenton reaction. To elucidate this 
possible mechanism, hydroxyl radicals (•OH) trapping were considered 
with DMPO as spin trap by EPR [44]. EPR can detect the presence of 
unpaired electrons, so, by EPR-based technique free radicals can be 
recognized quickly and specifically. In order to formation of stable 
agents, spin probes or spine traps was used to react with reactive radi­
cals. For peroxidase enzyme, the spin-trapping reaction in liquid can be 
shown as follows: DMPO + •OH → DMPO-•OH [45]. As can be seen in 
Fig. 4A, without Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 no spins were captured. How­
ever, the •OH radicals generated by interaction of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/ 
WO3 with H2O2, resulting in EPR signal of DMPO-•OH adduct. The 
signal increases with increasing concentration of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 
(Fig. 4A). Fig. 4B presents that when the H2O2 concentration is varied, 
the •OH signal increases, indicating more generation of DMPO-•OH and 
higher catalytic activity of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3. The signal increases 
at decreasing pH, with increasing amounts of DMPO–•OH adducts being 
observed at lower pH when the reaction was performed in the presence 
of a constant amount of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3. The EPR signal of 
DMPO was maximum when the reaction was performed at pH=5 
(Fig. 4C). The ESR spectra of DMPO-•OH showed a quartet pattern in­
tensity and hyperfine coupling constants. Multiple unanticipated peaks 
are appeared in between the characteristic peaks of DMPO-•OH. These 
peaks could be attributed to the nitrosyl radicals arising from the little 
degradation of DMPO trap [15].

3.4. Colorimetric detection of cancer cells 
Thereafter, the intrinsic peroxidase-like activity was applied for se­
lective colorimetric detection of cancer cells. As mentioned, folic acid 
(FA) was adopted as a recognition element to functionalize Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3. FA can effectively trap many tumor cells that overexpress 
folate receptor on the cell membrane [52]. Based on this merit Co(OH)2/ 
FeOOH/WO3 as a transducer probe selectively target cancerous cells via 
attached FA targeting ligand and generate quantitative colorimetric 
signal. The working principle is displayed in Scheme 1. In this assay, we 
chose HeLa cell line (human cervical cancer cells) overexpressing folate 
receptors on the cell membrane, and a normal cell line HEK 293 (human 
embryonic kidney cells) with lack of folate receptors as a control. Firstly, 
the selectivity test was performed by monitoring the color generation 
from the TMB-H2O2 solution catalyzed by Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 using 
HEK 293 as a negative control. The cell lines were grown at 37 ◦C for 1 
day and then incubated with FA/Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 (50 μg mL−1) 
for 2 h. In our cancer cell detection process, the specific generation of 
color was observed in the case of HeLa cells (Fig. 7A), whereas for the 
wells containing HEK 293 cell and buffer medium without any cell lines, 
no significant color change was observed. It could be judged that FA/Co 
(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 could differentiate between target cells and control 
cells. Subsequently, to further evaluate the ability of the nanocomposite 
as a colorimetric probe for quantitative detection of different numbers of 
cancer cells, a suitable amount of FA/ Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 (50 μg 
mL−1) was incubated with various concentrations of HeLa cells in a 96 
well plate. Fig. 7B shows the absorbance spectra changes at 450 nm in 
the presence of varying amounts of HeLa cells from 50 to 50000. The

3.5. Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanocomposite for highly efficient PDT 
As shown in previous part, the Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nano­
composite has intrinsic catalase activity in alkaline solutions. So, the 
application of the proposed nanocomposite as an artificial enzyme and 
methylene blue as photosensitizer was investigated in photodynamic 
therapy process. To study the feasibility of PDT with the self-supplied O2 
based on the catalase-like activity of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3, methyl 
thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay was accomplished. The cytotoxicity of 
Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 (5 µg/mL) and MB (0.5 µg/mL) to HeLa cells was 
examined in both presence and absence of irradiation (Fig. 8A). The 
results reflected that Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 is noncytotoxic, and the MB 
showed negligible cytotoxicity in both dark and light condition. How­
ever, the significant cell death induced by Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 
-mediated PDT was observed. As shown in Fig. 8B, with increasing 
concentration of both Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 and MB the cell viability 
significantly decreased up to ~20%, indicating that the PDT process is 
highly O2-dependent and efficient catalase-like activity of proposed 
nanozyme toward production of O2. Indeed, in photodynamic therapy, 
photosensitizers generate highly cytotoxic reactive oxygen species 
(ROS), particularly singlet oxygen (1O2), under light, which contribute 
to the cytotoxic effects on cancer cells. Therefore, the therapeutic effi­
ciency of PDT could be enhanced via oxygen molecules generation to 
generate cytotoxic singlet oxygen [56]. For more validation of our 
experiment, cell toxicity was investigated applying same concentrations 
of nanomaterials used in tumor photodynamic therapy. As can be seen in 
Fig. 8C the cellular viability losses obtained in these studies were not

alone nanocomposite were no cytotoxic. These results indicated that the

4. Conclusion 
In this work, Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 nanoflowers with multi 
enzyme-like activity has been developed. The prepared nanocomposite 
exhibited peroxidase like activity in acidic solution and also catalase like 
activity in alkaline solution. The peroxidase-like catalytic behavior of Co 
(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 was analyzed using TMB and hydrogen peroxide. 
For investigation of catalase-like functional of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 
the amount of produced oxygen generated form H2O2 decomposition 
was measured with dissolved oxygen meter. As well as decomposition of 
H2O2 was verified through trace the fluorescent signal of terephthalic 
acid- H2O2 system. In the next step, Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 was func­
tionalized with folic acid for rapid, quantitative and, selective colori­
metric detection of cancer cells. Therefore, HeLa cancer cells assay 
showed good response with a range from 50 to 50,000 cells, which even 
the color change observed by the naked-eye. As well as, we used 
catalase-like activity of Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 for PDT against cancer 
cell. As a result, treatments of cancer cells with Co(OH)2/FeOOH/WO3 
and photosensitizers led to a great improvement of the PDT efficacy 
through enhance generation of O2. This work is important for expanding 
the applications of nanomaterials enzyme-mimics for biocatalyst, 
nanosensors, molecular carriers and other clinical diagnostics.
