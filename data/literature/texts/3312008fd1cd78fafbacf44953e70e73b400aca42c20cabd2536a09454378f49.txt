The in situ synthesis of silver nanoclusters inside a
bacterial cellulose hydrogel for antibacterial

An antibacterial Ag nanocluster-based hydrogel (Ag NC@BC) is
prepared by the in situ formation of Ag NCs on the nanofibers of
a natural bacterial-cellulose (BC) hydrogel. The Ag NC@BC exhibits
superior, broad-spectrum antimicrobial performance against both
Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, and has a long-acting
bactericidal eﬃcacy compared to pristine Ag NCs due to its
controlled-release feature for Ag species. Moreover, this fabricated
hydrogel also possesses excellent biocompatibility. All of these
advantages of Ag NC@BC endow it with great potentials in battling
bacterial infections.
Bacterial infections have been a major threat to public health
since the ancient times.1 Among various antibacterial strate-
gies, antibiotics are the most commonly used treatment against
bacterial infections;2 however, their rising issues in treating
drug-resistant pathogens have forced researchers to explore
novel antibacterial agents on drug resistant pathogens.3 In
recent years, Ag-based nanomaterials have emerged as a class
of high-eﬃciency antibacterial agents showing non-drug resistance,
low toxicity and wide-spectrum bacterial killing attributes, and have
been widely used in the treatment of biological scalds, burns,
wounds and other bacterial infections.4–8 Very recently, sub-2 nm
sized Ag nanoclusters (Ag NCs) have been demonstrated to be
promising in battling bacterial infections9–14 due to their ultrasmall
size, high antibacterial activity, rich yet controllable surface
chemistry, and good biocompatibility.15–34 However, the antibacterial
process of Ag NCs is not persistent due to its relatively fast
consumption and internalization by bacteria,4,14 which may
affect the long-term antibacterial activity of Ag NCs. Therefore,
it would be better if we can embed Ag NCs into a specific porous
material and control the release of Ag NCs so as to enhance their
antibacterial durability.
Hydrogels, as water-insoluble, cross-linked, three-dimensional
networks of polymer chains,35 are widely used for loading drugs
due to their high water absorption, large porosity, good bio-
compatibility, nontoxicity, high oxygen permeability, and rich
structural diversity.35–37 More importantly, hydrogel-loaded
drugs or antibacterial agents have been proven to possess
superior controlled release characteristics, rendering hydrogels
as ideal materials for loading antibacterial agents for long-
acting bacteriostasis. Therefore, considerable attention has
been paid for developing antibacterial hydrogel complexes.38,39
While synthetic hydrogels usually require some toxic crosslinking
agents (e.g., acrylic acid and polyvinyl alcohol), their natural
analogues are more attractive from the viewpoint of good
biocompatibility. Bacterial cellulose (BC), as a natural hydrogel,
has numerous advantages such as hydrophilic nature, flexibility,
and good biocompatibility, and is thus considered as a good
candidate for loading Ag NCs for improving the antibacterial
durability.40–42 Unfortunately, such explorations have not yet
been reported in the literature so far.
Herein we report the synthesis of an Ag NC-decorated BC
hydrogel (Ag NC@BC) via the in situ formation of Ag NCs on the
nanofibers of the BC hydrogel. The as-prepared Ag NC@BC
could release Ag NCs over a longer time, exhibiting longer and
better antibacterial activities against both Gram-positive and
Gram-negative bacteria as compared to pristine Ag NCs. In
addition, by inheriting low- or non-toxicity for both Ag NCs and
natural BC hydrogel, the Ag NC@BC also shows superior
biocompatibility. This study is interesting because it further
exemplifies the potential of Ag NCs in practical biomedical
applications.

The in situ synthesis of DHLA-protected Ag NCs on the
surface of the BC hydrogel was conducted according to a
previously reported method with minor modifications.43–46 In
order to obtain the optical and size information of the Ag NCs,
we performed the synthetic experiments in the presence and
absence of the BC hydrogel for Ag NCs@BC hydrogel and pristine
Ag NCs under the same conditions. As shown in Fig. 2a, both the
pristine Ag NCs and Ag NC@BC hydrogel show red color (inset)
and display the same optical absorption features with three
distinct peaks at 327 nm, 428 nm, and 500 nm in their UV-vis
absorption spectra. Such optical features of the samples resemble
the characteristic absorption of DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs,47 which
exclude the possibility of forming large-sized Ag nanocrystals.48 It
is well documented that DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs exhibit strong
red luminescence, and thus we measured the luminescence
property of both the samples to gauge this issue. As shown
in Fig. 2b, both the samples show intensive red emission under
UV light irradiation (inset) with an emission peak at B652 nm
when excited at the maximal 420 nm, which shares similar
luminescence feature for the DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs and thus
provides a supportive evidence for the formation of Ag29 NCs.47 To
corroborate the size information of Ag NCs, transmission electron
microscopy (TEM) and electrospray ionization mass spectrometry
(ESI-MS) were performed. The TEM image (Fig. 2c) shows that the
as-synthesized Ag particles are all below 2 nm in diameter. ESI-MS
results (Fig. 2d) clearly reveal that Ag29 NCs protected by 11 or
12 DHLA molecules are the predominant species in the sample,
which confirms the core size of the Ag NCs being that of Ag29.
A detailed ESI-MS analysis can be found in Fig. S1 (ESI†).
Ag29 NCs in the Ag NC@BC sample are well dispersed in the
BC hydrogel, which is supported by a series of experimental
results. First, as shown in Fig. 3a, while the plain BC hydrogel
shows opalescence, the overall color of the Ag NC@BC is red,
suggesting the homogeneous distribution of Ag NCs in the BC
hydrogel. Moreover, TEM images show that abundant ultra-
small Ag NCs are decorated on the nanofibers (Fig. 3b and
Fig. S2, ESI†). In addition, energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy
(EDS) mapping was also performed to examine the distribution
of Ag NCs on the BC hydrogel (Fig. 3c–f). As shown, the C, O and
Ag elements are uniformly distributed throughout the hydrogel
structure, which suggests that the in situ synthesis of Ag NCs on
the nanofibers was successful.
Another interesting issue is the porosity of Ag NC@BC,
which would directly influence the eﬃcient release of Ag NCs.
As shown in Fig. 3a, the plain BC and Ag NC@BC hydrogel
exhibit similar dimensions, which indicate that the structure of
the BC hydrogel remained intact after decorating it with Ag
NCs. It can be seen from the field emission scanning electron
microscopy results (FESEM, Fig. S3, ESI†) that numerous ultra-
thin nanofibers are entangled with one another to form a 3D
network structure, which could potentially increase the porosity
of Ag NC@BC. Moreover, the structure of Ag NC@BC remained
unchanged after the Ag NC decoration (Fig. S4, ESI†). The
porosity of Ag NC@BC was investigated and compared with
that of the plain BC via a solution substitute method (Fig. S5,

The as-synthesized Ag NC@BC with an optimized amount of
Ag NCs exhibits excellent antibacterial activity against both
Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria. Of note, the amount
of Ag NCs inside BC was optimized with the purpose of enhancing
the antibacterial activity to the maximal extent. By using Gram-
positive Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) as a model bacterium, a
series of Ag NC@BC samples with diﬀerent loadings of Ag NCs
were fabricated for antibacterial tests. As shown in Fig. S6 (ESI†),
the antibacterial activity results show that the inhibition zone
treated with the Ag NC@BC (prepared in a 10 mM AgNO3 solution)
is the largest, which adequately suggests 10 mM as the optimal
concentration of the Ag precursor solution. The corresponding
decoration amount of the Ag species in Ag NC@BC (prepared
under the condition of 10 mM AgNO3 solution) was estimated to
be 11.25 mg g1 via inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry
(ICP-MS). On this basis, the wide-spectrum antibacterial per-
formance of Ag NC@BC was studied by selecting four bacteria
strains as model bacteria including two Gram-positive bacteria
S. aureus and Bacillus subtilis (B. subtilis), and two Gram-
negative bacteria Escherichia coli (E. coli) and Pseudomonas,
and two Gram-negative bacteria Escherichia coli (E. coli) and
Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa). As shown in Fig. 4a,
while the plain BC does not show any antibacterial activity
against such four strains, the fabricated Ag NC@BC displays
excellent bacterial killing performance against both Gram-
positive and Gram-negative bacteria, which is evidenced from
the large inhibition zones generated (marked by the dashed
line), as shown in Fig. 4b.
More interestingly, benefited by the 3D network structure,
Ag NC@BC displays better long-acting antibacterial perfor-
mance than that shown by the pristine Ag NCs. As shown in
Fig. S7 (ESI†), while Ag NCs possess good bacterial killing eﬀect
within 2 h, the as-designed Ag NC@BC hydrogel displays
bacterial killing activity over a much longer period (12 h). The
long-acting antibacterial activity of Ag NC@BC is benefited
from the controlled release of antibacterial Ag species from
the BC hydrogel, which is not shown by the pristine Ag NCs. As
shown in Fig. 4c, B40% of the Ag species are released in the
first 30 min, which corresponds to the release of the physically
adsorbed Ag NCs; while the majority of Ag species (50%) are
gradually released between 2 and 8 h, which corresponds to the
Ag NCs decorated on the surface of the BC nanofibers. Such a
controlled release of Ag species from Ag NC@BC may avoid the
fast consumption of Ag antibacterial species by membrane
adhesion as well as endocytosis of bacteria, which usually
occurs in the case of the pristine Ag NCs as antibacterial agents.
In addition, it should be mentioned that the decoration of Ag
NCs on the surface of the nanofibers of the BC hydrogel is also
helpful for improving the antibacterial activity. As shown in
Fig. S8 (ESI†), we compared the antibacterial eﬃcacy of Ag
NC@BC with the sample prepared by physically immersing the
BC hydrogel into the Ag NC solution (denote as Ag NC/BC), and
found that Ag NC@BC performs better than Ag NC/BC in terms
of antibacterial activity using S. aureus as a model strain. This
result indicates that Ag NCs in Ag NC@BC have a decent
interaction with the nanofibers of the BC hydrogel, which made
the controlled release of Ag species possible, demonstrating the
advantages of the in situ synthesis of Ag NCs on the surface of
BC hydrogel’s nanofibers.
The antibacterial mechanism of Ag NC@BC was also studied.
We performed experiments to check the production of reactive
oxygen species (ROS) in S. aureus treated with Ag NC@BC and
the plain BC (S. aureus treated with ultra-pure water served as a
control group) under room light irradiation or dark conditions.
As seen in Fig. S9 (ESI†), the ROS level of the sample treated
with the Ag NC@BC hydrogel under room light irradiation is
comparable to that under dark condition, which excludes the
possibility that the visible light influences the ROS generation
as well as antibacterial eﬃcacy. Moreover, the ROS levels of
samples treated with the Ag NC@BC hydrogel are much higher
than those with plain BC (approximately 3 folds), which reveals
that the decorated Ag NCs could induce the generation of ROS
and subsequently trigger the antibacterial process.
Another prominent feature of Ag NC@BC is its good bio-
compatibility. As shown in Fig. 5, we conducted the cytotoxicity
assay of Ag NC@BC on the HeLa cells with AgNO3 and water (as
a negative control) for comparison. In such an assay, the HeLa
cells in red color are dead, while those green cells are alive. As
seen, almost all the HeLa cells treated with Ag NC@BC and
ultrapure water are green, which indicates a superior survival
rate, while a large number of cells appear dead when treated with
AgNO3. The results suggest that the Ag NC@BC hydrogel has
excellent biocompatibility, which is crucial and advantageous for
its biological applications.

In summary, we have developed an in situ synthesis strategy
to fabricate an antibacterial Ag NC@BC hydrogel where Ag NCs
were formed on the surface of the nanofibers of a natural BC
hydrogel. DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs were found to be homo-
geneously distributed inside the BC hydrogel. With an optimized
amount of Ag NCs decorating the BC hydrogel, the as-designed Ag
NC@BC showed a superior antibacterial activity against both
Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. More importantly,
Ag NC@BC exhibited good long-acting bacterial killing eﬃcacy
due to its controlled release attribute for Ag species. Moreover,
the ROS production of bacteria was identified as the antibacterial
mechanism of Ag NC@BC. In addition, the Ag NC@BC hydrogel
showed excellent biocompatibility on the HeLa cells. Overall, the
Ag NC@BC could be a good candidate as a high-eﬃciency anti-
bacterial material for the treatment of various drug-resistant
superbugs.
