ABSTRACT: Alzheimer’s disease and tauopathies are partly caused by tau protein fibrillation. The tau’s PHF6/β1 sequence
306VQIVYK311 plays a central role in protein self-assembly, acting as a nucleation center. We aimed to identify new therapeutic
hexapeptides that inhibit tau fibrillation by targeting the 306VQIVYK311 nucleation site. We designed hexapeptide batches derived
from PHF6, with varying sequences but preserving key amino acid positions involved in the tau nucleation process. These
hexapeptides were characterized and classified using a newly optimized biochemical method that we developed. Soluble hexapeptides
were preselected because of their therapeutic potential and were selected by using the in vitro PHF6/β1 fibrillation model.
Hexapeptides strongly inhibiting fibrillation rates at substoichiometric levels were selected as leads. Unlike related peptides classified
as amyloids, the leads were found to be completely innocuous to neuroblastoma cell lines, even at high concentrations. Hexapeptides
selected as leads are promising candidates for therapeutic purposes and can be used to develop molecules for biochemical diagnosis
and probes for positron emission tomography (PET) imaging in the context of tauopathies, particularly Alzheimer’s disease.
■INTRODUCTION
Tauopathies are neurodegenerative pathologies characterized
by dysfunction and aggregation of tau, which is encoded by
MAPT (microtubule-associated protein tau).1 Among them,
Alzheimer’s disease (AD), discovered in 1906,2 is the major
cause of dementia. AD accounts for around 60−80% of cases.
In the United States, approximately 6.9 million people are
predicted to be diagnosed with AD in 2024.3 By 2040, around
13 million people are expected to be diagnosed with
Alzheimer’s in Europe,4 making AD a major public health
issue of the 21st century. This pathology is characterized by
the accumulation of so-called senile plaques (SPs) and
neurofibrillary tangles (NFTs) in the brain.5,6 SPs are
extracellular deposits of beta-amyloid peptide (Aβ) generated
following a faulty cleavage of the transmembrane amyloid
precursor protein (APP).7 NFTs are filaments of approx-
imately 10 nm in diameter resulting from the self-assembly of
tau protein.5 The accumulation of these deposits disrupts
cellular signaling pathways and affects neuronal plasticity,
leading to cellular dysfunction and neuronal death.8,9 These
structures spread throughout the brain, diffusing from one
neuron to another via a prion-like phenomenon.10,11
Consequently, Aβ and tau are considered to be the most
important targets for the development of therapeutic drugs for
AD drugs. Currently, no therapeutic approach to Alzheimer’s
disease has proven to be effective in clinical trials. Early
research focused on developing treatments for the disease
targeting amyloid protein Aβ or tau. Recently, one therapeutic
approach has consisted of the development of anti-Aβ
antibodies, which have shown modest improvements in

Tau belongs to the family of microtubule-associated proteins
(MAPS),14 Under physiological conditions, tau stabilizes
microtubules, playing essential roles in neuronal growth and
intracellular transport.15 Alternative splicing of MAPT’s 16
exons leads to the expression of six isoforms in the human
brain. These isoforms, which include 331 to 441 amino acids,
differ by the presence or absence of N-terminal segments (N1,
N2) and contain three or four microtubule-binding repeat
domains.16 Under pathological conditions, it is assumed that
tau is hyperphosphorylated. The largest isoform (2N4R)
contains 85 potential phosphorylation sites, including several
residues that are particularly interesting because they are
preferentially phosphorylated in AD.17 Tau hyperphosphor-
ylation inhibits the protein’s physiological activity by detaching
it from microtubule surfaces.18 Free cytoplasmic tau tends to
self-associate by adopting distinct structures that appear
specific to different tauopathies.19−22 Tau protein filaments
extracted from pathological brains or induced in vitro23 have in
common the highly amyloidogenic sequence 306VQIVYK311
(PHF6/β1).19 PHF6/β1 alone self-assembles into a stack of
parallel β-sheets, leading to amyloid fibers.24−26 Several studies
have highlighted the role of PHF6/β1 in the formation of tau
amyloid filaments27−29 and the sequence’s deletion results in a
loss of the protein’s ability to aggregate.30 Previous work has
targeted PHF6/β1 via antibodies specific to tau’s microtubule-
binding site.31 Others have developed non-natural peptides
made from D-amino acids32−34 or hybrid molecules such as
naphthoquinones tryptophan .35,36
In this work, we selected new hexapeptides with the aim of
inhibiting tau fibrillation by specifically targeting the 306VQI-
VYK311 (β1) sequence involved in filament nucleation. The
most promising hexapeptides were tested on the neuro-
blastoma cell line SH-SY5Y, and among them, candidates for
biochemical or in vivo diagnostic purposes were identified. Our
results thus pave the way for developing novel therapies or
diagnostics for tauopathies in general and Alzheimer’s disease
in particular.
■RESULTS
Experimental Rationale and Design. New hexapeptides
designed to inhibit tau fibrillation by specifically targeting the
306VQIVYK311 (β1) sequence involved in filament nucleation
were developed. The hexapeptides contained the same amino
acids as β1 (I, K, Q, 2 V, and Y) but arranged in a different
order. Based on 22 published hexapeptides37 and the published
Tau filament structure resolved by cryo-electron micros-
copy,19−22 two sets of 10 potential therapeutic hexapeptides
each were generated. Our goal was to compete with β1−β1
sheet formation and/or the interdigitation of β1−β8 side
chains observed in fiber structures . To achieve this, we
designed a first set of therapeutic peptides as follows: amino
acids in odd positions were fixed, while amino acids in even
positions were varied relative to β1 or the opposite (peptides
0tp to 9tp). For the second set of therapeutic peptides, we
reversed the sequence of the previous ones, preserving the
relative position of even or odd amino acids (0tpi to 9tpi). All
hexapeptides identified as soluble, i.e., neither aggregative nor
amyloidogenic, were selected and tested using the in vitro β1
fibrillation model and the neuroblastoma cell line SH-SY5Y.
Diversity of the 42 Starting Hexapeptides. First, the
presence of our peptides in known proteins across species was
determined. The amino acid sequence, target species, and the
protein distributions of 20 new hexapeptides are shown in
Table 1 (data for 22 pioneer hexapeptides designed initially
(2p to 23p) are reported).37 We identified matches to two
peptides in vertebrates (1tp, 4tpi), one in Nematoda (5tp),
three in viral proteins (2tp, 5tp, 6tpi), four in fungi (8tp, 9tp,
3tpi, 4tpi) and eight in bacteria (3tp, 4tp, 5tp, 6tp, 1tpi, 5tpi,
6tpi, 8tpi). Six hexapeptides were not detected among protein
sequences referenced in Prosite (0tp, 7tp, 0tpi, 2tpi, 7tpi, 9tpi).
Eight hexapeptides were found in only one protein (3tp, 4tp,
6tp, 8tp, 8tp, 1tpi, 3tpi, 5tpi, 8tpi), while other candidates are
present in several proteins across species (e.g., 5tp was found
in proteins from Nematoda, virus, and bacteria). Only two
hexapeptides were present in the human proteome (1tp, 4tpi)
and 12 were identified in prokaryotic proteomes (2tp, 3tp, 4tp,
5tp, 6tp, 8tp, 9tp, 1tpi, 3tpi, 5tpi, 6tpi, 8tpi).
The Predicted Amyloidogenicity of Related Hexapep-
tides is Highly Heterogeneous. The amyloid properties of
candidate peptides were predicted as previously described37,38
using PASTA 2.0 and TANGO.39,40 Amyloidogenic features of
42 hexapeptides are shown in Figure 1A. We classified the
candidates into nonamyloid cases (<20%; 3p, 4p, 5p, 6p, 9p,
11p, 16p, 21p, 22p, 23p, 0tp, 4tp, 0tpi, and 4tpi) and groups for
which amyloid property scores were higher (13p), equivalent
(8p, 12p, 17p, 20p, 2tp, 5tp, 6tp, 7tp, 8tp, and 9tp) or lower
(2p, 7p, 10p, 14p, 15p, 18p, 19p, 1tp, 3tp, 1tpi, 2tpi, 3tpi, 5tpi,
6tpi, 7tpi, 8tpi, and 9tpi) than for β1. Hexapeptide sequence
inversion (batch 1 →batch 2) led to a 2-fold reduction of the
amyloid score in those cases that showed a score similar to β1
(2tp, 5tp, 6tp, 7tp, 8tp, and 9tp versus 2tpi, 5tpi, 6tpi 7tpi, 8tpi,
and 9tpi). Conversely, no variation was observed for
hexapeptides showing a low amyloid score (0tp, 1tp, 3tp,
and 4tp versus 0tpi, 1tpi, 3tpi, and 4tpi).
Biochemical Classification for Selecting Soluble
Hexapeptides. To identify hexapeptides possessing ther-
apeutic potential, the biochemical properties of the candidates
were analyzed using a recently published method.37 GD-plot
positioning (Figure 1B) and aggregation threshold determi-
nation (Table 1) identified five amyloid hexapeptides (0tp, 5tp,
7tp, 8tp, and 9tp), whereby 7tp and 5tp showed
amyloidogenicity scores higher than β1 (122 μM−1 and 447
μM−1, respectively; Table 1). Likewise, nine aggregative
hexapeptides (2tp, 3tp, 2tpi, 4tpi, 5tpi, 6tpi, 7tpi, 8tpi, and
9tpi) showed aggregation thresholds below 640 μM (Table 1)
as previously defined.37 Consequently, six additional soluble
hexapeptides (1tp, 4tp, 6tp, 0tpi, 1tpi and 3tpi) were identified
and combined with nine known candidates (3p, 4p, 5p, 6p, 8p,
9p, 13p, 14p, and 23p) for further analyses using the in vitro β1
fibrillation model.

Identification of Six Lead Hexapeptides Efficient at
Substoichiometry. It was observed that 4p gradually
decreased the β1 fibrillation rate with increasing stoichiometry
(Figure 2C,C1), while 4tp strongly inhibited fibrillation (a
decrease of 80 to 90%) at 0.5:1 (Figure 2D,D1). In the
presence of 4p, the final fluorescence intensity after 100 min of
fibrillation at 0.5:1 was found to be twice as high as that of the
control (Figure 2C,C2). As opposed to that, the fluorescence
intensity related to 4tp was approximately three times lower
than that of the control at any ratio (Figure 2D,D2). While in
the case of 4p the fluorescence intensity reached a plateau at a
stoichiometry of 2:1 but not at 0.5:1 or 1:1 (Figure 2C), the
opposite effect was observed for 4tp (Figure 2D). Sub-
sequently, the effect of eight candidate hexapeptides on the
maximum β1 fibrillation rate at stoichiometries ranging from
0.25:1 to 2:1 (Figure 2E) was analyzed. Significant inhibition
of β1 fibrillation was observed at all stoichiometries for five
hexapeptides (4p, 1tp, 4tp, 6tp, and 1tpi), at stoichiometries
>0.5 in two cases (0tpi and 3tpi) and at 2:1 in the case of 5p.
Based on these results, 5p was excluded due to its weak
inhibitory effect, and 4p was excluded due to the elevated
fluorescence intensity at substoichiometric concentrations.
Thus, 6 leads were selected (Table 2).
Lead Hexapeptides’ Effect on Sedimentable Struc-
tures after Long-Term Incubation. While it was obvious
that the lead hexapeptides strongly inhibited the β1 fibrillation
rate, some of them did not seem to reduce the final
fluorescence intensity at low stoichiometry, which suggests
that a cofibrillation process could have occurred. Therefore,
the long-term effect of the lead hexapeptides on β1 fibrillation
was tested at lower stoichiometry. Only 1tp showed lower
fluorescence than the control, while the remaining hexapep-
tides elicited no effect (4tp, Figure 3A) or increased the
fluorescence signal (6tp, 0tpi, 1tpi, and 3tpi, Figure 3A,B). It
was noted that, in the case of the controls, the kinetics
suddenly slowed down at ∼100 min, and the fluorescence at
the plateau tended to slowly decrease during the incubation
time. This made it difficult to measure the bona fide effect of
the hexapeptides. The experiment was, therefore, repeated at
different stoichiometries. It was found that, in the case of 1tp
(at 0.5:1, 1:1, and 2:1), 4tp (1:1), and 3tpi (1:1 and 2:1), the
fluorescence signal was lower than the control (Figure 3C). To
reveal possible long-term effects of the lead hexapeptides on
the formation of β1 amyloid structures, the concentration of
sedimentable structures after 24 h of incubation was measured.
Under these conditions, the quantity of sedimentable
structures was determined to be ∼86 μM for the β1 control.

In Silico Modeling and Leads’ Behavior with Respect
to Tau and Its Structures. To further address the question
of the peptide’s structure/function properties, 4tp behavior in
silico was studied. The results indicate that 4tp can compete
with the folding of the β1-β8 nucleation center, which we
simulated using RaptorX (Figure 5A). Furthermore, 4tp is able
to interact with the R3 and R4 repeat domains, particularly
with the sequences β1, β4, and β8, as well as with the
extremities of Tau’s PHFs (Figure 5B,C).
■DISCUSSION
Although AD was characterized 120 years ago, and key
proteins involved in the etiology of this neuropathology
amyloid APP and tau proteinwere identified 40 years ago,
no effective treatment is currently available. The cascade of
underlying events remains poorly understood, and while
numerous studies have focused on APP and its Aβ peptides
as therapeutic targets,41 tau has more recently attracted interest
in the context of AD.33
Our work aimed at developing novel therapeutic hexapep-
tides for the treatment of AD and tauopathies by specifically
targeting the tau nucleation process, in which the sequence
306VQIVYK311 plays a central role. To this end, hexapeptides
related to the VQIVYK (β1) peptide were produced; they are
composed of the same natural L-amino acids but have different
sequences. In previous work, amyloid hexapeptides were
studied.37 Here, 20 new hexapeptides were generated for
therapeutic purposes: the first batch of 10 hexapeptides (0tp−
9tp), for which key amino acids at odd or even positions were
conserved compared to β1 (306VQIVYK311) and the second
batch of 10 hexapeptides (0tpi to 9tpi) having reversed
sequences. Maintaining the amino acids in either even or odd
positions preserves a hydrophobic surface capable of
interacting with the nucleator peptide β1,24−26 while
simultaneously disrupting the formation of additional regular

Based on these results, the question addressed is whether the
hexapeptides possess consensus sequence features. 1tp and 4tp
share a common sequence of VXIXYX with β1 (1tp and 4tp),
or show the inverted sequences XYXIXV (0tpi, 1tpi, and 3tpi)
or YXIXVX (6tp). The order and alternation of the amino
acids valine-isoleucine-tyrosine appear to be crucial for their
biochemical and cytological properties. We demonstrated that
all selected peptides strongly inhibit β1 in an in vitro model.
This model is the model of choice for selecting inhibitors
because most of the interactions involved in β1 hexapeptide
fibrillation are also important for the nucleation of tau
fibrillation.28 Indeed, these therapeutic hexapeptides directly
interfere with β1 fibrillation by competing both with the lateral
interaction, and therefore with the establishment of parallel β
sheets, and with peptide stacking generated by the
interdigitation of amino acid side chains.
The in silico results confirm that lead hexapeptides could
have a double inhibition effect by targeting both β1−β1
intermolecular interactions as well as β1−β8 intramolecular
interactions involved in nucleation processes during the
progression of Alzheimer’s disease19 or β1−β4 in Pick’s
disease.20 They seem to inhibit fiber growth by interacting with
their ends. Therefore, the studied peptides are also of interest
for treating other tauopathies.
By contrast to previously reported peptides, which were
effective only under overstoichiometric conditions,34,42,43 the
tested hexapeptides strongly inhibit the fibrillation rate even at
substoichiometric concentrations (40−60% and 60−80% at
0.25:1 and 0.5:1, respectively). This clearly indicates that the
selected hexapeptides exert their effect due to their capacity to
bind not only to free target proteins and fiber ends (Figure 5)
but also to early nucleation nuclei composed of just a few
protein subunits. In this scenario, they act like a “grain of
sand,” whose presence halts both the nucleation and
elongation processes. Determining fluorescence intensities
was, a priori, a suitable approach to quantify the effect of
hexapeptides on the fibrillation rate. However, the effect of
hexapeptides on the quantity of fibers formed after long-term
incubation was difficult to reliably measure. Indeed, aberrant
results were obtained due to the precipitation of the structures
over time or the variable fluorescence of Thioflavin T
depending on the structures formed. To circumvent this
technical problem, the quantity of sedimentable structures at
24 h was measured, and most of the hexapeptides were able to
reduce the quantity of sedimentable structures in the long
term.

or no cytotoxicity. Contrary to other glycosylated peptides or
peptides composed of D amino acids or artificial macrocycles
that cause cell death,34,42−44 it was evidenced that the tested
peptides do not induce detectable morphological changes or
cell mortality, even after 48 h of treatment at a concentration
of 100 μM, while β1 and 7tp controls showed strong toxicity. It

■CONCLUSION
By employing the biochemical method previously developed,37
we selected therapeutic peptides for AD and tauopathies that
possess great clinical potential (Table 3). Among the six
selected peptides, several display high stability in biological
environments. Notably, peptides 1tp, 4tp, and 0tpi exhibit an
estimated half-life of approximately 100 h in mammalian
reticulocytes in vitro (ProtParam, ExPASy; see Table 3). These
candidates will therefore be prioritized for in vivo evaluation.
Ongoing work uses the h.Tau40 P301S transgenic mouse
model to study the therapeutic potential of leader peptides.
They are also being used to develop a biochemical diagnostic
test for human cerebrospinal fluid samples. Moreover, the
radiolabeling of these hexapeptides with positron-emitter
radionuclides for Positron Emission Tomography imaging is
under investigation. It should be noted that the present
approach is, in principle, applicable to any amyloid pathology
for which the peptide sequence involved in the fiber nucleation
process has been identified. Future studies will reveal the full
potential of these findings.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Hexapeptide Solubilization and Storage. PHF6 and
derivative hexapeptides were synthesized and lyophilized in the
modified form Ac-[V; Q; I; V; Y; K]-NH2. The degree of
purity was verified using ESI-MS or MALDI-TOF MS by
Proteogenix, and it was greater than 98%. The peptides were
resuspended in sterile Milli-Q water at a final concentration of
∼2 mg·ml−1, vortexed, sonicated for 10 min in a sonicator
water bath (Advantage-Lab AL04−04), and maintained at 4
°C for at least 24 h. Subsequently, the peptides were
sedimented by ultracentrifugation at 100,000g for 15 min at
20 °C (Beckman Coulter, Optima MAX-XR, MLA-50 rotor).
The supernatant was collected, and the peptide concentration
was determined by measuring UV absorption at 280 nm using
a molar extinction coefficient of 1490 M−1 cm−1. Finally, the
samples were aliquoted and stored at −20 °C.

Hexapeptides Preparation. For all experiments, after a
quick defrost, hexapeptide aliquots were prediluted in sterile
Milli-Q water to reach a concentration close to the working
concentration. Prediluted samples were then incubated for at
least 4 h at 25 °C. Hexapeptide concentrations were measured
by UV absorption at 280 nm, as previously described, and
adjusted to 1.25 times the working concentration. For all
experiments, hexapeptide fibrillation was initiated by the
addition of 5X fibrillation buffer (50 mM MOPS, 750 mM
NaCl, pH 7.2), with a ratio of 1 volume of 5X fibrillation buffer
to 4 volumes of hexapeptide. Samples were polymerized for at
least 1.5 to 2 h at 25 °C. Depending on the initial hexapeptide
concentration (less than 1.25 times the working concen-
tration), fibrillation was induced by the addition of 11X (110
mM MOPS, 1650 mM NaCl, pH 7.2) or 21X (210 mM
MOPS, 3150 mM NaCl, pH 7.2) fibrillation buffers, after
adjusting the peptide concentration. When fibrillation was
monitored by spectrofluorimetry, samples were supplemented
with 40 μM Thioflavin T (ThT).
Quantifying the Aggregative Properties of Hexapep-
tides. Hexapeptides at the indicated concentration were
incubated for 60 min at 25 °C in a final 1X fibrillation buffer
(50 mM MOPS, 750 mM NaCl, pH 7.2) and centrifuged at
16,900g 15 min at 25 °C (5425R centrifuge, Eppendorf).
Supernatants were recovered, and the concentrations of
hexapeptides were determined by UV absorption, as described
in the ″hexapeptide preparation” section. The concentrations
of sedimentable hexapeptide structures were then deduced by
calculating the difference between the initial and postsedi-
mentation concentrations. Results were expressed as a
percentage of the working concentration indicated in the
legend (Figure 1B).

tides. Hexapeptides were incubated at 800 μM for 90 min.
Once fibrillation was achieved, 40 μL of the sample was taken
and diluted in 360 μL of 1X fibrillation buffer (10 mM MOPS,
150 mM NaCl, pH 7.2) containing 40 μM Thioflavin T (ThT)
in 0.2 × 1.0 cm thermostated black quartz cuvettes.

Fibrillation was induced using 20 μL of 5X fibrillation buffer.

experiment, a sample of β1 alone was used as a control. The

fluorescence, and/or the quantity of sedimentable structure

Cultured Cell Viability. Cell culture: SH-SY5Y cells were
obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC,
VA). Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle
Medium F12 Nutrient Mixture 1X (HAM; DMEM/F-12)
supplemented with 8% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum
(Biowest), 0.1 mM nonessential amino acids (Glycine,
L-
Alanine, L-Asparagine, L-Aspartic Acid, L-Glutamic Acid, L-
Proline, and
L-Serine; Gibco), and 0.1 mM antimycotic
antibiotics (0.1 mM penicillin, 0.1 mM streptomycin, and 0.1
mM amphotericin; Life Technologies). Cells were maintained
at 37 °C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Cells used for
cell proliferation assays were grown in 24-well plates. Cells
were plated at a density of 6.4 × 104 cells and allowed to attach
for 24 to 48 h.
Cell Treatment. Before cell administration, hexapeptide
solutions prepared at 1000 μM were diluted 1:10 in DMEM/
F12 1X to achieve the required concentration of 100 μM. For
each well, DMEM/F12 1X was removed, and 1 mL of
Dulbecco’s Phosphate Buffered Saline (DPBS) was added to
wash the cells. DPBS was then removed, and 1 mL of each
condition was added; cells were incubated at 37 °C for 24 or
48 h in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. For each
experiment, a water solution was diluted 1:10 in DMEM/
F12 1X as control.
Cell Proliferation. Cell proliferation was measured by
counting the cell number per milliliter using a Neubauer
chamber (Hausser Scientific, Horsham, USA). For each well,
treatments were removed, and 1 mL of DPBS was added to
wash the cells. DPBS was then removed, and 100 μL of 0.5%
Trypsin-EDTA solution (0.05% trypsin in 0.02% EDTA) was
added to each well. The cells were incubated at 37 °C for 3
min in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere. Four hundred
microliters of medium were added to each well to stop the
trypsin action. After homogenization, 12 microliters were taken
and transferred to a Neubauer chamber. The cell number was
calculated as follows:
Number of cells = [(Number of cells in squares used
(excluding the border cells) + Number of cells in the upper
and left sides of squares used)/Number of squares used] × 0.5
mL × 104. Results were normalized, considering that the
control had a result of 100%.
In Silico Analysis of 4tp/6tp Hexapeptide Interaction
with Tau. The β1−β8 structure in parallel double sheets
interacting in a “head-to-tail” manner, as found in PHFs, was
reconstituted in silico using the RaptorX Structure Prediction
server.45−47 For this, we submitted an artificial sequence
alternating the sequences β1-β8 (VQIVYK−HKLTF), sepa-
rated from each other by glycines, for prediction. The number
of intercalating glycines and the number of peptide pairs were
optimized, and the best results were obtained for spacers of 7−
8 glycines with 4 β1−β8 pairs. Then, one, two, three, or four
6tp (YQIVVK) peptides were intercalated into the sequence,
always retaining spacers of 7 glycines. To measure interfacial
contacts between 4tp (VKIQYV) and R3 R4 tau domains, we
used the RaptorX Complex Contact Prediction.47 The
predicted score was calculated by adding the values obtained
between the 6 amino acids of the 4tp peptide and a sliding
window of 6 amino acids from the R3 and R4 repeat domains
of the tau protein. Two interaction modes were considered:
“head-to-tail” interaction (N→C/C→N) and “collateral”
interaction (N→C/N→C). Prediction of the interaction of
the therapeutic peptide 4tp (VKIQYV) with the PHF end was
performed using the PEP-SiteFinder server.48 The results
obtained for the different inhibitory peptides were comparable.
