A simple and sensitive sensor for lactose based on cascade reactions in Au
nanoclusters and enzymes co-encapsulated metal-organic frameworks

A B S T R A C T
In this work, one kind of zeolite imidazole frameworks containing bovine serum albumin stabilized Au na-
noclusters (AuNCs), β-galactosidase (β-Gal) and glucose oxidase (GOx) (AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8) were ob-
tained to detect lactose. Compared with other fluorescent nano-materials, AuNCs show distinct advantages as a
guest species in ZIF-8, specifically their extremely small size (< 1 nm), simple synthesis, excellent biocompat-
ibility and high stability. Furthermore, the bovine serum albumin on their surfaces can promote the formation of
ZIF-8 coating; thus, AuNCs were co-encapsulated in ZIF-8 with the enzymes together. X-ray diffraction (XRD)
analysis indicates the composite possesses the similar crystalline structure with pure ZIF-8. Fluorescence mi-
croscope images, Fourier transform infrared spectra and energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy indicate the
presence of AuNCs in the composite. Owing to the high local concentrations of the fluorescent probe and the
quenching agent in AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8, the quenching rate was enhanced 3.4-fold that of free AuNCs and
enzymes in solution.
1. Introduction
Lactose intolerance is a common genotype that accounts of func-
tional lactose deficiency (phenotype) in adulthood (Ingram, Mulcare,
Itan, Thomas, & Swallow, 2009; Itan, Jones, Ingram, Swallow, &
Thomas, 2010). Up to 65% of the world's population are lactose intol-
erance
(Ugidos-Rodriguez,
Matallana-Gonzalez,
&
Sanchez-Mata,
2018). Lactose intolerance may give rise to flatulence, stomach ache
and diarrhea after ingestion of lactose-containing dairy products.
(Ugidos-Rodriguez et al., 2018). Lactose-free products have been de-
veloped to not only to meet the dietary needs of lactose intolerance
populations, specifically for calcium and high-quality protein, but also
consumer preferences for intake of these nutrients via dairy-type pro-
ducts. Therefore, there is a demand for easy determination of lactose at
low concentrations in lactose-free products (Churakova et al., 2019;
Lopez, Ma, Ludwig, Schuhmann, & Ruff, 2017).
At present, there are several techniques available for the determi-
nation of lactose, such as gas chromatography, ultra-high-performance
chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry and nuclear
magnetic resonance (Churakova et al., 2019). However, most of these
methods require expensive equipment and complicated sample pro-
cesses (Das, Sarkar, Rakshit, & Datta, 2018). Fluorescent detection
methods are more practical and are popular because of their opera-
tional simplicity and speed, sensitivity and selectivity (Chen, Shu, &
Wang, 2016; He et al., 2018). The principles of fluorescent detection are
based on catalytic reactions of β-galactosidase (β-Gal) and glucose
oxidase (GOx). Lactose is first decomposed into galactose and glucose
by β-Gal, and then the resulting glucose is decomposed into gluconic
acid and H2O2, which cause the quenching reactions. These methods
have fatal flaws in widespread applications, such as cost and poor
stability.
The zeolite imidazole framework (ZIF-8) recently reveals unique
advantages in improving the applicability of enzyme (Wu, Ge, Yang,
Hou, & Liu, 2015). On the one hand, ZIF-8 coating can enhance the
stability of enzyme remarkably by simple mixing with the precursor of
ZIF-8 (Della Rocca, Liu, & Lin, 2011; Liang et al., 2015; Zhu et al.,
2019). On the other hand, ZIF-8 coating can enhance the catalytic ef-
ficiency of cascade reaction in multiple enzymes co-encapsulated ZIF-8
owing to the high local concentrations of the reactants in ZIF-8 (Chen,
Vázquez-González, Zoabi, Abu-Reziq, & Willner, 2018). Therefore, ZIF-

Gal and GOx) are co-encapsulated in ZIF-8 coating (AuNCs/β-Gal/

an excellent emission peak at about 650 nm at 375 nm excitation. When
lactose exists in the system, lactose is first decomposed into galactose
and glucose by β-Gal, and then the resulting glucose is decomposed into
gluconic acid and H2O2 by GOx in the presence of O2 in the air. The
reaction of H2O2 and Fe2+ produces hydroxyl radicals, which lead to
the fluorescence quenching of AuNCs (Peng et al., 2019). The detection
of lactose in lactose-free milk drinks samples is realized based on the
above principles.

2.2. Synthesis of the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite
Firstly, the AuNCs were prepared according to the previous report
(Cao, Li, Yue, & Wu, 2013). HAuCl4 aqueous solution (10 mL, 10 mM)
were mixed with BSA aqueous solution (10 mL, 50 mg/mL) at 37 °C
under vigorous stirring of magnetic stirrer. After 5 min, NaOH solution
(1 mL, 1.0 M) was added. After 24 h under vigorous stirring, the color
of the system changed from light yellow to deep brown. Finally, the
mixture was dialyzed in ultrapure water for 2 days to remove unreacted
impurities. Secondly, the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite was
prepared as follows. 1 mL of 2-methylimidazole, 800 µL of AuNCs,
1500 µL of 0.4 mg/mL β-Gal, 1000 µL of 0.5 mg/ml GOx and 1 mL of
Zn(Ac)2 were mixed and reacted for 9 h at room temperature. The
product was collected through centrifuging at 3000 rpm for 15 min and
washing three times with deionized water (Zhu et al., 2019).
2.3. Fluorescent detection of lactose
Potassium phosphate buffer (10 mM) was used to prepare the
AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 solution. The pH of buffer was adjusted to
the required pH with 0.1 M NaOH or HCl solution. The pH was mea-
sured by pH measuring instrument. 0.5 g AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8
was dissolved 10 mL potassium phosphate buffer (10 mM, pH 7.0) to
obtain AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 (50 mg/mL) solution. 5 μL lactose
solutions with different concentration, 50 µL 0.5 mM of Fe2+ solution,
945 μL of AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 (50 mg/mL) solution were mixed
and reacted for 15 min in a 37 °C water bath. Then, the fluorescence
spectra of the solutions were measured under 375 nm excitation.
2.4. Detection of lactose in lactose-free milk drinks
The determination methods of lactose in lactose-free milk drinks
were as follows. The sample was pretreated to eliminate the inter-
ference of glucose. 10 μL GOx (10 U/mL) and 1 mL sample were mixed
and reacted 25 min at room temperature. And then the mixture was
heated to 80 °C for 10 min. Thus, the glucose in milk drinks and the
producing H2O2 were decomposed, and the added GOx lost the activity.
Lactose in lactose-free samples was detected by the standard addition
method. First, different quantities of lactose were added into the milk
drinks, and then the solution was filtered through a membrane

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Preparation and characterization of the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8
composite
Fig. 1 shows the preparation method of the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@
ZIF-8 composite and the detection mechanism of lactose. By the bio-
mineralization effect of the β-Gal, GOx and BSA, the AuNCs/β-Gal/
GOx@ZIF-8 composite was obtained by simple mixing of AuNCs, β-Gal,
GOx, Zn2+ and 2-methylimidazole at room temperature (Liang et al.,
2015).
Fig.
S1
shows
UV–visible
absorption,
photoemission
(λex = 375 nm) and photoexcitation (λem = 650 nm) spectra of of the
AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite. The fluorescence intensity at
650 nm under 375 nm excitation reaches the highest, so the excitation
wavelength is fixed at 375 nm. The fluorescence intensity at 650 nm
can be quenched by hydroxyl radicals from the following reactions
(Peng et al., 2019). Under the catalytic effect of β-Gal/GOx, lactose is
first decomposed into galactose and glucose, which can be decomposed
into gluconic acid and H2O2. Then, hydroxyl radicals come into being
from the Fenton reaction of H2O2 and Fe2+. In order to obtain higher
sensitivity, the dosage of AuNCs, β-Gal and GOx in the synthesis reac-
tion of AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite was optimized. The op-
timal dosage of AuNCs was 800 µL according to the highest fluores-
cence intensity (Fig. S2A). As shown in Figs. S2B and 2C, the dosage of
β-Gal and GOx was optimized to 1500 µL and 1000 µL according to the
highest fluorescence quenching rate.
The
morphology
of
AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8
composite
was
characterized by scanning electron microscope. As shown in the
Fig. 2A, the composite shows a rod-like structure with the average di-
mensions of 0.7 × 9.2 µm. Fig. 2C shows XRD pattern of the pure ZIF-8
and AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite. The XRD pattern of the
composite is consistent with that of the ZIF-8 indicating that the in-
troducing of nanomaterials has no obvious influence on the crystal
structure of ZIF-8 (Ma et al., 2018; Wu et al., 2015). The fluorescence
microscope, FT-IR spectra and EDS studies were carried about to testify
the existence of AuNCs, β-Gal and GOx in the composite. The fluores-
cence microscope image of the composite is shown in Fig. 2B. The
composite exhibits red under the UV excitation, which indicates the
presence of AuNCs. Furthermore, the FT-IR spectra of the composite
prove that AuNCs, GOx and β-Gal exist in the composite (Fig. 2D). The
peak at 1640–1660 cm−1 corresponds to the C]O stretching bands,
indicating the existence of BSA, β-Gal and GOx in the composite (Zhu
et al., 2019). As shown in Fig. S3 and Table S1, EDS analysis reveal the
existence of C, N, O, S, Zn and Au in the composite.
3.2. Catalytic activity
In order to study the reaction mechanism of AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@
ZIF-8 for the lactose detection, the effect of Fe2+ on the fluorescence
quenching was studied. As shown in Fig. 3A, the fluorescence intensity
decreases to a large extent in presence of Fe2+. The enhancement of
quenching degree can be attributed to ·OH from Fenton reaction be-
tween Fe2+ and H2O2 (Peng et al., 2019). In contrast to H2O2, ·OH has
stronger oxidation ability, and can oxidize Au(0) in the AuNCs into Au
(I), thus reduce the fluorescence intensity of the composite (Wang et al.,
2018).
The influence of the ZIF-8 coating on the fluorescence quenching
rate was discussed. As shown in Fig. 3B, the fluorescence quenching
rate of (a) AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 system (62.5%) is 3.4 times that of
(b) the free AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx system (18.20%). To discuss the reasons

3.4. Selectivity and stability of the fluorescence sensor
To verify the selectivity of the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite
as fluorescent probe for lactose, the effects of other sugars (glucose,
mannose, xylose, fructose, maltose, ribose, trehalose, sucrose, cello-
biose, melibiose and galactose) on the detection of lactose were studied.
Fig. 5a shows the fluorescence intensity of the composite in presence of
lactose (750 µM) and other sugars (1500 µM). Glucose can quench the
fluorescence of the system. Concerning the interference of glucose, the
real sample should be pretreated with GOx (see Section 2.4). The
1500 µM of other sugars have no great influence on the signals of
750 µM of lactose. The result demonstrates the high selectivity of the
sensing system of lactose. The long-term storage stability of enzymes is
vital for the practicability of sensor. The relative activity of AuNCs/β-
Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 and the free enzyme system after long-term storage in
PBS 7.4 at room temperature was calculated according to the relative
fluorescence quenching rate (Fig. 5b). After storage of 3 days, the re-
lative activity of the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 and the free enzyme
systems is 76.83% and 5.30% of initial overall activity, respectively.

3.5. The detection of lactose in lactose-free milk drinks samples
In order to test the applicability and practicability of the fluores-
cence sensor, lactose in lactose-free milk beverage samples was de-
tected by the standard addition method. As can be seen from Table S3,
the recovery of lactose ranges from 98.65% to 105.42%, and the re-
lative standard deviation (RSD) is less than 3% (n = 5). These results
indicate that the fluorescence sensor displays good practicability in
detecting lactose-free milk drinks samples.
4. Conclusion
In conclusion, the AuNCs/β-Gal/GOx@ZIF-8 composite as the
fluorescent sensor for lactose was obtained at room temperature. The
composite exhibits excellent emission at 650 nm, which can be quen-
ched by hydroxyl radicals from lactose. Owing to the local high
