Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), an environmentally benign oxidant with burgeoning applications in chemical synthesis, energy storage, 
and water purification, is predominantly produced via the energy-intensive anthraquinone process [1,2]. Solar-driven H₂O₂ synthesis 
from O₂ and H₂O offers a promising alternative [1,3-5], yet conventional photocatalysts (e.g., metal oxides, plasmonic nanoparticles) 
suffer from critical limitations: (1) Uncontrollable reaction pathways where competing one-electron oxygen reduction (generating 
detrimental ·O₂⁻ radicals) compromises H₂O₂ selectivity; (2) Metal-centric active sites with intrinsically limited orbital tunability, 
hindering precise regulation of O₂ activation thermodynamics [4]. Therefore, developing novel photocatalysts with both exclusive 
H2O2 selectivity and superior H2O2 producibility is urgently demanded [3].  
In recent years, ultrasmall metal nanoclusters (NCs), atomically precise aggregates of several to hundreds of metal atoms (typically 
<2 nm) protected by an organic ligand shell, emerge as a novel class of functional nanomaterials [6-8]. Benefiting from their 
interesting molecule-like properties (e.g., photoluminescence (PL) [9,10], chirality, and discrete electronic transitions) and atomic-level 
metal core-surface ligand control over functionality [11-13], metal NCs are widely applied in sensing [14], imaging [15-18], 
biomedicine [6,19,20], energy conversion [21], environmental protection [22], optoelectronics [23,24], and catalysis [25-28]. Among 
these applications, metal NCs harvest intensive research attentions in catalysis because they provide an ideal platform to precisely 
manipulate structure/surface chemistry-dependent application performance based on atomic-level metal core-surface ligand regulation. 
However, conventional NC designs predominantly exploit the metal core for catalysis, while ligands serve merely as stabilizers, a 
significant underutilization of their chemical potential [29]. This paradigm constrains both reaction selectivity (e.g., inability to 
suppress ·O₂⁻ formation) and activity (e.g., poor reactant accessibility to metal sites). 
Herein, we transcend this limitation through bioinspired ligand engineering. Inspired by catechol oxidase enzymes that utilize 
organic cofactors (e.g., tyrosine-quinone motifs) for selective O2 activation, we design dopamine-grafted lipoic acid-protected Au NCs 
(DA-LA-Au NCs for short) as a ligand-centric photocatalytic nanozyme for H2O2 production via photocatalytic O2 reduction (Fig. 1a),

Solar-driven hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) production offers a sustainable alternative to the energy-
intensive anthraquinone process, yet conventional photocatalysts suffer from poor selectivity 
and uncontrollable reaction pathways. Inspired by catechol oxidase enzymes, we design 
dopamine-grafted lipoic acid-protected gold nanoclusters (DA-LA-Au NCs) that shift 
photocatalytic active sites from the metal core to redox-active ligands. This ligand-centric 
architecture leverages dopamine’s reversible catechol/quinone interconversion to gate a 
biomimetic proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET) pathway, enabling exclusive 2e⁻ oxygen 
reduction with near-100% H₂O₂ selectivity while completely suppressing superoxide radical 
(·O₂⁻) generation. Transient absorption spectroscopy and molecular dynamics confirm enhanced 
electron transfer kinetics and O₂ enrichment at ligand sites. Encapsulation in ZIF-8 stabilizes the 
system, achieving a record H₂O₂ yield of 1198 μmol/L under visible light without sacrificial 
agents and a 0.39% solar-to-chemical conversion efficiency, surpassing most reported 
photocatalysts. This "ligand-as-catalyst" strategy merges enzymatic precision with nanocluster 
functionality for sustainable oxidant synthesis.

Instruments: Transmission electron microscope (TEM) images and energy dispersive X-ray (EDX) element mapping were acquired 
on a JEOL JEM 2100F microscope. The UV-visible absorption was recorded on a Shimadzu UV-1800 photospectrometer. Fourier 
transforms infrared (FTIR) spectra were gained from a VERTEX 70 spectrometer. The in-situ FTIR spectra were obtained on the in-
situ diffuse reflectance infrared Fourier transform spectrometer (INVENIO S, Bruker) equipped with a liquid N2 cooled MCT detector 
and a high temperature reaction chamber (Praying Mantis, Harrick).The steady-state photoluminescence (PL) spectra were collected on 
a PerkinElmer FL-8500 fluorescence spectrometer with an excitation wavelength of 567 nm. The PL decay results were collected on a 
photoluminescence spectrometer (FLS-1000, Edinburgh Instruments Ltd.). Transient absorption spectroscopy (TAS) was performed on 
a commercial Ti:Sapphire laser system (Spitfire, Spectra-Physics; 100 fs, 3.5 mJ, 1 kHz). Solution samples in 1 mm path length 
cuvettes were excited by the tunable output of the optical parametric amplifier (OPA) serving as the pump source. The X-ray 
photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis was carried out on an AXIS SUPRA spectrometer using a standard Al Ka X-ray source. 
Electron spin resonance (ESR) measurements were carried out on a Bruker EMX PLUS spectrometer. The electrochemical behaviors 
of the sample were tested by cyclic voltammetry on an electrochemical workstation (Chenhua, CHI760E), with 0.5 mol/L Na2SO4 as 
the electrolyte. 
Density Functional Thoery (DFT) and Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation method and model: Theoretical calculations were 
performed by using spin-polarized DFT calculations package. The exchange–correlation energy was calculated using Perdew-Burke-
Ernzerhof (PBE) functional [30]. Moreover, the DFT-D3 method was employed to calculate the van der Waals (vdW) interaction. The 
semi-core pseudopotential based on density functional theory and the double numerical plus polarization basis set were chosen to 
represent the metal ion cores and the valence electron functions, respectively [31]. The geometry optimization cannot be stopped until 
the energy convergence tolerance is 1 × 10−5 Ha, the maximum force is 2 × 10−3 Ha/Å, and the maximum displacement is 5 × 10−3 Å. 
Subsequently, to account for thermodynamic contributions at finite temperature, the Gibbs free energy (G) was calculated for the 
optimized structures using the harmonic approximation based on vibrational frequency analysis, employing the formula: G = EDFT + 
ZPE − T × S, where EDFT is the total electronic energy obtained from the DFT calculation, ZPE is the zero-point energy correction, S is 
the entropy, and T is the temperature. 
The MD simulation box with an edge length of roughly 4.4 nm was first build, followed by adding 2000 H2O, 100 ligands and O2. 
The COMPASSII force field was selected to describe the nonbond interactions. Before the MD simulation, the energy of all models 
was minimized using the steep descent+ABNR+Quasi-Newton algorithm. Subsequently, the isothermal-isobaric (NPT) ensemble was

Synthesis of DA-LA ligand: DA-LA was synthesized by a simple EDC/NHS crosslinking strategy. In brief, 48.31 mg EDC and 14.5 
mg NHS were added into the 504 µL of methanol containing 5.2 mg LA, followed by introducing 504 µL of MES buffer (prepared by 
dissolving 9.8 mg MES in 504 µL of 0.2 mol/L NaOH solution) and 1.5 mL of methanol. After stirring for 30 min, the pH of the 
solution was adjusted to 7-8 by introducing 100 µL of saturated NaHCO3 solution. Subsequently, 1.26 mL of DA solution (20 mmol/L) 
was introduced into the solution, followed by stirring at room temperature for 6 h. Finally, DA-LA was obtained by the organic rotary 
evaporation of the solution.  
Synthesis of DA-LA-protected Au NCs (DA-LA-Au NCs): 0.025 mmol/L DA-LA (8.6 mg) was added to 15.6 mL aqueous solution 
containing 80 μL NaOH (1 mol/L), followed by stirring until all DA-LA was completely dissolved. Subsequently, 160 μL HAuCl4 
(51.82 mmol/L) was added to the solution and stirred until the solution turned colorless. After that, 320 μL NaBH4 (50 mmol/L) was 
added slowly into the solution under vigorous stirring. Finally, the reaction solution was kept stirring overnight to obtain LA-Au NCs.  
Synthesis of LA-Au NCs: LA-Au NCs were prepared by the same preparation procedure as for DA-LA-Au NCs except for the 
replacement of the DA-LA with LA. 
Synthesis of ZIF-8: The ZIF-8 was synthesized according to the previously reported procedure. In brief, 1.17 g of Zn(NO3)2 was 
dissolved in 8 mL of water to serve as solution A. 22.7 g of 2-Melm was dissolved in 80 mL of water to serve as solution B. 
Subsequently, solution A was dropped into solution B under stirring. After stirring for 10 min at room temperature, the reaction 
solution was transferred into a refrigerator overnight. Finally, the ZIF-8 was collected through centrifugation, followed by washing five 
times with water, and vacuum dried at 30 °C overnight. 
Synthesis of ZIF-8 encapsuled DA-LA-Au NCs (DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8):162.80 μL of Zn(NO3)2 was added into 40 mL DA-LA-
Au NCs (0.5 mmol/L) solution, followed by stirring for 1 h. Afterwards, 1.62 mL of 2-Melm (276.50 mmol/L) was added into the 
solution under stirring. After stirring for 10 min at room temperature, the reaction solution was transferred into a refrigerator overnight. 
Finally, the DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8 was collected through centrifugation, followed by washing five times with water and vacuum 
dried at 30 °C overnight. 
Photocatalytic H2O2 production over the photocatalysts: 5 mL of DA-LA-Au NCs (0.4 mmol/L) was added in a quartz glass reactor, 
followed by bubbling O2 into the solution in the dark for 30 min to achieve the adsorption/desorption equilibrium of O2. After that, the 
solution was exposed to a 300 W Xe lamp (λ ≥ 400 nm) with a power density of 100 mW/cm2. 100 μL of the reaction solution was 
pipetted at given irradiation time intervals (0, 15, 30, 45, and 60 min) to determine the concentration of H2O2, which was quantified 
using the Quantitative Peroxide Assay Kit (aqueous). In addition, XTT was added to the reaction solution as a superoxide radical 
trapping agent to investigate the H2O2 formation pathway. The pH of the reaction system was adjusted with HClO4 or NaOH. The 
solar-to-H2O2 conversion efficiency was calculated as following:  
Ssolar-to-H2O2 conversion efficiency (%) = [ΔG for H2O2 generation (J/mol) × H2O2 (mol)]/[total input power (W) × reaction time 
(s)]×100%    (1) 
where the free energy for H2O2 generation (ΔG) is 117 kJ/mol. The photocatalytic H2O2 production over LA-Au NCs (0.4 mmol/L), 
ZIF-8 (1 mg/mL), and DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8 (1 mg/mL) was tested by the same procedure as for LA-Au NCs.  
In this study, DA-LA-Au NCs were directly synthesized using DA-LA molecules as ligands, necessitating prior preparation of the 
DA-LA molecules. DA was grafted to LA via EDC/ NHS coupling chemistry. Successful formation of DA-LA was confirmed by 
electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (Fig. S1 in Supporting information). Subsequently, DA-LA-Au NCs were prepared through 
a modified "NaOH-mediated NaBH₄ reduction" method [32]. Briefly, an aqueous mixture of HAuCl₄ and DA-LA ligands was 
sequentially treated with NaOH and reductant NaBH₄, followed by 12-h reaction to yield the NCs (Fig. 2a). For comparison, LA-Au 
NCs without DA on the surface were synthesized identically using LA ligands. 
As shown in Fig. 2b, both DA-LA-Au NCs and LA-Au NCs exhibit brown coloration and similar UV-vis spectral features, lacking 
the characteristic surface plasmon resonance (SPR) peak at ~520 nm. This confirms their NCs nature rather than larger plasmonic Au 
nanoparticles [33]. Notably, DA-LA-Au NCs display significantly stronger absorbance than LA-Au NCs, attributable to the intense 
absorption peak of oxidized DA derivatives at 480 nm [34]. This enhanced light-harvesting capability suggests superior photocatalytic 
potential of DA-LA-Au NCs relative to their DA-free counterparts. 
TEM was employed to assess the core dimensions of both Au NCs. TEM images (Fig. 2c and Fig. S2 in Supporting information) 
reveal that DA-LA-Au NCs and LA-Au NCs exhibit sub-2 nm diameters with comparable average sizes of ~1.2 nm. This size 
uniformity confirms that the ligand chemistry (DA-LA vs. LA) negligibly influences Au NC dimensions under identical synthesis 
conditions, attributable to the potent size-confinement effect of thiol groups through strong Au-S bonding. Complementary FTIR 
spectroscopy characterized the interfacial chemistry (Fig. S3 in Supporting information), revealing: (1) Preservation of amide bond 
signatures in DA-LA-Au NCs at 1645 cm-1 (amide I, C=O stretch) and 1550 cm-1 (amide II, N-H bend), which verifies DA-LA ligand 
retention on Au cores; and (2) disappearance of the S-H stretching vibration at 2361 cm-1 alongside the disulfide bond signal at 519 cm-
1, supporting the Au-S bond formation. 
Comprehensive characterization via zeta potential, PL spectroscopy, PL lifetime, XPS, and TAS delineates fundamental distinctions 
between DA-LA-Au and LA-Au NCs. Initial zeta potential analysis reveals DA-LA-Au NCs exhibit near-neutral surface charge (-11.1 
mV), contrasting sharply with the pronounced electronegativity of LA-Au NCs (-45.9 mV, Fig. S4 in Supporting information). This 
divergence originates from hydroxyl-terminated surface versus deprotonated carboxylates on respective NCs. Critically, the anionic

PL emission spectra demonstrate a 2.8-fold quenching in DA-LA-Au NCs relative to LA-Au NCs (Fig. S5 in Supporting

The TAS results of LA-Au NCs and DA-LA-Au NCs are presented in Figs. 2f and g, respectively. Under 365 nm excitation, the 
kinetics at 460 nm for both LA-Au NCs (Fig. 2f) and DA-LA-Au NCs (Fig. 2g) were analyzed. Three decay lifetimes were resolved

Au NCs, the lifetimes were τ1 = 838.2 fs, τ2 = 31.23 ps, and τ3 > 1 ns (Fig. 2i). These components were assigned to internal conversion 
(IC), vibrational relaxation, and electron-hole recombination processes within the NCs [35]. The observed differences in decay 
lifetimes are attributed to energy level modifications induced by the DA ligand. At 560 nm, a distinct rising component with a time 
constant < 15 ps was observed (Fig. 2j), attributed to electron transfer from the Au core to the DA ligand.

The photocatalytic H₂O₂ production performance of DA-LA-Au NCs was evaluated against LA-Au NCs under visible light 
irradiation (λ ≥ 400 nm; 100 mW/cm2) in aqueous solution without sacrificial agents. H2O2 quantification employed a commercial 
peroxide assay kit (Fig. S9 in Supporting information). Pre-optimization of reaction pH revealed a distinctive trend: H₂O₂ yield over 
DA-LA-Au NCs increases significantly with pH elevation (Fig. 3a), contrasting sharply with conventional photocatalysts exhibiting 
pH-inversed activity. This anomalous enhancement correlates with pH-dependent quinone formation (Fig. S10 in Supporting 
information), which provides efficient electron-accepting sites on the NC’s ligand shell. Subsequent experiments were conducted at pH 
8.6-the native pH of DA-LA-Au NCs solutions. Critically, DA-LA-Au NCs achieved 373.99 μmol/L H2O2 within 60 min, 
outperforming LA-Au NCs (161.58 μmol/L) by a factor of 2.31 (Fig. 3b). This 131% enhancement unequivocally demonstrates DA's 
pivotal role in intensifying photocatalytic H2O2 generation. 
Building upon the achievement of high catalytic activity, enhancing catalyst durability became our subsequent objective. This focus 
stems from the recognition that while high activity enables greater H₂O₂ production, the resulting elevated concentrations of H₂O₂ may 
potentially compromise catalyst stability. Consequently, we conducted a long-term photocatalytic H2O2 production test over DA-LA-
Au NCs to evaluate their photostability and durability. As depicted in Fig. S11 (Supporting information), the H2O2 production rate over 
DA-LA-Au NCs increased gradually within the first 3 h but reached a plateau thereafter. This suggests unsatisfied photocatalytic 
durability, potentially linked to structural changes in the DA-LA-Au NCs during the reaction. This structural evolution was 
corroborated by observed changes in their optical absorption (Fig. S12 in Supporting information). 
To mitigate structural degradation and enhance photostability, DA-LA-Au NCs were protectively encapsulated within a porous 
zeolitic imidazolate framework (ZIF-8), forming DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8 (Fig. 3c). TEM (Fig. 3d) and EDX elemental mapping (Fig. 
3e) confirmed the uniform confinement of the DA-LA-Au NCs within the porous ZIF-8 matrix. The photocatalytic H2O2 production 
performance of DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8 was then assessed, with the results shown in Fig. 3f. Notably, the H2O2 concentration 
increased progressively with illumination time, reaching 1198 µmol/L within 7 h. This H₂O₂ yield significantly surpasses those 
reported for other metal NCs-based catalysts in photocatalytic H2O2 production (Table S1 in Supporting information), demonstrating 
the superior efficacy of our design strategy for such catalysts. This approach also offers valuable insights for developing other 
nanomaterial photocatalysts. Moreover, the calculated solar-to-chemical conversion (SCC) efficiency was 0.39%, exceeding that of 
most previously reported photocatalysts (Fig. 3g) [36-51]. This highlights the significant potential of metal NCs for photocatalytic 
H2O2 generation. Furthermore, control experiments confirmed that ZIF-8 itself contributed negligibly to H2O2 production (yielding 
only 12.33 µmol/L). This result unequivocally establishes DA-LA-Au NCs as the dominant active component within the composite.

To elucidate the underlying mechanism for the superior photocatalytic H₂O₂ production of DA-LA-Au NCs, we systematically 
probed the roles of DA-LA ligands. As known, photocatalytic processes fundamentally involve three critical stages: light harvesting, 
charge carrier separation/transfer, and surface reactions [52]. The strategic incorporation of DA-LA ligands in Au NC design 
concurrently optimizes all three aspects: enhanced visible-light absorption via DA-derived chromophores; regulated electron transfer 
through catechol/quinone redox mediation; and accelerated surface reactions by O2 enrichment at ligand sites. This tripartite 
enhancement establishes DA-LA-Au NCs as a paradigm-shifting photocatalyst architecture. 
Specifically, the aforementioned analyses have confirmed their dual function in enhancing light-harvesting capability (Fig. 2b) and 
modulating electron transfer dynamics (Figs. 2d-j and Figs. S5-S8 in Supporting information). To definitively establish whether DA-
LA-Au NCs retain the electrochemical characteristics of free DA, cyclic voltammetry was performed across pH 5–8.6. As shown in 
Fig. 4a, both anodic and cathodic peaks shift cathodically with increasing pH, mirroring the behavior of unbound DA molecules. 
Critically, the formal potential (Ef = (Epa + Epc)/2) exhibits a linear dependence on pH with a slope of −59 mV/pH (Fig. S13). This 
aligns with the theoretical Nernst value (−59 mV/pH per electron) for a two-proton-coupled two-electron redox process 
[53], unambiguously demonstrating that DA-LA-Au NCs inherit DA’s redox behavior. Moreover, the progressive enhancement of PL 
quenching in DA-LA-Au NCs with increasing pH (Fig. S14 in Supporting information) directly correlates with accelerated electron 
transfer from the Au core to DA-LA ligands. This pH-dependent charge redistribution rationalizes the concomitant improvement in 
photocatalytic H2O2 production efficiency. Concurrently, DA-LA-Au NCs exhibit reduced average PL lifetime (τave) under N2 
atmosphere relative to O2-treated counterparts (Fig. 4b). This phenomenon directly correlates with O2-driven oxidation of catechol 
moieties to quinone forms, which enhances interfacial electron transfer and consequently prolongs charge carrier lifetimes. 
Consequently, DA-LA ligands likely function as surface reaction centers to enable photocatalytic O2 reduction via efficient electron-
proton transfer. 
To further validate DA's role in O₂ reduction, in situ FTIR spectra were acquired under contrasting conditions: (1) O2 atmosphere in 
darkness and (2) N2 atmosphere during illumination. Under dark/O₂ exposure, progressive intensification of the C=O stretch (1737 cm-1, 
Fig. 4c) concomitant with attenuation of the -OH vibration (3484 cm-1, Fig. 4d) confirms direct oxidation of catechol moieties to 
quinone by molecular O2. Conversely, illumination under N₂ induces inverse spectral trends -C=O diminution (Fig. 4e) and -OH 
recovery (Fig. 4f), evidencing quinone reduction to catechol by photoinduced electrons. This reversible quinone-catechol 
interconversion directly demonstrates DA-LA ligands functioning as photocatalytic reaction centers for H2O2 synthesis.

Given that ·O2⁻ are critical intermediates in the sequential pathway, we detected ·O2⁻ using DMPO as a spin-trapping agent. As 
shown in Fig. 5a, distinct signals for DMPO-·O2⁻ adducts were observed in LA-Au NC systems. Conversely, these signals were 
negligible in DA-LA-Au NC solutions (Fig. 5a). This demonstrates that surface-bound DA suppresses the one-electron reduction 
pathway, enabling selective (>99%) two-electron O₂ reduction to H2O2. This alignment with DA’s inherent two-electron/two-proton 
redox behavior confirms our design strategy: engineering ligand-anchored reaction centers to enhance photocatalytic activity. 
Two fundamental questions arise regarding the mechanistic shift in H₂O₂ photocatalysis: (1) Why does simple DA functionalization 
redirect the catalytic active site from the metal core to the ligand surface? (2) How does surface-bound DA dramatically enhance H2O2 
production efficiency? To address these questions, we conducted DFT calculations to analyze the photocatalytic pathways on both DA-
LA-Au NCs and LA-Au NCs. First, we computed the O2 binding energies and O2 reduction reaction (ORR) energy barriers over DA-
LA-Au NCs at two potential active sites: metal core vs. DA ligands (Figs. 5b and c). As shown in Figs. 5b and c, O2 adsorption is the 
rate-determining step for H₂O2 production via ORR. The energy barrier for O2 adsorption on the DA-LA ligand (ΔE = 0.57 eV) is 
lower than that on the Au core (ΔE = 0.63 eV). This indicates that O2 adsorption and subsequent reaction are more favorable on the 
DA-LA ligand than on the Au core in DA-LA-Au NCs. These computational results demonstrate that surface-anchored DA functions 
as a molecular switch, redirecting photocatalytic activity from the metal core to the ligand interface. 
Beyond enhancing O2 adsorption, DA ligands facilitate O2 accessibility at reactive sites. To quantify ligand-O₂ contact probability—
a critical factor governing surface reactivity—we computed radial distribution functions (RDFs) via MD simulations. RDF analysis 
provides the normalized probability of locating a molecule at a given radial distance, enabling quantitative assessment of ligand-O2 
interactions. Figs. 5d and e display MD simulation snapshots of LA and DA-LA ligand systems at 298 K in O₂/H₂O environments. 
Corresponding RDFs for O₂ surrounding terminal hydrogen atoms in –COOH (LA) and DA moieties (DA-LA) are presented in Fig. 5f. 
Notably, the RDF for DA-bound H atoms exhibits pronounced peaks at 3.6 Å and 4.9 Å, contrasting with the weaker features observed 
for LA. This demonstrates significantly enhanced O₂ accessibility at DA sites, thereby promoting photocatalytic O2 reduction for H2O2 
generation. Taken together, employing DA-LA ligands as the protective layer for Au NCs confers several key advantages in 
photocatalytic H2O2 production: (1) enhanced visible light absorption; (2) shifting the photocatalytic active sites from the Au core to 
the ligand shell; (3) facilitated transfer of excited electrons from the Au core to the ligands; and (4) enhanced O2 adsorption and 
increased reaction probability at the ligand-centered active sites. Collectively, these advantageous properties of DA-LA-Au NCs 
account for the significant enhancement in H2O2 production.

Based on the preceding analysis, a plausible mechanism and potential pathway for H2O2 generation over DA-LA-Au NCs are 
proposed in Fig. 1a. Specifically, the quinone moieties act as electron acceptors, capturing electrons from the photoexcited NCs. This 
electron transfer facilitates the reduction of quinone to catechol via a coupled two-electron, two-proton process. Subsequently, the 
catechol species can be oxidized back to quinone by molecular O2, simultaneously generating H2O2 through another two-electron, two-
proton pathway. This reversible catechol/quinone redox cycle enables the DA ligands on the NC surface to function as continuous 
reaction centers for photocatalytic H2O2 production. Thus, this shift from metal-centric to ligand-centric photocatalysis circumvents the 
one-electron O2 reduction pathway typically involved in the two-step formation of H₂O₂.

This work unveiled a bioinspired "ligand-as-catalyst" strategy using DA-LA-Au NCs for solar-driven H₂O₂ synthesis. By relocating 
catalytic sites from the Au core to redox-active DA ligands, we achieved exclusive 2e⁻ oxygen reduction with near-100% selectivity, 
completely suppressing ·O2⁻ generation, as verified by ESR. The DA ligands serve as molecular switches, enabling PCET via 
reversible catechol/quinone interconversion, while simultaneously enhancing visible-light absorption and electron transfer kinetics. 
Ligand-engineered nanoenvironments boosted reactant-catalyst collision frequency (validated by MD simulations). Encapsulation 
within ZIF-8 (DA-LA-Au NCs@ZIF-8) stabilizes the system, delivering a record H₂O₂ production of 1198 μmol/L under visible light 
without sacrificial agents and a SCC efficiency of 0.39%, surpassing most reported photocatalysts. This ligand-centric paradigm, 
merging enzyme-like precision with NCs functionality, establishes a versatile platform for sustainable oxidant production and selective 
photocatalysis.
