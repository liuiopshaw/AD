A B S T R A C T   
In this work, the Pt nanoclusters with an average size of 1.7 ± 0.3 nm were prepared by a facile and green 
method using polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) as both stabilizer and reductant. The oxidase-like activity of PVP- 
stabilized Pt nanoclusters (PVP-Pt NCs) was investigated by the catalytic oxidation of o-phenylenediamine 
(OPD). The mechanism studies indicated that PVP-Pt NCs performed as oxidase enzyme mimics by catalyzing the 
dissolved oxygen to generate the reactive oxygen species (singlet oxygen and superoxide anion) intermediates. 
Ascorbic acid (AA) could also be oxidized to dehydroascorbic acid (DHA) which could react with OPD to 
generate a fluorescent compound. On the basis of the robust oxidase-like activity of PVP-Pt NCs, both colori­
metric assay and fluorescence method for AA determination were established. Compared with colorimetric assay, 
the fluorescence method showed higher sensitivity and selectivity for AA detection. The proposed fluorescence 
method with the LOD of 1.17 μM could overcome the interference of most reducing substances and showed 
promising applications in biosensor.   
1. Introduction 
Ascorbic acid (AA), known as vitamin C, is widely found in fruits and 
vegetables. AA plays an important role in human metabolism processes 
such as the metabolism of amino acids, the utilization of metal ions, the 
growth of bones and teeth, the formation of antibodies, and so forth [1, 
2]. The deficiency of AA is closely linked to many diseases like scurvy, 
Alzheimer’s disease, cancer, cardiovascular disease and Parkinson’s

disease [3–6]. Unfortunately, humans can not produce AA themselves 
because of the absence of gulonolactone oxidase. As a water-soluble 
vitamin, AA flows away through metabolism every day. Therefore, the 
AA should be supplemented in daily diet [7]. Owing to its biosafety and 
antioxidant properties, AA is widely used as an additive in beverages, 
food and medicines. Considering the crucial roles of AA in human 
health, food and drug safety, it is significant to develop a fast, conve­
nient and selective method for the determination of AA. 
Traditional methods for AA determination including electrochem­
istry [8], chemiluminescence [9], spectrophotometry [10] and high 
performance liquid chromatography [11], usually suffer from some 
drawbacks such as bulky and expensive instruments, complicated 
operation, time consuming, or insufficient selectivity or sensitivity. 
Fluorescence method has drawn considerable interest due to its 
simplicity and high sensitivity. A large number of fluorescent nanop­
robes such as metal nanoclusters [12], carbon nanomaterials [13] and 
semiconductor quantum dots [14], have been synthesized and used to 
determine AA in recent years. Most of these fluorescence methods are at 
the foundation of the charge transfer between nanoprobes and AA which 
could change the fluorescence intensity of nanoprobes. Hu and co­
workers [15] synthesized high fluorescent carbon dots which were 
employed for AA determination. The Cr6+ ions could quench the fluo­
rescence property of carbon dots. Once introducing AA which could 
reduce Cr6+ to Cr3+, the fluorescence property of carbon dots was 
recovered. 
Because of the low-cost, rapidity and easy detection by naked eyes, 
colorimetric assay has attracted intense attention on biosensing, envi­
ronmental monitoring and food safety [16]. A variety of nanomaterials 
such as metal, metal oxide, and carbon materials, with intrinsic 
peroxidase-like or oxidase-like property have been used for colorimetric 
detection of AA [17–21]. Pt nanomaterials, which possessed 
multi-enzyme mimics including oxidase, peroxidase, catalase and su­
peroxide dismutase, have been widely explored and applied in bio­
sensing, environmental monitoring [22,23]. The monodisperse Pt 
nanoclusters with extremely high enzymatic activities were more 
attractive as biosensors in recent years. However, some expensive tem­
plate molecules such as polyamidoamine dendrimer, protein, heparin 
and yeast extract, were needed to inhibit the growth of nucleation and 
prevent the aggregation of Pt nanoclusters in the synthesis process 
[24–27]. Hence, it’s important to develop a low-cost and simple method 
for preparation of monodisperse Pt nanoclusters. Polyvinylpyrrolidone 
(PVP), a commercial nonionic surfactant with high water solubility and 
high biosafety, is an excellent stabilizer in noble metal nanomaterials. 
PVP could protect Pt nanocrystals from aggregation due to the steric 
hindrance effect of PVP molecules and the Pt-N bond interaction be­
tween PVP and Pt nanocrystals[28]. It’s reported that PVP stabilized Pd 
nanoclusters, Ir nanoclusters and supported Pt nanoclusters have been 
successfully synthesized through the alcoholic reduction method 
[29–32]. Herein, we reported a green method for preparation of Pt 
nanoclusters by employing PVP as both reductant and stabilizer. The 
prepared Pt nanoclusters exhibited high oxidase-like activities, which 
could be used for colorimetric detection of AA. 
The mentioned fluorescence methods and colorimetric assays for 
determination of AA mostly utilized the reducibility of AA to change the 
fluorescence properties of fluorescent nanoprobes or inhibited the color 
change of chromogenic substrates, respectively. Some interferential 
substances with reducibility like glutathione (GSH), cysteine, dopamine 
(DA) and sodium sulfite, could significantly affect the detecting results 
of AA [33]. Therefore, it’s essential to improve the selectivity of fluo­
rescence methods and colorimetric assays for AA detection to meet the 
demand of practical applications. 
In this work, a high selective method for AA detection was estab­
lished by combining the colorimetric signal and the fluorescence prop­
erty of reaction solution based on the oxidase-like activity of Pt 
nanoclusters. The monodisperse Pt nanoclusters were synthesized in 
one-pot reaction by using PVP as both stabilizer and reductant. The 
oxidase-like activity of PVP-stabilized Pt nanoclusters (PVP-Pt NCs) was 
investigated by catalyzing the oxidation of OPD. The AA could also be 
oxidized to dehydroascorbic acid (DHA) by the robust oxidase-like ac­
tivity of Pt nanoclusters. Both the colorimetric assay and the fluores­
cence method for AA detection were developed on the basis of the 
oxidase-like activity of PVP-Pt NCs. The fluorescence method based on 
the specific reaction between DHA and OPD displayed enhanced selec­
tivity for AA detection, which could exclude the interference of reducing 
substances.

2.2. Synthesis of PVP-Pt NCs 
The PVP stabilized Pt nanoclusters (PVP-Pt NCs) were synthesized by 
reduction of H2PtCl6⋅6 H2O in pure water using PVP as both reductant 
and stabilizer. In a typical synthesis procedure, the sodium acetate tri­
hydtrate was added into 40 mL of H2PtCl6⋅6H2O solution (62 mg, 0.12 
mmol) to adjust the pH value of 7.0. Then, 20 mL PVP solution (Mw =
30,000, 2.7 g) was added dropwise under vigorous stirring. After stirring 
for 2 h, the obtained light yellow solution was immersed in oil bath and 
heated to reflux for 4 h at a temperature of 110 ◦C. The obtained black 
colloid was centrifugalized by 15 mL ultracentrifugal filter devices 
(Millipore Amicon Ultra-15, 50 kDa) to remove the residual PVP and 
washed by ultrapure water for several times. The concentration of PVP- 
Pt NCs, which was verified by the inductive coupled plasma optical 
emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES), was adjusted to 1 mg/mL (counted as 
Pt content) for further use. 
2.3. Oxidase-like activity of PVP-Pt NCs

2.4. Detection of AA 
A colorimetric assay for AA was developed based on the inhibition of 
AA to the oxidation of OPD. Experimentally, 10 μL of OPD solution (100 
mM), 10 μL of AA solution with different concentration and 10 μL of 
PVP-Pt NCs dispersion were mixed into sodium phosphate buffer solu­
tion (0.1 M, pH = 4.0) with a final volume of 500 μL. The absorbance 
intensity at 448 nm of reaction solution was monitored after reaction for 
10 min at 75 ◦C. 
The PVP-Pt NCs could catalyze the oxidation of AA due to their ox­
idase mimetic property. Meanwhile, the produced DHA could react with 
OPD to produce a fluorescent production, based on which a fluorescence 
method for selective detection of AA was established. Typically, 50 μL of 
OPD solution (120 mM) and 50 μL of AA solution with different con­
centration were mixed into sodium phosphate buffer solution (2.80 mL, 
0.1 M, pH = 7.0). Then, 100 μL of PVP-Pt NCs dispersion was added to 
catalyze the reaction. The fluorescence intensities at 434 nm and 564 nm 
were monitored by an excited wavelength of 370 nm when the reaction 
solution changed from colorless to yellow. 
The selectivity of the proposed methods for AA detection was carried 
out in the presence of interferential substances like carbohydrates, metal 
ions, and amino acids. The content of AA in lemon soda beverage, 
Vitamin C effervescent tablets and simulated serum was diluted and 
analyzed to evaluate the practical application. 
3. Results and discussion 
The mechanism of the mimetic oxidase property of PVP-Pt NCs and 
the determination of AA were well illustrated in Scheme 1. The as- 
prepared PVP-Pt NCs possessed intrinsic oxidase-like property by cata­
lyzing dissolved oxygen to reactive oxygen species (ROS, singlet oxygen 
and superoxide anion) which could oxide the OPD to OPDox (Fig. S1a). 
The inhibition of the catalytic oxidation of OPD by AA was employed to 
establish the colorimetric assay for AA determination. AA could also be 
oxidized to DHA by ROS intermediates (Fig. S1b), and the DHA could 
react with OPD to form a quinoxaline derivative (QD) (Fig. S1c) with a 
fluorescence emission peak at 434 nm. The fluorescence method for AA 
detection was carried out when the solution changed to yellow color 
which was the signal of the reaction terminal. Comparing with colori­
metric assay, the fluorescence method exhibited enhanced sensitivity 
and selectivity. 
3.1. Characterization of PVP-Pt NCs 
The PVP-Pt NCs were synthesized by one-pot reaction in aqueous 
solution utilizing the PVP as both reductant and stabilizer (Fig. 1a), 
which was a simple, green and economic synthetic procedure. The usage 
of NaBH4, a common reducing agent which is harmful to organisms and 
the 
environment, 
was 
avoided. 
Besides, 
compared 
with 
polyamidoamine dendrimers, heparin and mercaptans, PVP as stabilizer 
was cheaper and more eco-friendly. The color of reaction solution 
gradually changed from light yellow to black after 30 min reflux, indi­
cating the formation of PVP-Pt NCs (Fig. S2). Transmission electron 
microscopy (TEM) images displayed that PVP-Pt NCs were mono­
disperse and mostly quasi-spherical (Fig. 1b and Fig. S3). No agglom­
eration was observed, which could be ascribed to the steric hindrance 
effect of the PVP stabilizer on the surface of Pt nanoclusters. The average 
size of PVP-Pt NCs was approximate 1.7 nm by statistically analyzing 
more than 200 nanoparticles from TEM images (Fig. S4). The high res­
olution TEM (HRTEM) image inset into Fig. 1b displayed that the crystal 
lattice space of PVP-Pt NCs was 0.224 nm, corresponding well to the 
(111) facet of metallic Pt. The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) 
was performed to investigate the chemical states of as-prepared PVP-Pt 
NCs. In the survey spectrum, the strong peaks of Pt 4f, C 1s, N 1s and O 1s 
were clearly observed at 74, 285, 399 and 531 eV, respectively (Fig. S5). 
The C, O and N elements were mainly from PVP molecules. The high 
resolution XPS spectra of Pt 4f were presented in Fig. 1c. The peaks of 
binding energies at 70.7 and 74.1 eV corresponded to the 4f7/2 and 4f5/2 
of metallic species of Pt(0), respectively. The peaks at 71.7 and 76.1 eV 
could be ascribed to the oxidized species of Pt2+ [36]. No Pt4+ species 
were observed in PVP-Pt NCs, indicating the complete reduction of 
H2PtCl6. In the sedimentation experiment (Fig. S6), the PVP-Pt NCs 
dispersion was still uniform and there was no apparent precipitate after 
six months of placement, which suggested the good water soluble of 
as-prepared PVP-Pt NCs. 
3.2. Oxidase-like activities of PVP-Pt NCs 
The oxidase-like activities of PVP-Pt NCs were explored by catalytic 
oxidation of OPD in sodium phosphate buffer solution. As a typical 
chromogenic substrate, the colorless OPD could be oxidized to bright 
yellow 2, 3-diaminophenazine (OPDox), and the reaction equation was 
present in Fig. S1a. As shown in Fig. 2, the colorless OPD solution 
gradually changed to yellow color after adding PVP-Pt NCs. And the 
absorption spectrum of reaction solution showed a maximum absor­
bance intensity at 448 nm, implying the production of OPDox and the 
high oxidase mimetic property of PVP-Pt NCs. Neither color change, nor 
absorption peak at 448 nm was observed in the absence of PVP-Pt NCs, 
demonstrating that the PVP-Pt NCs were accountable for the catalytic 
oxidation reaction of OPD. After catalytic reaction, PVP-Pt NCs was 
centrifugalized and reused by a 4 mL ultracentrifugal filter device 
(Millipore Amicon Ultra-4, 10 kDa). The recycled PVP-Pt NCs remained 
high activity after five cycles reaction (Fig. S7) and the TEM image 
showed no aggregation of the recycled PVP-Pt NCs (Fig. S8), indicating 
the good stability and reusability of PVP-Pt NCs. 
The reaction conditions including temperature, pH and concentra­
tion of substrates, significantly affected the oxidase-like activity of PVP- 
Pt NCs, which was similar to natural enzymes. The effect of pH value

cysteine or GSH also displayed light yellow color, indicating the great 
influence of cysteine or GSH to the accuracy of AA detection. The

their reductive property which inhibited the catalytic oxidation of OPD

reducing substances. 
3.4. Fluorescence detection of AA 
To solve the problem of low selectivity of the colorimetric assay for 
AA detection, a fluorescence method based on the reaction between 
DAH and OPD was developed. The pH value of reaction solution was an 
important factor on the catalytic oxidation of AA by PVP-Pt NCs. The 
maximum absorption wavelength and molar absorption coefficient of 
AA vary with pH value (Fig. S14a). The average catalytic oxidation re­
action rate of AA per milligram of PVP-Pt NCs was calculated by Beer- 
Lambert Law, using 0.1 mM AA in sodium phosphate buffer with 
different pH value as reference solution. As presented in Fig. S14b, the 
oxidation rate of AA increased with the increasing of pH value, and 
displayed highest reaction rate at pH value range of 4.5–5.0. The reac­
tion between 0.1 mM of DHA and 2 mM of OPD was carried out to 
valuate the influence of pH value. The relative activity of reaction 
improved with the increase of pH value, and the reaction maintained 
high activity in the pH range of 6–8 (Fig. S15). The reaction extent be­
tween DHA and OPD determined the fluorescence intensity of reaction 
solution and the AA was unstable under alkaline condition. Therefore, 
the pH value of 7.0 was chosen in the following fluorescence experi­
ments. The influence of reaction time was performed with 0.1 mM of 
AA, 2 mM of OPD and 10 μL of PVP-Pt NCs at a pH value of 7, and the 
results were shown at Fig. S16. The fluorescence intensity at 434 nm 
increased with time, but gradually decreased after 20 min. At the 
beginning, AA was catalytically oxidized to DHA and the produced DHA 
reacted with OPD to generated a quinoxaline derivative (QD) with a 
fluorescence emission peak at 434 nm at the same time. The reaction 
equations were presented at Fig. S1. When AA was completely 
consumed, the PVP-Pt NCs began to catalyze the oxidation of OPD and 
the reaction solution gradually changed to yellow color. The produced 
OPDox could partially absorb the fluorescent light generated by QD, 
which led to the decrease of fluorescence intensity at 434 nm. Besides, 
another fluorescent peak appeared at 564 nm, which was ascribed to the 
OPDox. Interestingly, the sum of the fluorescence intensities (FLs) at 
434 nm and 564 nm almost remained constant (Fig. S17) after 20 min. 
While, the oxidase-like activity of PVP-Pt NCs was low at pH value of 7, 
which left enough time for the reaction between DHA and OPD. 
Therefore the color change could be considered as a signal of the com­
plete reaction between DHA and OPD. 
On the foundation of the color signal and the fluorescence property 
of QD and OPDox, a fluorescence method for AA detection was estab­
lished. As presented in Fig. 5a, the FLs intensity and the concentration of 
AA displayed a good linear correlation with the AA concentration range 
of 2–50 μM with R2 of 0.99551. And the fluorescence spectra of reaction 
solutions were shown in Fig. S18. The LOD for AA was estimated to be 
1.17 μM with equation of LOD = 3.3δ/S, which exhibited good perfor­
mance for AA detection comparing with many previous reports 
(Table S2). The selectivity of the proposed detection method was also 
explored by adding some potential interfering substances such as metal 
ions, amino acids and carbohydrates. As shown in Fig. 5b, the relative 
fluorescence intensity had no significant change comparing to control 
experiment, when the same concentration of interferential substance 
and AA were added together. Without AA, the reaction solution only 
showed a weak fluorescence peak at 564 nm which was ascribed to 
OPDox, and there was no fluorescence peak at 434 nm. Comparing with 
colorimetric assay, the proposed fluorescence method for AA detection 
exhibited higher selectivity which could excluded the interference of 
reducing substances like GSH and cysteine. 
3.5. Detection AA in real samples 
To further evaluate the feasibility of the proposed detection methods 
in practical applications, both fluorescence method and colorimetric 
method for AA detection in real samples were performed. Considering 
the cysteine concentration in healthy people’s serum or plasma is 
200–300 μM, the 5 wt% BSA solution containing 300 μM cysteine was 
used to imitate serum sample. Vitamin C effervescent tablets and lemon 
soda beverage were diluted to the linear range of proposed detection 
methods and analyzed respectively. The colorimetric detection results 
were listed in Table S3. The results of vitamin C effervescent tablet and 
lemon soda samples were consistent well with the specifications with the 
recoveries of 102.80–104.21%. However, the detection results in 
simulated serum (BSA sample) were much higher than adding content, 
which could be put down to the strong influence of cysteine. The results 
of fluorescence method for AA detection were displayed in Table 1. The 
AA contents in simulated serum, vitamin C tablets and lemon soda 
beverage acquired by our experiments were agreement with the speci­
fications. In addition, the recoveries reached to 97.39–101.37% with the 
relative standard deviation (RSD) of 1.12–3.44%. Taken together, the 
fluorescence method with good selectivity and sensitivity was feasible 
and dependable for AA detection in some real samples. 
4. Conclusion 
The PVP-Pt NCs with high oxidase-like activity were synthesized by a 
one-pot and green process utilizing the PVP as both reductant and sta­
bilizer. Mechanistic studies suggested that singlet oxygen and superox­
ide anion radicals generated by catalyzing the dissolved oxygen were 
responsible for the oxidase-like activity of PVP-Pt NCs. The colorimetric 
assay for AA was established and used in some real samples but had poor 
selectivity to reducing substances. To improve the selectivity, a fluo­
rescence method for AA detection based on the color signal of OPDox and 
the fluorescence property of QD was developed, which was free of the 
effect of reducing substances. The successful application on AA detec­
tion in real samples of the proposed fluorescence method demonstrated 
the potential application in pharmacy, food safety and biosensor.
