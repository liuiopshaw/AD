A B S T R A C T
This study developed a visible-light-driven photocatalytic system for efficient dechlorination and detoxification 
of bleaching wastewater, which primarily utilized wastepaper as the raw material. Under simulated natural light 
conditions, the dechlorination rates achieved 98.3% for 4-CP and 94.4% for 2,4,6-TCP within 30 min, and real 
pulp bleaching wastewater was dechlorinated by 97.3%. The system comprises an array formed by assembling 20 
permeable carbon plates (4 cm × 4 cm × 2 mm) as unit modules, and wastewater is pumped to circulate over this 
photocatalytic array. The carbon plates are modified with the semiconductor α-Fe2O3, and melamine was used as 
a nitrogen source for N-doping, while melamine does not convert into g-C3N4 as usual under heating, because the 
presence of cellulose introduces significant N-vacancies and O-substitutions into the structure. As a result, the 
carbon plates exhibit N-doping, N-vacancies and O-substitutions on the N sites, possessing an extremely narrow 
bandgap (Eg = 2.33 eV) and low work function. The formation of a Z-scheme heterojunction between the N- 
doped carbon plate (NCP) and α-Fe2O3 resulted in the composite Fe2O3@NCP, which showed excellent photo­
current response and high photogenerated electron yield. Based on the strong adsorption capacity of the carbon 
plates for chlorophenol pollutants, as well as the targeted attack on C-Cl bonds by e−and •O2
−generated in situ, a 
synergistic effect achieves high electron utilization efficiency for selective dechlorination. The photocatalytic 
system can be reused for 36 cycles, operating for 18 h while maintaining the dechlorination rate above 86.1%. 
This treating system for bleaching effluent introduces no secondary pollution and involves no powdered catalysts 
that are difficult to separate and recover, which can be extended to treat other organochlorine wastewater.
1. Introduction
Adsorbable organic halogens (AOCl), which are biologically toxic 
and carcinogenic organic compounds, are commonly found in waste­
water from the pulp industry, primarily generated during the pulp 
bleaching process through the reaction between decomposition products 
of chlorine dioxide (ClO2) (e.g., HClO) and lignin molecules [1–3]. 
Currently, the industrial emission standard for AOCl is 12.0 ppm in 
China. Due to the optimizations in bleaching technology, most leading 
enterprises are able to meet this standard. However, when the emission 
limits are further tightened in the future, companies will need to pri­
oritize the removal of AOCl. The biological treatment systems in paper 
mills exhibit limited efficiency in removing AOCl, owing to the micro­
bial resistance imparted by the chlorine group (-Cl) in chlorinated 
organic compounds such as chlorophenols (CPs) [4,5], which hinders 
the biological degradation. Therefore, implementing physicochemical 
dechlorination treatment for the bleaching wastewater represents a 
critical strategy for reducing AOCl levels in the effluent of pulp industry.
Physicochemical dechlorination technologies for CPs have garnered 
widespread attention among environmental researchers. Targeting 
either the cleavage of C-Cl bonds or the complete mineralization of 
organic molecules [6], current approaches primarily fall into two cate­
gories: advanced reduction and advanced oxidation. Advanced reduc­
tion processes (ARP), particularly electrocatalytic reduction at the

evolution at the anode can rechlorinate organic intermediates, under­
mining the dechlorination efficiency [11]. As a result, H-cell configu­
rations equipped with a membrane separator between compartments 
have become standard [12]. Yet, the high cost of proton-exchange 
membranes and the associated energy consumption render electro­
catalytic dechlorination economically challenging for practical imple­
mentation. On the other hand, advanced oxidation processes (AOPs), 
such as the Fenton system, are well-established and demonstrate 
consistent performance [13]. The main limitation of AOPs lies in the 
large volume of wastewater to be treated and a large number of electron 
donor coexisting substances interfere with degradation, which requires 
substantial amounts of oxidizing agents, leading to high operational 
costs [14–16]. Photocatalysis driven by visible light offers a promising 
alternative, visible light will not produce chlorine radicals, utilizing 
solar energy and operating at low cost [17]. This technique shows great 
potential for dechlorination. However, a major practical drawback of 
current photocatalytic systems is the use of powdered catalysts, which 
are difficult to recover from the treated water. Its dispersion and recy­
cling not only increase the total energy consumption but also raises the 
concerns about secondary pollution.
To address these challenges, we developed a novel approach by 
utilizing wastepaper from the paper industry with α-Fe2O3 to develop a 
visible-light-responsive catalytic carbon plate for ARP. Furthermore, by 
integrating multiple such plates, we constructed a water treatment de­
vice resembling a solar photovoltaic panel, enabling deep reduction of 
AOCl and rapid dechlorination of CPs in bleaching wastewater accom­
panied by a significant decrease in aquatic microbial toxicity. The bulk 
catalytic material exhibited stable performance over 6 h of continuous 
operation, and its catalytic activity lifespan was extended to 18 h after 
thermal regeneration. The outstanding dechlorination performance of 
the catalyst stems from two main mechanisms: Firstly, the carbon matrix 
enables efficient adsorption of CPs molecules [18,19], thereby promot­
ing the utilization of photogenerated electrons and in-situ generated 
reactive species; secondly, the surface nitrogen defects formed by the 
incorporation of nitrogen into waste-paper-derived carbon can act as 
electron traps within the carbon lattice and as adsorption sites for O2 
[20,21]. Furthermore, the Z-scheme heterojunction formed between the 
nitrogen-doped carbon plate (NCP) (NCP possesses a low work function 
and narrow bandgap) and α-Fe2O3 enables the continuous replenish­
ment of photogenerated h+ in the NCP by electrons from α-Fe2O3, 
effectively suppressing the recombination of photogenerated h+ and e−
pairs in the NCP. The chlorophenols adsorbed on the carbon plate are 
subsequently attacked by e−and in-situ generated •O2
−, facilitating the 
cleavage of the C-Cl bond. And the high oxidative h+ of α-Fe2O3 domi­
nated the further oxidizing degradation of the dechlorinated products. 
Compared with conventional methods, this study features two primary 
innovations. First, a scalable, waste-paper-derived carbon sheet array 
reactor was developed, which achieves superior visible-light utilization 
and stability over powder-based systems while eliminating the need for 
complex catalyst recovery. Second, by engineering spontaneous N-va­
cancies and O-substitution sites within the g-C3N4-like structure of NCP, 
we effectively lowered the work function of NCP (n-type semiconductor 
in the Z-scheme heterojunction), leading to a manifold increase in 
photogenerated electron yield.
In this work, a Z-scheme carbon-based bulk photocatalyst 
Fe2O3@NCP was synthesized via a facile annealing process using 
wastepaper and α-Fe2O3 as precursors. An assemblable and scalable 
visible-light-driven water treatment system was established using a 
modular array of these carbon-based units for the efficient dechlorina­
tion of real pulp bleaching wastewater. Combining XPS, EPR, DFT cal­
culations, etc., we demonstrate that N-vacancy and O-substitution 
engineering optimizes the band structure, while the internal electric 
field-driven Z-scheme pathway significantly enhances visible-light ab­
sorption and carrier separation. In addition, comprehensive evaluations 
involving bioassays, ECOSAR analysis for environmental assessments 
confirm the effective detoxification of 4-chlorophenol and its in­
termediates. This photocatalytic system’s outstanding and stable 
dechlorination performance in actual bleaching wastewater offers 
valuable technical references for advancing cleaner production pro­
cesses in the paper industry.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Chemicals and materials
The details of the chemicals used in this study are introduced in the 
Supplementary Material with Text S1. All purchased reagents were used 
without further purification. Deionized water with a conductivity of less 
than 18.2 MΩ⋅cm was used in all experiments. The pulp bleaching 
wastewater was collected from the bleaching section effluent of a pulp 
and paper mill in Sichuan province of China, and the wastepaper was 
collected from the office paper production workshop. The wastepaper 
was repulped, washed, screened, and then used for the material 
preparation.
2.2. Preparation of α-Fe2O3, g-C3N4, CP, Fe2O3@NCP and NCP
α-Fe2O3: α-Fe2O3 were prepared by simple hydrothermal method 
[22].The detailed fabrication method is described in the Supplementary 
Material with Text S2.
g-C3N4: 2.0 g of melamine was placed in a crucible and heated to 600 
◦C for 1.5 h at a heating rate of 2.5 ◦C min−1 in a muffle furnace. The 
resulting yellow solids were ground into a powder for use.
CP: The carbon plate was prepared by high-temperature annealing of 
a cleaned wastepaper pulp. The detailed procedure is provided in the 
Supplementary Material with Text S2.
Fe2O3@NCP (Fig. S1a): Weigh 1.0 g of melamine and add it to the 
ethanol solution containing 30.0 mg of α-Fe2O3. Stir until thoroughly 
mixed, then added a measured amount of cleaned pulp and mixed uni­
formly with a stirrer. The pulp suspension was poured into the mold for 
filtering, and then dried in an oven at 60 ◦C for 12 h to obtain the pulp 
block material. The pulp block material was heated from room tem­
perature to 600 ◦C at 2.5 ◦C min−1 in N2 atmosphere in a muffle furnace, 
and then kept at 600 ◦C for 1.5 h to prepare wastepaper-based photo­
catalytic carbon material (We simply refer to this nitrogen-doped com­
posite material containing α-Fe2O3 as Fe2O3@NCP).
NCP: The nitrogen-doped carbon material prepared by melamine and 
pulp without α-Fe2O3 is recorded as NCP.

2.4. Photocatalytic experiments and analytical methods
The experiment was carried out using a circulating flow system. 
Based on the investigation of light intensity and the water temperature 
(Fig. S2), the following optimal conditions were applied: 200 mW cm⁻2

equipped with a UV detector. The detection method of 2,4,6-TCP is

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Efficient dechlorination of pulp bleaching wastewater in the 
photocatalytic system
The removal rate of organochlorine reached 97.3% in 30 min 
(Fig. 1a-b and Table 1), demonstrating the rapid and highly efficient 
performance of the visible-light photocatalytic system in this study to 
treat the actual pulp bleaching wastewater (P.B.W.). The acute toxicity 
of bleaching wastewater was also significantly reduced after the pho­
tocatalytic treatment, with the survival rate of E. coli soaring from 16.7% 
to 71.8% (Table 1). The cycling durability of the materials in treating 
real wastewater treatment is excellent. We conducted a continuous 
operation lasting 6 h, during which fresh bleached wastewater was 
added to the system every 30 min. The experimental results showed that 
the dechlorination rate maintained exceeding 90.0% within 6 h 
(Fig. 1b). After washing with anhydrous ethanol and drying at 200 ℃, 
the dechlorination rate remained above 88.9% during a second 6-hour 
cycle (Fig. S3a). In the third 6 h, the dechlorination rate merely 
decreased from 91.1% to 86.1% as well (Fig. S3b). The cyclic stability of 
Fe2O3@NCP is demonstrated by the preservation of its structural and 
chemical integrity after repeated use, as verified by consistent charac­
terization results (Fig. S4-S6). In addition, leaching of Fe ions from 
Fe2O3 did not occur, which further confirmed the structural stability of 
α-Fe2O3 in the material. Furthermore, the practical performance of the 
photocatalytic system was evaluated under natural sunlight for the 
treatment of bleaching wastewater, demonstrating its exceptional solar- 
responsive activity and suitability (Fig. S7).
The material synthesis route in this study was designed based on the 
outstanding photocatalytic performance of Z-scheme α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4 
heterojunction [22,23]. Pulp disintegrated from wastepaper was 
employed as the self-supporting template precursor, with melamine 
serving as a nitrogen source. During the heat treatment in an inert at­
mosphere, the carbohydrates (i.e., cellulose) interfered with the for­
mation process of g-C3N4, and synthesized Fe2O3@NCP with α-Fe2O3. 
Fe2O3@NCP has distinct photocatalytic properties from those of typical 
α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4 (discussed in Sections 3.3 and 3.4). Therefore, the 
α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4@CP composite prepared by combining α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4 
with CP was used as an important comparison for the dechlorination 
performance (Fig. S1b and S8). The experimental results revealed that 
α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4@CP achieved merely 75.4% and 67.5% of dechlorina­
tion rates for 4-CP and the real bleaching wastewater within 30 min; 
obviously, Fe2O3@NCP demonstrated a superior dechlorination per­
formance. This phenomenon suggests that the semiconductor hetero­
junction within Fe2O3@NCP may possess significantly enhanced 
visible-light-harvesting capability and higher photoelectron generation 
compared to α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4.
It is worth noting that Fe2O3@NCP maintains excellent activity for 
dichlorination in the presence of anions and cations such as Cl−, SO4
2−, 
PO4
3−, Na+, Ca2+ and Mg2+ (as shown in Fig. 1c and Table S1). More­
over, it exhibits a broad effective operating pH range, maintaining the 
dechlorination rate higher than 85.0% within the pH range of 5.0−9.0 
(Fig. 1d); when pH = 3.0, the dechlorination rate remains at 83.0%. The 
pH range of 3.0−9.0 encompasses the acid-alkaline boundary (typically 
pH = 3.5−6.0) of bleaching wastewater dominated by ClO2 in sulphate 
pulp production [24], strongly supporting the application potential of 
this system for the detoxification of real bleaching wastewater. The 
studies successfully implementing visible-light photocatalytic systems 
for practical water dechlorination remain relatively scarce, and this 
work even demonstrates an advantage over the recent literature in terms 
of time-efficiency or dechlorination depth for contaminants such as 4-CP 
(Table S2).
Fe2O3@NCP is a composite material with fibrous graphite carbon as 
the framework, having a plate-like shape and excellent water 
permeability. It is like a carbon-made filter membrane and has the 
ability to adsorb organic substances in water. As demonstrated in Fig. 1e 
and Fig. S9a, Fe2O3@NCP exhibits a high adsorption capacity for 4-CP 
(73.0%) and 2,4,6-TCP (80.6%). It should be noted that the C-Cl 
bonds in organics such as 4-CP exhibit stable chemical properties, and 
visible light cannot directly induce dechlorination (Fig. S9b-c). The 
dechlorination rate for Fe2O3@NCP on 4-CP reached 98.3% and on 
2,4,6-TCP reached 94.4% (Fig. S9c and f), showing significantly 
enhanced photocatalytic performance. In contrast, α-Fe2O3 or NCP alone 
demonstrated poor photoactivity, achieving dechlorination rates of 
6.0% and 22.0%, respectively. It should be noted that the removal ef­
ficiency of 4-CP was 100.0%, which also confirmed that the adsorption 
process played an important role in the treatment process.
3.2. Dechlorination is based on an ARP with photoelectron and •O2
−
To understand the key reactive species governing the dechlorination 
process, a series of reactive species scavenging experiments was con­
ducted first. As shown in Fig. 1f, when CCl4 was added to scavenge •O2
−, 
the dechlorination efficiency of 4-chlorophenol was significantly 
reduced. Similarly, adding excess K2Cr2O7 to quench the photo­
generated e−
also markedly diminished the dechlorination rate. 
Conversely, quenching h+ exerted no substantial influence on dechlo­
rination. This indicates that dechlorination is primarily a photoelectron- 
dominated reduction process rather than a holes-dominated oxidative 
degradation pathway. The EPR measurements revealed: •O2
−capture via 
DMPO in methanol and •OH capture via DMPO in water, which 
demonstrated clear generation of •O2
−in the system, while no discern­
ible •OH was detected (Fig. 1g). The absence of •OH should be attrib­
uted to substantially lower redox potential of Fe2O3-h+ than that of •OH 
[25].
This dechlorination process ultimately originates from the genera­
tion of photoelectrons, but •O2
−plays a significant role under natural 
conditions. This is evident from the anaerobic and aerobic comparison: 
with purging O2 in the solution, the degradation rate of 4-CP slightly 
increased, achieving a 98.8% dechlorination rate within 25 min, sur­
passing the 90.4% under anaerobic conditions with N2 purged in the 
solution (Fig. S9h-i). The relatively high dechlorination efficiency 
observed under anaerobic conditions corroborates the direct reduction 
dechlorination process induced by photoelectron. This phenomenon, 
distinct from conventional photocatalytic systems, stems from the 
enrichment of chlorophenols by Fe2O3@NCP, enabling direct transfer 
and migration of photoelectrons through the conductive carbon network 
to attack C-Cl in the chlorophenols adsorbed.
The photogenerated h+ did not induce dechlorination, but it might 
have played an oxidation role, which was reflected in the further 
oxidation degradation of the dechlorinated products. The intermediates 
and products of the 4-CP degradation process were identified by LC-MS 
(Fig. S10 and Table S3), and the possible degradation pathway of 4-CP 
was described in Fig. 1h. It was demonstrated that 4-CP undergoes 
reduction to phenol (P1, m/z = 93.0), then partial molecules are 
oxidized by •O2
−to form a phenoxy radical (PhO•), which is unstable 
and further converts to p-benzoquinone (P2, m/z = 108.0). Subse­
quently, these intermediates undergo ring-opening under attack •O2
−or 
h+ to form small-molecule fragments including fumaric acid (P3, m/ 
z = 115.0), 4-hydroxybutyric acid (P4, m/z = 105.0), and crotonalde­
hyde (P5, m/z = 70.9). These intermediates could be mineralized into 
CO2 and H2O through the synergistic action of h⁺ and •O2
−. The reduction 
in biotoxicity of 4-CP after the photodegradation was assessed via mi­
crobial toxicity assays. The results indicated that the biological inhibi­
tion of Escherichia coli by 4-CP-treated water samples was significantly 
reduced after photocatalytic treatment, with cell survival rates 
increasing from 49.0% to 85.7% (Fig. S12). Furthermore, based on 
quantitative structure-activity relationships, we employed the toxicity 
assessment software tool ECOSAR to predict the potential ecotoxicity of 
4-CP and its degradation intermediates towards three aquatic organisms

3.3. Structural analysis and photochemical properties of the materials
To gain a deeper insight into the physical structure of Fe2O3@NCP 
and its relationship with the visible-light photocatalytic activity, sys­
tematic characterization tests were conducted on its morphological 
features and photoelectrochemical properties. SEM images of 
Fe2O3@NCP reveal that α-Fe2O3 has a uniform hexagonal nanosheet 
structure attached to the smooth surface of N-doped carbon fibers 
(Fig. 2a-d), while the typical g-C3N4 structure with porous network 
characteristics (Fig. S14) was not seen in Fe2O3@NCP. Results of EDS- 
mapping revealed the uniform distribution of C, N, O, and Fe elements 
throughout Fe2O3@NCP (Fig. 2e-f and Fig. S15). HR-TEM images 
(Fig. 2g-h) and the SAED results reveal lattice fringes with spacings of 
0.25 nm and 0.29 nm, corresponding to the (311) and (220) crystal 
planes of α-Fe2O3, respectively (Fig. 2i). N2 adsorption-desorption 
measurements were conducted to evaluate the BET surface area and 
pore size distribution of the prepared samples (Fig. S16 and Table S4). 
The results indicated a significant increase in the BET specific surface 
area of the Fe2O3@NCP composite (179.31 m2 g−1), substantially higher 
than that of CP (110.46 m2 g−1), providing more abundant surface 
adsorption sites. In addition, the relevant pore size distribution curves of 
the samples showed that the material had mesoporous characteristics, 
mesoporous structure can significantly increase the internal surface area 
of photocatalysts, increasing the accessibility of active sites [26–28].
The crystal structure and chemical composition of Fe2O3@NCP were 
further analyzed by XRD and FT-IR. The XRD pattern (Fig. 3a) reveals 
that the diffraction peaks of α-Fe2O3 correspond to those of the hematite 
standard card (PDF No. 39–1346). Peaks corresponding to the (220) and 
(311) crystal planes of α-Fe2O3 are discernible in the XRD pattern of 
Fe2O3@NCP. No characteristic peaks were observed in Fe2O3@NCP at 
13.1◦and 27.6◦, which correspond to the (100) interplanar stacking of 
triazine units and the (002) interlayer stacking of g-C3N4 (PDF No. 
87–1526) [29,30]. This indicates that during the formation of 
Fe2O3@NCP, the presence of carbohydrate (i.e., cellulose) strongly 
interfered with the conversion of melamine to g-C3N4, preventing the 
formation of g-C3N4 with a complete crystal structure. Although the 
crystal structure of g-C3N4 was not formed, its characteristic structure 
still exists. As evidenced by the FT-IR spectrum (Fig. S17), Fe2O3@NCP 
retained the signature chemical structures of g-C3N4: the broad peak at 
3000−3400 cm−1 corresponds to N-H stretching vibrations [31]; the 
series of peaks between 1237 and 1640 cm−1 originates from C-N and 
C––N stretching vibrations in the heterocyclic compounds [23]; and the 
sharp peak at 808 cm−1 stems from the characteristic vibration of the 
triazine unit [32]. Additionally, the Fe2O3@NCP material exhibits Fe-O 
peaks of α-Fe2O3 at 543 cm−1 and 469 cm−1 within the FT-IR spectrum, 
indicating that its crystalline structure is retained [23].
The full XPS spectrum (Fig. S18) revealed the elemental composition 
and surface chemical states of Fe2O3@NCP. Characteristic peaks in the 
C1 s spectrum of Fe2O3@NCP at 284.80, 285.88, and 289.63 eV are 
attributed to C-C and C-O/N bonds in NCP, as well as sp2-hybridised 
carbon within the N = C-N bonds (Fig. S19a). The O 1 s spectrum 
(Fig. S19b) indicates the coexistence of surface-adsorbed α-Fe2O3 and 
lattice oxygen from H2O (530.17, 531.85 eV), alongside surface hy­
droxylation and C-O-C bonds (533.57 eV) [33], which is also reflected in 
the FT-IR spectrum (Fig. S17). The N 1 s spectrum (Fig. 3b) exhibits four 
characteristic peaks: sp²-hybridised N in C––N-C (398.27 eV), sp3-hy­
bridised N in N-(C)3 (400.25 eV), Pyridine N (396.96 eV) and π excita­
tion in the heterocycle (402.45 eV) [22,34]. The Fe 2p spectrum 
(Fig. 3c) shows that Fe 2p3/2 is located at 710.76 and 723.96 eV, indi­
cating the presence of Fe2+; Fe 2p1/2 at 713.65 and 727.15 eV is 
attributed to Fe3+. The two satellite peaks at high binding energies 
(labelled Sat. in Fig. 3c) are attributed to vibrational excitation of Fe3+
[23,35]. Compared to the single component, the positive shift in Fe 2p

this as the Fe-O/N bond, arising from the chemical coupling between 
α-Fe2O3 and NCP. This provides unambiguous evidence for the forma­
tion of heterojunction between α-Fe2O3 and NCP.
The photocatalytic dechlorination capability of Fe2O3@NCP stems 
from its superior photoelectrochemical properties and charge separation 
efficiency. Photocurrent responses revealed that the photocurrent 
response intensity of Fe2O3@NCP is significantly stronger than that of 
α-Fe2O3 and NCP (Fig. 3h). Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy 
(EIS, Fig. S21) further corroborates this, revealing that Fe2O3@NCP 
exhibits the smallest semicircle diameter, which means its charge 
transfer ability is significantly enhanced [36]. Steady-state photo­
luminescence (PL, Fig. 3i) spectra showed that under 360 nm excitation, 
Fe2O3@NCP exhibits a significant decrease in emission intensity 
compared to NCP, indicating that the heterojunction formed by NCP and 
α-Fe2O3 can effectively inhibit the recombination of photoexcited 
electron-hole pairs.
3.4. The enhanced photocatalysis due to N-vacancies and O-substitutions 
optimizing the Z-scheme heterojunction
The construction and characterization of heterojunctions in 
Fe2O3@NCP are supported by the following experimental findings: First, 
UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy was employed to investigate the 
optical properties of the materials (Fig. S22a, c), and the Kubelka-Munk 
(Fig. S22b, d) function is transformed to determine the band gaps (Eg) of 
α-Fe2O3, g-C3N4 and NCP to be 2.42, 2.73 and 2.33 eV, respectively. 
Secondly, the Mott-Schottky plot (Fig. S22e) indicated that the samples 
were n-type semiconductors, with flat-band potentials of −0.31 V, 
−0.64 V and −0.69 V for α-Fe2O3, g-C3N4 and NCP, respectively (vs. Ag/ 
AgCl, pH = 7.0). By applying the standard potential conversion equation 
(ENHE = EAg/AgCl + 0.197 V) and considering that the conduction band 
edge (ECB) of n-type semiconductors is typically ~0.1 eV more negative 
than the flat-band potential [27,37], the conduction band positions of 
α-Fe2O3, g-C3N4, and NCP were estimated to be −0.21 V, −0.54 V and 
−0.59 V (vs. NHE), respectively; •O2
−(E0 (O2/•O2
−) = −0.33 V vs. NHE).

Subsequently, the valence band edges (EVB) were calculated using the

2.21 eV, 2.23 eV and 1.62 eV for α-Fe2O3, g-C3N4 and NCP, respectively, 
which are in agreement with the above calculations. Collectively, these 
results from band structure analysis and XPS characterization confirm 
the formation of a Z-scheme heterojunction in Fe2O3@NCP. Based on the 
comprehensive characterization and analysis, a schematic illustration of 
the charge carrier transfer mechanism in the Z-scheme heterojunction 
was established (Fig. 4a-b). Notably, the bandgap of NCP (Eg = 2.33 eV) 
is significantly narrower than that of pristine g-C3N4 (Eg = 2.73 eV), 
indicating the enhanced capability of Fe2O3@NCP for visible-light har­
vest 
[40,41]. 
Upon 
visible-light 
irradiation, 
photogenerated 
electron-hole pairs are generated in both α-Fe2O3 and NCP. At the het­
erojunction interface, electrons in the conduction band (CB) of α-Fe2O3 
recombine with holes in the valence band (VB) of NCP through a direct 
Z-scheme pathway. This process selectively preserves highly oxidative 
h+ in the VB of α-Fe2O3 (2.21 V vs. NHE) and reductive electrons in the 
CB of NCP, thereby promoting spatial separation of charge carriers.
The significantly narrowed bandgap of NCP compared to g-C3N4 is 
primarily attributed to the introduction of nitrogen vacancies (NVs) and 
the substitution of N by O atoms within the C-N-C structure in NCP. This 
mechanism substantially lowers the material’s work function (Φ), as 
evidenced by EPR, oxygen XPS, and DFT simulations. Firstly, the EPR 
signal at g = 2.003 reveals the presence of NVs in Fe2O3@NCP (Fig. 4d) 
[42,43]; the formation of NVs is due to the effect of cellulose on the 
heating conversion of melamine rather than the presence of α-Fe2O3, 
because NCP exhibits even a stronger signal of NVs and CP also con­
tained a small amount of NVs (Fig. S23); in contrast, g-C3N4 gave no 
signal of NVs due to the perfect graphite-like structure. NVs induce 
localized electron enrichment in the C-N-C network of NCP, effectively 
acting as n-type dopants that elevate the Fermi level closer to the con­
duction band [44]. Concurrently, NVs introduce defect energy levels 
into the band structure, effectively narrowing the apparent bandgap and 
consequently reducing the Φ of the material. On another hand, XPS full 
spectra (Fig. 4e) reveal significant oxygen content in NCP compared to 
g-C3N4, revealing the doping of oxygen. And the doping of oxygen 
induced the substitutions of N by O atoms in the C-N-C structure, which 
is supported by the high-resolution O 1 s spectrum of NCP (Fig. 4f), the 
characteristic peaks observed at binding energies of 530.98, 532.66, and 
533.88 eV are assigned to C––O, C-O-C, and C-O functional groups [45, 
46]. The presence of the C-O-C structure confirmed the O-substitutions 
on N sites, which introduced O 2p orbitals elevating the electron state 
density near the Fermi level, thus decreasing the value of Φ [47–49].

These inferences have been verified by the DFT calculations. As 
shown in Fig. 4c and g-i, the work functions (Φ) of α-Fe2O3 (311), 
pristine g-C3N4 (002), the graphitic carbon nitride with NVs (GCNNVs)

Fermi level of GCNC-O-C (-2.91 eV) and GCNNVs (-3.61 eV) are much 
higher than that of α-Fe2O3 (-5.70 eV). When two phases are in intimate 
contact to form a heterojunction, the difference in Fermi levels drives 
the free electrons on the surface of NCP migrate from NCP to α-Fe2O3 
until equilibrium is achieved, resulting in the formation of an internal 
electric field at the heterojunction interface. This field promotes direc­
tional migration of photogenerated carriers through the Z-scheme 
transfer path, preserving e−and h+ with strong redox potentials.
3.5. Summary for the mechanism in the photocatalytic dechlorination 
system
DFT calculations were conducted to analyze the Fukui index of 4-CP 
and 2,4,6-TCP (Fig. S24), identifying C-Cl as the primary electrophilic 
sites prone to the nucleophilic attack by e−and •O2
−. 
Based on the above, the reaction mechanism within the photo­
catalytic system can be summarized as follows: As wastewater flows 
through the permeable carbon plates, chlorophenols are adsorbed and 
retained on the interface of Fe2O3@NCP. At this interface, N-vacancies 
and O-substitutions modulation result in a lower work function of NCP, 
which facilitates the generation of photoelectron under visible-light 
excitation. Simultaneously, the photogenerated e−from α-Fe2O3 trans­
fer to the valence band of NCP, recombining with its h+, and facilitating 
the separation of e−and h+ in NCP. This preserves photoelectron within 
NCP, which migrate through the carbon network to attack the adsorbed 
chlorophenols, cleaving the C-Cl bond. Alternatively, these photo­
generated e−can be transferred to O2 molecules that are adsorbed on the 
interface, generating •O2
−that can also mediate the breakage of the C-Cl 
bond. Dechlorination reduces the chemical stability of the pollutant 
molecules. Subsequently, the dechlorinated products continue to be 
degraded into small organic molecules, CO2, and H2O by h+ and •O2
−.
4. Conclusion
This work presents a modular and scalable visible-light-driven 
wastewater-dechlorination 
system, 
which 
is 
constructed 
with 
wastepaper from the paper industry, enabling effective dechlorination 
and detoxification of pulp bleaching wastewater. A permeable carbon 
film with photocatalyst of Z-scheme heterojunction (i.e., Fe2O3@NCP) is 
the photocatalytic unit. Fe2O3@NCP demonstrates not only a strong 
adsorption capacity for chlorophenol pollutants, but also a significantly 
enhanced visible-light responsiveness compared to α-Fe2O3/g-C3N4 (a 
classical photocatalyst with Z-scheme heterojunction). The enhanced 
photocatalytic performance is attributed to the concurrent introduction 
of N-vacancies and O-substitutions at nitrogen sites, which synergisti­
cally modulate the electronic structure of the C-N-C network, leading to 
a reduced work function and a narrowed band gap. The visible-light- 
driven advanced reduction for dechlorination was facilitated by the Z- 
scheme heterojunction, proceeds through two primary pathways uti­
lizing the effectively separated low-energy-barrier photogenerated e−: 
Ddirect attack with reductive e−on the C-Cl of adsorbed pollutants, or 
mediation by •O2
−. The catalyst maintains over 86.1% dechlorination on 
real bleaching wastewater after 18 h of operation, demonstrating 
outstanding stability and promising for the practical application in the 
remediation of pulp bleaching wastewater.
