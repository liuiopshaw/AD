ABSTRACT: Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory skin disease that
affects millions worldwide. Current treatments are often limited by
side effects and high relapse rates. This study introduces an
ultrasmall platinum-nanocluster-based artificial enzyme (Pt NE) for
effective psoriasis therapy and relapse prevention. Pt NEs exhibit
multienzyme mimetic activitiessuperoxide dismutase, catalase,
and glutathione peroxidase-likewith an overall antioxidant
capacity 5-fold greater than Au or Cu analogues. Their superior
skin penetration (69.93% and 14.27% higher than Au and Cu NEs,
respectively) enables efficient reactive oxygen species scavenging
and modulation of the inflammatory microenvironment. In vivo, Pt
NEs achieve complete psoriatic symptom remission, comparable to a commercial calcipotriol/betamethasone ointment but without
steroid-associated adverse effects. Mechanistically, Pt NEs modulate immunity by downregulating pro-inflammatory genes and
upregulating genes related to proliferation inhibition and apoptosis. Excellent biocompatibility was observed, with no detectable
organ damage or systemic toxicity. This work proposes a safe, effective, and cost-efficient nanotherapeutic strategy with strong
potential for clinical translation in psoriasis management.
KEYWORDS: Pt nanocluster, psoriasis, reactive oxygen species, nanoenzyme, antioxidant therapy
■INTRODUCTION
Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory skin
disorder that affects approximately 2−3% of the global
population, characterized by erythematous, scaly plaques and
frequently accompanied by systemic comorbidities such as
cardiovascular disease and metabolic syndrome.1−3 Despite
advances in biologic and immunosuppressive therapies, current
treatment modalitiesincluding corticosteroids, vitamin D
analogues, and monoclonal antibodies4are often hampered
by significant limitations: systemic toxicity of corticosteroid-
based therapies,5 skin irritation and atrophy,6 high recurrence
rates (exceeding 90%) of symptoms postdiscontinuation of
medication,7 and prohibitive costs for using antipsoriatic
biologics.8 These challenges underscore an urgent need for
novel therapeutic strategies that are not only effective and safe
but also capable of preventing disease relapse.5,9
The core pathophysiology of psoriasis resides in immune
dysregulation, which drives inflammatory cell infiltration and
aberrant hyperproliferation of keratinocytes.10 This process
generates and is accompanied by substantial overproduction of
reactive oxygen species (ROS). The elevated ROS, in turn,
further exacerbates local inflammation and proliferative
signaling, establishing a self-perpetuating vicious cycle.11
Consequently, the effective scavenging of ROS presents a
rational therapeutic strategy to perturb this deleterious cycle
and alleviate disease symptoms.9,12,13 A promising therapeutic
strategy for psoriasis entails reducing the levels of ROS at the
affected tissue site. While endogenous antioxidant enzymes
such as SOD, CAT, and GPx play crucial roles in maintaining
ROS balance, their dysfunction in psoriatic lesions exacerbates
disease progression.14−16 Although exogenous antioxidant
supplementation has been explored, its efficacy is limited by
poor stability, low bioavailability, and the inability to
sustainably modulate the inflammatory microenvironment.
Recent years have witnessed the emergence of nanozymes
nanomaterials with enzyme-mimetic activitiesas promising
therapeutic agents for ROS-related diseases.17,18 For instance,
Cu−Sm bimetallic oxides have been shown to scavenge
multiple ROS and alleviate inflammation, yet their relatively

remarkable multienzyme activity through precise coordination
tuning, but they generally require carriers for practical
application, and their skin penetration capability remains less
explored.20 Meanwhile, β-cyclodextrin-modified CeO2 nano-
particles combine drug delivery with enzyme-mimetic activity,
but their catalytic efficiency and depth of mechanistic insight at
the atomic level are often limited.21 Given these challenges,
ultrasmall metal nanoclusters22−25 (NCs, core sizes <2
nm26,27) offer a compelling platform. They exhibit unique
atomic-precision size/structure,28−30 engineerable surface
chemistry,31−35 intriguing molecule-like properties36−39 (e.g.,
luminescence37,40,41 and chirality31,42,43), atomic-level tuna-
bility of physicochemical properties such as multienzyme
catalytic activity,44 and excellent biocompatibility including
renal clearance45,46 (i.e., hydrodynamic diameter of ≤5.5 nm),
making them attractive for biomedical applications.47 Through
deliberate design, an ultrasmall Pt nanocluster-based artificial
enzyme (Pt NE) could be engineered to integrate several
distinctive advantages for psoriasis therapy: (1) an atomically
precise, carrier-free ultrasmall architecture that would facilitate
superior skin penetration and potential renal clearance;46 (2)
exceptionally high multienzyme (SOD/CAT/GPx) activities
arising from exposed, under-coordinated Pt sites; (3) a well-
defined ligand shell that concurrently ensures colloidal
stability, enhances biocompatibility, and preserves catalytic
exposure; and (4) the potential for comprehensive in vivo
validation of both preventive and therapeutic efficacy without
steroid-associated side effects.48 However, the development of
a Pt NE specifically designed for psoriasis treatment
combining the above-mentioned advantagesremains unex-
plored.
Herein, we present the design of a Pt NE protected by
mono(6-mercapto-6-deoxy)-beta-cyclodextrin (SH-β-CD) for
the prevention and treatment of psoriasis (Figure 1. The as-
developed Pt NE shows superior epidermal penetration and
impressive CAT-, SOD-, and GPx-like activities. Density
functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal that optimal
adsorption energy, electron delocalization, and the lowest
activation energy barrier synergistically constitute the molec-
ular-scale enzymatic mechanism of the Pt NE. In vivo studies
demonstrate the potent prophylactic and therapeutic efficacy
of the Pt NE against psoriasis, comparable to the therapeutic
efficacy of calcipotriol/betamethasone dipropionate ointment
(BC) but without the side effects associated with steroid drugs.
The excellent effectiveness of the Pt NE can be attributed to its
ability to eliminate ROS-suppressing inflammatory factors in
psoriatic skin tissue. Additionally, the Pt NE could modulate
immunity, which downregulates inflammatory genes and
upregulates genes that suppress proliferation and promote
apoptosis of epidermal cells. Furthermore, the Pt NE displays
low cytotoxicity and excellent biosafety with no organ damage
and unaltered blood biochemistry and liver functions,
highlighting the potential of the Pt NE as a safe, effective,
and cost-efficient therapeutic option for psoriasis treatment.

and extended XAFS spectra (EXAFS) (Figure S3) indicate that
a valence state of the Pt NE lies between Pt(0) and Pt(IV),
consistent with XPS analysis. The Fourier transform function
extended X-ray absorption fine structure (FT-EXAFS) spectra

corresponding to Pt−S and Pt−Pt bonds, respectively.

exhibits lower coordination numbers and slightly longer bond

transform results (Figure 2H−J) provide a three-dimensional

NE features a mix of 0-valent and II-valent states with
unsaturated coordination, which likely underpins its excep-

subsequently measured the zeta potentials of the three types of
NEs. All exhibited negative surface charges with closely
comparable values of around −20 mV (Figure S12), indicating
similar surface charge properties. Furthermore, stability tests
were performed on Au, Cu, and Pt NEs in both PBS and
simulated sweat. No significant changes were observed in their
UV−vis absorption spectra or size distribution over time
(Figures S13−S15), confirming their structural stability and
supporting their potential applicability for psoriasis treatment.
Multienzyme Mimetic Activities of Pt NE
Capitalizing on its unique structural features, we systematically
evaluated the multienzyme mimetic activities of the Pt NE (i.e.,
SOD-, CAT-, and GPx-like activities; see Figure 3A). We first
employed density functional theory (DFT) calculations to
probe the adsorption energy (Eads) of the key reactant
molecules, a fundamental descriptor of catalytic activity. The
Pt NE demonstrated the strongest spontaneous adsorption for
all tested reactantssuperoxide anion (O2•−), hydrogen
peroxide (H2O2), glutathione (GSH), and ABTS•+ radical
compared to similarly sized Au and Cu NEs (ABTS denotes
2,2′-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid); Figure
3B,E,H,K; Supporting Information Note SII). This optimal
adsorption facilitates efficient interfacial electron transfer,
priming these molecules for reaction.
Experimentally, the Pt NE demonstrated exceptional SOD-
mimetic activity, achieving 67% of the activity of natural SOD
and surpassing the performance of Au and Cu NEs by factors
of 3.31 and 2.42, respectively (Figure 3C,D; Supporting
Information Note SIII). Furthermore, when compared to a
range of artificial nanozymesincluding six metal-based NEs,
three commercial enzyme formulations, and a single-atom-
based analoguethe Pt NE maintained superior SOD-like
performance (Figure S16), highlighting its outstanding
potential for ROS management.
The CAT-like activity of the Pt NE was equally impressive.
Specifically, the rate of H2O2 degradation by the Pt NE is
positively correlated with the concentrations of both Pt NE
and H2O2 (Figure S17). Two critical enzymatic parameters,
maximum reaction velocity (Vmax) and the Michaelis constant
(Km), which reflect the enzyme−substrate affinity (where a
higher Km indicates lower affinity), were evaluated using the
Lineweaver−Burk plot (Figures 3F, S18, and S19). The Pt NE
achieves a Vmax of 8.09 mM/min and a Km of 20.13 mM,
outperforming natural CAT (2.22 mM/min, 28.69 mM) and
other NEs. Its activity is pH-dependent (Figure S20) and leads
to rapid O2 generation (Figure S21). Quantitatively, the Pt NE
shows 6.68-, 8.38-, and 1.16-fold higher CAT-like activity than
the Au NE, Cu NE, and natural CAT, respectively (Figure 3G
and Supporting Information Note SIII).

mg) and far exceeded that of the other NEs (Figure 3I,J;

Upon determination of the enzymatic activities of the Pt NE,
one intriguing question arises: what is the source of these
enzymatic activities in the Pt NE? To address this question, we
synthesized the following materials: Pt(II)−S-β-CD com-
plexes, their glutaraldehyde-cross-linked analogues, and the
Pt NE stabilized by 4-mercaptobenzoic acid (MBA)a ligand
chosen for its minimal steric hindrance. These systems were
designed to discern whether the enzymatic activity arises from
the surface Pt(II)−S-β-CD motifs or from exposed Pt(0) sites
(Figures S22−S24). Both types of Pt(II)−S-β-CD complexes
and Pt NE@MBA exhibit negligible CAT-like activity (Figures
S25−S27 and Supporting Information Note SIV). Based on
the low catalytic activities observed for the Pt(II)−S-β-CD
complex and for Pt NE@MBA, together with the pronounced
steric hindrance of SH-β-CD, we propose that the primary role
of SH-β-CD is to prevent overgrowth and aggregation of the Pt
core. Its bulky structure is also likely to limit the packing
density of ligands on the NE’s surface. This configuration may
facilitate the dynamic exposure of a larger population of under-
coordinated or uncoordinated Pt atomscompared to systems
using small, densely packed ligandswhich are considered the
principal contributors to the observed multienzyme activity.
The dynamic valence transition of those uncoordinated Pt
atoms on the surface of the Pt NE, shifting from 0 to II during
the catalytic process, is likely the key to Pt NE’s exceptional
enzyme-like activity. These findings not only clarify the origin
of enzymatic activities in the Pt NE but also highlight the
meticulous design strategy of using large SH-β-CD molecules
as protective ligands to optimize the enzymatic performance.
To decipher the underlying mechanism for the superior
performance of the Pt NE, we conducted in-depth DFT
simulations. Despite similar sizes and identical ligands, the
intrinsic electronic properties of the metal cores dictate their
catalytic prowess. Based on ICP-MS-derived metal−sulfur
ratios (Pt/S = 1.7:1, Au/S = 2.2:1, Cu/S = 2.2:1), structural
models simplified with −SCH3 ligands were constructed and
optimized for stability (Figure 4A−C). Geometric analysis
reveals that the calculated bond lengths are 2.74 Å (Au−Au),
2.54 Å (Cu−Cu), and 2.71 Å (Pt−Pt), with metal−sulfur
bonds of 2.46 Å (Au−S), 2.21 Å (Cu−S), and 2.31 Å (Pt−S).
The relatively moderate Pt−S bond facilitates electron transfer
from exposed Pt active sites to adjacent S atoms. Further
characterization of surface electron distribution via electron
localization function (ELF) (Figure 4D−F) demonstrates that
regions with high ELF values (>0.7) correspond to strongly
localized states arising from covalent bonds or lone pairs,
which impede electron transfer. The ELF values at exposed
metal active sites follow Cu NE (0.25) < Pt NE (0.32) < Au
NE (0.41), indicating electron delocalization decreases in the
order Cu NE > Pt NE > Au NE. Consequently, electron
transfer capability diminishes progressively from Cu NE to Au
NE.
As shown in Figure 4G−I, the highest occupied molecular
orbital-lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (HOMO−
LUMO) gaps of Au, Cu, and Pt NEs are 0.64, 1.21, and
0.14 eV, respectively. The Pt NE exhibits the smallest gap
(0.14 eV), indicating a very low energy barrier for electron
excitation. Although the Cu NE has a larger gap (1.21 eV), it
shows adsorption energies comparable to those of the Au NE
(Figure 3) and the lowest ELF value (0.25) at active sites.
These featuresfavorable adsorption and pronounced elec-
tron delocalizationlead to slightly higher enzymatic activity
for the Cu NE compared to the Au NE. Density of states
(DOS) analysis further reveals enhanced electron excitation
capability in Cu and Au NEs relative to Pt at the Fermi level
(Ef) (Figure 4J−L). Crucially, enzymatic activity scales
inversely with the energy of unoccupied states near Ef and
proportionally to their intensity. Projected DOS (PDOS)
confirms that the Pt NE possesses lower-energy unoccupied
states (0−1 eV) with heightened peak intensity (Figure S28),
consistent with the observed activity trend (Figure 3L−M).
Collectively, our comprehensive analysis consistently identifies
the origin of the exceptional catalytic performance of the Pt
NE. The markedly lower activation energy observed in the
catalytic process (Figure 4N,O) is a direct consequence of its
unique electronic and structural properties. These include
robust substrate adsorption (Figure 3B,E,H,K), an exception-
ally narrow HOMO−LUMO gap that minimizes the energy
cost for electron excitation (Figure 4G−I), and an optimal
electronic structure characterized by favorable Pt−S bond
lengths and pronounced electron delocalization (Figure 4A−
F). Together, these factors enable superior electron transfer
and culminate in the highest catalytic activity for the Pt NE.
Subsequently, the cooperative action of Pt NE’s SOD-like
and CAT-like activities is summarized in Figure 4M. The Pt
NE first catalyzes the dismutation of O2
•−to generate H2O2,
which is then decomposed by its CAT-like function. Together,
these combined activities enable the Pt NE to mimic the
sequential ROS-scavenging function of natural antioxidant
enzymes, effectively clearing both superoxide anions and
hydrogen peroxide in a concerted manner.52−54 Theoretical
calculations confirm that the NE’s SOD-like mechanism
follows the native catalytic cycle of O2
•−disproportionation
(Figure 4N), while their CAT-like activity proceeds through
multistep redox reactions involving radical intermediates
(Figure 4O). Given the potential kinetic competition between
these pathways, the concurrent presence of metastable radicals
(e.g.,
•OH, O2
•−, and H2O2) necessitates the analysis of
transition state stability. In both SOD and CAT pathways, the
Pt NE exhibits the lowest activation energy per step, whereas
Au and Cu NEs show comparable and higher energy barriers.
As predicted by the Arrhenius equation, the reaction rates of
SOD-like and CAT-like catalysis follow the same trend: Pt NE
> Cu NE ≈Au NE. These results collectively highlight Pt NE’s
remarkable multienzyme activity and robust antioxidant
capacity, underscoring its strong potential for in vivo ROS
scavenging.

NE, along with the Au NE and Cu NE, exhibit commendable
biocompatibility, maintaining cell viability above 80% at
concentrations up to 0.8 mM, taking human immortalized

Given that excessive ROS is one of the key symptoms of

feature of psoriasis is the uncontrolled proliferation of

the Pt NE significantly curtails the proliferation of M5-treated

Pt, Au, and Cu NEs in psoriasis prevention.

To elucidate the core signaling pathway through which the
Pt NE exerts its preventive effects, we integrated RNA-seq
enrichment analysis with downstream experimental validation.
Transcriptomic profiling identified multiple inflammatory and
immune-related pathways, including NOD-like receptor signal-
ing, cytokine−cytokine receptor interaction, and chemokine
signaling, all of which are known to converge on the activation
of NF-κB as a common downstream hub. This positions NF-
κB as a central signaling integrator in the psoriatic
inflammatory network. Importantly, NF-κB directly regulates
the expression of key pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α,
IL-6, IL-1β) that drive the characteristic epidermal hyperplasia
and immune infiltration in psoriasis. To functionally validate
this axis, we performed RT-qPCR on classic NF-κB−driven
effector genes (TNF-α, IL-6) in skin tissues. The results
confirmed that Pt NE treatment significantly downregulated
their expression (Figure S48), demonstrating effective
suppression of NF-κB-mediated transcriptional output. To-
gether, these data indicate that the Pt NE ameliorates psoriatic
inflammation largely through modulation of the NF-κB
signaling hub, thereby disrupting the downstream cytokine
cascade and mitigating disease progression.
The downregulation of both ROS-related genes and
inflammatory pathways (e.g., NF-κB, NOD-like receptor) in
our transcriptomic data reflects a key mechanistic feature of
psoriasis: a self-amplifying feedback loop between oxidative
stress and inflammation. In this cycle, elevated ROS act as
upstream signaling molecules that activate key inflammatory
cascades, including the IKK/IκB/NF-κB axis and the NLRP3
inflammasome. Once activated, these pathways drive the
production of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-
1β), which in turn recruit and stimulate immune cells to
generate further ROS via respiratory burst. The Pt NE
intercepts this vicious cycle at a critical node by efficiently
scavenging ROS via its multienzyme activity. This reduces the
primary oxidative trigger for inflammatory signaling, leading to
a decreased level of cytokine production and subsequent
attenuation of secondary ROS generation. Thus, Pt NE’s
therapeutic effect can be understood as a concerted disruption
of the ROS-inflammation circuit, resulting in the coordinated
downregulation of both oxidative and inflammatory tran-
scriptomic signatures observed in our study.
Verification and Functional Analysis of the Key Core
Proteins
In addition to mitigating inflammatory infiltration and
suppressing excessive ROS expression, the Pt NE upregulates
critical genes involved in inhibiting gene transcription,
modulating chemokine trafficking, and regulating cellular
autophagy, apoptosis, and proliferation. In this regard, data
analysis and visualization of the protein−protein interaction
(PPI) network were conducted by using the STRING database
and Cytoscape software, identifying significant modules within
the PPI network. As shown in Figure S49, DE-mRNAs were
divided into three modules according to the clustering of gene
phenotypes, including the blue module, turquoise module, and
gray module. Based on this, the turquoise module and the blue
module were analyzed based on the p-value (Figure 7H). In
these two modules, Dr1, Ndst1, Smarce1, and Atg5 were
identified as hub genes with linkage degrees of 35, 31, 15, and
11, respectively. These upregulations of the four genes play
crucial roles in reducing cell proliferation and alleviating
inflammation. Figure 7I−L illustrates their relative expression
levels, which are significantly higher in the IMQ + Pt NE group
compared to the control group. This gene upregulation likely
inhibits the overproliferation of psoriatic epidermal cells,
promotes the apoptosis of proliferating cells, and ultimately
improves the progression of psoriasis with Pt NE treatment.
Effective Treatment of Psoriasis by the Pt NE
To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the Pt NE, we
established a psoriasis mouse model using a six-day IMQ
induction followed by a five-day treatment with the Pt NE,
compared against the Au NE, the Cu NE, and a commercial
calcipotriol/betamethasone (BC) ointment (Figure 8A). Mice
body weight and dorsal skin photographs were meticulously
documented throughout the treatment duration (Figures S50,
8B, and S51). Notably, the body weights of mice treated with
the Pt NE, Au NE, and Cu NE exhibit a slight increase in body
weight, whereas those receiving BC ointment or PBS maintain
stable weights (Figure S50). Visually, both the Pt NE and BC
ointment treatment groups exhibit comparable amelioration of
psoriasis symptoms, slightly outperforming the Au NE and Cu
NE groups (Figures 8B and S51). This improvement was
quantitatively supported by reduced PASI scores (Figure 8C−
F). Post-treatment with the Pt NE, inflammatory cytokines in
the skin, including IL-23, IL-12, IL-17, and IL-1β, are
significantly reduced even with IMQ induction, mirroring the
effects observed with BC ointment, as confirmed by both RT-
qPCR (Figure 8G−J) and ELISA results (Figure S52).
Additionally, in vivo assessments further reveal that the Pt
NE, along with the Au NE and Cu NE, substantially decreases
ROS levels and mitigates oxidative stress (Figure S53).
Histological and immunohistological examinations show that
Pt NE treatment notably reduces the presence of CD3+ T cells
and Ki67+ keratinocytes, similar to outcomes in the BC
ointment group (Figure S54). Furthermore, after six days of
treatment with the Pt NE, Au NE, and Cu NE, no
abnormalities were detected in blood or liver function tests
in the mice (Figure S55). Ultimately, residual Pt in the
psoriatic skin, blood, other major organs (heart, liver, spleen,
lungs, kidneys, and brain), and dorsal muscle of mice was
measured, with corresponding ICP−MS results indicating
effective elimination within 48 h (Figures S56 and S57). This
elimination is chiefly due to its ultrasmall size, enabling easy
penetration of the skin vasculature and facilitating its
metabolism and excretion via the bloodstream, thus posing
no biosafety issues of metal accumulation.58,59
Based on the aforementioned data analysis, it is demon-
strated that the Pt NE, through its nanozyme activity,
scavenges excessive ROS, thereby inhibiting upstream signaling
such as TLR4/PI3K and directly blocking the core NF-κB
inflammatory pathway. This leads to a reduction in the release
of downstream pro-inflammatory cytokines, significantly
ameliorates the local inflammatory microenvironment, and
cooperatively regulates the expression of proliferation- and
apoptosis-related genes, ultimately effectively alleviating the
pathological features of psoriasis.

■CONCLUSIONS
In summary, we have successfully developed an ultrasmall Pt
NE that effectively addresses the key challenges in psoriasis
treatment: oxidative stress, chronic inflammation, and high
recurrence. Through a rational design leveraging CO reduction
and SH-β-CD stabilization, the Pt NE achieves a uniform size,
high catalytic activity, and superior skin penetration. Its
multienzyme mimetic functionality enables efficient ROS
clearance and reprogramming of the pro-inflammatory micro-
environment, leading to significant therapeutic outcomes in
both preventive and curative settings. Notably, the Pt NE
outperforms analogous gold and copper nanozymes and
matches the efficacy of standard steroid-based therapy without
the associated adverse effects. Comprehensive biosafety
evaluations confirm its excellent biocompatibility and renal
clearance potential. These findings not only establish Pt NE as
a promising nanomedicine for psoriasis management but also
provide a general strategy for designing enzyme-mimetic
nanomaterials for inflammatory skin diseases. We anticipate
that this work will inspire further clinical exploration of metal
NC-based therapeutics in dermatology and beyond.
■METHODS
Synthesis of the Pt NE
Stock solutions of H2PtCl6 (20 mM) and SH-β-CD (10 mM) were
prepared in water and 0.1 M NaOH, respectively. For the synthesis of
the Pt NE, 1.5 mL of SH-β-CD solution was added into a 20 mL glass
bottle containing 8.0 mL of water under stirring, followed by the slow
addition of 0.5 mL of H2PtCl6 solution. CO was then bubbled
through the mixture for 5 min. Finally, the flask was sealed and stirred
for 24 h to obtain a brown Pt NE.
Synthesis of the Au NE
Aqueous solutions of HAuCl4 (20 mM, 0.5 mL) and SH-β-CD (10
mM, 1.5 mL) were vigorously mixed in water. After reaction for 10
min, NaOH solution (1 M, 0.3 mL) was added, followed by adding
NaBH4 solution (∼112 mM, prepared by dissolving 43 mg of NaBH4
in 10 mL of 0.2 M NaOH). The reaction proceeded for 2 h, and the
resulting Au NE was stored at 4 °C for further application.
Synthesis of the Cu NE
Aqueous solutions of CuSO4 (20 mM, 0.5 mL) and SH-β-CD (10
mM, 1.5 mL), prepared in water and 0.1 M NaOH, respectively, were
sequentially added into water (3.75 mL). Afterward, 0.05 mL of
NaBH4 solution (∼112 mM) was slowly introduced, and the reaction
proceeded at 80 °C for 8 h in an oil bath.
SOD-like Activity
The O2
•−elimination ability of the Pt NE and natural SOD was
identified by electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) measurements
(Bruker EPR200-Plus spectrometer, Germany). Pt NE (1 mg/mL) or
natural SOD (1 mg/mL) was added to a mixture of 25 mM DMPO, 1
mM xanthine, and 0.0125 U/mL xanthine oxidase in PBS solutions
(pH = 7.4). The SOD-like activity of the Pt NE, Au NE, Cu NE, and
natural SOD was further assessed via the WST-1 assay kit (C0036).
CAT-like Activity
The CAT-like activity test of the Pt NE, Cu NE, Au NE, and natural
CAT was verified via measuring the absorbance change of H2O2 at
240 nm (39.4 M−1 cm−1, the slope of the standard curve) on a kinetic
mode of UV−vis spectrophotometer. The pH dependence experi-
ments were conducted in PBS solution (pH = 3 to 11), including Pt
NE (125 μg/mL) and H2O2 (10 mM) in 2.5 mL of solution, and the
absorbance was collected at 240 nm. In the meantime, the kinetic
curve was implemented by altering the concentrations of enzymes (Pt
NE: 0−125 μg/mL, natural CAT: 0−75 μg/mL) in PBS solution in
the presence of H2O2 (10 mM) and by altering the concentrations of
H2O2 from 0 to 20 mM with a fixed concentration of enzymes (Pt
NE, Au NE, and Cu NE: 1 mg/mL, natural CAT: 1 mg/mL). The
CAT-like activity of the Pt NE, Cu NE, Au NE, and natural CAT was
further determined by a CAT assay kit (S0051), and the absorbance
at 520 nm was tested by a UV−vis spectrophotometer.
GPx-like Activity
The GPx-like activity of metal NE with different concentrations from
0 to 8 μg/mL was verified by collecting the absorption of H2O2 at 240
nm (39.4 M−1 cm−1) in the presence of glutathione by UV−vis
spectroscopy. The GPx-like activity of the Pt NE, Cu NE, Au NE, and
natural GPx was further determined by a glutathione reductase assay
kit with DTN (S0055), and the absorbance at 421 nm was collected
by a UV−vis spectrophotometer.

Animal Disease Model of Psoriasis
All animal experiments were performed in strict accordance with the
NIH guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals (NIH
Publication No. 85−23 Rev. 1985) and were approved by the
Biomedical Ethics Committees of Qingdao University of Science and
Technology (Ethics Approval Number: QKDLL-2025-54) and Anhui
University (Ethics Approval Number: IACUC(AHU)-2023-30).
On day 0, depilated Balb/c mice, aged 6−8 weeks, were randomly
assigned to distinct treatment cohorts. Each mouse received a daily
topical application of 62.5 mg of commercial IMQ cream (Aldara,
5%) over a shaved dorsal area of approximately 3 cm × 3 cm for six
consecutive days. This treatment induced a psoriasiform model by day
7, which was used for further studies.
Evaluation of Efficacy in the “Prevention Mode”
On day 0, the depilated Balb/c mice were randomly assigned to one
control group and three treatment groups (n = 5). From days 1 to 6,
all mice received a daily topical application of 62.5 mg of IMQ cream
to a defined dorsal area (15 × 25 mm). After 30 min, the following
treatments were applied topically via gauze soaked with 200 μL of
PBS containing corresponding agents: (i) no further treatment
(IMQ); (ii) 6 μM Pt NE (IMQ + Pt NE); (iii) 6 μM Au NE (IMQ +
Au NE); (iv) 6 μM Cu NE (IMQ + Cu NE). Each area was secured
with a Tegaderm film.
Psoriasis severity was scored daily using a modified PASI system
(0−4) based on erythema, scaling, and thickness. Body weight was
also recorded daily. On day 7, mice were euthanized. Treated skin
sections were rinsed with PBS, excised into 3 × 10 mm strips, and
processed for histology and molecular analysis. Whole blood was
collected via cardiac puncture under anesthesia for systemic
inflammatory assessment, and major organs were harvested for
pathological evaluation.

On day 0, depilated Balb/c mice (6−8 weeks old) were divided into
one control and three treatment groups (n = 5). From days 1 to 6, all
mice received daily topical applications of 62.5 mg of IMQ cream.
