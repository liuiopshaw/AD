A B S T R A C T
The ligand effects have been extensively investigated in Au and Ag nanoclusters, while corresponding research
efforts focusing on Cu nanoclusters remain relatively insufﬁcient. Such a scarcity could primarily be attributed to
the inherent instability of Cu nanoclusters relative to their Au/Ag analogues. In this work, we report the
controllable preparation and structural determination of a hydride-containing Cu28 nanocluster with a chemical
formula of Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3. The combination of Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3 and previously
reported Cu28H10(SPhoMe)18(TPP)3 constructs a structure-correlated cluster pair with comparable structures and
properties. Accordingly, the ligand effects in directing the geometric structures and physicochemical properties
(including optical absorptions and catalytic activities towards the selected hydrogenation) of copper nanoclusters
were analyzed. Overall, this work presents a structure-correlated Cu28 pair that enables the atomic-level under-
standing of ligand effects on the structures and properties of metal nanoclusters.
1. Introduction
Atomically precise nanoclusters are an emerging class of program-
mable nanomaterials bridging the metallic complexes and nanoparticles
[1–8]. Owing to their apparent quantum size effects and discrete elec-
tronic energy levels, metal nanoclusters and cluster-based nanomaterials
displayed adjustable and structure-dependent physicochemical proper-
ties, such as photoluminescence, magnetism, chirality, catalysis, etc.
[9–18]. Several efﬁcient approaches have been developed to dictate the
geometric/electronic structures and chemical/physical properties of
metal nanoclusters, including kernel alloying [19–23], ligand engineer-
ing [24–27], intermolecular assembling [28–32], and so on. Among
them, the ligand engineering has attracted increasing attention because
such a strategy can not only direct the structure transformation of metal
nanoclusters at the molecular level, but also has the potential to trigger
the construction of cluster-based nanomaterials at the supramolecular
level. Indeed, with the assistance of ligand engineering (or the ligand
exchange), several nanocluster pairs comprising cluster precursors and
products with comparable structures and properties have been fabricated
[33–35].
Compared to the extensive investigation of Au and Ag nanoclusters
towards the ligand engineering, the corresponding research efforts
focusing on Cu nanoclusters remain relatively insufﬁcient. This scarcity
could primarily be attributed to the inherent instability of Cu nano-
clusters relative to their Au/Ag analogues [36,37]. In addition, the
ligand engineering (or the ligand-exchange process) might amplify the
intrinsic instability of Cu nanoclusters and output several metastable
clusters with spontaneous decomposition characterizations. In this
context, obtaining structure-related Cu cluster series and then exploring
their atomic-level structure-property correlations call for more effort.
Besides, Cu nanoclusters or their assembled nanomaterials have po-
tential applications in catalysis or photoelectric conversions [38–44],
motivating us to further develop Cu cluster analogues with comparable
structures and properties.

state of “þ2” of the two Cu28 nanoclusters, ESI-MS results indicated that
the Cu28-DPPOE cluster framework should contain ten hydride ligands
(i.e., 5  2 ¼ 10). In addition, the nominal electron count of the Cu28-
DPPOE nanocluster should be zero (i.e., 28 (Cu) – 18 (SR) – 10 (H) ¼ 0),
the same as that of the Cu28-TPP nanoclusters [45,49]. In this context, the
electrically neutral cluster molecule was difﬁcult to ionize in mass spec-
trometry. Then, two Cu ions and one DPPOE ligand tended to anchor onto
the cluster molecule, and the obtained [cluster þ 2Cu þ DPPOE] could be
detected in ESI-MS. Furthermore, the ten hydride ligands were also
recorded by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy. The 2H
NMR spectrum of Cu28-DPPOE-D (dissolved in CHCl3) displayed four
distinct signals at 1.60, 4.40, 5.63, and 8.06 ppm with an area ratio of
d:c:b:a ¼ 3:1:3:3, and the signal at 7.26 ppm came from the solvent peak of
CHCl3 (Fig. 1(C)). First, the area ratio of 3:1:3:3 suggested that there
should be 10 (or some multiple thereof) hydride ligands in the nano-
cluster, matching perfectly with the ESI-MS result. In addition, the ten
hydride ligands should be located onto/into the nanocluster framework
with four different types of coordination modes. Taken that the structure
framework of Cu28-DPPOE followed a μ3 axial symmetry, we speculated
that one hydride ligand was located on the axis of symmetry, and the other
nine were arranged with a triple symmetric mode.
2.2. Structure analysis
Structurally, Cu28-DPPOE and Cu28-TPP nanoclusters exhibited a
similar molecular framework. However, due to the different coordination
modes between bidentate DPPOE and monodentate TPP ligands with
vertex Ag atoms of nanoclusters, their vertex structures were totally
different (Fig. 2). Speciﬁcally, the Cu28-DPPOE nanocluster contained a
distortion face-centered cubic (FCC) Cu13 kernel surrounded by a
Cu9(SR)9(DPPOE)3 ring-like shell to make up a Cu22(SR)9(DPPOE)3
structure (Fig. 2(A–C)). In comparison, the Cu13 kernel of Cu28-TPP
followed a more standard FCC conﬁguration, while its Cu9(SR)9(DP-
POE)3 shell was incomplete to a certain extent (Fig. 2(G–I)). In addition, a
Cu6(SR)9 motif was further arranged onto the Cu22(SR)9(DPPOE)3 or
Cu22(SR)9(TPP)3 structure, giving rise to the overall structure of Cu28-
DPPOE (Fig. 2(D–F)) and Cu28-TPP (Fig. 2(J–L)), respectively. Both
frameworks of Cu28-DPPOE and Cu28-TPP nanoclusters followed a μ3
axial symmetry with a C3 symmetry axis passing through the innermost
Cu atom. Of note, such a triple axis symmetry has also been suggested by
NMR results. Furthermore, compared with the TPP ligands of Cu28-TPP,
the DPPOE ligands of Cu28-DPPOE distorted the cluster framework and
resulted in (i) a rotation of the Cu6(SR)9 motif on the Cu22(SR)9(DPPOE)3
structure and (ii) the distortion of the twisted FCC Cu13 kernel. Such a
transmission effect of peripheral ligands on the conﬁguration of the
innermost kernel has been discovered in several Ag-based nanoclusters
[50–52]. Meanwhile, the coordination of the bidentate DPPOE ligands in
Cu28-DPPOE resulted in a more regular conﬁguration of the Cu9(SR)9
motif structure relative to that in the Cu28-TPP nanocluster.

2.3. Theoretical calculation
The locations of hydride ligands were challenging to determine with
SC-XRD due to their hard-to-detect electronic clouds. To determine the
locations of ten hydrides in Cu28-DPPOE nanocluster, density functional
theory (DFT) calculations were performed, and the initial nanocluster
model was constructed based on the crystal structure of Cu28(SPhpO-
Me)18(DPPOE)3. By referring to previously reported metal hydride
nanoclusters and the NMR results in this work, we mainly placed the ten
hydride ligands near the cluster kernel, and such hydride ligands were
arranged with a μ3 axial symmetry with one hydride locating on the C3
axis of symmetry.
As shown in Fig. S11, the Cu13 kernel of Cu28-DPPOE cluster con-
sisted of eight triangular and six tetragonal facets, which could be
divided into four layers: top layer (containing one triangular), second
layer (containing three triangular and three tetragonal facets), third layer
(containing three triangular and three tetragonal facets) and bottom
layer (containing one triangular). Due to the C3 symmetry of the Cu13H10
kernel structure after hydride doping, one hydride might be located on
the top and bottom triangular planes of the Cu13 kernel, and the other
nine hydrides could be distributed in a ratio of 3:3:3:0 on the triangular
and tetragonal facets of the second and third layers. In this context, there
should be C2

kernel with a hydride ratio of 1:3:3:3, such ligands exhibited different
coordination modes: seven followed a μ3-H coordination mode while
three followed a μ4-H coordination.

3. Conclusion
In summary, a hydride-containing Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3
was prepared and structurally determined, which constructed a Cu28
cluster pair with previously reported Cu28H10(SPhoMe)18(TPP)3 with
correlated structures and comparable properties. The two Cu28 nano-
clusters exhibited analogous frameworks; however, due to their different
bonding modes of bidentate DPPOE—Ag and monodentate—Ag coordi-
nation, the kernel conﬁguration, kernel-surface interaction, and vertex
structures of Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3 were more twisted than
those of Cu28H10(SPhoMe)18(TPP)3. Besides, the two Cu28 nanoclusters
displayed different optical absorptions and catalytic activity towards the
hydrogenation of p-nitrophenol. The ﬁndings in this work highlighted
the ligand effects on both geometric/electronic structures and physical/
chemical properties of metal nanoclusters, hopefully providing some
guidance for the controllable preparation of Cu-based nanoclusters with
customized structures and properties.
4. Experimental
4.1. Chemical materials
All following reagents were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich and used
without further puriﬁcation, including copper (I) chloride (CuCl, 98%
metal basis), copper (II) acetate monohydrate (Cu(CH3COO)2⋅3H2O, 98%
metal basis), 4-methoxythiophenol (98%), 2-methylthiophenol (98%),
bis(2-diphenylphosphinophenyl)ether (DPPOE, 98%), triphenylphos-
phine (TPP, 99%), sodium borohydride (NaBH4, 99%), sodium boro-
deuteride (NaBD4, 99%), methanol (HPLC grade), dichloromethane
(HPLC grade), trichloromethane (HPLC grade), ethanol (HPLC grade),
hexane (HPLC grade), and acetonitrile (HPLC grade).
4.2. Preparation of Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3
40 mg of Cu(CH3COO)2⋅H2O was dissolved in a mixed solution of
5 mL of EtOH and 20 mL of CH2Cl2. After 20 min, 50 mg of DPPOE and
50 μL of 4-methoxyphenthiophenol were added, followed by adding 5 mL
of the ethanol solution of NaBH4 (8 mg mL1). The reaction was then
proceeded for 12 h. The obtained solution was centrifuged at 10000 rpm
for 3 min, and the supernatant was collected and evaporated to get the
crude product which was ﬁnally washed three times with hexane and
methanol to get the Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3 nanocluster.
4.3. Preparation of Cu28D10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3
The synthesis procedure of Cu28D10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3 is iden-
tical to that of Cu28H10(SPhpOMe)18(DPPOE)3, except that NaBD4 was
used instead of NaBH4.
