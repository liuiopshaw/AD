Decorating Pt@cyclodextrin nanoclusters on C3N4/
MXene for boosting the photocatalytic H2O2
production†
Haiguang Zhu,
‡ Qiang Xue,‡ Guangyan Zhu, Yong Liu,
Xinyue Dou
and Xun Yuan

Photocatalytic H2O2 production has become a promising alternative to obtaining H2O2. However, the
performance of conventional photocatalysts is usually constrained by their insuﬃcient active sites, low
carrier separation, and poor light-harvesting. Herein, we report a high-performance photocatalyst
fabricated by decorating per-6-thio-b-cyclodextrin (SH-b-CD)-protected platinum nanocluster (Pt@b-
CD NCs) on C3N4/MXene (Ti3C2) (C3N4-M for short) heterojunctions. The key of this design is the
employment of Pt@b-CD NCs, which not only serve as proper electron acceptors for accelerating the
carrier separation and oﬀer abundant active sites (the Pt core in NCs), but also provide plentiful “delivery
channels” (the hydrophobic cavity of SH-b-CD) to promote the diﬀusion of reactant (O2) to active sites.
In addition, the hybridization of MXene with C3N4 largely improves the visible-light harvesting.
Leveraging the complementary properties of the Pt core, reactive SH-b-CD ligand, C3N4, and MXene,
the Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M photocatalyst showed a 6 times higher production of H2O2 (147.1 mM L1) than
pristine C3N4 (24.2 mM L1).
1.
Introduction
Atomically precise metal nanoclusters (NCs) are a family of
ultrasmall particles (<3 nm) stabilized with organic ligands, and
have stimulated increasing interest in diverse elds of catal-
ysis,1–6 energy conversion,7–9 the environment,10 and biomedi-
cine.11–13 Owing to their high surface to volume ratio, adjustable
size/structure, rich surface chemistry, and unique molecular-
like properties, such as strong luminescence, highest occu-
pied molecular orbital–lowest unoccupied molecular orbital
(HOMO–LUMO) transitions, and adjustable visible (Vis)-light
absorption,3,7,14–20 such metal NCs have emerged as promising
co-photocatalysts in the photocatalytic eld, which has greatly
boosted the advance of many photocatalytic applications, such
as water splitting,21,22 nitrogen xation,8 organic pollutants
degradation,23–25 and organic synthesis.26,27 However, the same
does not occur in the photocatalytic production of hydrogen
peroxide (H2O2), which may be due to the lack of eﬃcient design
on the NC-based photocatalyst. Indeed, H2O2 production is an
extremely important synthetic unit because H2O2 is an imper-
ative chemical reagent for medical disinfection and industrial
production.28–30 Therefore, there is an urgent demand to design
a high-performance NC-based photocatalyst for the green
production of H2O2 driven by Vis-light irradiation.
For a typical NC co-catalyst, its catalytic activity is mainly
inuenced by two aspects: its metal core and surface ligand.
While most previous studies focused on investigating the
impact of the NC's metal core (e.g., size, composition, and
atomic packing mode) on the photocatalytic activities, the
inuence of NC's surface ligands (e.g., hydrophilicity, steric
hindrance, functional group, and interaction with reactants) in
this area is usually neglected.2,31,32 It is a common belief that
surface ligands on metal NCs could serve as a physical barrier
that limits the diﬀusion of reactants to approach active sites on
the NC's metal core, which would have a negative eﬀect on the
photocatalytic activity of NC-based photocatalysts.22,33–35 For
example, such an eﬀect may become worse if the NC's ligand
shell is denser. Although the full or partial removal of ligands
from the NC's surface may address this issue, the attributes of
the NC's metal core, such as size/structure/composition, oen
change during the process of ligand removal as well as during
photocatalysis, resulting in a recession of its photocatalytic
performance. By contrast, we believe that engineering the
surface chemistry of metal NCs could favor the interaction and
subsequent reaction of reactants with the NC's co-catalyst via
host–guest interactions or others, which is also one of the
driving forces of this study.

decent semiconductors to hybridize with metal NCs is of equal

Herein we report our design of a high-eﬃciency Pt NCs-
based photocatalyst, which was realized by loading ultrasmall
per-6-thio-b-cyclodextrins (SH-b-CD)-protected platinum NCs
(Pt@b-CD NCs) on the surface of a pre-fabricated C3N4-MXene
(C3N4-M) hybrid semiconductor (Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M, Scheme 1).
This photocatalyst had several merits: (1) the Pt NCs were
coated with active SH-b-CD ligands with a hydrophobic cavity of
0.62 nm in diameter,37 and such cavities could act as “delivery
channels” to transfer the reactant O2 from the solution onto the
active sites of Pt NCs, which may be benecial to the O2
reduction for H2O2 formation; (2) the Pt cores of the Pt NCs
could serve not only as photoinduced e acceptors but also as
active sites, which may promote the separation/migration of
charge carriers and favor the surface photocatalytic reactions;
(3) the C3N4-M heterojunction is a Vis-light-driven photo-
catalyst, with suitable CB position at around 1.3 V (vs.
Reversible Hydrogen Electrode (RHE)),38–40 which is more
negative than the potentials for O2 reduction (0.33 V vs. RHE,
pH ¼ 7),41 and it had a stronger capability in Vis-light harvesting
because of the full-spectrum light absorption ability of the
MXene (Ti3C2).42,43 By leveraging the complementary properties
of Pt cores (photoinduced e acceptor and catalytic active site),
active SH-b-CD ligands (delivery channels), C3N4 (photoinduced
e generator), and MXene (Ti3C2) (enhanced Vis-light absorber),
the as-designed Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M photocatalyst achieved a high
production capability for H2O2 of 147.1 mM L1, which was six
times higher than that of pristine C3N4. This study may shed
light on the design of other NCs-based photocatalyst, poten-
tially promoting the advances of various photocatalytic terminal
applications.

Experimental section
2.1
Materials
Ultrapure water (18.2 MU) was used in the whole experiment. All
the chemicals used in this work were purchased commercially
and used without further renement. Chloroplatinic acid
hexahydrate (H2PtCl6$6H2O) was purchased from Merck, SH-b-
CD was purchased from Shandong Binzhou Zhiyuan Bio-
Technology CO., Ltd (China). Hydrouoric acid (HF, 40%),
thiourea, 4-benzoquinone (BQ), tripeptide glutathione (GSH),
glucose, sodium hydroxide (NaOH), sodium oxalate (Na2C2O4),
and carbon monoxide (CO) were ordered from Sinopharm
Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. Commercial Ti3AlC2 powder was
obtained from Beijing Forsman Technology Company.
2.2
Instruments
The UV-Vis absorption spectra of the samples were measured on
a Shimadu UV-1800 spectrophotometer. The XRD patterns of
the samples were recorded by the D-Max 2500/PC powder
diﬀractometer. The X-ray photoelectron spectra (XPS) of the
photocatalysts were obtained on an ESCALAB MK II Axis Uitra
Dld system using Al-a as an exciting source. The binding energy
of the C 1s level of adventitious carbon at 248.8 eV was taken for
energy calibration. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was con-
ducted on a NETZSCH TG209F1 system with a heating rate of
10 C min1 under an air atmosphere. Transmission electron
microscopy (TEM) images and energy dispersive X-ray (EDX)
element mapping of the samples were acquired on a JEOL JEM
2100F microscope. Electron spin resonance (ESR) analysis of
the photocatalysts was carried out on a Bruker A300 spectrom-
eter to detect superoxide radicals (cO2
) in a methanol disper-
sion with 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) as the spin-
trapping reagent. The morphology of MXene (Ti3C2) was
observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM, JSM-6700F).
Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were obtained on
a Bruker Vertex 70 FTIR spectrometer.

Preparation of MXene (Ti3C2). MXene (Ti3C2) was synthesized
according to a previously reported method.44 In a typical

Preparation of the C3N4-M. C3N4-M was prepared by a hydro-
thermal process according to a previous report.45 Typically,
200 mg of C3N4 and 10 mg MXene (Ti3C2) were dispersed in
50 mL ultrapure water containing 100 mg of glucose, which was
then transferred into a hydrothermal reactor and heated at
160 C for 24 h. The purpose of using glucose was to protect the
MXene (Ti3C2) from being oxidized throughout the hydro-
thermal reaction. Finally, the sample solution was washed with
water three times and then dried at 60 C overnight to obtain
the C3N4-M.
2.3.3
Preparation of the Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M photocatalyst.
Five solutions of the Pt@b-CD NCs with diﬀerent volumes (i.e.,
3.1, 6.3, 9.5, 12.8, and 16.2 mL) were separately mixed with
60 mg C3N4-M, followed by adding some water to make the total

of Pt NCs named as Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M-X (note: X ¼ 1, 2, 3, 4, and
5 wt%, which denote the weight proportions of Pt species
instead of the whole Pt@b-CD NCs in the sample).
For the reference sample Pt@GSH/C3N4-M, the synthesis of
Pt@GSH NCs was similar to that for Pt@b-CD NCs except for the
replacement of SH-b-CD with GSH, and the fabrication of the
Pt@GSH/C3N4-M was the same as that for Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M.

Electrochemical measurements
The transient photocurrent response curves and Mott–Schottky
(MS) plots were obtained on a CS380 electrochemical worksta-

set as 10 mV.

It should be emphasized that the synthesis of Pt NCs is still
a major challenge in the NC eld, which does not match with
the prosperous advances of Au and Ag NCs. One major reason
for this remaining issue is that conventional NaBH4-reduction
based synthetic methods for Au or Ag NCs cannot be directly
applied for the synthesis of Pt NCs due to the fast-catalysis
decomposition of the NaBH4 reductant by Pt species.50 As
a demonstration, the Pt(II)–b-CD complexes cannot be reduced
for the formation of Pt@b-CD NCs within the NaBH4-reduction
system (Fig. S4†), and a large quantity of H2 bubbles could be
instantly released out once the NaBH4 solution was introduced
into the solution of Pt(II)–b-CD complexes. In this study, we
delicately utilized the specic interaction between the Pt and
CO, and achieved the successful synthesis of the b-CD-protected
Pt NCs within the CO-reduction system, which oﬀers a feasible
strategy for the synthesis of various Pt NCs with rich functions
and may stimulate extensive exploration in the applications of
Pt NCs.
In terms of the fabrication of C3N4-M, MXene (Ti2C3) was
rst prepared by etching the Ti3AlC2 MAX phase in the HF
solution to remove the Al layer. The obtained product showed
a multilayer structure with an opened up interspace based on
the SEM (Fig. S5a†) and TEM images (Fig. S5b†), indicating the
successful formation of MXene (Ti2C3). Aer that, the MXene
(Ti2C3) was deposited on the C3N4 surface to fabricate the C3N4-
M hybrid material via a hydrothermal treatment. The TEM
results revealed that the MXene (Ti2C3) layers were deposited on
the surface of two-dimensional C3N4 nanomaterial through
comparing the C3N4 before and aer MXene (Ti2C3) deposition
(Fig. S6†). Of note, the deposition of MXene (Ti2C3) on C3N4
could boost the harvesting of Vis-light because of its full-
spectrum light absorption ability.
With both the Pt@b-CD NCs and the C3N4-M nanomaterial
in our hands, we hybridized them for the fabrication of the
Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M photocatalyst via a simple UV irradiation
method (see the Experimental section for details). TEM and
HRTEM techniques were used to characterize the morphologies
of the as-synthesized samples. As shown in Fig. 2a, b and S7,†
ultrasmall Pt NCs were uniformly distributed on the surface of
the C3N4-M nanomaterial. FTIR was also employed to charac-
terize these Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M samples (with pristine Pt@b-CD
NCs as the reference; Fig. S8†). As shown, the typical bands of
C3N4 were detectable in all ve Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M samples (e.g.,
the band at 810 cm1 was the typical bending vibration of the
triazine units, while the band at 1250 cm1 indicated the
presence of –C–N–C, and the band at 3100 cm1 was ascribed to
–NH and/or –NH2). More importantly, the stretching vibration
of –OH in b-CD at 3560 cm1 in the FTIR spectra clearly indi-
cated the successful loading of Pt@b-CD on the surface of C3N4-
M. Meanwhile, the HRTEM image (Fig. 2b) disclosed obvious
lattice fringes with a measured spacing of 0.35 nm, corre-
sponding to the (006) lattice space of the MXene (Ti3C2), further
corroborating the deposition of MXene (Ti2C3) on the C3N4
surface. In addition, EDX element mapping was also applied to
investigate the elemental distribution over the sample of Pt@b-
CD/C3N4-M. As displayed in Fig. 2c, the elements of C, N, Ti, and
Pt were distributed uniformly across the Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M.
Therefore, based on these results, we may conclude that
abundant Pt@b-CD NCs had been uniformly deposited on the
C3N4-M surface, which could be favorable for the latter photo-
catalytic application.

The as-fabricated Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M possessed an excellent
harvest capability for Vis-light, which is one of key factors for
outstanding photocatalytic performance and could be evi-
denced by its UV-Vis diﬀuse reectance spectrum (DRS). As
shown in Fig. 3d, while the DRS of pristine C3N4 showed an
absorption band at around 450 nm, both C3N4-M and Pt@b-CD/
C3N4-M exhibited a broader yet stronger absorption in the Vis-
light region from 400 to 800 nm, which could be attributed to
the powerful performance of MXene (Ti3C2) in Vis-light har-
vesting.55,56 Furthermore, owing to the additional light adsorp-
tion of the Pt@b-CD NCs, Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M displayed a higher
optical absorption than C3N4-M in the Vis-light region, which
may imply the higher eﬃciency of Vis-light utilization of the Pt
NCs-based photocatalyst in photocatalytic applications. In
addition, the deposition of Pt NCs onto the C3N4-M could adjust
the energy gap of the as-designed photocatalyst, which is
another salient feature of the as-fabricated Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M
photocatalyst. For example, based on the UV-Vis DRS absorp-
tion spectra, the energy band gaps of the pristine C3N4, C3N4-M,

The surface b-CD ligands of the Pt@b-CD NCs play a signi-
cant role in illuminating the photocatalytic performance of the
C3N4-M semiconductor through serving as “delivery channels”
to transfer the reactant O2 from the water onto the active sites of
the Pt NCs. To corroborate this, another type of Pt NCs pro-
tected by GSH molecules was synthesized for the construction
of Pt@GSH/C3N4-M as a reference sample. TEM conrmed that
the Pt NCs@GSH had comparable sizes to the Pt@b-CD NCs
(<2 nm, Fig. S16a†), which was further supported by their
similar UV-Vis absorption and solution color (Fig. S16b† and

transfer of reactants (such as O2), whereas the hydrophobic
cavity of the SH-b-CD ligands as “transfer channels” favored the
transfer of the O2 reactant to approach the active sites on the Pt
cores. This result proved our hypothesis that engineering the
surface chemistry of Pt NCs is an eﬃcient way to improve the
photocatalytic activity of the NCs-based photocatalysts.

sequential one-electron reduction of O2, benzoquinone (BQ) as
a typical scavenger of cO2

obvious decrease in H2O2 production, implying that the
sequential one-electron reduction of O2 is the main route for
photocatalytic H2O2 production over Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M. To
conrm this issue, ESR was used to check the existence of cO2

. It should be noted that our

two-electron reduction of O2 is thermodynamically more

is more positive than that of O2 to cO2

7, see eqn (3)). The unexpected “sequential one-electron
reduction of O2” pathway for H2O2 production may be caused
by the hydrophobic cavity of the NC's surface b-CD ligands,
which permitted the transport of dissolved O2 onto the active
sites of the Pt core but prevented the hydrophilic H+ from water
passing through. In this way, the one-electron reduction of O2 to
cO2
 was facilitated, and the resulting cO2

(NH4)2C2O4 as a well-known h+ scavenger was also introduced to
the photocatalytic system (Fig. 5e), and the yield of H2O2

(NH4)2C2O4 could eﬃciently suppress the charge carriers'
recombination via the continuous consumption of photo-

utilized to reduce O2 to H2O2.

Conclusions
In summary, we successfully developed a high-performance Pt
NCs-based photocatalyst for H2O2 production via depositing
ultrasmall Pt@b-CD NCs on the surface of C3N4-M. The as-
designed photocatalyst integrated the complementary proper-
ties of the Pt core, surface SH-b-CD ligands, C3N4, and MXene
(Ti3C2), leading to a signicant enhancement (6.0 times) in
H2O2 yields compared to pristine C3N4. This boosting eﬀect
relied on the following factors: (1) the Pt cores of Pt@b-CD NCs
could serve as e acceptors and active sites to promote the
separation of e/h+ pairs as well as the photocatalytic reactions,
while the unique hydrophobic cavity of SH-b-CD ligands made
the Pt cores more accessible to O2 by serving as “transfer
channels” of O2; (2) the combination of MXene (Ti3C2) with
C3N4 guaranteed the strong harvesting ability for Vis-light,
elevating the solar utilization as well as the photocatalytic eﬃ-
ciency of the Pt@b-CD/C3N4-M photocatalyst. We believe this
interesting strategy and the ndings revealed in this work
would
provide
a
good
platform
to
explore
novel
high-
performance photocatalysts for H2O2 production.
