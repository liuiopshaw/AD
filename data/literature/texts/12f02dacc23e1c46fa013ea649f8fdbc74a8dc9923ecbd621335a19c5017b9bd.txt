Intraperitoneal injection of Anti-ASC speck nanobodies reduces aortic stenosis in a
murine model
B. Bartsch1, I. Hawwari2, F. Schmidt2, G. Nickenig1, S. Zimmer1, B.S. Franklin2
1Heartcenter Bonn, University Hospital Bonn, Department of Internal Medicine-II, Bonn, Germany
2University of Bonn, Institute of Innate Immunity, Bonn, Germany

Background: Aortic stenosis is the most common degenerative valve disease. While surgical or interventional valve replacement are
well-established treatment options in advanced disease stages, no pharmaceutical therapy currently exists to limit disease progression. In early
disease stages, inﬂammasome activation followed by pro-inﬂammatory cytokine maturation plays a key role in disease development. In recent
years, adaptor protein apoptosis-associated speck-like protein containing a CARD (ASC) speck-dependent inﬂammasome activation has been
identiﬁed as a key regulator of inﬂammasome formation.
Hypothesis:
We hypothesize that ASC nanobody therapy dismantles inﬂammasome formation and reduces aortic stenosis in a murine model of aortic
stenosis.
Methods: ASC nanobodies were synthesized from heavy chain-only antibodies derived from immunized alpacas. To enhance bioavailability,
ASC nanobodies were bound to albumin, extending their half-life to approximately 48 hours. Aortic stenosis was induced in mice using a
previously established protocol involving ultrasound-guided wire injury of the aortic valve. Treatment consisted of two intraperitoneal injections
of either ASC nanobodies or control nanobodies, administered on days 1 and 2 post-injury. Heart and valvular function were assessed via
transthoracic echocardiography at 1 and 4 weeks post-injury.
Results: No differences in procedure-related mortality were observed following wire injury. Echocardiographic assessment demonstrated
preserved ejection fraction in all animals, with no signiﬁcant differences between groups. However, echocardiographic markers of aortic
stenosis were signiﬁcantly reduced following ASC nanobody therapy at both 1 and 4 weeks post-injury: peak velocity (1 week: Control: 2460
mm/s, anti-ASC: 2070 mm/s, p = 0.02; 4 weeks: Control: 2543 mm/s, anti-ASC: 2118 mm/s, p = 0.01), peak gradient (1 week: Control: 21.3
mmHg, anti-ASC: 15.9 mmHg, p = 0.04; 4 weeks: Control: 22.6 mmHg, anti-ASC: 13.6 mmHg, p = 0.01), and mean gradient (1 week: Control:
7.1 mmHg, anti-ASC: 4.6 mmHg, p = 0.04; 4 weeks: Control: 7.3 mmHg, anti-ASC: 4.3 mmHg, p = 0.03).
Conclusion: Intraperitoneal ASC nanobody administration is safe and feasible in mice. Aortic stenosis was signiﬁcantly reduced following ASC
nanobody treatment, highlighting the critical role of inﬂammasome activation in early disease progression and its potential as a future
pharmaceutical target.
