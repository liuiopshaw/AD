A Bioinspired Single-Atom Fe Nanozyme with Excellent
Laccase-Like Activity for Eﬃcient Aﬂatoxin B1 Removal
Lei Wang, Zixuan Liu, Lili Yao, Shuai Liu, Qiuping Wang, Hao Qu, Yuen Wu, Yu Mao,*
and Lei Zheng*
The applications of natural laccases are greatly restricted because of their
drawbacks like poor biostability, high costs, and low recovery eﬃciency. M/NC
single atom nanozymes (M/NC SAzymes) are presenting as great substitutes
due to their superior enzyme-like activity, excellent selectivity and high
stability. In this work, inspired by the catalytic active center of natural enzyme,
a biomimetic Fe/NC SAzyme (Fe-SAzyme) with O2-Fe-N4 coordination is
successfully developed, exhibiting excellent laccase-like activity. Compared
with their natural counterpart, Fe-SAzyme has shown superior catalytic
eﬃciency and excellent stability under a wide range of pH (3.0–9.0),
temperature (4–80 °C) and NaCl strength (0–300 mm). Interestingly, density
functional theory (DFT) calculations reveal that the high catalytic performance
is attributed to the activation of O2 by O2-Fe-N4 sites, which weakened the
O─O bonds in the oxygen-to-water oxidation pathway. Furthermore,
Fe-SAzyme is successfully applied for eﬃcient aﬂatoxin B1 removal based on
its robust laccase-like catalytic activity. This work provides a strategy for the
rational design of laccase-like SAzymes, and the proposed catalytic
mechanism will help to understand the coordination environment eﬀect of
SAzymes on laccase-like catalytic processes.
1. Introduction
Laccases are natural enzymes found in bacteria, fungi and
plants that catalyze the oxidation of a variety of phenolic and
non-phenolic aromatic compounds with the reduction of dioxy-
gen molecule to water.[1] Owing to their environmental friendly
capabilities for phenolic degradation, laccases are considered as
biological green catalysts,[1a] which has been used in pulp and pa-
per industry,[2] food processing,[1d]sensing,[3] and environmental
applications.[1d,4] Unfortunately, as a natural enzyme, laccases

have certain inherent drawbacks, such

are a class of nanomaterials with natural
enzyme-like catalytic activity, which have
been explored in recent years.[6] They are
superior to natural enzymes in many as-
pects, such as high stability and low cost.
However, their catalytic eﬃciency and selec-
tivity are not satisﬁed when comparing to
natural enzymes.[6c–f]
Single-atom nanozymes are a unique
class of nanozymes with well-deﬁned geo-
metric structures containing single-atoms
that are well dispersed on supports.[7]
Therefore, every metal atom on surface can
be used as an active site for catalysis, thus
providing high catalytic eﬃciency, excel-
lent selectivity and unique quantum size
eﬀect. Among them, M/NC single atom
nanozymes (M/NC SAzymes),[8] the metal-
nitrogen-doped carbon support SAzymes,
have good stability and environmental resis-
tance due to the existence of carbon matrixs.
Meanwhile, the formation of geometric
M-Nx sites which are similar to the catalytic active centers of natu-
ral enzymes endows M/NC SAzymes with the advantages of high
enzyme-like activity and excellent selectivity.[6c,9] Compared with
other M/NC SAzymes, Fe/NC SAzymes exhibit the superiorities
like excellent catalytic performance, low toxicity and low cost,[10]
which have great potential in varies ﬁelds, including food safety
monitoring,[10a,b,11] environmental treatment[12] and theraputic
applications.[10c-e,13] In addition, some studies have found that the
co-coordination of other coordination atoms (such as O, S, and
P) can regulate the electronic environment of the active center of
SAzyme, thus enhancing its enzyme-like catalytic activity.[13c,14]
Herein, we report a nitrogen-doped carbon-based iron SAzyme
(Fe-SAzyme) as a highly eﬃcient biomimetic laccase, which
displayed excellent laccase-like activity, remarkable stability
and wonderful recyclability. Structural characterizations demon-
strated that iron atoms were atomically dispersed in Fe-SAzyme
with well-deﬁned O2-Fe-N4 sites, which were similar to the ac-
tive centers of natural enzyme. Theoretical calculations sug-
gested that the high catalytic activity of Fe-SAzyme was at-
tributed to the activation of O2 by O2-Fe-N4 sites. Further-
more, this Fe-SAzyme was applied for eﬃcient aﬂatoxin B1
(AFB1) removal based on its robust laccase-like catalytic activity

2. Results and Discussion
Fe-SAzyme was prepared by two-step annealing method.[15] As
a contrast, nanoparticles without Fe-doping were prepared and
named Z-NC. The morphology and size distributions were
characterized using high-resolution transmission electron mi-
croscopy (HRTEM). As shown in Figure 1a, Fe-SAzyme exhibited
the typical rhombic dodecahedral geometry. The average diame-
ter of Fe-SAzyme was characterized to be 155.0 nm by HRTEM
image (Figure 1a) or 182.56 nm by a nanoparticle-tracking anal-
ysis (NTA) system (Figure S1, Supporting Information). Mean-
while, the high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld scanning transmission
electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) image displayed an uni-
form distribution of bright dots on the support, which could be at-
tributed to the isolated Fe atoms (Figure 1b; Figure S2a, Support-
ing Information), and no agglomerated Fe nanoparticles existed
in Fe-SAzyme. Figure S2 (Supporting Information) revealed that
the distance between two adjacent Fe sites was ≈0.292 nm. The
X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) patterns of Z-NC and Fe-SAzyme showed
that there were no characteristic peaks of Fe in Fe-SAzyme
(Figure 1c). All the above results indicated that there were no crys-
talline iron species in Fe-SAzyme. The energy dispersive spec-
tra (EDS) further demonstrated that C, N, O, and Fe elements
were uniformly distributed throughout the whole structure of
Fe-SAzyme (Figure 1d). Furthermore, the Raman spectra of Fe-
SAzyme reﬂected a highly defective structure, as evidenced by the
intensity ratio (ID/IG = 1.22) of D-band (1333.84 cm−1, defective
carbon characteristic peak) and G-band (1576.97 cm−1, graphitic
carbon characteristic peak) (Figure 1e). The N2 adsorption–
desorption isotherms identiﬁed that the Brunauer–Emmett–
Teller (BET) surface area of Fe-SAzyme was 1080.71 m2 g−1 and
the adsorption average pore width by BET of Fe-SAzyme was
2.54 nm (25.4 Å) (Figure 1f,g; Figure S3 and Table S1, Sup-
porting Information). The loading of Fe in Fe-SAzyme was de-
termined to be 1.01 wt% by inductively coupled plasma-mass
spectrometry.
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was conducted to fur-
ther investigate the existing states of key elements in Fe-SAzyme.
The survey spectra conﬁrmed the existence of C, N, O, and Fe
in Fe-SAzyme (Figure S4a, Supporting Information), which was
consistent with the result of EDS. The C 1s XPS spectrum of Fe-
SAzyme was divided into four peaks located at 284.6, 285.3, 286.4,
and 288.9 eV, assigning to C─C, C─N, C─O, and C═O, respec-
tively (Figure S4b, Supporting Information). The N 1s analysis
revealed that the presence of pyridinic N, Fe-Nx species, pyrrolic
N, graphitic N, and oxidized N, corresponding to the ﬁve peaks
at binding energies of 398.5, 399.5, 400.6, 401.5, and 403.3 eV,
respectively (Figure 2a), among which the Fe-Nx species revealed
the coordination of the Fe atom and N atom in Fe-SAzyme.[9c,16]
Moreover, the presence of both Fe2+ and Fe3+ species were iden-
tiﬁed in Fe-SAzyme by the Fe 2p XPS spectrum. Both the Fe
2p1/2 and Fe 2p3/2 doublets represented the existence of Fe–N
and Fe–O (Figure 2b).[17] The O 1s XPS spectrum of Fe-SAzyme
was deconvoluted into three peaks located at 530.4, 531.2, and
532.2 eV, belonging to Fe─O, C═O, and C─O, respectively
(Figure 2c).[18]

the normalized X-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES)
spectrum of Fe-SAzyme displayed a weak pre-edge peak at
7113.8 eV stemming from the characteristics of square-planar Fe-
N4 moieties.[14g,16b] Additionally, the XANES spectra also demon-

We employed density functional theory (DFT) calculations in
combination with the results reported in the literature[25] to fur-
ther gain the underlying theoretical insight into the process of
laccase-like catalytic reaction between Fe-SAzyme and 2,4-DP.
The surface reaction intermediate states and the relative energy
changes involved in the reaction process were shown in Figure 4d
and Figure S12 (Supporting Information). The pathways for the
catalytic reaction between 2,4-DP and O2 by Fe-SAzyme and the
nine surface reaction intermediates involved were illustrated. In
the reaction system, 4 molecules of 2,4-DP (R) were oxidized by
one molecule of O2 through a single Fe atom active site on the sur-
face of Fe-SAzyme to form four molecules of oxidation products
ox2,4-DP (P) and two molecules of H2O (4R + 1O2 →4P + 2H2O).
First, a O2 molecule was adsorbed to the Fe site to form an OO-
Fe-SAzyme (int 1). Int 1 was propitious to adsorb a R molecule
and form R-OO-Fe-SAzyme (int 2) through the formation of O–
H. Then, a H was dissociated from R molecule to form HOO-Fe-
SAzyme (int 3) and a P molecule. Int 3 could easily adsorb a R
molecule to form R-HOO-Fe-SAzyme (int 4) and could weaken
the O─O bond in O2. Subsequently, the weakened O─O bond in
O2 broke, which resulted in the formation of a H2O molecule, an-
other P molecule and the O-Fe-SAzyme (int 5). Finally, through
a process similar to the above, two R molecules were reduced to
two P molecules by int 5 and a H2O molecule was formed at the
same time. DFT calculations revealed that O2-Fe-N4 site activated
a O2 molecule, thus weakening the O─O bonds in the oxygen-
to-water oxidation pathway, which resulted in the high catalytic

of AFB1 within 1 h were 96.94% and 76.99% respectively. AFB1
was rapidly degraded to AFQ1 from 0 to 1 h, and then AFQ1 was
gradually decomposed from 1 to 36 h (Figure 5c). Additionally,
Figure 5d showed that Fe-SAzyme signiﬁcantly decreased AFB1
under a wide pH range from pH 3.0 to pH 9.0, while AFB1 it-
self was not or rarely degraded under this pH range (Figure S13,
Supporting Information). Figure 5e showed the corresponding
changes in AFQ1 content. These results demonstrated that AFB1
could be eﬀectively degraded to AFQ1 by taking advantage of
the excellent laccase-like activity of Fe-SAzyme. Furthermore, as
mentioned in BET results (Table S1, Supporting Information),
the average pore size of Fe-SAzyme (25.4 Å) was slightly larger
than the molecular size of AFB1 (8.7 Å × 10.8 Å),[29] which con-
tributed to the outstanding adsorption potential of Fe-SAzyme for
AFB1. Excellent AFB1 removal eﬃciencies of Fe-SAzyme were
also observed in complex samples, such as peanut milk, corn
juice, crushed corn, and white vinegar (Figure S14, Supporting
Information). In conclusion, Fe-SAzyme had an excellent adsorp-
tion and removal ability for AFB1, having great potential in the
area of green biocatalysis.
3. Conclusion
Inspired by the catalytic center of natural enzyme, we had suc-
cessfully developed a biomimetic laccase, Fe-SAzyme, which was
an iron-nitrogen-doped carbon supported SAzymes with O2-Fe-
N4 as catalytic moiety. The speciﬁc activity, catalytic eﬃciency and
stability of Fe-SAzyme were signiﬁcantly higher than that of nat-
ural laccase. The excellent laccase-like catalytic activity was at-
tributed to the atomic dispersion of Fe and the unique active site
of O2-Fe-N4, which weakened the O─O bond by the activation
of O2 in the O2-to-H2O pathway. Moreover, the results demon-
strated that Fe-SAzyme had remarkable stability and exceptional
recyclability, which overcame the drawbacks of natural laccase.
Furthermore, Fe-SAzyme was succesfully applied for the eﬃcient
removal of AFB1, showing great potential in the area of green
biocatalysis. This work not only provides a guidance for design-
ing SAzymes with enzyme-like catalytic activity, but also bene-
ﬁt our understanding of the coordination environment eﬀect of
SAzymes on enzyme-like catalytic activity, which will broaden
the potential application of bioinspired SAzymes in biocatalysis
ﬁelds.
