Tailoring Enzyme-Like Activities of Gold Nanoclusters 
by Polymeric Tertiary Amines for Protecting Neurons 
Against Oxidative Stress
 Ching-Ping  Liu ,  Te-Haw  Wu ,  Yu-Lung  Lin ,  Chia-Yeh  Liu ,  Sabrina  Wang , 
 and  Shu-Yi  Lin * 
 1.  Introduction 
 The exploitation of nanomaterial-based artiﬁ cial enzymes 
(i.e., nanozymes) has received a great deal of interest 
due to their robustness and low cost compared to natural 
enzymes. [ 1,2 ] Nanozymes have been found to possess single 
or multiple enzyme-like activities, such as peroxidase, cata-
lase, and superoxide dismutase (SOD) mimics. Interestingly, 
several nanozymes exhibit pH-switchable catalase-like and 
peroxidase-like activities. [ 3 ] For instance, iron oxide nano-
particles (IONPs), [ 4 ] cerium oxide (CeO 2 ) nanoparticles, [ 5,6 ] 
and platinum nanoparticles (PtNPs) [ 7,8 ] exhibited the cat-
alase-like activity at neutral pH, whereas they behaved as 
peroxidase mimics in acidic conditions. This suggests that at

 The cytotoxicity of nanozymes has drawn much attention recently because their 
peroxidase-like activity can decompose hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 ) to produce 
highly toxic hydroxyl radicals (•OH) under acidic conditions. Although catalytic 
activities of nanozymes are highly associated with their surface properties, little is 
known about the mechanism underlying the surface coating-mediated enzyme-like 
activities. Herein, it is reported for the ﬁ rst time that amine-terminated PAMAM 
dendrimer-entrapped gold nanoclusters (AuNCs-NH 2 ) unexpectedly lose their 
peroxidase-like activity while still retaining their catalase-like activity in physiological 
conditions. Surprisingly, the methylated form of AuNCs-NH 2 (i.e., MAuNCs-N + R 3 , 
where R = H or CH 3 ) results in a dramatic recovery of the intrinsic peroxidase-like 
activity while blocking most primary and tertiary amines (1°- and 3°-amines) of 
dendrimers to form quaternary ammonium ions (4°-amines). However, the hidden 
peroxidase-like activity is also found in hydroxyl-terminated dendrimer-encapsulated 
AuNCs (AuNCs-OH, inside backbone with 3°-amines), indicating that 3°-amines are 
dominant in mediating the peroxidase-like activity. The possible mechanism is further 
conﬁ rmed that the enrichment of polymeric 3°-amines on the surface of dendrimer-
encapsulated AuNCs provides sufﬁ cient suppression of the critical mediator •OH 
for the peroxidase-like activity. Finally, it is demonstrated that AuNCs-NH 2 with 
diminished cytotoxicity have great potential for use in primary neuronal protection 
against oxidative damage.

detoxify hydrogen peroxide (H 2 O 2 ) to produce O 2 through 
their catalase-like activity. Instead, they decompose H 2 O 2 
into hydroxyl radicals (•OH) through their peroxidase-like 
activity in this condition. [ 4,7 ] Since •OH is highly active and 
detrimental to cells, it is a source of cytotoxicity in biolog-
ical systems. Due to that, an important issue is whether pH-
switchable nanozymes can kill or protect cells once they 
are trapped inside cellular organelles such as endosomes 
(pH ≈ 5.5) and lysosomes (pH ≈ 4.8) or distributed in cytosol 
(pH ≈ 7.4). So far, metal, metal oxide, and carbon-based 
nanomaterials have been found to be mainly peroxidase 
mimics. [ 9 ] Therefore, enzymatic activity-based cytotoxicity 
of nanozymes could pose a major obstacle to their use in 
long-term clinical applications due to the concern of their 
biosafety. 
 Although enzyme-like properties have been accepted 
as being the nature of nanomaterials, [ 3 ] the catalytic perfor-
mance of nanozymes can be suppressed once losing their 
surface accessibility. [ 9–11 ] Since the superﬁ cial atoms are 
key catalytic sites, decorating nanozymes with various 
coating molecules could potentially block their active sites 
to decrease and/or inactivate the enzyme mimic functions. 
Recent advances have provided several shielding com-
pounds to efﬁ ciently inhibit the intrinsic peroxidase-like 
activity of nanozymes, such as sulﬁ des, [ 12 ] catecholamines, [ 13 ] 
nucleic acids, [ 14,15 ] and proteins. [ 16 ] Alternatively, nanozyme 
aggregation is more straightforward to decrease surface 
active sites followed by degradation of the peroxidase-like 
activity. [ 17–19 ] However, these strategies of strong surface 
protection and low stability (i.e., aggregation) could poten-
tially inactivate other enzyme-like activities simultaneously. 
Otherwise, nanozymes might change their original biocom-
patibility and these shielding compounds could be another 
toxic source. Once the surface protection is reversible, [ 15 ] it 
is difﬁ cult to achieve in living systems due to easy forma-
tion of protein coronas around nanomaterials. [ 20 ] Therefore, 
nanozymes urgently await the development of a funda-
mental approach for inhibiting their intrinsic peroxidase-
like activity in order to diminish their cytotoxicity while 
also retaining surface accessibility for other enzyme-like 
activities. 
 Thiol-containing molecules are the 
most common stabilizing ligands in gold 
nanoparticles (AuNPs); however, catalytic 
activities of AuNPs are easily poisoned 
through the absorption of 
SH group to 
block surface active sites of AuNPs. [ 21 ] In 
addition, densely packed capping layers, 
such as molecules with n-alkane chains, 
can easily affect the accessibility of reac-
tants to the surface of dispersive AuNPs 
as well. [ 21,22 ] Dendrimers has been dem-
onstrated to not only stabilize AuNPs 
but also allows molecules to access their 
surface for catalysis, [ 23 ] which should 
be a superior candidate for protection 
of gold nanoparticles and nanoclusters 
as nanozymes. However, the biological 
application of dendrimer-stabilized gold nanoparticles might 
be limited by the cytotoxicity of PAMAM dendrimers. Since 
covering more amine-terminated dendrimer is similar to 
use a high-generation amine-terminated dendrimer, which 
would cause severely cytotoxicity. [ 24 ] Recently, we have found 
that gold nanoclusters (AuNCs) embedded in the cavity of 
PAMAM dendrimers resulted in the structural contraction 
of dendrimer via an irreversible backfolding of periphery 
primary amines (1°-amines), thereby eliminating toxicity sig-
niﬁ cantly. [ 25 ] This inspired us to further investigate enzyme-
like activities on dendrimer-stabilized gold nanoclusters, 
which has not yet been explored. In this study, we found for 
the ﬁ rst time that AuNCs encapsulated in the amine-termi-
nated PAMAM dendrimer (hereafter denoted as AuNCs-
NH 2 ) unexpectedly lost their peroxidase-like activity while 
still retaining their catalase-like activity under different pH 
conditions relevant to biological microenvironments. Surpris-
ingly, the methylated form of AuNCs-NH 2 (i.e., MAuNCs-
N + R 3 , where R = H or CH 3 ) resulted in a dramatic recovery 
of the intrinsic peroxidase-like activity while blocking most 
periphery 1°-amines and interior tertiary amines (3°-amines) 
of dendrimers to form quaternary ammonium ions (4°-amines). 
However, the hidden peroxidase-like activity was also found 
in hydroxyl-terminated dendrimer-encapsulated AuNCs 
(hereafter denoted as AuNCs-OH), in which the inside 
backbone of hydroxyl-terminated dendrimers typically 
possess numerous 3°-amines and outfacing groups without 
1°-amines. Thus, it was concluded that the 3°-amines are 
dominant in blocking the intrinsic peroxidase-like activity 
of AuNCs-NH 2 and AuNCs-OH. This possible mechanism 
was proposed that the enrichment of polymeric 3°-amines 
on the surface of AuNCs-NH 2 provided signiﬁ cant suppres-
sion of the critical mediator •OH for the peroxidase-like 
activity, which was conﬁ rmed by  1 H NMR measurements 
with the evidence of 3°-amine oxidation in the presence 
of •OH. After that, we also demonstrated that dendrimer-
encapsulated AuNCs have great potential for use in primary 
neuronal protection against oxidative damage (as shown in 
 Scheme  1 ) via the catalase-like activity. Simultaneously, the 
H 2 O 2 -induced cell damage would not be enhanced dramati-
cally because their peroxidase-like activity was inhibited by 
polymeric 3°-amines.

 2.  Results and Discussion 
 2.1.  Examination of Enzyme-Like Activities in AuNCs-NH 2 
 The fourth-generation, amine-terminated PAMAM den-
drimer was used to prepare the AuNCs-NH 2 according to our 
previous work described elsewhere. [ 26 ] In order to test any 
potential of AuNCs-NH 2 to consume H 2 O 2 , 3,3′,5,5′-tetra-
methylbenzidine (TMB), the horseradish peroxidase (HRP) 
substrate, was used as a colorimetric assay to detect H 2 O 2 
due to the oxidation of TMB can produce either a blue-color 
intermediate or a yellow-color end product. [ 27 ]  Figure  1 A 
showed a signiﬁ cant decrease of absorbance corresponding 
to the H 2 O 2 level in the presence of AuNCs-NH 2 until essen-
tially all H 2 O 2 was exhausted. Accordingly, AuNCs-NH 2 
can behave as enzymes. However, the dendrimer of G 4 NH 2 
alone did not affect the H 2 O 2 level. This initial ﬁ nding sug-
gested that the depletion of H 2 O 2 by AuNCs-NH 2 was not 
dependent on the template of the dendrimer, but instead was 
caused by the inside Au due to its own intrinsic enzyme-like 
activities. [ 28,29 ] To further clarify this possibility, the perox-
idase-like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 was ﬁ rst evaluated since 
BSA-AuNCs [ 30–32 ] and lysozyme-stabilized AuNCs [ 33 ] have 
previously been reported to behave similarly to peroxidase 
in an acidic environment. Unexpectedly, AuNCs-NH 2 showed 
almost no activity to catalyze the colorless substrate of TMB, 
whereas the addition of HRP as the positive control resulted 
in yellow color TMB in the acidic buffer solution (Figure  1 B). 
Similar to the H 2 O 2 /TMB system, the characteristic brown 
color of the 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) substrate was only 
observed with the addition of HRP. Therefore, these tests 
conﬁ rmed that AuNCs-NH 2 could not catalyze H 2 O 2 to oxi-
dize peroxidase substrates, such as TMB and DAB, indicating 
the loss of the intrinsic peroxidase-like activity. 
 Next, it is interesting to consider whether the depletion of 
H 2 O 2 results from the catalase-like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 . 
For this purpose, the dynamic process of forming gas bubbles 
was obtained by adding catalase and AuNCs-NH 2 in H 2 O 2 
solutions, respectively (Figure  1 C). It should be noted that no 
bubbles were seen in the control with H 2 O 2 alone. To further 
conﬁ rm that the bubbles produced by the addition of either 
catalase or AuNCs-NH 2 were O 2 , EPR oximetry in conjunc-
tion with the spin labeling of 3-carbamoyl-2,2,5,5-tetrame-
thyl-3-pyrroline-1-yloxyl (CTPO) was performed because 
the super hyperﬁ ne structure of the EPR signal of CTPO 
strongly depends on the O 2 concentration. [ 34 ] According to 
EPR spectra (Figure  1 C), when catalase was added into the 
H 2 O 2 solution with CTPO, the sharp super hyperﬁ ne struc-
ture of the EPR signal became diminished and eventually 
ﬂ attened with decreased intensity. In addition, the different 
extent of diminution in the CTPO structure was exhibited 
when AuNCs-NH 2 were added instead of catalase under the 
same condition. These results suggest the formation of O 2 
during decomposition of H 2 O 2 assisted by either catalase or 
AuNCs-NH 2 , which provides direct evidence on the catalase-
like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 .

 2.3.  Mechanism of Inhibition in the Peroxidase-Like Activity 
of AuNCs-NH 2 
 Since amines are easily oxidized, [ 39 ] we hypothesized that 
the •OH-scavenging ability of AuNCs-NH 2 was attributable 
to the highly active •OH reacting with abundant amines 
from the hyper-branched dendrimer (i.e., the periphery 
1°-amines and the interior 3°-amines). In other words, the 
peroxidase-like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 should be recovered 
from the elimination of amines on the surface of AuNCs-
NH 2 . For this purpose, 1°- and 3°-amines on AuNCs-NH 2 
were modiﬁ ed by methyl iodide to form AuNCs with qua-
ternary ammonium ions (4°-amines; i.e., MAuNCs-N + R 3 
and MAuNCs-OH). Since most nanozymes exhibit highly 
pH-dependent peroxidase-like activities, [ 4,6–8 ] we were 
careful to investigate the peroxidase-like behavior of 
AuNCs-NH 2 , AuNCs-OH, MAuNCs-N + R 3 and MAuNCs-
OH in two intracellular pH of 7.4 and 4.8 with respect to the 
microenvironments of cytosol and lysosome, respectively. 
At pH 4.8, both MAuNCs-N + R 3 and MAuNCs-OH could 
catalyze the oxidation of substrate 2,2′-azinobis(3-ethylb-
enzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (diammonium salt, ABTS 2− ) 
in the presence of H 2 O 2 , which was accompanied with the 
appearance of a characteristic dark green color  ( Figure  3 A). 
This indicated that both MAuNCs-N + R 3 and MAuNCs-OH 
showed great peroxidase-like activities in the acidic condi-
tion. In contrast, the H 2 O 2 /ABTS 2− reaction solutions with 
either AuNCs-NH 2 or AuNCs-OH remained transparent at 
pH 7.4 and 4.8, respectively, verifying their lack of peroxi-
dase-like activity. However, the surface charge characteris-
tics between nanoparticles and peroxidase substrates could 
inﬂ uence the catalytic activity of nanoparticles as well. [ 40 ] 
Since ABTS 2− is a negatively charged substrate, it is likely 
to have strong afﬁ nity to positively charged MAuNCs-N + R 3 
(+42.8 mV) rather than neutral AuNCs-NH 2 (+2.3 mV), 
leading to essentially color change in peroxidase substrates 
(Figure  3 A). To exclude this possibility, DAB and TMB with 
positive surface charges were chosen as other peroxidase 
substrates for peroxidase-like activity comparison. In 
Figure  3 B,C, whether DAB or TMB showed consistent 
results with ABTS 2− , indicating that the catalytic reaction 
was independent of the charge characteristics of MAuNCs-
N + R 3 and AuNCs-NH 2 towards peroxidase substrates. 
Therefore, the elimination of amines on the surface of 
AuNCs-NH 2 or AuNCs-OH could dramatically recover their 
intrinsic peroxidase-like activity. By comparing the peroxi-
dase-like activity of AuNCs-OH and MAuNCs-OH (their 
outfacing groups without 1°-amines), it suggested that poly-
meric 3°-amines play a key role in mediation of the intrinsic 
peroxidase-like activity.

 On the basis of the above observations, we speculated that 
the 3°-amines on the surface of AuNCs-NH 2 and AuNCs-OH 
are possibly oxidized by •OH, which may contribute to the 
inhibition of their intrinsic peroxidase-like activity. The reac-
tion of •OH with triethanolamines (i.e., with 3°-amines) has 
been reported to be about 100 times faster than the reaction 
of •OH with ethanolamines (i.e., with 1°-amines), and the rate 
constants for the protonated amines were found to typically 
be lower (by one order of magnitude) than those for their 
neutral forms. [ 39 ] This supported that the interior 3°-amines 
on the hyperbranched dendrimer are predominant in reacting 
with •OH compared to the easily protonated, periphery 
1°-amines. To conﬁ rm amines in the hyper-branched den-
drimer of G 4 NH 2 being oxidized by •OH,  1 H NMR spec-
troscopy was carried out. The Fenton reaction (Figure S2A, 
Supporting Information) and the H 2 O/X-ray system (Figure 
S2B, Supporting Information) were used to produce the •OH 
for amine oxidization, respectively. The product of hydroxy-
lamine appeared around 5.5–6.5 ppm was consistent with 
G 2 OH oxidation via the Cope elimination reaction in our 
previous work, [ 41 ] conﬁ rming the oxidation of 3°-amines by 
•OH. Again, the •OH-scavenging ability of AuNCs-NH 2 at 
pH 4.8 supported their hidden peroxidase-like activity in 
the acidic condition (Figure S3A, Supporting Information). 
In addition, the methylated G 4 NH 2 (i.e., MG 4 N + R 3 , where 
R = H or CH 3 ) showed a relatively poor ability to deplete 
•OH compared to its original form (G 4 NH 2 ) (Figure S3B, 
Supporting Information), indicating that the intrinsic perox-
idase-like activity of MAuNCs-NH 2 and MAuNCs-OH could 
be expressed. It is noted that MG 4 N + R 3 was used instead 
of MAuNCs-N + R 3 to examine its •OH-scavenging ability 
due to the interference from the peroxidase-like activity of 
MAuNCs-NH 2 to generate •OH in the presence of H 2 O 2 
(i.e., the Fenton reaction). However, the previous report 
on acetylated G 9 PAMAM dendrimer-encapsulated PtNPs 
(Ac-G 9 PtNPs), [ 7 ] in which only 1°-amines were removed by 
acetylation, still demonstrated their intrinsic peroxidase-like 
activity even though 3°-amines were still existed in the Ac-G 9 
dendrimer. This discrepancy may be due to the considerably 
poor accessibility of 3°-amines in the hydrophobic cavity of 
the Ac-G 9 dendrimer compared to those in the G 4 NH 2 .

 2.5.  Effects of AuNCs-NH 2 in Protecting Primary Neuron 
Cells Against H 2 O 2 -Induced Cytotoxicity 
 As reactive oxygen species (ROS) are the fundamental medi-
ators of neurotoxicity in ischemia/reperfusion injury, [ 43,44 ] 
antioxidant enzymes such as catalase have been used to 
neutralize ROS-mediated injury. [ 45 ] However, their safe and 
effective delivery to the central nervous system remains 
a challenge due to their short half-life and poor uptake by 
the cells (i.e., neurons and astrocytes). [ 46 ] Recently, the 
poly(lactic- co -glycolic acid) nanoparticle-mediated catalase 
delivery strategy was demonstrated to protect human neu-
rons from oxidative stress. [ 45 ] This inspired us to directly use 
the catalase-like activity of nanozymes for neuroprotection, 
which is relatively simple and without the requirement for 
enzyme encapsulation and controllable release. AuNCs-NH 2 
were chosen because their catalase-like activity is superior 
to that of AuNCs-OH and their peroxidase-like activity is 
hidden so that it has diminished cytotoxicity. Before the in 
vivo test of neuroprotection by AuNCs-NH 2 , we ﬁ rst exam-
ined the cytotoxicity in primary mouse neurons. Cultured 
neurons treated with AuNCs-NH 2 at concentrations of 
500 µg mL −1 and above showed signiﬁ cant cytotoxicity 
(Figure S4, Supporting Information), indicating that neuronal 
cells are more sensitive to AuNCs-NH 2 than cancer [ 25 ] and 
other cells (Figure S5, Supporting Information). Therefore, 
the concentration of AuNCs-NH 2 about 100 µg mL −1 was 
used for further neuronal culture experiments. 
 Since AuNCs-NH 2 with bright blue photoluminescence 
has been applied in cellular imaging [ 26 ] via the endocytosis 
pathway, [ 25 ] the cellular uptake and localization of AuNCs-
NH 2 in neuronal cells were examined by confocal micro-
scopy. Neuronal cells incubated with AuNCs-NH 2 for 24 h 
exhibited bright blue ﬂ uorescence, indicating that AuNCs-
NH 2 were localized inside the cells (Figure  4 B). This prop-
erty makes AuNCs-NH 2 superior to native enzymes, which 
do not permeate cells under normal conditions to neutralize 
ROS intracellularly. [ 47 ] Figure S6 (Supporting Information) 
showed that AuNCs-NH 2 were internalized by neurons via 
endocytosis and then localized in the endosomes or lys-

contribute to cytotoxicity via their peroxidase-like activity 
if they were trapped in acidic microenvironments (i.e.,

 To further evaluate the ability of AuNCs-NH 2 to protect 
neurons against H 2 O 2 -induced toxicity, cell viability was deter-

live cells [ 48 ] and an MTT assay ( Figure  5 ). Figure  5 A showed

that of the control. However, neurons pretreated with AuNCs-
NH 2 kept more cells alive after H 2 O 2 challenge, displaying red 
ﬂ uorescence similar to that of the control. In addition, neu-
rons treated with AuNCs-NH 2 alone had no inﬂ uence on cell 
survival as compared with the untreated control. Figure  5 B 
showed the quantitative assessment of cell viability for neuron 
cells exposed to H 2 O 2 with or without pretreatment of AuNCs-
NH 2 . Obviously, neurons treated with H 2 O 2 (at 100 × 10 −6 or 
200 × 10 −6 m ) caused signiﬁ cant loss of cell viability. However, 
as compared with H 2 O 2 treatment alone, pretreatment with 
AuNCs-NH 2 followed by H 2 O 2 challenge preserved cell via-
bility similar to the untreated control. These results conﬁ rmed 
that the pretreatment of primary neuron cells with AuNCs-
NH 2 exhibited excellent protection from the H 2 O 2 -induced 
toxicity. It should be noted that IONPs and Ac-G 9 PtNPs have 
been reported to enhance H 2 O 2 -induced cell damage dramati-
cally due to their intrinsic peroxidase-like activity under H 2 O 2 
challenge. [ 4,7,49 ] In contrast, neurons treated with AuNCs-NH 2 
did not enhance cell death after H 2 O 2 challenge, supporting 
that the peroxidase-like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 was negligibly 
weak intracellularly. Since the intrinsic 
peroxidase-like activity of AuNCs-NH 2 
has been blocked by surface 3°-amines, the 
cytotoxicity from highly toxic •OH can be 
diminished; therefore, AuNCs-NH 2 could 
be of great potential for safe, long-term use 
in clinical applications. 
 3.  Conclusion 
 We report herein for the ﬁ rst time that 
dendrimer-encapsulated 
AuNCs 
unex-
pectedly lost their peroxidase-like activity 
while still retaining their catalase-like 
activity under different pH conditions 
relevant 
to 
biological 
microenviron-
ments. To investigate the mechanism for 
inhibition of the intrinsic peroxidase-like 
activity, the AuNCs-NH 2 and AuNCs-OH 
(i.e., 
hydroxyl-terminated 
dendrimer-
encapsulated AuNCs), as well as their 
corresponding methylated forms (i.e., 
MAuNC-N + R 3 and MAuNCs-OH), were 
systematically investigated their cata-
lytic ability to oxidize various peroxi-
dase substrates. The results indicated that 
polymeric 3°-amines were dominant in 
blocking 
the 
intrinsic 
peroxidase-like 
activity of AuNC-NH 2 (or AuNCs-OH). 
The possible mechanism was proposed 
that the enrichment of polymeric 3°-amine 
on the surface of AuNCs-NH 2 (or AuNCs-
OH) provided sufﬁ cient suppression of the critical mediator 
•OH, which is responsible for the peroxidase-like activity. The 
evidence of 3°-amine oxidation in the presence of •OH by  1 H 
NMR measurements was further conﬁ rmed this mechanism. 
By taking advantage of AuNCs-NH 2 with diminished cyto-
toxicity due to the inhibition of their intrinsic peroxidase-like 
activity, AuNCs-NH 2 with the catalase-like activity have great 
potential for use in protecting primary neuronal cells against 
H 2 O 2 -induced toxicity. Our study provides a simple way to 
speciﬁ cally block the intrinsic peroxidase-like activity of gold 
nanozymes and to retain their catalase-like activity simulta-
neously by polymeric 3°-amine, which might guide the design 
of other nanozymes for different biological applications.

 Synthesis of AuNCs-OH, AuNCs-NH 2 , MAuNCs-OH, and MAuNCs-
N + R 3 (R = H or CH 3 ) : According to our previous work, [ 26 ] HAuCl 4 
(200 µL, 150 × 10 −3 M ) was ﬁ rst added to 20 mL of deionized water 
containing G 4 NH 2 (94.9 µL, 5 µmol, 20 wt% methanol solution). The 
mixed G 4 NH 2 and HAuCl 4 solution was incubated at 4 °C overnight 
before being irradiated by microwave (300 W/120 °C for 30 min). 
The precipitate and AuNCs-NH 2 were then ﬁ ltered after reduction 
through a 3 kDa MWCO membrane ﬁ lter, and the remaining AuCl 4 − 
were removed using anionic exchange chromatography to obtain 
the puriﬁ ed AuNCs-NH 2 . Then the internal tertiary amine groups 
and the surface amine groups of the AuNCs-NH 2 were treated with 
methyl iodide in  N,N ′-dimethylformamide/H 2 O (1 mL/1 mL) at 
room temperature overnight. The reaction mixture was extracted by 
dichloro methane three times and then dried under a vacuum and dis-
solved in water (1 mL). The resulting solution was then lyophilized to 
derive a white solid (i.e., methylated products named MAuNCs-NH 2 ). 
The emissions of AuNCs-NH 2 and MAuNCs-N + R 3 were measured by 
a  ﬂ uorescence spectrophotometer (Varian, Cary Eclipse) with excita-
tion at 390 nm and monitored emission at 460 nm. 
 Determination of the H 2 O 2 Level : To test the consumption of 
H 2 O 2 by AuNCs-NH 2 , TMB solution including 200 × 10 −6 M H 2 O 2 
mixed with AuNCs-NH 2 (25 × 10 −6 M ) for a period of time and then 
added HRP solution (1.5 × 10 −3 M , 1 µL) for remaining H 2 O 2 detec-
tion. After that, the color formation was monitored at 450 nm by 
UV-Vis spectroscopy (DU-800, Beckman Coulter). The control group 
only replaced AuNCs-NH 2 with the same concentration of G 4 NH 2 
(25 × 10 −6 M ) to repeat the experiment. 
 Electron Paramagnetic Resonance Spectroscopy : EPR spin label 
oximetry was used to investigate oxygen generation. A stock solu-
tion containing CTPO and H 2 O 2 was deoxygenated with N 2 . Different 
concentrations of catalase or AuNCs-NH 2 were then added and the 
samples were transferred into a quartz ﬂ at cell (≈700 µL), and then 
sealed and placed in the EPR cavity. The ﬁ nal concentrations were 
0.1 × 10 −3 M CTPO and 0.1 × 10 −3 M H 2 O 2 in buffer solution (pH 4.8 
or 7.4). EPR spectra of samples in aqueous solutions were taken on 
a Bruker E580 spectrometer using 1 mW microwave power, 0.04 G 
ﬁ eld modulation, and 5 G scan range at ambient temperature. 
 In Vitro Peroxidase-Like Activity Assays : The peroxidase-like 
activity of the same concentration of AuNCs-NH 2 , AuNCs-OH, 
MAuNCs-N + R 3 and MAuNCs-OH (5 × 10 −3 M ) were evaluated at 
pH 4.8 and 7.4, with TMB, DAB, and ABTS 2− as peroxidase sub-
strates in the presence of 2 × 10 −3 M H 2 O 2 . Photographs were taken 
at 5 min and after 1 h. 
 Determination of • OH Scavenging Activity : The •OH scavenging 
activity of AuNCs-NH 2 was determined by the DCFH-DA and APF 
assays. The •OH was generated by a Fenton reaction and an X-ray 
system, respectively. Note that the reactivity of the DCFH solu-
tion results from the chemical hydrolysis of DCFH-DA at a basic 
pH to allow the use of DCFH-DA in cell-free assays. Various con-
centrations of AuNCs-NH 2 , 10 µL DCFH, and Fe 2 O 3 nanoparticles 
(5.0 mg mL −1 ) were added into 500 × 10 −6 M H 2 O 2 solution. The 
ﬂ uorescence of the DCF product was detected by a ﬂ uorescence 
spectrophotometer (Varian, Cary Eclipse) with excitation at 500 nm 
and monitored emission at 520 nm. The ﬂ uorescent probe of APF 
offers greater speciﬁ city and stability toward •OH than DCFH-DA 
due to auto-oxidation of DCFH-DA during light irradiation. There-
fore, the •OH scavenging activity of AuNCs-NH 2 aqueous solution 
upon X-ray irradiation was determined by APF. 
 Primary Mouse Neuron Cultures : Mice neurons were isolated 
and cultured from brain tissues of new born mice. Seven-week-old 
male ICR mice (25–30 g) were used for these studies. The mice 
used in this study were kept on a 12-h-light/12-h-dark cycle at 
24 ± 2 °C and at 50 ± 10% relative humidity; water and food were 
available ad libitum. The mice were purchased from BioLASCO 
(Taipei, Taiwan) and transported in a temporary vivarium (an inde-
pendently ventilated cage) to the Animal Molecular Imaging Core 
Facility at the National Health Research Institutes, where they were 
acclimated. On the day of the experiments, the mice were grouped 
into separate metabolism cages. Animals were handled in accord-
ance with standard animal husbandry practices and regulations; 
they were treated humanely and with regard to alleviation of suf-
fering throughout the study. The neurons were seeded at the density 
of 0.1 × 10 6 cells cm −2 in 6, 12, or 24-well plates (FNC Coating Mix 
AthenaES). After 2 weeks, neuronal cultures were assessed for purity 
as described previously. [ 50 ] The culture enriched with >90% neurons 
was utilized in neurotoxicity/neuroprotection experiments. All exper-
iments were performed on cell cultures grown for at least 2 weeks in 
vitro. During treatments, the medium was supplemented with serum-
free B-27 deprived of antioxidants (Gibco Life Technologies) added 
with stabilized H 2 O 2 in the presence or absence of AuNCs-NH 2 . 
 Primary Mouse Neuron Cultures and Treatments : To determine 
the uptake of AuNCs-NH 2 by primary mouse neurons, cells were 
treated with 100 µg mL −1 AuNCs-NH 2 for 24 h. The blue ﬂ uores-
cence of AuNCs-NH 2 within the cells was observed by a confocal 
ﬂ uorescence microscope (Olympus FV10i). 
 Determination of Intracellular ROS : The intracellular ROS was 
determined by the DCFH-DA assay. Neuron cells growing on a 
35-mm diameter µ-slide (ibidi) dish were incubated with serum-
free medium (neurobasal medium with B-27). Neuron cells were 
treated with 150 µg mL −1 AuNCs-NH 2 for 24 h and then washed 
with Hanks’ balanced salt solution (HBSS, Invitrogen) to remove 
the AuNCs-NH 2 extracellularly. Then cells were incubated with 
H 2 O 2 (150 × 10 −6 M ) for 1 h at 37 °C. Intracellular ROS were quan-
titatively determined by ﬂ ow cytometry. Cells were resuspended in 
HBSS containing 10 × 10 −6 M DCFH-DA. After incubation for 30 min 
at room temperature, cells were removed from buffer and returned 
to HBSS. Flow cytometric analysis was performed using a FACS-
Calibur (BD Biosciences) at an excitation wavelength of 488 nm. 
 Evaluation of Cell Viability and Toxicity of AuNCs-NH 2 : Cell via-
bility and toxicity of the AuNCs-NH 2 were determined by measuring 
mitochondrial activity using 3-[4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl]2,5-diphe-
nyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) assay. Microscopic images from at 
least ﬁ ve random ﬁ elds of multiple replicates of each experimental 
condition per experiment were taken with a confocal ﬂ uorescence 
microscope (Olympus FV10i).
