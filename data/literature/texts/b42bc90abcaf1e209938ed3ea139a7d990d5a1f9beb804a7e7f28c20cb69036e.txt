A B S T R A C T   
Injectable hydrogels are very advantageous in skin tissue-engineering, but generally suffer from several issues 
including infection, insufficient hair follicle regeneration, and scarring. In this paper, an injectable antibacterial 
hydrogel is designed for wound healing via integrating antibacterial Ag29 nanoclusters (NCs) and mangiferin 
(MF) molecules into the 3D network-structured chitosan (CH) hydrogel to eliminate bacterial infection and 
promote hair follicle/tissue regeneration. This design not only endows the hydrogel with excellent wide- 
spectrum antibacterial activities, superior biocompatibility, decent injectability, adequate swelling, and good 
degradability, but also favors the regeneration of capillary vessels/hair follicles/sweat glands for wound heal­
ings, which is the “holy grail” of skin tissue engineering. The antibacterial performance of the hydrogel is further 
improved through the controlled release of Ag species, the formation of a high local concentration of antibac­
terial Ag species on the hydrogel surface, as well as the capture of bacteria on the hydrogel surface via elec­
trostatic interaction. In vivo experiments reveal that the hydrogel could eliminate bacterial infections and 
prompt the regeneration of hair follicles/capillaries of wounds, which significantly promotes the skin flap wound 
healing. This study could shed light on the design of multi-functional Ag NCs-based hydrogel for applications of 
skin tissue engineering and wound treatments.   
1. Introduction 
Injectable hydrogels, as a class of highly hydrated polymer scaffolds, 
are most advantageous in skin tissue engineering because of their high 
water swell-ability, flexible texture, high oxygen permeability, benign 
biocompatibility and biodegradability [1–4]. For example, the integra™ 
injectable hydrogel has been developed to be the world’s leading tissue 
engineering product, which is effective in acute resurfacing of burns/ 
wounds with the function of promoting wound healings [5,6]. However, 
such commercial products are constrained by their major drawbacks 
including insufficient hair follicle recovery, scarring and infection. As 
known, while skin tissue-engineering technologies developed four de­
cades ago have offered many alternatives to traditional skin grafting, 
none of them can regenerate skin appendages (e.g. hair follicles and 
sweat glands). Therefore, reliable recovery of such skin appendages 
including hair follicle, capillary vessels, and sweat glands remains the 
“holy grail” of skin tissue engineering and skin wound regeneration 
[7,8]. In addition, wound infections have been a major threat to human 
health because they can prolong wound healing, and lead to severe 
problems such as failed surgery syndrome and even mortality [2,9]. 
Currently, while antibiotic-loaded hydrogels are widely applied to treat 
wound infections, the intrinsic limitations of antibiotics including 
narrow-spectrum bactericidal efficacy and drug resistance to bacteria 
have dragged their antibacterial applications into a vicious circle 
[10,11]. Moreover, other side effects of antibiotics such as high cyto­
toxicity, short-acting bactericidal activity, and slow wound healing, also 
raise further concerns for their applications [12,13]. Therefore, devel­
oping an injectable hydrogel with functions of wide-spectrum yet long- 
acting bactericidal efficacy and promoting hair follicle/tissue regener­
ation to address such conventional drawbacks is urgently demanded for 
skin tissue engineering and wound healings.

hydrogels in fighting wound infections for skin tissue engineering. 
However, similar to conventional antibiotics, Ag NC-based antibacterial 
agents could be quickly consumed by bacteria via membrane adhesion 
and endocytosis, which made long-acting antibacterial efficacy impos­
sible. In parallel, Ag NCs need to be further improved toward 
outstanding biocompatibility. We hypothesize that if we can engineer 
the surface chemistry of the Ag NCs to overcome the above-mentioned 
problems of Ag NCs, and then embed them as well as other functional 
molecules into hydrogel [33–35], we may develop an excellent inject­
able hydrogel, achieving desired performances in wound healing by 
eliminating bacterial infection and promoting hair follicle/tissue 
regeneration. 
In terms of the surface engineering of Ag NCs, it is documented that 
some antioxidant molecules (e.g., glutathione and cysteine) could pro­
mote the proliferation of mammalian cells [36,37]. Such molecules can 
be used to modify the surface of Ag NCs [38], which could not only make 
the antibacterial activity of NC’s Ag cores unaffected, but also render Ag 
NCs active in promoting wound healing via improving cell/tissue 
regeneration [39,40]. To further improve the performance of the 
injectable hydrogel in skin tissue engineering and wound healings, 
previous studies inspired us to incorporate other functional molecules 
such as mangiferin (MF) into the hydrogel. It is known that MF as a 
natural glucosyl xanthone commonly found in mango and papaya, has 
multi-functions such as anti-oxidation, promoting angiogenesis and 
inhibiting cell apoptosis [1,41]. Therefore, we rationalize that 
combining cytoprotective MF with antibacterial Ag NCs into the 
hydrogel design could further improve the skin flap survival rate under 
hypoxic conditions, which may favor the regeneration of hair follicles, 
resulting in the fast wound healing of skin flap by effectively reducing 
cell apoptosis at the wound and promoting the generation of blood 
vessels for nutrient transmission. 
Besides, the intrinsic 3D-network structure of hydrogels may allow 
the controlled release of Ag NCs for longer antibacterial efficacy 
[10,42]. More importantly, the surface of hydrogels may be engineered 
to carry certain positive charges, which could enrich both the released 
negatively charged antibacterial Ag species for a high local concentra­
tion and negatively charged bacteria on the gel surface, enabling bac­
terial killing with a higher efficiency [43]. Of particular note, several 
recently developed hydrogel systems using thiol-containing poly­
ethylene glycol (SH-PEG) with silver nitrate (AgNO3) or gelatin meth­
acrylate as scaffold materials have gained excellent performance in 
promoting wound healings due to their injectable, self-healing, anti­
bacterial, angiogenic, and some other intriguing properties [44,45], 
which markedly contributed to the development of multi-functional 
hydrogels for wound healing applications. However, either the neutral 
SH-PEG or negatively charged gelatin methacrylate molecules may not 
be used for the construction of positively charged hydrogel in this study. 
By contrast, chitosan (CH) not only has some similarities with extra­
cellular matrix in composition and structure, benign biocompatibility, 
good antibacterial and hemostatic properties, but also carries certain 
positive charges (from NH2 groups), which enables it to be an ideal 
functional material for the hydrogel design in this study. In addition, 
additional attributes of hydrogels such as the injectability and cell 
adhesion/proliferation can be achieved by using polyethylene glycol 
(PEG) molecules as the main backbone material of hydrogels due to their 
attractive functions: (1) PEG’s hydroxyl groups (–OH) could form 
hydrogen bonds with the –OH groups of the CH molecules, so as to 
maintain the stretching state of the CH chain segments and keep a 
certain fluidity of hydrogels; (2) the interpenetrating network formed by 
PEG and CH can improve the mechanical strength of hydrogels and 
support the adhesion and proliferation of cells [46]; (3) PEG as a sta­
bilizer could be beneficial to the stability of Ag NCs and other functional 
components. 
Herein we report our design on an injectable antibacterial Ag NCs- 
based hydrogel for wound healing via eliminating bacterial infection 
and promoting hair follicle/tissue regeneration. The key of this design is 
to embed antibacterial Ag NCs (passivated with anti-oxidant molecules) 
and MF molecules (encapsulated in liposome (Lip) micelles, MF@Lip for 
short) into the 3D network-structured CH hydrogel (named as 
AgNCs@CH-MF, see Scheme 1 for the synthetic illustration). The 
AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel exhibits synergistic and multiple effects on 
wound healing: (1) Ag NCs protected by antioxidant dihydrolipoic acid 
(DHLA) molecules have intrinsic wide-spectrum antibacterial activities 
with cyto-protective properties; (2) MF molecules are favorable for the 
regeneration of capillary vessels/hair follicles/tissues in the wound, 
thereby promoting wound healings. More importantly, the delicate 
design on the controlled release of Ag species, the formation of a high 
local concentration of antibacterial Ag species on the hydrogel surface, 
as well as the capture of bacteria on the hydrogel surface via electro­
static interaction may greatly improve the low-dosage yet long-acting 
bactericidal performance. As expected, the Ag NCs-based hydrogel 
shows excellent bactericidal activities against both gram-negative and 
gram-positive bacteria with a bactericidal rate of 99.999%, and displays 
negligible cytotoxicity to mammalian cells with a cell viability of 94.3 ±
2.5% (here mouse fibroblasts L929 cells are used as cell model). Results 
of in vivo experiments reveal that the hydrogel could efficiently elimi­
nate wound infection and prevent large-area tissue necrosis in the skin 
wound section. Moreover, the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel promotes the 
cell proliferation of L929 cells with up to 2.8 folds within 48 h and 
prompts the regeneration of capillary vessels/hair follicles in the skin 
flap sections. All of such features of the hydrogel favor the rapid healing 
of skin flap wounds. This study could shed light on the design of multi- 
functional antibacterial Ag NCs-based hydrogel for applications of skin 
tissue engineering and wound treatments. 
2. Results and discussion 
To achieve both high antibacterial activity and low cytotoxicity, we 
delicately selected DHLA molecules as surface ligand for the synthesis of 
Ag NCs because DHLA as a natural coenzyme in human body with the 
function of anti-oxidation and immunity enhancement is widely used for 
medicine and healthcare [47]. The DHLA-protected Ag NCs synthesized 
via a simple “one-pot” method were characterized in size and optical 
properties. As shown in Fig. 1a, transmission electron microscope (TEM) 
image shows that all the Ag particles are below 2 nm in core size, which 
proves the successful synthesis of ultrasmall Ag NCs. UV–visible 
(UV–vis) absorption spectroscopy was applied to examine this reddish 
brown Ag NC solution (inset of Fig. 1b), and three distinct peaks at 329 
nm, 427 nm and 497 nm are observed in the spectrum (Fig. 1b). Such 
optical absorption peaks of Ag NCs match nicely with the characteristic 
absorptions of thiolated Ag29 NCs [48–50], which suggests the forma­
tion of Ag29 NCs. In order to acquire size information at atomic preci­
sion, we further employed electrospray ionization mass spectrometry 
(ESI-MS) to characterize the Ag NCs (Fig. 1c, and Figure S1), and 
confirmed that Ag29 NCs protected by 11 or 12 DHLA ligands are 
dominant species in the sample solution. 
After synthesizing DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs, both the Ag29 NCs and 
liposome-encapsulated MF molecules (denoted as MF@Lip hereafter) 
were mixed with hydrophilic CH and PEG molecules to fabricate 
AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel in the presence of glutaraldehyde (GTA) as 
crosslinking agent (see Scheme 1). Of note, the purpose of using Lip 
micelles is to encapsulate hydrophobic MF molecules, which made the 
incorporation of hydrophobic MF with hydrophilic hydrogel possible 
[51]. The UV–vis absorption spectrum of the MF@Lip shows the typical 
absorption feature of MF, i.e., four dominant peaks at 240 nm, 257 nm, 
367 nm, and 317 nm (Figure S2), which is probably unable to support

To better investigate the effect of introducing Ag29 NCs and MF@Lip 
on the physiochemical properties of the hydrogel, we comprehensively 
analyzed the AgNCs@CH-MF with blank CH hydrogel (without incor­
porating Ag NCs and MF@Lip) and AgNCs@CH (without incorporating 
MF@Lip) as reference. Firstly, as shown in Fig. 2c, comparing to the 
blank CH hydrogel and AgNCs@CH, the as-fabricated AgNCs@CH-MF 
displays two distinctive peaks at 367 nm and 427 nm in its UV–vis ab­
sorption spectrum, which correspond to the typical absorption features 
of MF (Figure S2) and Ag29 NCs (Fig. 1b), respectively, suggesting the 
successful embedment of both in the hydrogel. In addition, all these 
three hydrogels exhibit a porous structure with 3D networks. As shown 
in Fig. 2d-2f, while the blank CH hydrogel is highly porous with large yet 
irregular pores, the porous structures of both AgNCs@CH and 
AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogels are relatively loose with smaller pores. This 
result indicates that both hydrophilic Ag NCs and MF@Lip would affect 
the backbones of the hydrogel, and thus influence the pore structures. 
Elemental mapping results based on energy disperse spectroscopy (EDS) 
could well reveal the influence of Ag NCs and MF@Lip on the backbones 
of the hydrogel. As shown in Fig. 2g, both elements Ag, S from Ag NCs 
and P element from Lip micelles could be uniformly detected in the 
backbones (mainly containing C, O and N elements) of the hydrogel 
instead of the large pores, which suggests the well embedment of Ag NCs 
and MF@Lip in the hydrogel backbones. This is further supported by the 
EDS elemental mapping results of the AgNCs@CH hydrogel 
(Figure S9a), where similar landscape of Ag element could be observed. 
The quantitative information of the EDS analysis (Figure S10) reveals 
that the content of Ag and S in the AgNCs@CH hydrogel is 0.112% and 
0.225%, respectively (Figure S10b), while the content of Ag, S and P in 
the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel is estimated to be 0.14%, 0.23% and 1.5%, 
respectively (Figure S10c). These results are consistent with the feeding 
amount of Ag NCs (0.17 mM) and MF (0.33 mg/mL) during the hydrogel 
fabrication. Of note, the results of AgNCs@CH-MF and AgNCs@CH are 
distinctive from that of the blank CH hydrogel (Figure S9b and S10a) in 
this aspect. 
The porosities of the AgNCs@CH-MF and reference hydrogels were 
further evaluated. As seen in Fig. 2h, while the AgNCs@CH has very 
close porosity (82.8 ± 3.8%) to the blank CH hydrogel (82.9 ± 4.3%), 
the porosity of AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel markedly decreases to 74.2 ±
5.0%. These results suggest that Ag29 NCs have negligible influence on 
the porosity of the hydrogel, which is in stark contrast to the MF@Lip 
that makes the hydrogel’s network structure more compact and thus 
reduces the porosity. In addition, the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel exhibits 
adequate swelling rate of water (977 ± 34%, Fig. 2i), which is lower 
than that of the blank CH hydrogel (1085 ± 54%) and the AgNCs@CH 
(1171 ± 51%). Such a low swelling rate of the AgNCs@CH-MF is in 
accordance with its shrunken porosity. More importantly, the as- 
fabricated hydrogel exhibits degradable ability. As shown in Fig. 2j, in 
the presence of lysozyme (1 mg/mL) for simulating the in vivo micro­
environment, the hydrogel degradation rate is fast at the beginning 
because the absorbed lysozyme in hydrogel pores could rapidly degrade 
the hydrogel via enzymolysis. However, with increasing the degradation 
time, the degradation rate gradually slows down due to the difficult 
degradation of the gel framework composing of large molecules. After 
15 days of enzymolysis, the reference CH and AgNCs@CH hydrogels 
have remaining mass of 20.9 ± 2.8% and 16.5 ± 2.0%, respectively, 
which are lower than that of the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel (27.6 ± 3.4%) 
probably because its relatively dense networks are more resistant to 
dissociation. Moreover, owing to the possibly varied pH microenviron­
ments of wounds, enzyme-catalyzed degradation of the AgNCs@CH-MF 
hydrogel was also examined at pH = 4, 7, and 10 (Figure S11), and the 
results suggest that the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel has relatively poor 
stability at lower pH, which is probably because that the uncross-linked 
CH (>50%) could be dissociated from the hydrogel under acidic con­
ditions, thereby making the hydrogel’s structure loose and thus facili­
tating the enzymatic hydrolysis. 
The as-fabricated AgNCs@CH-MF exhibits excellent antibacterial 
activity against both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria. Prior to 
testing its antibacterial performance, the content of Ag NCs inside the 
AgNCs@CH hydrogel was determined to be the optimal value (~0.17 
mM) by a flask-shaking method with Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) as 
bacterium model (Fig. 3a and Figure S12). In addition, as shown in 
Fig. 3a, both the Ag NCs and blank CH display obvious antibacterial 
activity (70.339 ± 9.482% for Ag NCs, and 75.708 ± 4.531% for blank 
CH) against S. aureus [56,57]. By contrast, the Ag NCs@CH composing 
of the same amount of Ag NCs and CH exhibits excellent antibacterial 
activity (99.999 ± 0.289%) against the same bacterium, suggesting the 
synergistic effect of Ag NCs with CH hydrogel on improving the anti­
bacterial efficacy. In comparison, the CH-MF hydrogel has an antibac­
terial activity of 83.508 ± 4.965% against S. aureus. This result implies 
that the MF has a certain antibacterial enhancement comparing to the 
blank CH hydrogel (75.708 ± 4.531%). However, the antibacterial ac­
tivity of the CH-MF hydrogel (83.508 ± 4.965%) is lower than that of the 
Ag NCs-loaded CH hydrogels (>85.612 ± 6.053% for AgNCs@CH with a 
loading of 0.01 mM Ag NCs, see Figure S12), which further testified that 
the antibacterial activity of the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel is originated 
from the synergistic effect of the Ag NCs with CH and MF molecules. 
Antibacterial experiments of the AgNCs@CH-MF (with the same Ag 
content as the AgNCs@CH) disclose that its bactericidal activities 
against both gram-positive bacteria including S. aureus and Bacillus 
subtilis (B. subtilis), and gram-negative bacteria such as Escherichia coli 
(E. coli) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (P. aeruginosa) reach to >99.999 ±
0.289% (see Fig. 3b-c, Figure S13, and Table S1), which implies the 
excellent performance of the AgNCs@CH-MF in killing broad-spectrum 
bacteria. 
While the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel shows excellent antibacterial 
activity, the equally important issue is about its cytotoxicity, which is a 
prerequisite to the biomedical applications of the hydrogel. By using 
fibroblasts (L929 cells) of mouse as cell model, the cytotoxicity experi­
ments disclose that the AgNCs@CH-MF has no obvious cytotoxicity with 
a L929 cell viability of 94.3 ± 2.5%, which is comparable to that treated 
with pristine Ag29 NCs (99.3 ± 0.5%) and MF@Lip (99.4 ± 0.5%), but 
higher than that with either blank CH hydrogel (78.4 ± 3.0%) or 
AgNCs@CH hydrogel (82.4 ± 3.2%; see Fig. 4a, 4c, and Figure S14). 
Interestingly, it is found that the viabilities of the L929 cells with pristine 
Ag29 NCs (99.3 ± 0.5%) and with MF@Lip (99.4 ± 0.5%) are even 
higher than that with PBS control (98.0 ± 0.6%), and the addition of 
both into the hydrogel improved its cell viability from 78.4 ± 3.0% to 
94.3 ± 2.5%, which suggests that both Ag29 NCs and MF@Lip have 
certain improvement in anti-apoptosis because of the anti-oxidative 
DHLA and MF molecules with certain cytoprotective function. Of note, 
the functions of the DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs and MF molecules such as 
anti-apoptosis and promoting blood vessel regeneration were also 
employed to further improve the biocompatibility of the hydrogel. For 
example, by integrating the Ag29 NCs and MF molecules into the 
hydrogel, the AgNCs@CH-MF owns an additional function of promoting 
cell proliferation, which is reflected by the fact that the proliferation rate 
of L929 cells treated with the AgNCs@CH-MF increased up to ~282% 
within 48 h (Fig. 4b, and 4d). Taken together, the AgNCs@CH-MF 
hydrogel not only has good biocompatibility, but also has a positive 
effect on cell proliferation, which may facilitate the wound healing.

promoting skin wound healings through eliminating bacterial infection 
and prompting hair follicles/vessels regeneration. As shown in Fig. 5, we

The interfacial engineering is another merit of the AgNCs@CH-MF 
hydrogel featuring a positively charged surface (note: CH has posi­
tively charged NH3
+), which created a high local concentration of anti­
bacterial Ag species on the hydrogel surface though absorbing the 
negatively charged DHLA-protected Ag species that released from the 
bulk phase of the hydrogel (DHLA has a –COO– group), and concurrently 
captured the negatively charged bacteria via electrostatic interaction. 
This naturally improved the antibacterial efficiency of the designed 
hydrogel. A rising concern is that the potential repelling interaction 
between the negatively charged DHLA-protected Ag29 NCs and the 
negatively charged bacteria may be detrimental to the bacterial killing 
efficacy of the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel in this study. However, the 
interfacial design of the positively charged CH hydrogel with the Ag NCs 
may counteract such possible repelling interaction, which may favor the 
intimate interactions of the hydrogel with the negatively charged bac­
terial cell wall, leading to outstanding overall antimicrobial efficacy. As 
seen in Fig. 7, we investigated the interface of the hydrogel-bacteria by 
SEM. Based on SEM images (Fig. 7c-6e), it is found that a large number 
of bacteria were adsorbed on the hydrogel surface. Particularly, the 
S. aureus cells on the surface of blank CH hydrogel show intact 
morphology without membrane damage, while those on the surface of 
AgNCs@CH and AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogels display severely deformed 
morphology with broken membranes. The bacterial loads of hydrogels 
were quantitatively analyzed (Fig. 7f). As shown, the quantity of the 
adsorbed alive bacteria on the hydrogel surface follows the trend: blank 
CH > AgNCs@CH > AgNCs@CH-MF, which is completely opposite to 
the trend of the bactericidal efficacy of such hydrogels, further sug­
gesting the highest antibacterial activity of the AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel 
given that the adsorption abilities of such three hydrogels to bacteria are 
comparable. These results not only confirm the adhesion of negatively 
charged bacteria on positively charged CH hydrogel, but also demon­
strate the high efficiency in bacterial killing of the Ag NCs-based 
hydrogel. It should be emphasized that the high-efficiency antibacte­
rial activity of the hydrogel also minimizes the high-dosage induced side 
effect in wound treatment [60].

AgNCs@CH-MF (Figure S17b-c), although the former hydrogel carries 
more antibacterial CH species. This result is expected because the 
AgNCs@2CH-MF with a denser network structure could partially pre­
vent the release of antibacterial Ag species and MF molecules 
(Figure S17d), which inevitably constrains or even prohibits the anti­
bacterial activity of the hydrogel. 
3. Conclusion 
In summary, we have developed an injectable, high-efficiency anti­
bacterial and biocompatible AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel for promoting 
skin wound healings, which is based on the special design that using CH- 
functionalized hydrogel to embed antibacterial Ag29 NCs and MF@Lip. 
The as-fabricated AgNCs@CH-MF hydrogel has many advantages such 
as injectability, adequate swelling, good degradability, and good 
controlled release property of functional molecules. More importantly, 
by integrating the distinctive functions of the Ag NCs, CH and MF into 
one, the AgNCs@CH-MF exhibited superior antibacterial activity against 
both gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria by killing the adhered 
bacteria on the hydrogel surface with a high local concentration of Ag 
species, and possessed excellent biocompatibility by enhancing the cell 
proliferation. These functionalities of the hydrogel enabled it effective in 
eliminating bacterial infections and prompting the regeneration of hair 
follicles/capillaries of wounds, which significantly promoted the skin 
flap wound healing. This study is interesting because it not only pro­
vided a paradigm in the design of injectable multifunctional Ag NCs- 
based hydrogel for wound healing, but also demonstrated the great 
application potential of ultra-tiny Ag NCs in high-efficiency bacterial 
killing, eliminating infection, and accelerating wound healings.
