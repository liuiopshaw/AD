Ameliorative effects of probiotics in ­AlCl3‑induced mouse model 
of Alzheimer’s disease
Maryam Hamid1 · Saadia Zahid1 
Received: 13 April 2023 / Revised: 27 June 2023 / Accepted: 6 July 2023

Abstract 
In recent years, gut microbiome alterations have been linked with complex underlying mechanisms of neurodegenerative 
disorders including Alzheimer’s disease (AD). The gut microbiota modulates gut brain axis by facilitating development 
of hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis and synthesis of neuromodulators. The study was designed to unravel the effect 
of combined consumption of probiotics; Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG®) and Bifidobacterium BB-12 (BB-12®) 
(1 × ­109 CFU) on ­AlCl3-induced AD mouse model in comparison with potent acetylcholine esterase inhibitor drug for AD, 
donepezil. Mice were randomly allocated to six different study groups (n = 8). Behavioral tests were conducted to assess 
effect of ­AlCl3 (300 mg/kg) and probiotics treatment on cognition and anxiety through Morris Water Maze (MWM), Novel 
Object Recognition (NOR), Elevated Plus Maze (EPM), and Y-maze. The results indicated that the combined probiotic 
treatment significantly (p < 0.0001) reduced anxiety-like behavior post ­AlCl3 exposure. The ­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12®-
treated group showed significantly improved spatial (p < 0.0001) and recognition memory (p < 0.0001) in comparison to 
­AlCl3-treated group. The expression status of inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α and IL-1β) was also normalized upon treat-
ment with LGG® and BB-12® post ­AlCl3 exposure. Our findings indicated that the probiotics LGG® and BB-12® have 
strong potential to overcome neuroinflammatory imbalance, cognitive deficits and anxiety-like behavior, therefore can be 
considered as a combination therapy for AD through modulation of gut brain axis.
Key points
• Bifidobacterium BB-12 and Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG were fed to AlCl3-induced Alzheimer’s disease mice.
• This combination of probiotics had remarkable ameliorating effects on anxiety, neuroinflammation and cognitive deficits.
• These effects may suggest that combined consumption of these probiotics instigate potential mitigation of AD associated 
consequences through gut brain axis modulation.

Introduction
Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a senescence-related neurode-
generative disorder evident by memory loss along with a 
plethora of different cognitive challenges such as impair-
ment in decision-making and judgment, language difficulties 
and inability to recognize common objects (Breijyeh and 
Karaman 2020). In conjunction with these symptoms, AD 
patients also exhibit neuropsychiatric symptoms like psy-
chosis, aggression, anxiety, and apathy (Li et al. 2014). The 
disease is marked by wide spread neuronal loss and tissue 
atrophy resulting in brain volume loss (Marino et al. 2019). 
The brain regions most affected by cerebral atrophy include 
the hippocampus, cingulate gyrus, prefrontal cortex, parietal 
and temporal lobes (Traini et al. 2020).
The gut brain axis links the central nervous system’s (CNS) 
emotional and cognitive functions to the enteric nervous 
systems peripheral functions such as secretion of digestive 
enzymes and mucous, absorption of water, electrolytes and 
nutrients (Arneth 2018). The gut microbiome is a functional

There are an estimated 1 × ­1013 to 1 × ­1014 microbes in the 
gastrointestinal tract (GIT) (Sender et al. 2016). The mature 
microbiota of an adult comprises of > 1000 bacterial species 
and 7000 different strains (Cryan and Dinan 2012) while the

thought to be determined by genetics of host (Grenham et al. 
2011). Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG®), a rod-shaped 
gram-positive facultative anaerobe found ubiquitously in geni-
tourinary tract of females and GIT (Albarillo et al. 2020). It is 
suggested that LGG® modulates emotional behavior by regu-
lating GABA receptor expression (Bravo et al. 2011) and sup-
press several pro inflammatory cytokines (Tong et al. 2021). 
Likewise, Bifidobacterium animalis subspecies lactis (BB-
12®), a non-motile and non-spore anaerobic forming microbe 
(Reuter 2001), is a gram-positive, rod-shaped bacteria that 
produce lactic acid and commonly inhabit the GIT. BB-12® 
inhibits pathogen-mediated colonization of gut through vari-
ous mechanisms such as activation of host immune system, 
competition for adherence sites on intestinal epithelial cells, 
toxin removal and production of inhibitory substances like 
bacteriocins, ­H2O2 and organic acids (Jungersen et al. 2014).
In recent years, gut microbiome alterations and dysbiosis 
are linked with neurodegeneration and AD (Zhuang et al. 
2018). Alterations in microbial composition of gut microbi-
ome leads to dysregulation of several metabolic and immune 
pathways such as dysregulation of anti-inflammatory P-gly-
coprotein pathway (Tiwari et al. 2023; Haran et al. 2019). 
As the gut associated inflammatory responses along with 
aging and poor diet contributes to AD, enhancement of gut 
microbiome and enrichment of probiotics may instigate a 
compensatory mechanism to overcome the damage caused 
by the altered gut microbial composition. Thus, consider-
ing the emerging association of gut microbiome with AD 
pathology, the present study investigates the combinatorial 
potential of probiotics LGG® and BB-12® in attenuating 
the cognitive deficits, anxiety and neuroinflammation in 
aluminum chloride ­(AlCl3)–induced mouse model of AD. 
The present combined treatment strategy using LGG® and 
BB-12® will provide a novel therapeutic option for AD 
without any adverse effects.
Materials and methods
Experimental animals and treatment
A total of 48 Balb/c mice (male, age: 8 weeks) were obtained 
from an institutional laboratory animal facility. Mice were 
housed in standard cages at 25 ± 2 °C and maintained in a 
standard 12-h light/dark series with free access to animal 
diet and water throughout the study.
The mice were randomly segregated into 6 different 
groups of 8 animals each. Groups 1, 2 and 3 were given 
double distilled water ­(ddH2O) while groups 4, 5, and 6 were 
treated with Aluminium Chloride ­(AlCl3) (300 mg/kg) in 
­ddH2O for 15 days. Group 4 was switched to water only, 
after 15 days till the end of the treatment period. Group 
1 was continued with ­ddH2O while Group 2 and 5 were 
administered donepezil (15 mg/kg) orally and groups 3 and 
6 were administered a commercially available probiotic 
formulation Imutec™, packaged and marketed by Searle 
Pakistan Pvt Limited, Karachi, Pakistan, containing LGG® 
and BB-12® (1 × ­109 CFU/day) (strain collection number 
DSM33156 and DSM15954 respectively, manufactured by 
Chr. Hansen, Hoersholm, Denmark), post aluminum expo-
sure. The donepezil and probiotic treatment were carried out 
for 28 days (Fig. 1).
Behavior studies
Elevated plus maze (EPM)
EPM was performed in accordance with pre-established pro-
tocol by Walf and Frye (2007). The apparatus comprised 
of 4 arms of which 2 were enclosed alleys and 2 were open 
alleys. The apparatus was made up of opaque iron alloy, was 
elevated 75.5 cm from the ground and had 30 × 5 cm arm 
each. Each mouse underwent a single 5-min trial by being 
positioned at the intersection of the maze facing away from 
experimenter and towards any one of the two enclosed arms. 
A video was recorded for behavior analysis focusing on the 
overall number of entrances into the open arms and cumula-
tive time spent in both open arms.
Y‑maze
The protocol was adapted from Conrad et al. (1996). The 
Y-maze is composed of three arms each at a 120° angle 
from the adjacent one. The arms were categorized as start 
arm, other arm and novel arm. The start arm served as the 
location where a mouse was initially placed and allowed 
to explore along with the other arm during the habituation 
phase. The novel arm was kept blocked using a thick wooden 
plank during the first trial and unblocked during the second 
one. The maze was held in an undisturbed quite room at 
25 ± 2 °C. Different visual cues were hung in all three arms 
such that they were in the line of sight of the test subject. 
The first trial lasted 10 min while the test subject explored 
the Y-maze. After the 10-min inter-trial interval, the sub-
ject was placed back into the maze to explore for another 
5 min with novel arm unblocked. The data was analyzed as

Novel object recognition (NOR) task
NOR task was performed as described by Mirza et al. (2021). 
It consists of two test trials of 10 min each with 20 min of 
inter-trial interval. The subjects were allowed to acclima-
tize to a square wooden box of (42 cm × 42 cm × 42 cm) for 
5 min 24 h prior to familiarization and test session.
The first session (familiarization) was performed by plac-
ing two alike objects into the box being used to carry out 
NOR test and the mouse was allowed to interact freely with 
the objects. After termination of this session, one of the two 
alike objects was replaced with a new object, termed as the 
novel object. After completion of inter-trial interval, a mouse 
was permitted to explore and interact with both objects. 
Sniffing or physically touching the object was regarded as 
exploration. Discrimination index (DI) was measured as the 
change between the time spent interacting with novel object 
and the time spent with familiar and novel object.
Morris water maze (MWM) test
MWM test was performed with minor modifications to 
procedure (Bromley-Brits et al. 2011). MWM consists of 
a 5-day acquisition phase and then a single day probe trial. 
The MWM consists of a circular metal pool having four 
starting positions or quadrants (east, west, north, south) 
with visual cues placed in each quadrant. The pool is filled 
with water (22 °C ± 2 °C) containing a platform, placed in 
the northwest quadrant. The acquisition phase consisted 
of 5 days. On each day, five trials were conducted with 
inter-trial interval of 10 min per mouse. The mice were 
introduced into the pool from different directions. The cut-
off time was 90 s. If the mouse was unsuccessful to reach 
the platform within the cutoff, it was guided and held on 
platform for 20 s. While if the mouse found the platform 
before cutoff time; it was allowed to sit there for an addi-
tional 5 s before being removed. On day 6, the platform 
was detached and the mice were allowed to explore the 
maze within the cutoff time. A video was recorded and 
escape latency over 5 days during acquisition phase, num-
ber of crossings over platform during probe trial, number 
of entries into target quadrant during probe trial and time 
spent in target quadrant during probe trial were carefully 
measured.
Gene expression analysis
The expression alterations in the mRNA transcripts of 
IL-1β and TNF-α were evaluated via Quantitative Real-
Time PCR (ABI Prism 7300 Sequence Detection Sys-
tem, Applied Biosystem, 7300) as described by Khalid 
et al. (2020). Concisely, the extracted RNA was reverse 
transcribed into cDNA and later amplified through gene-
specific primers and qPCR master mix using an iCycler 
(Bio-Rad, USA). The thermocycling conditions were set 
at 50 °C (2 min), 95 °C (10 min), 40 cycles (95 °C for 
30 s), 60 °C (1 min), 72 °C (1 min), followed by a final 
dissociation step. Agarose gel electrophoresis and disso-
ciation curves were used to measure the quality of the 
PCR products. Expression values were normalized with 
β-actin and levels were assessed by ­2−ΔΔCT method (Livak 
and Schmittgen 2001). The samples were run in tripli-
cates. The primer sequence for IL-1β, TNF-α, and β-actin 
is mentioned in Supplementary Table 1.

Statistical variations between the groups were assessed using

Results
Behavioral analysis
LGG® and BB‑12® treatment displayed strong anxiolytic 
effect
Performance on EPM is a measure of anxiety to assess 
anxiolytic effects of LGG® and BB-12® in comparison 
to donepezil. Animals experiencing severe anxiety have a 
tendency to remain in closed arms, whereas animals expe-
riencing lower levels of anxiety tend to explore the open 
arm more. Entries in open arm in ­AlCl3-treated group 
(4.12 ± 0.63) were lower (p < 0.01) when compared to the 
number of entries made by control group (8.37 ± 0.49). 
­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12®-treated group (7.62 ± 0.62) 
displayed significantly increased (p < 0.001) entries into 
open arms in comparison to ­AlCl3 + donepezil-treated group 
(1.87 ± 0.61) (Fig. 2a).
Donepezil-treated group (7.62 ± 0.99) spent significantly 
less (p < 0.05) amount of time in open arms in comparison 
to control group (16.75 ± 1.70). LGG® and BB-12®-treated 
group (20.38 ± 1.61) spent significantly more (p < 0.01) 
time than donepezil-treated group (7.62 ± 0.99). The 
­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12®-treated group (27.75 ± 4.15) 
has significantly improved (p < 0.0001) performance in 
comparison to ­AlCl3-treated group. The ­AlCl3 + LGG® and 
BB-12®-treated group (27.75 ± 4.15) also had significantly 
higher performance (p < 0.0001) in contrast to ­AlCl3 + done-
pezil-treated group (3.37 ± 0.70). In conclusion, the probi-
otic LGG® and BB-12® treatment displayed strong anxio-
lytic effect in both LGG® and BB-12® and ­AlCl3 + LGG® 
and BB-12®-treated group. This indicates that this combina-
tion of probiotics has strong anxiolytic properties (Fig. 2b).

Discussion
The present study establishes an empirical basis for devel-
oping combined oral administration of LGG® and BB-12® 
probiotics for suppression of AD-associated pathological 
consequences in ­AlCl3-treated mouse model of AD. It is 
notable that approximately 40% of the patients of AD suffer 
from anxiety, a common neuropsychiatric symptom in AD 
(Mendez 2021).
We demonstrate that the ­AlCl3-treated group exhibited 
anxiety-like effects as evident through EPM test. We found 
that the oral consumption of probiotics improves anxiety-like 
behavior. Various studies also revealed interesting effects of 
probiotics. For instance, LGG® administration in C57BL/6 
pregnant female from the ­18th day of gestation until birth 
and offspring for 5 days, showed increased expression of 
GABA receptors, BDNF and serotonin transporter due to 
LGG® colonization leading to anti-anxiety like behavior 
(Zhou et al. 2022). Correspondingly, LGG® and prebiotic 
combination showed improvement in anxiety-like behavior 
with a decrease in mRNA expression of GABA A2 in com-
parison to untreated Sprague–Dawley rats (Neufeld et al. 
2019). Also, mice administered with Lactobacillus rham-
nosus (JB-1) for a period of 28 days followed by chronic 
social defeat exhibited decreased anxiety to social stress con-
trary to placebo group (Bharwani et al. 2017). Moreover, 
the probiotic regimen may be a potential therapeutic route 
to attenuate anxiety and depression in mothers postpartum 
as concluded through a clinical trial on 423 women partici-
pants administered with probiotic Lactobacillus rhamnosus 
(HN001) from time of recruitment till 6 months post-partum 
and had shown lower anxiety scores in contrast to placebo 
group (Slykerman et al. 2017). A single arm study, also elu-
cidated the effect of BB-12® on state anxiety in athletes 
where the intervention resulted in significant improvement 
in somatic anxiety, emotion anxiety and cognitive anxiety 
(Dong et al. 2021).
It is interesting that Donepezil, an acetylcholinesterase 
(AChE) inhibitor produces promnesic effects in humans 
and rodents but evokes an unclear response when study-
ing its effects on anxiety phenotype (Ben-Azu et al. 2019). 
AChE levels are positively correlated to decrease in anxiety 
as observed in transgenic AD mice (Ye et al. 2015). Simi-
larly, administration of physostigmine into hippocampus of 
C57BL/6 J male mice results in blockade of AChE activ-
ity and an increase in hippocampal acetylcholine that ulti-
mately led to exhibition of anxiety-like behavior (Mineur 
et al. 2013). It is also documented that donepezil does not

Our study corroborated the existing evidence that probiot-
ics attenuate anxiety-like behavior. Combination therapy of 
LGG® and BB-12® caused significant decrease in anxiety-
like behavior in both control + LGG® and BB-12®-treated 
group and ­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12®group suggesting 
that LGG® and BB-12® treatment is effective in decreas-
ing anxiety-like behavior associated with AD.
The study further assessed spatial, reference and long-
term spatial memory to elucidate the effect of probiotic 
treatment on deficits of memory and learning in AD. A 
significant decrease was observed in both spatial and refer-
ence memory parameters while long-term spatial memory 
and learning was also poor as observed in escape latency. 
A study showed that the combined treatment of Lactobacil-
lus rhamnosus, Bifidobacterium infantis and Lactobacillus 
reuteri improved spatial memory which was rendered by 
a decrease in oxidative stress and inflammation in probi-
otics-treated group post administration of Aβ1-40 intra-hip-
pocampal injection (Mehrabadi and Sadr 2020). Moreover, 
combination of B. bifidum and L. plantarum also attenuates 
cognitive decline particularly in spatial learning and mem-
ory (Shamsipour et al. 2021). Our findings demonstrate that 
during the MWM, ­AlCl3-treated group showed an atypical 
behavior, i.e., the time spent in target quadrant was not less 
related to other groups although clear memory impairment 
was observed. This may be due to the presence of “floaters”, 
i.e., the mice that are anxious and displayed hypolocomo-
tion. Therefore, MWM results must be elaborated carefully 
with all four parameters within MWM being considered con-
comitantly. ­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12® displayed a signifi-
cant improvement in all parameters of memory tests. The 
improvement in cognition was comparable to that of control. 
It can be concluded that this combination of probiotic treat-
ment is effective in improving cognitive decline in AD.
The promnestic effects of donepezil are documented pre-
viously. Prevention of memory impairment was observed 
in hairless rats administered with 3 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg 
scopolamine. Rats pre-treated with donepezil before sco-
polamine intraperitoneal injection displayed significantly 
higher % spontaneous alternation than those that received no 
pre-treatment (Shin et al. 2018). Likewise, APP/PS-1 trans-
genic mice significantly improved performance in NOR and 
MWM following administration of donepezil by attenuating 
expression of CD68 which is involved in IL-1β, TNF-α and 
microglial activation (Guo et al. 2015). The current study 
also observed improved performance of ­AlCl3 + donepezil-
treated mice in % spontaneous alternation, DI and escape 
latency.
Interestingly, the ­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12® mice also 
exhibited improved activity in % spontaneous alternation, 
DI and escape latency observed through NOR test. It is evi-
dent that administration of Bifidobacterium lactis Probio-M8 
to APP/PS-1 transgenic mice led to enhanced α and β-gut 
diversity. Treated mice also displayed significant improve-
ment in % spontaneous alternations revealing that treatment 
with probiotics improved cognitive decline in APP/PS-1 
transgenic mice (Cao et al. 2021). A similar effect of Lacto-
bacillus rhamnosus GR-1 was observed on cognitive deficits 
via remodeling the gut microbiota (Gu et al. 2022).
Moreover, increase in systemic inflammation mediated by 
pro-inflammatory cytokines plays a major pathophysiologi-
cal role in AD (González-Reyes et al. 2017). LGG® substan-
tially modulates the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines 
(Bootorabi et al. 2021). While attenuation of TLR2 cytokine 
expression by BB-12® is also reported in young adults 
(Meng et al. 2017). In addition, L. rhamnosus, B. infan-
tis and L. reuteri showed significant reduction in levels of 
IL-1β and TNF-α (Mehrabadi and Sadr 2020). A similar 
anti-inflammatory effect was reported for Bifidobacterium 
breve supplementation that may be associated with the atten-
uation of microglial activation (Abdelhamid et al. 2022). The 
significant decline in mRNA expression of pro-inflammatory 
cytokines observed in our study, prominently evident in both 
­AlCl3 + LGG® and BB-12® and ­AlCl3 + donepezil-treated 
groups further substantiate the effectiveness of combined 
administration of LGG® and BB-12® to mitigate AD-like 
pathology and associated consequences.
Modulation of cognitive functions through probiot-
ics presents a promising area of research that can provide 
futuristic preventive and treatment strategies against AD. 
The gut brain axis is complex and require in-depth analysis 
to determine the effectiveness of probiotics and how they 
attenuate the severity of disease. Our findings indicated that 
the probiotics LGG® and BB-12® have strong potential to 
overcome neuroinflammatory imbalance, amyloid toxicity, 
cognitive deficits and anxiety-like behavior therefore can 
be considered as a combination therapy for AD through 
modulation of gut brain axis. However, role and effects of 
probiotics on amyloid-β accumulation and associated neu-
rotoxicity, tau hyperphosphorylation, oxidative stress, hip-
pocampal neurogenesis, and other related mechanisms of 
AD pathology will further provide insight on potential role 
of gut microbiota in AD.
