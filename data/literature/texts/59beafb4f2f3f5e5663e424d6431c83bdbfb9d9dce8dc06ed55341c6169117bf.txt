Atomic Engineering of Single-Atom Nanozymes for
Biomedical Applications
Ji Shen, Jian Chen, Yuping Qian, Xinqiang Wang,* Dingsheng Wang,* Hongge Pan,
and Yuguang Wang*
Single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes) showcase not only uniformly dispersed
active sites but also meticulously engineered coordination structures. These
intricate architectures bestow upon them an exceptional catalytic prowess,
thereby captivating numerous minds and heralding a new era of possibilities
in the biomedical landscape. Tuning the microstructure of SAzymes on the
atomic scale is a key factor in designing targeted SAzymes with desirable
functions. This review ﬁrst discusses and summarizes three strategies for
designing SAzymes and their impact on reactivity in biocatalysis. The eﬀects
of choices of carrier, diﬀerent synthesis methods, coordination modulation of
ﬁrst/second shell, and the type and number of metal active centers on the
enzyme-like catalytic activity are unraveled. Next, a ﬁrst attempt is made to
summarize the biological applications of SAzymes in tumor therapy,
biosensing, antimicrobial, anti-inﬂammatory, and other biological applications
from diﬀerent mechanisms. Finally, how SAzymes are designed and regulated
for further realization of diverse biological applications is reviewed and
prospected. It is envisaged that the comprehensive review presented within
this exegesis will furnish novel perspectives and profound revelations
regarding the biomedical applications of SAzymes.

1. Introduction
As a vital cohort of biocatalysts, enzymes
stand as pillars of signiﬁcance, wielding ex-
ceptional activity and exquisite speciﬁcity
in orchestrating diverse biochemical re-
actions within the realm of physiological
conditions.[1] However, due to the inacti-
vation, structural instability, and easy to
be degraded by proteases of natural en-
zymes, it is diﬃcult to develop various
drugs for disease treatment in vivo.[2] The
concept of “nano enzymes” was ﬁrst pro-
posed in 2004 and originally referred to as
nanoparticle-based enzyme mimics.[3] Un-
til 2007, Fe3O4 nanoparticles (NPs) were
found to possess peroxide (POD)-like ac-
tivity, which has since shattered the tradi-
tional notion that inorganic materials are bi-
ologically inert and sparked widespread in-
terest in exploring other novel enzyme-like
nanomaterials.[4] Subsequent to this semi-
nal milestone, the realm of nano enzymes,
exemplifying nanomaterials endowed with
enzyme-like activity, has embarked upon a remarkable trajectory
of swift advancement.[3b,5] With the development of metal particle
size to the single-atom level, single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes)
emerged, which may push nanoenzymes into a new era.[6]
Notably, driven by rapidly advancing nanotechnology, vari-
ous single-atom catalysts (SACs) nanomaterials are emerging
as promising candidates for the treatment of tumor-related
diseases,[7] antibacterial therapy,[8] biosensor detection,[9] etc.
SACs, akin to their enzymatic counterparts, manifest an ar-
ray of remarkable attributes encompassing geometric, chemi-
cal, and electronic structures at the atomic scale. SACs grant
us streamlined synthesis strategies, unparalleled control over
their active centers and morphologies, and tunable coordi-
nation environments.[10] Exhibiting a catalytic prowess that
knows no bounds, SACs grace us with superlative activity, all
while embracing the virtues of cost-eﬀectiveness and ease of
commercialization.[11] In this resplendent tapestry of scientiﬁc
exploration, SACs illuminate the path toward unraveling the in-
tricate mechanisms underlying catalytic reactions, opening doors
to a realm of understanding that transcends the conﬁnes of nat-
ural enzymes and nano enzymes.[12] A variety of SAzymes with
unique physical and chemical properties and catalytic activities
are being reasonably designed and have shown promising appli-
cations in a wide range of biocatalysis ﬁelds.

SACs are catalysts in which catalytically active metal atoms are
monodisperse loaded onto a carrier.[13] The nature of the carrier,
the unique active center’s electronic symphony, and the coordi-
nation environment can greatly inﬂuence the chemical state and
coordination structure of the metal single atoms,[14] and even reg-
ulate the adsorption and desorption behaviors of the reactants
or products in the catalytic reaction to aﬀect their enzyme-like
activities.[15] Therefore, a more comprehensive understanding
and regulation of the carrier types, local coordination microen-
vironment, and active centers of SACs can help to develop a new
generation of nano-enzymatic materials for various biomedical
applications (Figure 1).[16] First, Figure 1 illustrates that SAzyme
can be rationally designed according to the structure of SAzyme
by adjusting the carrier, the coordination environment of the
ﬁrst/second shell layer, and the metal active site to control the
reactivity of SAzymes; in addition, Figure 1 illustrates the appli-
cation of SAzyme in biosensing, tumor therapy, antibacterial, and
anti-inﬂammatory therapies according to diﬀerent development
mechanisms. When adding metal active centers, the dual-atom
nano enzymes (DAzymes) come to the fore.[17] The synergistic
eﬀect of the second reaction center introduced by DAzymes, a
family member of SAzymes, aﬀects the d-band center position,
which contributes to better enzyme-like activity.[18] However, how
single-atom regulatory strategies promote enzyme-like activities
to apply to biocatalysis has not been well summarized and out-
lined. In addition, there is a lack of in-depth discussion on the
mechanism of action of SAzymes in biocatalysis ﬁelds such as
tumor treatment and biosensing, etc.
Herein, we review the atomic engineering modulation and
development mechanisms of SAzymes in biomedical applica-
tions in recent years based on theoretical and experimental
results. First, for SAzymes, we emphasis the preparation of
SAzymes, the regulation of the coordination microenvironment
from ﬁrst/second shell, and the number of active sites. After-
ward, we present several notable breakthroughs of SAzymes in
biocatalysis, including tumor therapy, biosensing, antibacterial,
and anti-inﬂammatory therapies. We also enumerate for the ﬁrst
time the mechanisms (endogenous/exogenous stimuli) by which
SAzymes develop in diﬀerent biomedical reactions. Finally, chal-
lenges and perspectives for the rational design of SAzymes in
future biomedical applications are presented.
2. SAzymes
SACs, serving as a bridge between homogeneous and multi-
phase catalysis, not only oﬀer a straightforward and optimal
framework for designing atomic-level rational nano enzymes
but also furnish a well-suited platform for investigating the ori-
gins of enzyme-like activities due to the inherent advantages
of their homogeneous active sites and single atoms within a
low-coordination environment.[12a,19] SAzymes exhibit a diverse
array of enzyme properties encompassing POD-like, catalase
(CAT)-like, oxidase (OXD)-like, superoxide dismutase (SOD)-
like, and glutathione oxidase (GSHOx)-like activities.[20] Specif-
ically, POD/CAT are bestowed with the remarkable ability to har-
ness hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) as an electron acceptor, thereby
orchestrating the oxidation of a substrate, all the while eﬃca-
ciously decomposing H2O2 into its constituent water molecules
(H2O);[21] OXD, in their exquisite enzymatic prowess, engage
O2 as an esteemed electron acceptor, orchestrating the reduc-
tion of O2 into both H2O and H2O2;[22] SOD emerges as a cat-
alyst par excellence, facilitating the extraordinary dismutation of
superoxide into O2 and H2O2;[23] and GSHOx catalyzes the pro-
duction of H2O2 from glucose.[24] The main factors aﬀecting the
enzyme-like catalytic activity of SAzymes are synthesis strategy,
carrier morphology, numbers, types and positions of coordina-
tion atoms, active metal centers, etc., and therefore the enzyme-
like catalytic activity of SAzymes exhibits the potential for en-
hancement through deliberate manipulation of these inﬂuential
factors.

2.1. Preparation of SAzymes
2.1.1. Synthesis Method
Reasonable
design
and
preparation
of
high-performance
SAzymes herald a revolutionary frontier in the realm of arti-
ﬁcial enzyme design and optimization, oﬀering a promising
avenue for simulating the intricate functionalities exhibited by
their natural counterparts.[20,25] The disadvantage of migratory
agglomeration of SACs due to their high surface energy needs
to be avoided for stable anchoring of single metallic atoms to the
carriers, and several synthetic strategies have been developed to
achieve this.[26] For example, Zhu et al. constructed a polyglycol
manganese (Mn)/ PSAE by the coordination of single atomic
Mn and nitrogen (N) atoms in a hollow zeolite imidazole salt
framework (ZIF), which can eﬀectively eradicate tumor cells
through the generation of a diverse array of potent reactive
oxygen species (ROS) in direct response to tumor microen-
vironment (TME) stimuli.[27] Firstly, the hollow structure of
ZIF-8 NPs was generated by the controlled chemical etching
method, and the single atomic Mn was coordinated with N
atoms in the framework of hollow zeolite imidazolium salts by
ion exchange method. Further pyrolysis was carried out at a
temperature of 900 °C under an Ar atmosphere. This thermal
treatment facilitated a transformative metamorphosis wherein
the organic metal–organic framework (MOF) ligands underwent
a remarkable conversion, giving rise to nitrogen-doped carbon
structures. Simultaneously, the Mn atoms were adeptly ensnared
by the N-rich porous carbon, culminating in the successful syn-
thesis of the desired Mn/SAE composite material (as depicted in
Figure 2a). Through the utilization of the aberration-corrected
high-angle toroidal dark-ﬁeld scanning transmission electron
microscopy (AC HAADF-STEM), the presence of isolated Mn
atoms dispersed upon a carbon framework was unequivocally
revealed (Figure 2b). Furthermore, the energy dispersive X-ray
energy spectrum (EDS) map (Figure 2c) corroborated the ho-
mogeneous distribution of key elements, namely Mn, carbon
(C), and N, throughout the Mn/SAE composite material. And,
the Brunauer Emmett–Teller (BET, Figure 2d) validated that
Mn/SAE prepared by this strategy had a large surface area,
which enabled the active site Mn to catalyze the reaction to the
maximum extent and thus attained excellent antitumor prop-
erties. These ﬁndings served as a testament to the meticulous
design and precise execution of the pyrolysis process, resulting
in the creation of the composite material with its distinctive
structural and elemental characteristics.
The direct atomization of NPs into single atoms represents
a transformative approach that unlocks the full potential of
metal-catalyzed active sites, thereby oﬀering unprecedented op-
portunities to engineer both structural and electronic properties
that can signiﬁcantly improve enzyme catalytic performance.[28]
Chen et al. report an innovative approach for obtaining high-
performance SAzyme by directly thermally atomizing platinum
(Pt) NPs into Pt SACs via the reversal of the thermal sintering
process (Figure 2e), and Pt NPs were reduced to Pt SACs by
1050 °C sintering in Figure 2f.[29] To substantiate the crucial role
of high temperatures in facilitating the activation energy required
for atomic diﬀusion and overcoming the energy barrier associ-
ated with Pt–Pt bond fracture to form Pt SACs, the process of Pt
SACs formation was performed by pyrolysis of Pt NPs at diﬀerent
temperatures. Upon subjecting the sample to pyrolysis at 950 °C
for 5 h, a coexistence of Pt NPs and Pt SACs was observed, sug-
gesting that the complete dispersion of Pt NPs into single atoms
was not achieved at lower temperatures. As the temperature was
increased, the Pt SACs underwent reaggregation, forming Pt par-
ticles that gradually grew in size (Figure 2g–i). In order to gain
insight into the atomization process of Pt NPs to Pt SAC, the py-
rolysis time was increased from 1 to 5 h at 1050 °C to undergo
a size reduction process, probably due to the collision with the
carbon substrate, and the Pt NPs were ﬁnally converted to sin-
gle atoms. Finally, the prepared PtTS-SAzyme showed outstand-
ing POD-like activity and kinetics, and could decompose H2O2
into highly toxic ROS, thus destroying the bacterial membrane
and inducing bacterial apoptosis (Figure 2j).
In summary, we have made a brief literature review of simple
pyrolysis strategies and thermal migration strategies to convert
bulk NPs into SACs in biological applications. Numerous estab-
lished synthesis strategies have been successfully employed in
the fabrication of SACs (refer to Table 1). To unlock the full poten-
tial of SACs in biomedical applications, additional optimization
of the carrier materials and surface modiﬁcations are necessary.
The next sections will delve into comprehensive discussions on
carrier selection and the inﬂuence of the surrounding environ-
ment on the surface of SACs.
2.1.2. Carrier categories
The aggregation of single atomic metals into NPs is often at-
tributed to weak interactions between the metals and their car-
riers. To overcome this challenge, it is crucial to identify suit-
able carrier materials that can serve as a robust platform for
the construction of SAzymes.[36] Diﬀerent support materials
(carbon-based materials,[37] metal oxides,[38] MOF materials,[39]
two-dimensional (2D) materials,[40] Figure 3) endow the SACs
with versatile material properties. Single atoms on these support
s modiﬁed the local electronic structure of the carriers, and strong
metal- support interactions were formed between the supports
and the metals, which altered the adsorbed species’ spatial state
and energy distribution and optimized the catalytic performance
of the catalysts. Currently, the more widely studied carriers for
SACs in biomedical applications are mainly nitrogen-doped car-
bon materials and other materials have been reported one after
another. In the case of carbon as a carrier, not only the charge-
carrier separation and transfer eﬃciency can be promoted by
metal-support interactions, but also the coexistence of metal sin-
gle atoms and N vacancies improves the catalytic eﬃciency and
demonstrates synergistic catalytic eﬀects;[41] the MOF materials
themselves have large cavities that can strongly trap metal sin-
gle atoms; oxides are commonly used carrier materials, and their
surface oxygen vacancy defects can be used as sites for stabilizing
single-atom metals; metal atoms loaded on 2D materials not only
form a band structure with non-metal atoms, but also with their
surrounding metal atoms, resulting in complex and ﬂexible envi-
ronments to regulate the catalytic eﬃciency of enzyme-catalyzed
reactions.[42]

Expanding beyond the realm of CeO2 carriers, diﬀerent forms
of metal oxide TiO2 have been employed as popular and versa-

tributes, including ecological friendliness and remarkable chem-
ical stability.[52] These inherent properties further enhance the
appeal of TiO2 carriers as an ideal platform for accommodating
single atoms, thereby unlocking the immense potential for in-
novative and sustainable biomedical applications in the scientiﬁc
landscape. For, example, Wang et al. embarked on the design and
fabrication of a TiO2-supported silver (Ag) SAzyme (Figure 4h) as
an eﬃcient antiviral nanomaterial to achieve an eﬀective antiviral
eﬀect against SARS-CoV2.[53] AC HAADF-STEM images certiﬁ-
cated that Ag atoms could be evenly dispersed on TiO2 support,
and Ag, titanium (Ti), and O elements were evenly distributed
in space (Figure 4i).[54] UV–vis spectra in Figure 4j showed that
the band gap of TiO2 in Ag/TiO2 was signiﬁcantly reduced, pro-
viding compelling and conclusive evidence of the profound im-
pact of the strong metal support interaction. 1 wt% Ag-doped
Ag-TiO2 SAN showed higher POD-like activity than conventional
Ag and TiO2 with the Vmax reaching 8.67 × 10−7 m s−1, higher

Compared with the control group, spike-mediated cell-cell fusion
was signiﬁcantly inhibited in the Ag-TiO2 SAN treatment group
(Figure 4k), indicating that Ag-TiO2 SAN was a blocking agent
for virus entry into host cells and achieved an antiviral eﬀect.
This work held that the strong metal-support interaction between
single-atom metals and TiO2 showed good potential in biomedi-
cal reactions.
Immobilization of enzymes in biocompatible carriers repre-
sents a widely adopted approach to enhance enzyme stability and
prolong their service lifespan. Due to the advantages of high spe-
ciﬁc surface area, tunable pore size, diverse components, facile
surface functionalization, and exceptional biocompatibility, the
utilization of MOF materials has garnered signiﬁcant attention
and showcased immense potential in carrying biomolecules and
even SAzymes.[55] For example, Fang et al. designed a string of
single atom copper (Cu)-modiﬁed UIO-66 / UIO-67 at diﬀerent
spatial positions for hydrolysis of phosphorylation bonds, and
the catalytic sites introduced by the multifunctional MOF car-
rier produced diﬀerent oxygen vacancies and Lewis acids, and
screened out the single Cu-modiﬁed UIO MOFs with the highest
hydrolytic activity, which was subsequently employed for catalytic
hydrolysis of single and double-stranded DNA.[43b] The XPS spec-

the presence of Cu, Zirconium (Zr), and oxygen (O) elements

2.2. First Shell Coordination Modulation
Since SAzymes are stabilized by coordination interaction be-
tween metal and support, the catalytic performance of SAzymes
is highly sensitive to its local coordination environment from
the perspective of the coordination microenvironment.[39b,66] The
current regulation of the coordination microenvironment for
SAzymes is mainly based on carbon supports, and the exam-
ples described in Sections 2.2, 2.3, and 2.3 are all mainly on
carbon supports. The coordination microenvironment modu-
lation of ﬁrst and second coordination shells can greatly af-
fect the electronic and geometric structures of metal centers in
SAzymes, and play a crucial role in improving catalytic activity
and selectivity.[14a,39b,67] The coordination eﬀect generated by the
coordination atoms in the ﬁrst shell layer can directly aﬀect the
catalytic activity of SAzymes, so ﬁguring out the inﬂuence of the
coordination microenvironment in the ﬁrst shell layer is crucial
for the design of high-performance SAzymes. And, modiﬁcation
of the coordination microenvironment of SAzymes can further
improve the catalytic activity of SAzymes in speciﬁc complex re-
action systems.

their catalytic performance.
2.2.2. Modulation of Coordination Heteroatoms
Carbon-based SAzymes metal atoms form M-Nx active sites
mainly by coordination with N atoms. In addition to N-
coordination, the doping of other heteroatoms (phosphorus
(P), sulfur (S), boron (B), O) provides additional opportuni-
ties to adjust the charge-helicity structure of the metal sin-
gle atoms and stabilize single atomic species.[72] Even with
the same metal center, the diverse array of coordination
atom species of SAzymes holds profound implications that
can inﬂuence intrinsic biomedical activity. Ji et al. encapsu-
lated Fe ions and monomers of poly-(cyclotriphospazeneco-4,4′-
diaminodiphenylether) (PZM) monomers onto the surface of
ZIF-8, and then pyrolyzed the complexes to obtain Fe SAzyme
with Fe1N3P1 sites (Figure 7a).[73] The EDS analysis of Figure 7b
demonstrated that the elements Fe, C, N, and P were uniformly
distributed throughout the structure of FeN3P-SAzyme. Exper-
imental ﬁndings derived from FT-EXAFS at the Fe K-edge for
FeN3P-SAzyme and FeN4-SAzyme revealed that the intensity of
the main peak of FeN3P-SAzyme was signiﬁcantly weaker than
that of FeN4-SAzyme which was due to the introduction of phase
cancellation interferences in the ﬁrst Fe-N coordination shell
layer after the partial substitution of N by P (Figure 7c). To gain
further insights into the coordination conﬁgurations, a quanti-
tative curve-ﬁtting analysis of EXAFS in Figure 7d showed that
the best matches to FeN3P-SAzyme were the three coordination
shells of Fe-N/O, Fe-P, and Fe-C, with coordination numbers of
3.6, 1.1, and 4.3, respectively, which provided stronger evidence
for the rationality of the structural model of FeN3P-SAzyme. In
addition, the experiment in Figure 7e showed that the measured
speciﬁc activity of FeN3P-SAzyme (316 U mg−1) was almost 10-
fold higher activity than FeN4-SAzyme (33.8 U mg−1). The next
in vitro and in vivo tumor cell inhibition experiments elabo-
rated that FeN3P-SAzyme selectively catalyzed the production of
more oxidative species and thus induced more tumor cell death
in the acidic environment of the cytosolic lysosome relative to
FeN4-SAzyme (Figure 7f). Theoretical calculations in Figure 7g
demonstrated that the low barrier to O and OH generation on
FeN3P-SAzyme (<0.5 eV) facilitated the catalytic reaction rela-
tive to the diﬃcult conversion of OH to H2O molecules on the
FeN4-SAzyme surface (1.05 eV). These ﬁndings shed light on the
favorable catalytic properties of FeN3P-SAzyme relative to FeN4-
SAzyme.
We can ﬁnd that the coordination atoms around the metal cen-
ter of the ﬁrst shell layer have a robust coordination ability, and
the modulation of the coordination atom species of SAzymes
exerts a profound inﬂuence on the catalytic performance. Fine-
tuning the coordination atom species allows for the optimiza-
tion of the d-band center of the central metal atom, facilitating
charge distribution polarization and development towards the
Fermi energy level. This, in turn, eﬀectively reduces the acti-
vation energy of intermediate states involved in catalytic reac-
tions. However, the large-scale synthesis of SAzymes with asym-
metric coordination conﬁgurations and charge distributions re-
mains a formidable challenge, and the design of heteroatom-
doped SAzymes for biocatalysis is still in its infancy.
2.3. Second Shell Coordination Modulation

regulation strategies, the focus was primarily on adjusting the
number and types of coordination atoms in the ﬁrst shell layer
of SAzymes. However, it is important to note that the coordina-
tion microenvironment of SAzymes is modiﬁed not only by the
ﬁrst shell atoms but also by the second shell and even higher shell
atoms (typically the M-N4 sites). Unlike the direct coordination of
atoms in the ﬁrst shell layer, the coordination strength of atoms

modulation of the ﬁrst shell layer, the doping of the second shell
layer with heteroatoms (S, B, P, etc.) also has a non-negligible
eﬀect on the catalytic ability, which can modulate the intrinsic
activity of the central atoms of the SAzymes, thus optimizing the
adsorption of the reaction products by the SAzymes to promote
the catalytic reaction.[74] Zhu et al. undertook precise regulation
of nickel (Ni) SAzyme (S-N/Ni PSAE, Figure 8a), incorporating
rich-border modiﬁcations of S and N atoms.[75] Sulfated S-N/Ni

2.4. Metal Active Centers Adjustment
2.4.1. The Types of Metal Active Centers
The metal active center plays the most crucial role in the enzy-
matic activity of SAzymes and serves as the heart of SAzymes.
Diﬀerent metal active centers endow SAzymes with distinct cat-
alytic activities.[78] Jiao et al. compared the diﬀerences in POD-
like activity of SAzymes with diﬀerent active centers such as Fe-
N4, Co-N4, and Zn-N4 sites in Figure 9a.[79] The experimental
results in Figure 9b exhibited that Fe–N–C SAzymes possessed
the best enzyme-like activity relative to Co–N–C and Zn–N–C
SAzymes. The reactive species •OH generated at diﬀerent active
sites was directly detected by electron paramagnetic resonance
(EPR) spectroscopy (Figure 9c), the results of which showed that
Fe-N-C SAzymes had the highest EPR signals, generated more
•OH, and exhibited higher POD-like activity. DFT theoretical
calculations showed revealed that the diﬀerent charge transfer
mechanisms and adsorption energies of reactants at the active
sites of FeN4and CoN4, thereby inﬂuencing the catalytic capac-
ities of the M-N-C SAzymes with diverse active centers. And,
the projected density of states (PDOS) yielded d-band centers
of −1.40, −1.67, and −4.95 eV for single metal atoms bound to
the two adsorbed OHs in the Fe–N–C, Co–N–C, and Zn–N–C
models, respectively (Figure 9d). The enhanced d-band centers
closer to the Fermi energies indicated that bonding interactions
between the catalysts and the adsorbed OH were weakened and
that the OHs were most readily available to dissociate to form
free •OH from the Fe-N-C model adsorption sites. Thus, relative
to Co-N-C, Zn-N-C SAzymes, the higher catalytic ability of Fe-N-
C SAzymes were closely related to their higher ability to adsorb
H2O2 (higher adsorption energy) and greater ability to generate
•OH (lower energy barrier).
In addition, SAzymes with diﬀerent active centers also aﬀect
diﬀerent enzyme-like activities. Cheng and his co-workers paid
particular attention to how diﬀerent metal centers in SAzymes
aﬀected various enzyme-like properties. They investigated the en-
zyme mimetic activity and speciﬁcity of 20 diﬀerent well-deﬁned
M-N active site SAzymes (Figure 9e). Further studies were found
that the catalytic activity of the enzyme-like enzyme strongly de-
pends on the active metal center. As shown in Figure 9f,g, Cu-
SAzyme-POD-like activity was the best and Co-SAzyme-POD-like
activity was the lowest and Fe-SAzyme demonstrated the highest
CAT-like catalytic activity and the fastest kinetics. Taking Mn, Fe,
Co, Ni, and Cu as examples, DFT calculations yielded charges
of –1.10|e| and –1.34|e| for the Fe and Mn centers, which were
notably lower than those of the Co, Ni, and Cu centers, which
strongly supported the notion that the diﬀerent metal-active cen-
ters did indeed exhibit diﬀerent catalytic activities (Figure 9h).
Thus, the properties of SAzymes are closely linked to speciﬁc
atomic metal centers. The precise adjustment of the active single
atomic center is a direct and eﬀective strategy for the rational and
deliberate design of targeting artiﬁcial enzymes. Carefully select-
ing appropriate active metals based on the desired enzyme simu-
lation will greatly advance the research process of SAzymes. And,
the utilization of multiple metal associations may potentially ex-
hibit enhanced multiple enzyme mimetic activities, which will
also be elaborated in the next section.
2.4.2. The Numbers of Metal Active Centers
As we know, SAzymes have a clear active center, with its ac-
tivity and selectivity adjustable in a targeted way. However, the
random distribution of single atoms on the support makes it
diﬃcult to catalyze cooperatively with other metals, and SACs
with a single active site are very challenging in complex cat-
alytic systems.[17b,81] Dual/multi-atomic catalysts (DACs/MACs),
as an extension of the SACs family, have been emerging in re-
cent years, and the synergistic eﬀect of two/multi metal atoms
(homonuclear/heteronuclear) in DACs/MACs signiﬁcantly im-
proves the catalytic activity of catalysts.[4b,82] This development
has opened up exciting avenues in the ﬁeld of biocatalysis,
as DACs/MACs oﬀer higher metal atomic loading capacities
and more adaptable active sites compared to SACs.[83] For ex-
ample, Ma et al. proposed a dual-single atomic nano enzyme
(DSAzyme) comprising Zn and Mo, which exhibited synergis-
tic eﬀects and POD-like activity and was used for serum glucose
detection, ascorbic acid (AA) detection in beverages, cholesterol
determination, and intracellular H2O2 biosensing detection.[84]
First of all, as shown in Figure 10a, Zn/Mo DSAzyme was syn-
thesized by using non-covalent nano-assemblies of polyoxometa-
lates, supramolecular coordination complexes as metal atom pre-
cursors, and macroscopic aerogels as supporting substrates. The
distribution of individual atoms of Mo and Zn was conﬁrmed by
Elemental mapping using energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy.
To provide further evidence of the atomic distribution of Mo and
Zn within the aerogel matrix, EXAFS spectra were analyzed from
the K-edge of Mo and Zn (Figure 10b,c), and conﬁrmed that Mo
and Zn atoms were indeed atomically distributed on the aerogel
matrix. The catalytic properties of Zn SAzyme, Mo SAzyme, and
their simple mixtures were compared, and the results showed
that the Zn and Mo-based SAzyme also had POD simulated activ-
ity, but the performance was only 30% and 33% of that of Zn/Mo
DSAzyme. Therefore, there was an indispensable synergistic ef-
fect between Zn and Mo within the constructed DSAzyme. The
Eads, OH value calculated by the DFT in Figure 10d showed that the
dual Zn/Mo site was indeed superior to the other two single-atom
sites because its Eads, OH value (3.01 eV) was closer to the optimal
value of 2.6 eV than the other two sites (2.17 eV for Zn SAzyme
and 3.44 eV for Mo SAzyme). All the above results proved that
the binding of Zn and Mo sites synergistically increased POD-
like activity.
To delve deeper into the synergistic interactions among the
multi-atoms and improve the catalytic performance, the MACs
as gradually come into view in the biocatalysis ﬁeld. Building
upon this concept, triatomic catalysts in the presence of trimetal-
lic FeCoZn sites were designed (named FeCoZn–TAC/SNC,
Figure 10e), which showed signiﬁcantly higher catalytic perfor-
mance than the single/dual-site catalysts (FeZn, CoZn, FeCo-
DAC/NC, and Fe, Zn, Co-SAC/NC), demonstrating a synergistic
eﬀect, and moreover showing the potential to serve as triatomic
nano enzyme sensors for biologically relevant assays.[85] The Fe,

3. Biomedical Application
3.1. Tumor Therapy
The atomic-scale nano-catalytic therapy strategy has emerged as
a promising approach for the local TME to produce speciﬁc toxic
substances from nontoxic substances while not aﬀecting normal
tissues, reduce the adverse reactions caused by traditional ther-
apies, and achieve non-invasive antitumor therapy.[86] And, with
the in-depth exploration of the development mechanism of tu-
mor therapy, it has been found that SAzymes mainly trigger the
catalytic reaction of TME through endogenous and exogenous
stimuli, disrupt the intracellular redox homeostasis, and can fur-
ther induce the programmed apoptosis or necrosis of tumor cells
to achieve antitumor therapy.[87] According to the source of these
stimuli, these can be divided into three categories, namely, en-
dogenous stimulus-responsive, exogenous stimulus-responsive,
and endogenous/exogenous co-stimulus-responsive.
3.1.1. Endogenous Stimulus Responsive
The endogenous triggering mechanism primarily relies on spe-
ciﬁc chemical compounds present in the TME, such as H+,
H2O2, GSH, and metal atoms are employed as the active site
to trigger catalysis reactions.[88] In the presence of speciﬁc weak
acids and H2O2 in the TME, it can trigger a fenton-like reac-
tion to produce •OH with strong oxidation capacity and gener-
ate more highly toxic ROS to damage cell DNA chains, thus ef-
fectively killing cancer cells.[89] Fenton reaction is a catalytic re-
action mediated by Fe ions under acidic conditions. At present,
SACs with Fe as active centers have been extensively investigated
for their enzyme-mimicking properties in anti-tumor therapy.[90]
Tang et al. developed a bionic mesoporous Fe SAzyme system
(CSD) to regulate endogenous H2O2 levels, thereby enhancing
the production of •OH mediated by Fe SAzyme. This, in turn,
induced mitochondrial damage, reduced adenosine triphosphate
(ATP) content, and further improved the therapeutic outcome
(Figure 12a).[91] The cytotoxicity was evaluated in Figure 12b,
the CSD group achieved optimal tumor cell inhibition eﬀect.
Compared to the control group, the CSD group had a lower cell
proliferation eﬀect and maintained a steady state of therapeu-
tic activity over 15 days. (Figure 12c). Also, further terminal de-
oxynucleotidyl transferase-mediated dUTP-biotin nick end label-
ing (TUNEL) and Ki-67 staining of tumor tissues showed that
the CSD group exhibited the highest ﬂuorescence intensity for
ROS (Figure 12d), causing damage to the cell membranes, nu-
cleic acids, and proteins of tumor cells, ultimately leading to mas-
sive apoptosis of tumor cells.
In recent years, in addition to Fe, other metal-based materi-
als (such as Co, Mn, Cu, Ruthenium (Ru), Palladium (Pd), and
Pt, etc.) have also demonstrated the ability to H2O2 to •OH
through Fenton-type reactions.[92] Recently, Co-SAs@NC with
Co-N4 as the center showed excellent Fenton-like activity, produc-
ing enough O2
•−free radicals and eﬀectively killing tumor cells
(Figure 12e).[93] As a bifunctional nano enzyme, the Co-SAs@NC
not only catalyzed the decomposition of endogenous H2O2 into
O2 in the cascade enzyme reaction as a CAT mimic with Vmax of
81.6 μm. s−1, but also continuously catalyzes the conversion of O2
into highly cytotoxic O2
•−free radicals as an OXD mimic with the
Vmax of 0.459 μm. s−1, respectively. Taking into account the weakly
acidic condition of the TME, cell viability was measured simulta-
neously in cell cultures with pH 7.4 and pH 6.0. The cytotoxicity
of Co-SAs@NC was evaluated by incubating it with human breast
cancer cells (MDA-MB-231) for 24 h using the cell counting kit-8
(CCK-8) assay. Remarkably, Co-SAs@NC showed greater cytotox-
icity to cancer cells in a pH 6.0 medium than in a pH 7.4 medium
(Figure 12f), which is thought likely to be the production of toxic
O2
•−radicals by Co-SAs@NC under acidic conditions. To further
conﬁrm the generation by this SAzyme of more O2
•−free radicals
under slightly acidic conditions, ROS levels were measured with

induced cells to produce higher ROS levels under slightly acidic

could serve as a biocompatible platform for PDT, where SAzymes
themselves continuously catalyze O2 generation in a prolonged
hypoxic environment, thus providing a design strategy for pho-
tocatalytic 1O2 to enhance the eﬃcacy of photoconductive radio-
therapy.
Although PDT therapy has emerged as a promising strategy
for anticancer treatment, the eﬀectiveness of this approach is of-
ten limited by the short lifespan of ROS. Therefore, in order to
enhance and maintain ROS production in tumor cells, Huang
et al. developed a tumor exosome-mimicking SAZ/AIEgens cas-
cade catalytic system (CCS), which exploited the cascade reaction
between Cu SAzyme and an AIE molecule (TBP-2), enhancing
the production of •OH and promoting eﬀective tumor penetra-
tion, thereby boosting the eﬃcacy of PDT (Figure 13g).[100] To ver-
ify the ability of the CCS to generate •OH under light exposure,
H2O2 ﬂuorescence kit was employed to visualize and monitor the
•OH production of the cells. Figure 13h illustrated that the CCS
group after 1 h of light exposure had signiﬁcantly higher •OH
content in the synergy between Cu SAzyme and light. This en-
hancement can be attributed to the fact that TBP-2, acting as a
type I photosensitizer, generates a large number of •OH when
exposed to light, thereby enhancing the catalytic activity of Cu
SAzyme. In addition, the best tumor cell-killing eﬀect in the CCS
+ L group was conﬁrmed by cytotoxicity assay in a concentration-
dependent manner (Figure 13i). The subsequent anti-tumor ex-
periments were performed using the subcutaneous tumor model
of SGC-7901 gastric cancer, and the therapeutic eﬀect was signif-
icant and long-lasting in the CCS + L group relative to the other
experimental groups, with tumor inhibition consistently reach-
ing as high as 90% over the course of 15 days. (Figure 13j). The
therapeutic role of CCS in preventing tumor recurrence was in-
vestigated in the mouse CT26 tumor model. After treatment with
CCS + L, none of the mice experienced tumor relapse during the
monitoring period, which could signiﬁcantly improve the ther-
apeutic eﬀect of PDT (Figure 13k). These ﬁndings provide com-
pelling evidence that combining SAzymes with other drugs holds
great promise as a future direction for the treatment of metastatic
and recurrent cancers.
Unlike the response mechanism of PDT, PTT is a treatment
method that raises the temperature of the surrounding environ-
ment by utilizing the photothermal eﬀect, which collects light
energy and converts it into heat to kill cancer cells.[101] PTT of-
fers the potential to improve the eﬃciency of local photothermal
heating and selectively ablate tumor tissue in a minimally inva-
sive manner[25a,102] Although PTT has undergone extensive clin-
ical trials compared to the clinical use of PDT therapy, numer-
ous studies have highlighted its promise as an eﬀective cancer
treatment method. However, one challenge associated with PTT
is the potential damage to normal tissues surrounding the tu-
mor due to excessive heat. Cancer cells damaged by mild hyper-
thermia (38–43 °C) can be repaired through stress-induced heat
shock proteins (HSPs).[25a] Chang et al. proposed an innovative
strategy using Pd SAzyme with atom-utilizing economic catalytic
center with POD and GSHOx mimetic activity and photother-
mal conversion properties leading to iron death characterized by
the accumulation of lipid peroxides (LPOs) and ROS providing
a robust pathway for the cleavage of HSPs thereby achieving a
mild PTT (Figure 14a).[103] First, the photothermal conversion
performance of Pd SAzyme triggered at 1064 nm was investi-
gated and it can be seen from Figure 14b that the temperature of
Pd SAzyme increased with increasing irradiation concentration,
irradiation power density, and irradiation time. The simulated
POD and GSH enzyme activities of Pd SAzyme were studied at
simulated mild acidic TME (pH 6.5) and safe treatment temper-
ature (42 °C), with a Vmax of 1.51 × 10−7 and 1.97 × 10−5 m s−1,
respectively. Therefore, Pd SAzyme can eﬀectively simulate the
dual activity of POD and GSHOx at mildly elevated temperature,
greatly causing the increase of ROS in cancer cells. The cytotoxic-
ity of Pd SAzyme was assessed on breast cancer 4T1 cells. The cell
killing rate of 4T1 cells was enhanced with increasing Pd SAzyme
concentration (Figure 14c). Notably, the 4T1 cells were almost
completely killed when the 1064 nm laser further irradiated the
4T1 cells. Next, ROS ﬂuorescent probe was used to detect the gen-
eration of ROS in cells. As shown in Figure 14d, compared with
the Pd SAzyme group, the Pd SAzyme + 1064 nm group could
detect stronger ﬂuorescence, indicating that mild PTT could
signiﬁcantly improve the POD-simulated enzyme activity of Pd
SAzyme. At the same time, the Pd SAzyme + 1064 nm treatment
group showed more pronounced intracellular GSH consumption
than the Pd SAzyme group (Figure 14e), conﬁrming the mild
heat-enhanced GSHOx-mimics enzyme activity of Pd SAzyme at
the cellular level and realizing that PTT eﬀectively promoted ROS
production by Pd SAzyme. Figure 14f shows that tumor growth

showed that the cancer cells in Pd SAzyme + 1064 nm group
had the most serious damage. It could be seen that the treatment
of Pd SAzyme + 1064 nm could eﬀectively make cancer cells sen-
sitive to mild PTT with high eﬃcacy (Figure 14g).

X-ray stimulus-responsive: X-ray-induced nano catalytic ther-
apy is a method of killing cancer cells by using X-rays, harness-
ing the excellent penetration capabilities to target deep-seated tu-
mors within the body in the organism and cause them to pro-
duce ROS. By irradiating the tumor region, X-rays induce the
production of ROS, leading to the selective killing of cancer cells.
This strategy allows for precise tumor ablation while minimiz-
ing the side eﬀects on normal tissues, combining the advantages
of photodynamic therapy and radiotherapy. Although current
research is limited, X-ray-induced nano-catalytic therapy holds
great potential for clinical applications.[105] Zhu et al. demon-
strated that the introduction of ﬁeld X-rays enhanced the nano
catalytic therapy of Fe SAzyme, importantly, the use of X-rays
improved the entire unit point cascade enzymatic reaction pro-
cess by increasing the conversion frequency of FeII/FeIII.[106] Ini-
tially, FeN4-SAzyme was synthesized by salt template method,
and subsequently engineered by GOD to obtain a radiolytic en-
zyme therapeutic agent (GOD@FeN4-SAzyme) with radiation-
accelerated self-cascade catalysis and H2O2 self-supply capabil-
ity. Let’s say H2O2 as the substrate, this FeN4-SAzyme exhibited
intrinsic POD-like activity, catalyzing a reaction that followed typ-
ical Michaelis–Menten saturation kinetics with a higher catalytic
eﬃciency (Vmax = 6.20 × 10−6 m min−1 at pH 6.49) than the Fe3O4
nano-enzyme (Vmax = 2.60 × 10−7 m min−1 at pH 6.49). In terms
of GSHOx-like activity, FeN4-SAzyme also exhibited Michaelis–
Menten kinetics, yielding a Vmax of 3.64 × 10−5 m min−1. Next,
under X-ray irradiation, FeN4-SAzyme showed an increased cat-
alytic eﬃciency in decomposing H2O2 (Vmax = 5.93 × 10−6) and
consuming GSH (Vmax = 4.73 × 10−5), demonstrating that X-rays
were able to promote FeN4-SAzyme self-cascade activity, indicat-
ing the potential for radiolytic enzyme therapy by combining en-
hanced self-cascade therapy with radiotherapy of the nanomate-
rial FeN4-SAzyme. This was further tested by cytotoxicity and an-
titumor properties in mice bearing 4T1 tumors, where the best
tumor killing was achieved and the growth of 4T1 tumors was
signiﬁcantly inhibited for up to 14 days after continuous irradia-
tion with X-rays. In conclusion, X-ray stimulus-responsive ther-
apy holds promise for the targeted treatment of cancer. The in-
troduction of X-rays indeed enhances the nano-catalytic therapy
of Fe SAzyme.
Ultrasound stimulus-responsive sonodynamic therapy (SDT)
is a non-invasive and precise new method of tumor treatment.[107]
SDT takes advantage of the “targeting” characteristics of the
high concentration of acoustic sensitizers gathered in the tu-
mor cells and the tumor neovascular endothelial cells, and then
activates the acoustic sensitizers with special ultrasound under
non-invasive conditions after precise positioning by imaging.[108]
After precise positioning by imaging under non-invasive condi-
tions, the sensitizer is activated by ultrasound, which generates
unilinear oxygen by ultrasonic chemical reaction, seizing elec-
trons from the tumor cells, causing damage to the organelles of
the tumor cells and ultimately leading to the death of the tumor
cells. At the same time, SDT therapy damages the tumor vascu-
lar epithelial cells, resulting in the release of thromboxane, which
will form a thrombus inside the tumor blood vessels and cause
ischemic necrosis in the tumor tissues, thereby it can achieve the
goal of killing the tumor accurately and completely without dam-
aging normal cells.[109] A study reported the synthesis of a carbon
nanostructure PMCS containing a single atom Zn.[110] The high
speciﬁc surface area and porous structure possessed excellent gas
adsorption performance, which provided more sites for lowering
the cavitation threshold intensity and laid the foundation for en-
hanced SDT. Cytotoxicity experiments showed that PMCS itself
was not signiﬁcantly toxic even at high concentrations, but under
US (1.0 MHz, 1 W cm−2, 1 min) irradiation, the cell viability of
4T1 was decreased with an increase in the SAzymes concentra-
tion; when the concentration reached 50 μg mL−1, the cell viabil-
ity began to fall below 50%. These results preliminarily demon-
strated that this SAzyme was not only reasonably biocompatible,
but also had high acoustic toxicity. Meanwhile, as conﬁrmed by
DFT calculations, the single-atom structure and suitable energy
gap showed excellent ultrasound sensitization eﬀect, generating
more ROS and tumor inhibition eﬃciency of up to 85% for 18
days in comparison with the organ porphyrin Zn. These ﬁndings
highlight the potential of SDT therapy for accurate and eﬃcient
tumor ablation while sparing normal cells.

3.2. Biosensing
Biosensing technology has emerged as a crucial approach for
fundamental medical research and clinical disease diagno-
sis because of its exceptional speciﬁcity and sensitivity.[21c,115]
SAzymes, with their atomic-level reaction sites, have remark-
able sensitivity and selectivity for biomolecular detection, which
meet the requirements of accurate and rapid diagnosis, and show
highly promising as detectors for applications in the ﬁeld of
biosensing.[116] In recent years, signiﬁcant advancements have
been made in the utilization of SAzymes for colorimetric single-
mode biosensing, electrochemical single-mode biosensing, other
single-mode biosensing, and muti-mode biosensing with poten-
tial catalytic mechanisms.
3.2.1. Colorimetric Single-Mode Biosensing
Colorimetric single-mode detection oﬀers the advantages of
strong visual color response, simple detection mechanism,
portable real-time visual detection, etc., and has great application
prospects in the construction of SAzymes sensors.[117] Kang et al.
reported that Pt SAzymes exhibit exceptional OXD-mimicking
activity (8.35 U mg−1) due to fully exposed active sites, enabling
colorimetric detection of hydroquinone (HQ) and 𝛼-glucosidase
(𝛼-Glu) (Figure 16a,b).[118] For HQ detection, the absorbance at
652 nm showed an excellent linear relationship with the concen-
tration, and the LOD for HQ was calculated to be 0.07 μm at 3
N/S (Figure 16c). The speciﬁcity of the sensing system was in-
vestigated and Figure 16d showed that the detection of HQ was
not interfered by other phenolics. The concentration of HQ in
real water samples was tested to assess the reliability and use-
fulness of the analysis of HQ, and the recoveries were in the
ranges of 98.5 ± 2.54% and 102.5 ± 3.90%, respectively. Further-
more, as the 𝛼-Glu activity increased from 0 to 15 U mL−1, the
absorbance of the reaction solution steadily decreased with the
gradual increase in the concentration of HQ formed in the solu-
tion (Figure 16e), and the limit of detection was calculated to be
0.0038 U mL−1 (3 N/S), demonstrating the speciﬁcity and high
sensitivity of the SA-Pt-CN sensor for 𝛼-Glu detection. Moreover,
the recoveries of this assay system ranged from 98.8 ± 1.8% to
105.5 ± 1.42% (Figure 16f), which was promising for the detec-
tion of 𝛼-Glu in complex matrices.
Combining SAzymes and other systems holds promise for un-
veiling novel insights into the construction of robust nano enzy-
matic biosensing systems. Wu et al. combined the Cu-N-C en-
zyme with natural acetylcholinesterase (AChe) and choline ox-
idase (Chox) to construct a triple-enzyme cascade reaction sys-
tem for the colorimetric detection of acetylcholine (ACh) and
organophosphorus pesticides (Figure 16g).[119] Notably, the pres-
ence of Cu–N–C induced a distinct absorbance peak at 652 nm
(Figure 16h), underscoring the pivotal role of individual Cu-based
species as active sites. The constructed three-enzyme system
for ACh measurement was based on cascade catalysis, where
ACh was ﬁrst hydrolyzed to choline by AChe, then next oxi-
dized to betaine and H2O2 by ChOx, and ﬁnally consumed by
Cu–N–C SAzyme for the catalytic reaction to achieve the col-
orimetric detection. Remarkably, the three-enzyme system dis-
played the highest absorption intensity (Figure 16i). The selec-
tivity of this three-enzyme system was investigated by select-
ing some common interfering molecules such as some pro-
teins, biomolecules, and metal ions, revealing minimal impact
on the detection of ACh (Figure 16j). Organophosphorus pesti-
cides (OP), as a class of pollutants, can be detected by evaluating
the activity of AChe because of its highly toxic inhibition of AChe
(Figure 16k). The OP was detected by AChe activity inhibition
rate (IR) method (Figure 16l). Under optimized conditions, the
LOD was 0.60 ng mL−1, and the linear range was 1–300 ng mL−1.
In addition, this method also has good selectivity for OP detec-
tion (Figure 16m). The recovery rates were 108.6% and 97.3%,
respectively, in tap water and lake water. Therefore, Cu SAzyme
combined with AChe and Chox oﬀers exceptional sensitivity and
selectivity in biosensing. Multi-enzyme biocatalytic cascade reac-
tions play an essential role in metabolic processes in organisms,
and cascade catalysis has a wide range of applications through
the rational design of SAzyme systems.

3.2.3. Other Single-Mode Biosensing
In order to broaden the application range of SAzymes in sens-
ing detection, the integration of SAzyme with various detec-
tion methods, such as paper-based lateral ﬂow immunoassay
(LFIA), ﬂuorescence detection, chemiluminescence (CL), and
multi-mode detection, has emerged as a new generation of re-
search methodologies.[124] LFIA is also applied for its simplic-
ity, rapidity, and multi-analytical capability compared to the above
techniques of electrochemical sensors and colorimetric sensors.
SAzyme, with its exceptional catalytic activity, can serve as a
marker in LFIA by inducing a chromogenic reaction that en-
hances the signal. For instance, Cai et al. harnessed the eﬃcient
catalytic properties of Fe-N-C SAzyme as a marker and catalyst
in LFIA for the detection of mycotoxins, speciﬁcally Aﬂatoxin B1
(AFB1) and fumonisin B1 (FB1).[125] First, the SAzyme probe was
synthesized by coupling the anti-AFB1 monoclonal antibody and
anti-FB1 monoclonal antibody to a 5 wt% load of Fe SAC. The
probes could bind both to mycotoxins in the sample and specif-
ically to competing antigens (AFB1-BSA or FB1-BSA) immobi-
lized on the test line. The assay was based on the following prin-
ciple: in the absence of AFB1 and FB1 mycotoxins (negative), the
probe was captured by both test lines T1 and T2, respectively,
resulting in the typical dark grey bands on the two test lines;
in the presence of mycotoxins (positive), the probe ﬁrst reacted
with AFB1 and/or FB1 in the sample, and then the rest of the
probe would bind to the antigens, resulting in a diminished or
unobservable dark grey test line. The concentration of mycotox-
ins could therefore be determined by the intensity of the color of
the test line as measured by the naked eye or by a smartphone. In
order to achieve a greater signal diﬀerence and increase sensitiv-
ity or broaden the detection range, the TMB+H2O2 color devel-
opment reagent is subsequently added to the test line. Due to the
excellent POD-like activity of the Fe-N-C SAzyme, the produced
free •OH could oxidize colorless TMB to blue oxidized TMB, as
a result, the color of the test line would change from dark grey
or colorless to blue, attaining a higher sensitivity and wider lin-
ear range because of this signal ampliﬁcation strategy. The LOD
of Fe-N-C SAzyme-based LFIA for AFB1 and FB1 without signal
enhancement was 0.5 and 2 ng mL−1, respectively. The vLOD for

3.3. Antibacterial
Diseases caused by pathogenic bacterial infections have emerged
as a signiﬁcant public health concern and currently rank as the
primary cause of human mortality in the early 20th century.
In recent decades, the overuse of antibiotics has led to bacte-
rial multi-drug resistance (MDR), posing a serious global health
challenge.[129] Unfortunately, the development of novel antimi-
crobial drugs has not kept pace with this issue.[130] To eﬀectively
combat bacterial resistance, there is an urgent need for the dis-
covery of new antimicrobial agents and therapies.[131] ROS can
simultaneously destroy vital substances (such as nucleic acids,
proteins and lipids) that are crucial to the physiological activities
of bacterial cells through oxidation.[132] Therefore, SAzymes ca-
pable of generating ROS eﬃciently remove drug-resistant bacte-
ria and are considered as a new antibacterial agent with broad
application prospects.[133]
To develop antibacterial SAzymes for promoting wound heal-
ing, Xu et al. invented Zn-N-C SAC derived from ZIF-8, which
showed excellent POD-like activity against P. aeruginosa strains
and proved to be an eﬀective antibacterial agent for wound treat-
ment (Figure 19a).[134] The catalytic performance of this Zn-
SAzyme was assessed by measuring the Michaelis-constant on
substrate H2O2 (Figure 19b). The •OH production was veriﬁed
through electron spin resonance (ESR) assay and Figure 19c
showed that the ESR signal intensity of •OH produced by this Zn
SAzyme was increased by 29.15%. In addition, the possible cat-
alytic mechanism of the interaction of unsaturated active Zn sites
with H2O2 was explored using DFT calculations (Figure 19d), i.e.,
ﬁrst, H2O2 molecules were adsorbed onto the Zn active site and
rapidly activated, and the subsequent hemolysis pathway led to
the decomposition of H2O2 to form 2 OH*, with 1 OH* escap-
ing from the active site to form •OH. Thus, the catalytic reac-
tion was easy to occur with the ΔG of 0.24 eV, resulting in a high
POD-like activity. Toxicological analysis of major organs in mice
showed no signiﬁcant toxicity of Zn SAzyme, indicating a reason-
able in vivo biosafety. The results of in vitro antimicrobial exper-
iments demonstrated that this Zn-SAzyme inhibited the growth
of P. aeruginosa with the participation of H2O2, reaching a sup-
pression rate of 99.87% (Figure 19e). The bactericidal eﬀect of the
system in vivo was further evaluated (Figure 19f). In mice with
infected wounds caused by P. aeruginosa, complete wound heal-
ing was observed by the 6th day of treatment, and the histologi-
cal staining results also conﬁrmed the degree of wound healing,
while the control group took a longer time.
Similar to the enhanced antitumor activity exhibited by
SAzymes in response to external stimuli, signiﬁcant antibacterial
activity can be achieved by the SAzymes combined with the exter-
nal stimulus.[135] Huo et al. combined a Fe SA on nitrogen-doped
carbon (SAF NCs) with 808 nm near-infrared laser irradiation
for localized hyperthermia of bacterial lesions, eﬀectively elimi-
nating bacteria of both Gram-positive (Staphylococcus aureus) and
Gram-negative (Escherichia coli) (Figure 19g).[136] The POD-like
activity of SAF NCs was enhanced by NIR laser (808 nm), indi-
cating a strong light absorption and photothermal conversion ef-
fect of SAF NCs, as shown in Figure 19h. Whether SAF NCs ex-
pressed stronger bactericidal performance under hyperthermia
was evaluated by co-incubation of the both strain cells with diﬀer-
ent groups of catalytic systems. The results of Figure 19i showed
that SAF NCs+H2O2+NIR group completely eradicated the bac-
terial cells surpassing the eﬃcacy of other groups. The capac-
ity of the SAF NCs with the NIR simulation to actually remove
propagating bacteria was tested in a BALB/c mice model of in-
cision bacterial infection. In the E. coli infection model, mice in
the SAF NCs + H2O2 + NIR group all showed sustained wound
closure and hardening on day 9, and wound closure was per-
formed on day 2 (Figure 19j), showing good bactericidal perfor-
mance. In the S. aureus infection experiment, SAF NCs + H2O2
+ NIR treated mice developed rapid wound hardening on day 5,
shorter than the SAF NCs + H2O2 group for 2 days (Figure 19k).

SAF-NCs induced cell membrane disruption in E. coli suspen-
sion bacterial cells were violently deformed, showing mechan-

3.4. Anti-Inﬂammatory Therapy
In the human system, ROS signiﬁcantly exerts signiﬁcant inﬂu-
ence on various physiological activities and plays a key role in di-
verse biological processes. Low levels of ROS can be employed as
an important secondary messenger for cell signaling pathways,
mitogenic reactions, cell proliferation/migration/diﬀerentiation,
and resistance to pathogen invasion.[137] Conversely, high ROS
levels further worsen local tissue damage, leading to chronic
inﬂammatory diseases such as wound sepsis, inﬂammatory
bowel disease, acute liver/kidney injury, and liver ﬁbrosis.[138]
Unlike the mechanisms employed for anticancer and antibac-
terial treatments, anti-inﬂammatory therapy focuses on re-
ducing intracellular ROS levels and restoring cellular redox
balance.[139] In recent years, a series of biocatalysts with unique
ROS scavenging capabilities have been designed, promising
to attenuate ROS levels for anti-inﬂammatory therapy.[47] No-
tably, SAzymes possessing enzyme-like characteristics have
shown great potential in safeguarding cells against oxidative
stress.

4. Summary, Challenges, Perspectives
4.1. Summary
In this review, we ﬁrst review the recent advances in the diﬀer-
ent synthesis methods, selection of suitable carriers, coordina-
tion microenvironment design in the ﬁrst/second shell layers
and the eﬀects of metal active sites of SAzymes, which are mainly
reﬂected in the charge distribution around the active centers
and the corresponding changes in the adsorption-dissociation
energies of the reaction intermediates. And, the corresponding
structure-property relationships are veriﬁed by a series of ex-
periments characterizing and theoretical results in simulating
enzyme-catalyzed substrates for biotherapeutic processes. Sub-
sequently, we provide an overview of the recent biomedical appli-
cations of SAzymes, encompassing tumor therapy, biosensing,
antibacterial, and anti-inﬂammatory treatments. Among them,
according to their diﬀerent catalytic mechanisms, within the
realm of tumor therapy we have divided them into endoge-
nous stimulation, exogenous stimulation, and combined endoge-
nous and exogenous stimulation; in the domain of biosensing,
we have classiﬁed them into colorimetric single-mode biosens-
ing, electrochemical single-mode biosensing, other single-mode
biosensing, and muti-mode biosensing; for antibacterial and
anti-inﬂammatory treatments, we also have systematically sum-
marized them. Finally, based on the above, we ﬁnd that research
on SAzymes is still at an early stage of development with many
challenges, which are presented in the next section, and at the
end of the review, we present perspectives towards SAzymes in
biomedical applications.
4.2. Challenges
So far, meaningful advancements have been achieved in the
biomedical application of SAzymes and their enzyme-like activi-
ties have been greatly improved by structural modulation. How-
ever, it still faces critical challenges that warrant careful consid-
eration and further improvement, and long-lasting eﬀorts are
needed to pave the way for the future sustained development of
SAzymes in biological applications. Since SAzymes were devel-
oped on the basis of nano enzymes, we compare their challenges
in Table 3 in three parts: structure, activity, and mechanistic stud-
ies. The challenges encountered by SAzymes are described be-
low:
1) Diﬃcult to control structure and inhomogeneous active site:
Currently, the main form of SAzymes is M-X (X = N, C, O,
P, S, etc.), and it is diﬃcult to control the M-X coordination
structure of SAzymes well during the synthesis process com-
pared to the synthesis of nano enzymes. And, challenges re-
main in the formation of homogeneous active sites during the
synthesis of SAzymes compared to these similar nanoenzyme
systems.
2) Poor biocompatibility: The poor water solubility and biocom-
patibility of carbon materials after high-temperature calcina-
tion as well as the need for further modiﬁcation by PEG, etc.,
have undoubtedly hampered the application of SAzymes. Al-
though oxide materials, 2D materials, etc. have been used as
carriers for SAzymes, they are also not universal, so there is
undoubtedly a need for more platforms suitable for use within
biological systems.

enzyme-like
catalytic
activity:

to
these similar nanoenzyme systems, the catalytic activity of
SAzymes is slightly reduced. Especially in the physiological
environment in organisms, it remains challenging to fully
utilize the active site to improve the enzyme-like catalytic per-
formance of SAzymes. DAzymes employing multiple metal
active sites can show surprising synergistic eﬀects in terms
of enzyme catalytic activity, but it is currently challenging to
accurately design DAzymes with higher enzyme activity.
4) Lack of understanding of the catalytic mechanism of
SAzymes: Compared to the action pathway of nano enzymes,
the point of action of SAzymes is only a single atom, and
the mechanistic study is still relatively basic. To better un-
derstand the complex structures and catalytic pathways of
SAzymes, more eﬃcient and advanced in situ characteriza-
tion, and computational techniques are yet to be further in-
vestigated.
5) Insuﬃcient catalytic selectivity and targeting: The insuﬃ-
cient substrate selectivity and targeting of most SAzymes are
due to the fact that SAzymes are inorganic catalysts lacking
biospatial structure. And, the catalytic selectivity of SAzymes
is highly dependent on the spatial structure near the active
center. Therefore, achieving selectivity and targeting similar
to that of natural enzymes remains a diﬃcult task.
6) Narrow range of applications and lack of clinical translation;
The application of SAzymes in biomedicine ﬁelds attains cer-
tain limitations, and the future also needs to broaden the
research ideas to improve its applicability. In addition, the
biomedical applications of SAzyme are still limited to the lab-
oratory stage, and there are still many challenges for its in-
dustrialization.

Based on the current challenges faced by SAzyme in biomedical
applications, we also provide recommendations and suggestions.
The details are as follows (Figure 21):
1) Reasonable and eﬃcient design methods of SAzymes: The
development of new synthetic strategies to precisely control
the structures of SAzymes is highly desirable. Precise place-
ment of hetero/secondary metal atoms into the local struc-
ture of single atom sites is feasible, which may be achieved
by controlling the distance between the metal active center
and the coordination atoms/second metal active centers.[143]
The molecular recognition strategy of coordination interac-
tions between guest molecules and metal-containing host
molecules can be used to prepare single-atom sites with tun-
able local coordination structures, which is worth exploring
further in the precise design of SAzymes.[144] Additionally, the
exploration of small molecule coordination and cofactor coor-
dination strategies, such as Aptamers, DNA, and antibodies,
could be valuable in constructing SAzymes.[145]
2) Suitable supports selection of SAzymes: Recently, new
“single-atom gel” catalysts are expected to gain signiﬁ-
cant attention in the next few years, due to their three-
dimensional (3D) atomic network structure and atomic-scale
pore structure.[146] And, the diversity of MOF species and the
unique advantages of 2D/3D materials in terms of surface
area as well as electron transfer have opened up the possibility
of designing more compliant single-atom systems.
3) From SAzymes to DA/MAzymes: In recent years, SAzymes
have demonstrated great potential in biological applications.
And, the emergence of DA/MAzymes, which possess mul-
tiple metal centers and exhibit enhanced performance due
to synergistic eﬀects, has garnered attention. The advan-
tages conferred by multiple metal active centers may also
enable to play an important role in enzyme-catalyzed cas-
cade reactions. Therefore, the simulation of multiple en-
zyme activities from SAzymes to DA/MAzymes (homoge-
neous atoms or adjacent heterogeneous atoms) is one of the
promising directions for the better application of SACs in
biocatalysis in the future. In addition, high-entropy SACs,
which refer to the controllable anchoring of multiple iso-
lated metal atoms into a single carrier, have been reported
in electrocatalysis, and[147] the applicability of having multi-
ple isolated metal atoms in biocatalysis is worth being further
explored.
4) Development of in situ characterization techniques: Although
the catalytic mechanisms of most SAzymes are based on well-
deﬁned active sites and structures, the speciﬁc catalytic mech-
anisms are still unclear, and more advanced characterization
tools and powerful theoretical calculations to deeply reveal
the fundamentals and mechanisms of action of SAzymes are
also a problem to be solved in the future. As we know, the in-
situ characterization techniques of SACs have gradually ma-
tured in electrocatalytic applications, and similarly, the in-situ

5) Combination of SAzymes and Artiﬁcial Intelligence: So far,
the selection of metal active centers and the optimization of
coordination environments in SAzymes have mainly relied on
trial-and-error strategies, which consume a lot of time and
resources in the lab on trial-and-error strategies. With the
rapid development of artiﬁcial intelligence, machine learning-
assisted high-throughput screening can expedite the evalu-
ation of material properties, and screen out some potential
high-performance materials before experimental trials, which
provides a reference point for further experiments later.[148]
The combination of machine learning and unique simulation
structures will facilitate the eﬃcient design of SAzyms with
speciﬁc properties. In addition, machine learning can also
further simulate their catalytic processes and gain deeper in-
sights into the catalytic mechanism, which aids in the unique
and irreplaceable role of SAzymes in the biological ﬁeld.
6) Rationally designed target SAzymes. SAzymes will inevitably
cause damage to normal cells while acting on tumor cells,
thus it is essential to rationally design target SAzymes to tu-
mor cells with satisfactory activity, speciﬁcity and selectivity
while reducing their damage to normal cells. In addition to
improving the targeting ability of SAzymes, novel SAzymes
with desirable properties should be further explored. Most
of the currently available SAzymes targeted mimetic oxidore-
ductases. Other types of SAzyme such as hydrolases, trans-
ferase, lyase, isomerase and ligase mimics can be considered
as new potential members of the SAzyme family.
7) Further expanding the applications of SAzymes. The single-
atom strategy provides a rational new way to ﬁnd highly ac-
tive and selective nano enzymes, although the biomedical
applications of SACs are still in infancy. To date, SAzymes
are mainly applied in the ﬁelds of tumor therapy, antibacte-
rial, and biosensing, speciﬁcally, for tumor therapy, coupling
SAzymes with a multiplatform approach can form an ideal
system for more eﬀective therapeutic performance. RON is
a product of NO-ROS interaction in organisms, which can
synergistically promote each other and is expected to reduce
its drug dose, bringing new opportunities for tumor therapy.
For biosensing, the design of more novel sensor structures
(e.g., in combination with microﬂuidics) is essential, and in-
expensive, small, portable, and easy-to-operate sensing de-
vices will be a key research hotspot for SAzymes in practi-
cal sensing applications. Additionally, there are few reports
on anti-inﬂammatory, diagnostic imaging, immunotherapy,
combined therapy neurodegenerative diseases, etc., which
can further increase research eﬀorts in these areas. Last but
not least, how to rationally utilize the properties of metals
and support materials to develop multifunctional materials by
integrating their respective properties is worth considering.
For example, how to design smart SAzymes that can selec-
tively generate ROS (cancer therapy) or scavenge ROS (anti-
inﬂammatory therapy) according to diﬀerent types of diseases
needs to be further investigated.
8) Clinical application of SAzymes. To facilitate the practical ap-
plication of SAzymes, eﬀorts must be made to scale up the
synthesis of SAzymes with high eﬃciency and reproducibil-
ity. The development of scalable synthesis methods and the
optimization of reaction conditions will be critical to meet
the demand for large-scale production. The cost-eﬀectiveness
and stability of SAzymes must be addressed to ensure their
commercial viability. Additionally, the pharmacokinetic and
toxicological studies of SAzymes are only at the level of sur-
face activity and application eﬀect, which is not conducive
to the comprehensive evaluation and clinical translation of
SAzymes, so researchers should conduct deeper studies to
emphasize the “all behaviors” of SAzymes. And, biosafety is
also an important issue to be considered in clinical transla-
tion, and improving the biocompatibility and biodegradabil-
ity of the materials will be the focus of future breakthroughs.
Currently, the research on SAzymes is still in the laboratory
stage, and there is a long way to go to overcome all the diﬃ-
culties and promote the results of the experimental results to
the clinical translation and application.
In conclusion, SAzymes have achieved initial success in the
ﬁeld of biocatalysis, but both opportunities and challenges still
exist. A family of SAzymes with well-designed geometries and
electronic space arrangements, combined with novel in situ char-
acterization techniques, theoretical studies, and artiﬁcial intelli-
gence techniques, will provide insights into the basic research
and future clinical applications of SAzymes for biological ﬁelds.
This will require the joint eﬀorts of multiple ﬁelds, including
chemistry, materials, biomedicine, and computing, and we look
forward to more exciting research progress.
