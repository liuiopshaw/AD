Ultrasmall metal nanoclusters (NCs) are promising in various photocatalytic applications, but usually suﬀer
from fast recombination of charge carriers, insuﬃcient active sites, and diﬃcult diﬀusion of substrates onto
active sites. Herein, we report the design of a Au NC-based photocatalyst for photocatalytic hydrogen
peroxide (H2O2) production based on the ligand engineering of Au NCs with porphyrin cobalt (Co-
TCPP). Besides enhancing the visible light absorption of Au NCs, the grafted Co-TCPP molecules on the
Au NC surface could serve as electron acceptors, which not only eﬃciently promote charge carrier
separation of Au NCs, but also provide abundant active sites for photocatalysis. On this basis, the as-
designed Au NC-based photocatalyst showed excellent photocatalytic performance in H2O2 production
(up to 235.93 mM), outperforming most previously reported semiconductor photocatalysts. In addition,
the mechanistic study revealed that the photocatalytic H2O2 production over the Au NC-based
photocatalyst mainly follows a two-electron O2 reduction pathway. This study is attractive as it
demonstrated the design of the high-performance Au NC-based photocatalyst via ligand engineering,
increasing the acceptance of metal NCs for photocatalytic applications.
1
Introduction
Photocatalysis as a green technology is extremely attractive in
energy conversion, environmental remediation, and organic
synthesis, and its progress is highly dependent on the devel-
opment of advanced photocatalysts.1–3 Among various photo-
catalysts, ultrasmall thiolated metal nanoclusters (NCs, <3 nm
in size) have shown promising potential in diversied photo-
catalytic applications due to their intriguing molecule-like
properties such as strong luminescence,4–10 rich yet tailorable
surface chemistry,11–15 and intriguing HOMO–LUMO transitions
(i.e., highest occupied molecular orbital-lowest unoccupied
molecular orbital),16–19 which fuels the research enthusiasm in
the design of high-eﬃciency metal NC-based photocatalysts.20–28
However, further deployment of these metal NCs as photo-
catalysts is usually curtailed by the following aspects: (1) the fast
recombination of charge carriers due to narrow HOMO–LUMO
transitions (around 1.5 eV);29,30 (2) insuﬃcient active sites on the
surface of metal NCs due to the capping of thiolate ligands;31–33
(3) diﬃcult diﬀusion of substrates across the ligand shell for
subsequent photocatalytic reactions on the surface active sites
of metal NCs due to the bulky steric hindrance of ligands.34–36
How to address these limitations of metal NC-based photo-
catalysts? By deciphering the metal core–ligand shell structure
of metal NCs, we believe that it is feasible to tackle this chal-
lenge via ligand engineering rather than conventional metallic
core modulation.37–43
On this basis, we hypothesized that if we can graa multi-
functional molecule on the surface ligands of metal NCs to turn
the “demerits” of thiolated metal NCs into “merits” by utilizing
the multifunctional molecules for promoting the separation of
charge carriers, providing additional active sites, and avoiding
long diﬀusion pathways of substrates for photocatalytic reac-
tions (i.e., the three-in-one function of the multifunctional
molecule), we may develop a high-performance metal NC-based
photocatalyst with enhanced photocatalytic activities. There-
fore, hunting for an appropriate multifunctional molecule
becomes the key to developing high-performance metal NC-
based photocatalysts via ligand engineering. With the afore-
mentioned three-in-one function criteria for choosing the suit-
able multifunctional molecule, metalloporphyrin molecules are
considered as good candidates in this regard.44,45 It is well
documented that metalloporphyrin molecules are one class of
macrocyclic compounds consisting of four pyrrolic rings

2
Results and discussion
2.1
Synthesis and characterization of Au-Co-TCPP
In this study, highly luminescent GSH-protected Au NCs were
selected as the NC model of the NC-based photocatalyst, and
were synthesized by a simple one-pot strategy (see details in the
ESI†). As shown in Fig. 1a, the as-obtained solution of Au NCs
displays a yellowish color (item 1 in the inset) with a shoulder
absorption band at 400 nm extending up to 530 nm in the UV-
visible absorption spectrum (solid black curve), which indicates
the successful formation of ultrasmall Au NCs, excluding the
possibility of forming large-sized Au nanoparticles (>3 nm, with
a typical surface plasmon resonance absorption at 520 nm).
Interestingly, the Au NCs could emit intense orange lumines-
cence (item 2 in the inset of Fig. 1a) with an emission peak of
615 nm when excited at 412 nm (dashed curves of Fig. 1a). The
luminescence quantum yield of this Au NC sample was deter-
mined to be 12.4%, which is several orders of magnitude higher
than that of many other Au NCs (typically 0.001–0.1%). Such
a high quantum yield of Au NCs might be benecial to improve
the photocatalytic activity because of the ease in generating
photoinduced charge carriers. Moreover, according to previous
investigations, this type of Au NC features aggregation-induced
emission (AIE) attribute with a relatively long-lived lumines-
cence lifetime up to several microseconds (ca. 5 ms),49,50 which
may provide enough time to guarantee the transfer of photo-
excited electrons. Meanwhile, as described above, another
advantage of the Au NCs is their large Stokes shi(>200 nm),
which implies that their luminescence is generally originated
from the relaxation of ligand-to-metal charge transfer (LMCT),51
largely reducing the possibility of luminescence self-quenching.
In addition, the size of Au NCs was further analyzed by
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and electrospray
ionization mass spectrometry (ESI-MS). As shown in Fig. 1b, the
TEM image reveals that all particles are below 2 nm in size,
further corroborating the formation of ultrasmall Au NCs. The
ESI-MS result provides more precise size information, and
suggests that the size of the obtained Au NCs ranges from
Au29(GSH)27 to Au35(GSH)28 (Fig. S1†). It should be mentioned
that the high GSH-to-Au ratio of the Au NCs based on their
molecular formula could ensure their rich yet tailorable surface
chemistry (e.g., one GSH carries one –NH2 and two –COOH
groups), which made chemical graing of Co-TCPP molecules
on the NC surface possible.

2.2
Application of Au-Co-TCPP for H2O2 production
Aer ligand engineering with Co-TCPP, the obtained Au NC-
based photocatalyst (i.e., Au-Co-TCPP) exhibits excellent pho-
tocatalytic performance in H2O2 production under visible light
irradiation (l $ 400 nm; a Xe lamp with 100 mW cm2 power
density) in the absence of any sacricial agent. Of note, the
H2O2 concentration in the photocatalytic system was quantied
using a quantitative peroxide assay kit (see more details in the
Experimental section and Fig. S4†). Prior to the photocatalytic
experiment, the pH of the photocatalytic system was rst opti-
mized because pH could severely inuence the formation of
H2O2 involving a proton-coupled electron transfer process. As
shown in Fig. 2a, the H2O2 concentration gradually increases
with decreasing the pH values and reaches the maximum when
pH ¼ 3. Further decreasing the pH value to 2 would lead to the
decline of the H2O2 concentration, which might be due to the
decomposition of H2O2 by excess H+ (Fig. S5†). As the original
pH of the Au-Co-TCPP solution is around 3.3 due to the
deprotonation of –COOH groups, the pH value of the photo-
catalytic system was set at 3.3 for convenience. Fig. 2b shows the
time-dependent
H2O2
production
over
diﬀerent
samples
including pristine Au NCs, Co-TCPP, Au NCs + Co-TCPP and Au-
Co-TCPP. As shown, while pristine Co-TCPP molecules are
inactive in photocatalytic H2O2 generation, the developed Au-
Co-TCPP photocatalyst shows a H2O2 production capability of
235.93 mM within 60 min, which is 2.2 times higher than that of
the pristine Au NCs (e.g., 107.11 mM). However, it should be
emphasized that Au-Co-TCPP has a better photocatalytic H2O2
production than the sample of blended Au NCs + Co-TCPP
although they share the same amounts of Co-TCPP, which is
attributed to the formation of chemical bonding (–CO–NH–)
between the Au NCs and Co-TCPP that promotes the charge
transfer between them. In addition to Co-TCPP, Ni-TCPP and
Cu-TCPP could also improve the photocatalytic activity if
chemically graed on the surface of Au NCs (Fig. S6†). Such
excellent photocatalytic performance of the metalloporphyrin
graed Au NCs strongly supports our design concept in the
metal
NC-based
photocatalysts
via
ligand
engineering.
Furthermore, the turn-over frequency (TOF) of Au-Co-TCPP for
photocatalytic H2O2 can be up to 3.33 h1. In addition, this Au-
Co-TCPP photocatalyst also performs better in H2O2 production
than most traditional semiconductor photocatalysts (Table
S1†), which exemplies the huge potential of metal NCs in
photocatalytic applications.
Considering that the formed H2O2 could be directly dis-
proportionated into O2 and H2O (2H2O2 / 2H2O + O2) or
decomposed into other species especially in the presence of
metal catalysts, the decomposition behavior of H2O2 in the
presence of diﬀerent photocatalysts under visible light illumi-
nation was investigated at an initial H2O2 concentration of 500
mM (Fig. 2c).53 Surprisingly, the decomposition rates of H2O2
over Au-Co-TCPP and pristine Au NCs are merely 1.34% and
2.59%, respectively, which are much lower than the reported
values over traditional semiconductor photocatalysts (e.g.,
$10%).54–56 Such low decomposition rates of both Au-Co-TCPP
and Au NCs for H2O2 are probably due to the adequate
capping of the Au core by relatively bulky GSH ligands that
largely reduce the encounter chance of the Au core of NCs with
H2O2 molecules. Furthermore, the morphology of Au-Co-TCPP
aer 1 h photocatalytic reaction was characterized by TEM
(Fig. S7†), conrming that Au-Co-TCPP has no remarkable size
variation aer the photocatalytic test. Furthermore, the size of
Au-Co-TCPP aer the 1 h photocatalytic test was analyzed by
TEM, and no signicant size variation is observed. In addition,
the long-term photocatalytic H2O2 production (10 h) over Au-Co-
TCPP was also performed. As displayed in Fig. S8,† while the
H2O2 production over Au-Co-TCPP could up to 295.11 mM aer

2.3
Mechanism of Au-Co-TCPP photocatalytic production of
H2O2
One interesting issue is why Au-Co-TCPP has excellent photo-
catalytic activity, and thus the roles of the graed Co-TCPP
molecules in photocatalytic H2O2 production were investigated.
In this regard, the optical absorption of Au-Co-TCPP was rst
measured by UV-visible diﬀuse reectance spectroscopy (DRS)
since the outstanding light-harvesting ability is a prerequisite of
high-eﬃciency photocatalysis. As shown in Fig. S9a,† the graing
of Co-TCPP on Au NCs dramatically intensies the visible light-
harvesting ability of Au-Co-TCPP, achieving a broader yet
stronger light absorption that covers the entire visible light region
from 400 to 780 nm. Furthermore, the graing of Co-TCPP on Au
NCs can promote the photoinduced charge carrier separation,
which is another crucial factor in governing the photocatalytic
activity. As shown in Fig. 3a, an apparent quenching eﬀect on the
photoluminescence (PL) of Au NCs could be observed aer
graing Co-TCPP molecules. Of note, the PL quenching eﬀect of
samples becomes more obvious when introducing more Co-TCPP
molecules, and reaches the maximum when 600 mg Co-TCPP is
used. Actually, the PL quenching capability of Co-TCPP on Au
NCs reects the separation ability for photoinduced charge
carriers from Au NCs to Co-TCPP, which is supported by the fact
that the PL quenching results of these Au-Co-TCPP samples are
consistent with their photocatalytic activities, i.e., graing 600 mg
Co-TCPP onto Au NCs achieves the best photocatalytic H2O2
production performance (Fig. S9b†).
In addition to improving the light harvesting and promoting
the charge carrier separation, the graed Co-TCPP molecules on
the surface of Au NCs could provide abundant metal active sites
for photocatalytic reactions. As shown in Fig. 3b, the electron
spin resonance (ESR) spectrum of Au-Co-TCPP shows a strong
signal
at
the
g-value
of
2.009
under
dark
conditions,
corresponding to the high-spin state of Co2+ in Au-Co-TCPP.57
By contrast, the intensity of this signal was decreased aer light
illumination for 15 min, which should be attributed to the
reduction of Co2+ by photoexcited electrons.58 Therefore, the
Co2+ species of the graed Co-TCPP are supposed to be active
sites which accept electrons to promote photocatalytic H2O2
production. On this basis, we may conclude that graing Co-
TCPP on Au NCs could enhance light harvesting, prompt
charge carrier separation, and oﬀer abundant surface active
sites, which are responsible for the improved photocatalytic
H2O2 production performance of the Au-Co-TCPP photocatalyst.
In addition, the Co2p XPS peak of Au-Co-TCPP shis toward the
binding energy side aer the photocatalytic reaction (Fig. S10†),
indicating reduction of Co2+ by photoexcited e.

(e) (see eqn (1)–(3)) and water oxidation by leaving holes (h+)53

To further verify the route of photocatalytic H2O2 produc-
tion, several control experiments were carried out under
diﬀerent atmospheric conditions (i.e., O2, and Ar). As shown in
Fig. 4b, Au-Co-TCPP shows excellent photocatalytic H2O2
production performance under O2-equilibrated conditions, but
is almost inert for H2O2 production (13.04 mM) under Ar-
equilibrated conditions, suggesting that O2 reduction is the
main route for H2O2 production in this photocatalytic system.
This speculation can be further supported by the time-resolved
transient luminescence decay proles of Au-Co-TCPP under
diﬀerent atmospheres (i.e., O2, Ar and air), where Au-Co-TCPP
displays the shortest average luminescence lifetime (save) in
an O2 atmosphere (Fig. 4c and Table S2†) because of the fast
transfer of photoinduced electrons to O2 molecules. Meanwhile,
it was found that the luminescent Au NCs are more favorable to
the photocatalytic H2O2 production than the non-luminescent
ones, which was evidenced by the low H2O2 generation over
the non-luminescent Co-TCPP graed Au25(SG)18 NCs (Au25-Co-
TCPP, Fig. S13†). This can be attributed to the easier generation
of charge carriers of luminescent Au NCs under visible light
irradiation for H2O2 production.
While O2 reduction is identied as the major route for
photocatalytic H2O2 formation in this system, a fundamental
question that may further arise is whether H2O2 was formed via
the sequential one-electron O2 reduction (eqn (1) and (2)) or the
two-electron O2 reduction (eqn (3)). To clarify this issue, we
introduced a typical scavenger of O2$, 2,3-bis(2-methoxy-4-nitro-

3
Conclusions
In summary, we have successfully developed an eﬃcient metal
NC-based photocatalyst (Au-Co-TCPP) by chemically graing
Co-TCPP with the ligands of Au NCs via a simple EDC/NHS
crosslinking strategy. By leveraging the three-in-one function
of Co-TCPP molecules, i.e., enhancing visible light absorption,
promoting the separation of charge carriers, and providing
surface active sites for O2 reduction with high selectivity toward
the two-electron route, the as-developed molecular Au-Co-TCPP
photocatalyst exhibited excellent capability in H2O2 production
with a yield of up to 235.93 mM. Au-Co-TCPP performed better in
photocatalytic H2O2 production than most previously reported
semiconductor photocatalysts, which will increase the accep-
tance of metal NCs as novel photocatalysts for photocatalytic
applications. We believe that this study could be very interesting
because it not only provides a paradigm for the design of high-
performance metal NC-based photocatalysts via ingenious
ligand engineering, but also contributes to the deeper under-
standing on the photocatalytic mechanism of H2O2 production.
