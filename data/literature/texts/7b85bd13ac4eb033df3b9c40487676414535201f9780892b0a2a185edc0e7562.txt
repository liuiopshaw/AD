Protection of cognitive decline and Alzheimer’s disease
progression by a human origin-probiotic biotherapy

Abstract
Background: Chronic inflammation is a key risk factor for age-related progression of
cognitive decline and Alzheimer’s disease (AD); however, its etiology remains elusive.
Multiple emerging evidence indicates that abnormalities in the gut microbiome (dys-
biosis) contribute to the pathology of cognitive decline and Alzheimer’s disease, by
elevating gut permeability (leaky gut) which in turn instigates inflammation. However,
remedies to mitigate cognitive decline and AD progression are not well known.
Method: We examined the effects of human origin probiotics (containing newly
isolated probiotics from infant’s gut) in APP/PS1 mice, with and without leaky gut.
Result: : We observed that probiotic feeding significantly reduced the burden of
AD markers like amyloid-beta accumulation, inflammation in the brain, along with a
lesser cognitive decline in treated mice. These improvements were linked with bene-
ficial changes in the microbiome, increased production of beneficial metabolites, and
reduced leakiness in the gut.
Conclusion: Our results demonstrated that a human origin probiotic cocktail reduced
the progression of age-related cognitive decline and AD by reducing abnormalities
in the microbiome-metabolites-leaky gut axis. Thus, microbial biotherapies like pro-
biotics ameliorating leaky gut can be beneficial in protecting cognitive decline and
AD.
