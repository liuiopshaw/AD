ABSTRACT 
In this study, a remote-control strategy for tuning the electronic structures of atomically precise Cu nanoclusters (Cu NCs) 
was developed, aiming to enhance the selectivity of the CO2 RR toward high-value hydrocarbons. Two Cu7 NCs protected by 
tridentate N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands with distinct functional groups on the remote benzimidazole ring, NHCH -Cu7 
and NHCMe -Cu7 , were synthesized via a green and efficient ball-milling approach. The main product obtained with NHCMe - 
Cu7 is CO, with a selectivity of 82.8%, whereas the Faradaic efficiency (FE) for hydrocarbon production using NHCH -Cu7 is 
74.0%, affording 50.4% CH4 and 23.6% C2 H4 at − 1.5 V versus the reversible hydrogen electrode (RHE). Comprehensive theoretical 
calculations and operando spectroscopic analyses demonstrate that the effect of the remote substituent on the NHC ligand provides 
a greater population of active electrons in the highest occupied d-orbital of NHCH -Cu7 compared to that in NHCMe -Cu7 , thus 
effectively regulating the binding strength of the *CO intermediate. This in turn promotes stabilization and further activation of 
the intermediate, ultimately steering the product selectivity from CO toward targeted hydrocarbons. This study establishes an 
atomic-level paradigm for ligand engineering of Cu NCs, enabling remote tuning of the electronic structure of the active sites to 
realize highly selective hydrocarbon generation.

Introduction 
Electrochemical CO2 reduction (CO2 RR) driven by renewable 
electricity offers a promising approach for upcycling CO2 into 
high-value chemicals and fuels [ 1–4 ]. Compared to the products 
(e.g., CO and HCOOH) of the relatively simple two-electron 
transfer reaction, deeply reduced hydrocarbon products such 
as CH4 and C2 H4 with higher energy density are more diffi- 
cult to produce, despite their great significance [ 5 ]. Recently, 
various Cu-based materials, including metallic Cu, Cu oxides, 
Cu-containing molecules, Cu single-atom catalysts, and Cu- 
based framework materials, were demonstrated as privileged 
catalysts for converting CO2 into hydrocarbons [ 6–15 ]. Nev- 
ertheless, simultaneously achieving high activity and selectiv- 
ity in hydrocarbon formation is a persistent challenge owing 
to the multiple-proton-coupled electron transfer process (i.e., 
CO2 →*CO→*CHO→hydrocarbons) [ 6, 16 ]. To overcome this 
limitation, various strategies for modulating the electronic

reduction to hydrocarbons, the corresponding structure-activity 
relationship remains unclear owing to the ambiguous composi- 
tion and structural reconstruction of Cu catalysts under CO2 RR 
conditions [ 20, 21 ]. 
Atomically precise Cu nanoclusters (NCs) featuring well-defined 
surface ligand and core have been established as an ideal platform 
for exploring the structure-activity relationship governing the 
CO2 RR [ 22–30 ]. The electronic structure of the Cu cores can 
be precisely tailored at the atomic level via ligand engineering, 
enabling direct regulation of the catalytic performance [ 22, 
23 , 31–33 ]. Recent studies demonstrated that manipulating the 
first/secondary coordination shells of Cu NCs via p-d orbital 
hybridization with the Cu center or short-range electron delocal- 
ization has a promotional effect on the CO2 RR [ 31 ], as exemplified 
by thiolate-protected Cu6 (MBD)6 [ 27 ], alkynyl-protected Ag9 Cu6 - 
Fc [ 34 ], phosphine-protected Cu6 Mo2 -R [ 35 ], and so forth. More- 
over, regulation of the first/secondary coordination shells has a 
non-negligible influence on the stability of the Cu NCs. Therefore, 
developing a remote-control strategy to strike a balance between 
the efficient tuning of electronic structure and the stability of Cu 
NCs is highly desirable [ 22, 36 ]. 
N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands have strong σ-donating 
and moderate π-accepting capabilities, positioning them as great 
candidates for stabilizing metal NCs during electrocatalytic pro- 
cesses such as the CO2 RR [ 19, 37–41 ]. The electronic and steric 
properties of NHC ligands can be precisely tailored by subtle 
adjustment and targeted functionalization of the nitrogen sub- 
stituents and imidazolium ring, thus modulating the electronic 
structure and reactivity of the coordinated metal centers [ 42–45 ]. 
Moreover, the π-conjugated backbone of NHC ligands enables 
remote modulation of the electronic structure of NHC-stabilized 
NCs while avoiding blocking the active sites and the ineffective 
electronic modulation associated with alkyl linkers, encountered 
in conventional ligand design [ 36, 46 , 47 ]. These features provide 
great potential for developing Cu NCs for achieving highly 
efficient CO2 RR, ultimately generating value-added products and 
providing insight into the reaction mechanism. 
Herein, a remote-control strategy was developed for realizing a 
switch in the CO2 electroreduction product from CO to higher- 
value hydrocarbons through precise modulation of the electronic 
structure of NHC-stabilized Cu NCs. Through the tailored syn- 
thesis of NHC ligands distinguished by the -H and -CH3 groups 
on the remote benzimidazole ring, two stable Cu7 NCs, NHCH - 
Cu7 and NHCMe - Cu7 , were obtained via a green ball-milling 
approach. NHCMe -Cu7 showed predominant CO selectivity in 
the CO2 electroreduction. Despite subtle differences in the remote 
-H group, NHCH -Cu7 exhibited exceptional FE toward hydro- 
carbons (50.4% CH4 and 23.6% C2 H4 ) at − 1.5 V versus RHE (all 
relevant data herein are referenced to the RHE) with a high partial 
current density of 202.2 mA ⋅cm− 2 for hydrocarbons. Operando 
spectroscopic analyses confirmed that the stronger binding of 
the *CO intermediate on NHCH -Cu7 impedes desorption and 
promotes further hydrogenation. Systematic density functional 
theory (DFT) analyses reveal that the -H group in the NHC 
ligands elevates the 3dx

and significantly decreases the free-energy barrier for *CHO

2.1 
Synthesis and Characterization 
Two pincer-type NHC precursors, 1,3-bis-(1-benzyl-1 H -1,2, 
3-triazol-4-yl)methyl)-1H-benzo[ d ]imidazol-3-ium 
hexafluoro- 
phosphate (H(taz)2 NHCH ⋅PF6 ) and 1,3-bis-(1-benzyl-1 H -1,2,3- 
triazol-4-yl)methyl)-5,6-dimethyl-1H-benzo[ d ] imidazole-3-ium 
hexafluorophosphate (H(taz)2 NHCMe ⋅PF6 ) were designed and 
synthesized through the Cu(I)-catalyzed click reaction (see the 
details in Scheme S1 and Figures S1 ‒S6 ). These precursors were 
then reacted with phenylacetylene (PhC ≡ CH), copper acetate, 
and copper powder through a one-pot ball-milling process, 
yielding NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 with distinct functional 
groups on the NHC ligands (Scheme 1 ). 
Single crystals of NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 suitable for 
single-crystal x-ray diffraction (SCXRD) were obtained by slowly 
diffusing isopropyl ether into a mixed CH2 Cl2 /MeOH solution 
of the clusters. The SCXRD analysis reveals that NHCH -Cu7 
crystallizes in the triclinic space group P 1, whereas NHCMe -Cu7 
crystallizes in the monoclinic crystal system with the C 2/c space 
group (Table S1 ). Both NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 consist of a 
Cu7 core, three NHC ligands, four PhC ≡ C ˉ ligands, and three PF6 ˉ
counter anions [ 48 ]. The charge state and the precise molecular 
composition of both clusters were further determined using 
high-resolution electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (HR- 
ESI-MS). The ESI-MS data presented in Figures S7 and S8 show 
dominant peaks at m/z = 1188.1351 and 1230.1814, which match 
well with the simulated data for [Cu7 (NHCH )3 (PhC ≡ C)4 (PF6 )]2 + 
(calculated: 1188.1326) and [Cu7 (NHCMe )3 (PhC ≡ C)4 (PF6 )]2 + (cal- 
culated: 1230.1794), respectively. 
The two Cu clusters can be considered as pseudo-isologues owing 
to their identical Cu atom counts and ligand arrangements. 
The Cu7 core of NHCH -Cu7 exhibits an hourglass-shaped geo- 
metric configuration composed of two vertex-sharing tetrahedra 
(Figure 1a ). The average Cu–Cu distance of 2.5155 Å between the 
central Cu atom (shown in yellow) and basal Cu atoms (shown in 
pink) indicates a strong cuprophilic interaction (Table S2 ). 
Furthermore, the two tetrahedra units are interlocked in a 
staggered configuration through relatively weaker Cu–Cu inter- 
actions, with bond lengths of 2.7957 (27) and 2.8494 (27) Å. The

3.6580 (34) to 3.8565(39) Å, resulting in “open-cube” structures, 
as illustrated in Figure 1b . All Cu atoms are four-coordinated.

The core-shell coordination pattern of NHCMe -Cu7 is similar to 
that of NHCH -Cu7 but is more distorted owing to the substitution 
of the -CH3 group in NHC ligands (Table S3 ). A relatively stronger 
cuprophilic interaction was observed in NHCMe -Cu7 because the 
Cu ─Cu bond lengths and interior angles are less than those in 
NHCH -Cu7 (Figure 1d,e ). Furthermore, the Cu ─Ccarbene bond in 
NHCMe -Cu7 is longer than that in NHCH -Cu7 (Figure 1f ). These 
results indicate that subtle variations in the NHC ligand can 
influence the structure of the Cu cluster, thereby potentially mod- 
ulating its electronic properties. Space-filling models reveal that 
both clusters feature three exposed copper centers, which could 
serve as active sites during the electrocatalytic CO2 RR process 
(Figure S10 ) [ 27 ]. Moreover, the packing diagrams of NHCH -Cu7 
and NHCMe -Cu7 show strong intermolecular C–H ⋅⋅⋅π and π⋅⋅⋅π
interactions between the protecting ligands (Figures S11 and S12 ). 
The x-ray powder diffraction (PXRD) patterns (Figure S13 ) of 
these clusters match well with those simulated from single-crystal 
diffraction data, indicating that the bulk samples are phase-pure 
and highly crystalline. 
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and x-ray absorption fine 
spectroscopy (XAFS) analyses reveal that the electronic state of 
the Cu in NHCH - Cu7 and NHCMe - Cu7 is significantly influenced 
by the subtle variations in the NHC ligand. The high-resolution 
Cu 2p spectrum of NHCH -Cu7 (Figure 2a ) shows two binding 
energy (BE) peaks at 952.8 (Cu 2p1/2 ) and 932.9 (Cu 2p3/2 ) eV, 
accompanied by a Cu LMM Auger peak at ∼ 915.6 eV (Figure 
S14 ), indicating that the Cu species are in the monovalent state, 
in agreement with the SCXRD analyses. As illustrated in Figure 
S15 , the N 1s binding energies of both clusters are negatively 
shifted relative to their imidazolium salt precursors, consistent 
with electron back-donation from occupied Cu orbitals into 
the π* system of the NHC ligands. Notably, this shift is more 
pronounced for NHCH -Cu7 ( − 0.47 eV) than for NHCMe -Cu7 
( − 0.21 eV), indicating stronger back donation in the H-modified 
system. Further support is provided by the Cu 2p spectra, which 
display a distinct positive shift for NHCH -Cu7 (Figure 2a ) [ 47, 49 ]. 
The Cu K-edge x-ray absorption near-edge structure (XANES) 
spectra (Figure 2b ) of both clusters exhibit a distinct Cu 1s →4p 
transition at 8980.0 eV, indicative of Cu(I) species, whereas the 
absence of 1s →3d transitions confirms the lack of Cu(II) centers. 
Furthermore, the absorption edges of these clusters are located 
between those of Cu foil and CuO, aligning more closely with 
Cu2 O reference, collectively demonstrating the presence of Cu(I) 
species [ 49, 50 ]. Notably, the negative shift in the absorption edge 
for NHCMe -Cu7 clearly reflects a decrease in the Cu oxidation 
state, corroborating the XPS results. Consequently, fine-tuning 
the functional groups on NHC ligands offers a viable strategy for 
remotely modulating the electronic state of both Cu NCs. The 
Fourier-transform extended x-ray absorption fine structure (FT- 
EXAFS) spectrum of NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 shows two 
peaks at 1.58 and 2.32 Å (prior to phase correction), assigned to the 
Cu-N/C and Cu-Cu coordination shells, respectively (Figure 2c ) 
[ 50 ]. The local coordination configurations of NHCH -Cu7 and 
NHCMe -Cu7 were further investigated by fitting the EXAFS 
curves. The best-fit results for both samples (Figure S16 and Table 
S4 ) include three backscattering paths: (1) Cu-N, (2) Cu-C, and 
(3) Cu-Cu. The coordination numbers of N and C around Cu are 
estimated to be 0.9 and 3.1, respectively, which are consistent with 
the coordination geometries of NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 
determined from the SCXRD analyses. The Fourier-transform 
infrared (FT-IR) spectra of the NHC precursors and the as- 
synthesized clusters (Figure S17 ) also support the conclusions 
drawn above. 
Comparison of the photophysical properties of NHCH -Cu7 and 
NHCMe -Cu7 (Figure 2d ) indicates that upon substituting -H with 
-CH3 in the NHC ligand, the absorption peaks at 292 and 362 nm 
for NHCH -Cu7 were shifted to 303 and 368 nm for NHCMe - 
Cu7 , respectively. The red shift of the peaks further confirms 
that the effect of the electron-rich -CH3 groups on the Cu species 
[ 51 ]. Time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) calcu- 
lations were carried out to gain deeper insight into the electronic 
structures of these clusters. The simulated absorption spectra 
show reasonable agreement with the experimental data (Figures 
S18 and S19 ). The energy gaps between the highest occupied 
molecular orbital and the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital for 
NHCH - Cu7 and NHCMe - Cu7 are 4.35 and 4.08 eV, respectively 
[ 32 ]. These results suggest that the variations in the electronic 
states of the Cu of both clusters coincide with the modulation 
of the substituents in the NHC ligand, as supported by XPS and 
XAFS analyses. Additionally, the time-dependent UV/vis spectra 
confirm that NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 remained stable for 
over 24 h (Figure S20 ). Thermogravimetric analysis (TG) further 
demonstrates the remarkable thermal stability of these species up 
to 250◦C (Figure S21 ), guaranteeing their structural robustness for 
electrocatalytic CO2 RR. 
2.2 
Electrochemical CO2 Reduction 
To evaluate the remote effect of the substituent of the NHC ligand 
on the CO2 RR performance of NHCH - Cu7 and NHCMe - Cu7 , 
electrocatalytic tests were conducted in a three-compartment 
flow cell. Linear sweep voltammetry (LSV) analysis of both 
catalysts revealed higher cathodic current densities under flowing 
CO2 conditions compared to those in an Ar-saturated electrolyte, 
indicating effective electrocatalytic CO2 reduction by both cata- 
lysts (Figure 3a ). Notably, NHCH -Cu7 exhibited higher current 
densities in the CO2 -saturated medium, reaching a maximum 
current density of 364 mA cm− 2 at − 1.6 V, highlighting its superior 
electrocatalytic performance for CO2 reduction. Furthermore, the 
absence of distinct reduction peaks in the LSV and CV profiles 
(Figure S22 ) indicates the high stability of the Cu(I) species in 
both clusters during the CO2 RR. 
The selectivity of the CO2 RR was quantified through periodic 
analysis of the gaseous and liquid products using online gas 
chromatography and 1 H NMR, respectively. With both catalysts, 
the only gaseous products were CO, C2 H4 , CH4 , and H2 , and 
no liquid products were detected (Figure S23 ). The FE for all 
products is summarized in Figure 3b,c . The reaction catalyzed 
by NHCMe -Cu7 produced CO as the major product, where FECO

To further investigate the variations in the chemical state and 
structural stability of the catalysts under realistic CO2 electrore- 
duction conditions, NHCH -Cu7 was analyzed using in situ XAFS 
(Figure S35 and Figure 4a ). Initially, in the CO2 -saturated 0.5 M 
KHCO3 mixture solution under open-circuit voltage (OCP), the 
Cu K-edge exhibited a distinct positive shift compared to that of 
the pristine sample, indicating charge transfer from Cu 3d to the 
carbon 2p orbital of CO2 and spontaneous chemical adsorption 
at the active Cu sites [ 53 ]. When the potential was tuned from –
0.9 to –1.6 V, the edge progressively shifted toward lower energies. 
Upon applying a potential of –1.2 V, the absorption edge returned 
to the high-energy side, revealing the reversible redox behavior 
of Cu species under potential bias. The EXAFS k1 χ(k) functions 
displayed distinct oscillation frequencies compared to those of 
pristine NHCH -Cu7 , further confirming the rapid adsorption 
of CO2 at the Cu sites (Figure S36a ) [ 54 ]. With increasing 
CO2 adsorption and application of the voltage bias, the Cu–

intermediates bound via carbon atom at the Cu sites. In contrast, 
the constant Cu–N and Cu–Cu coordination numbers reflect the 
structural integrity of NHCH -Cu7 . These results indicate that 
NHCH -Cu7 simultaneously affords high structural stability and 
efficient CO2 activation capability, serving as an effective catalyst 
for the CO2 RR.

no peaks were detected approximately 2000 cm− 1 for NHCMe -

catalyst surface. Additionally, the *CObridge stretching frequency

coverage was higher compared to that of NHCMe -Cu7 [ 56 ]. This 
shift reflects a weakening of the C-O bond due to enhanced

Cu-*CO interaction. Fundamentally, the conversion of CO2 to 
hydrocarbons proceeds via the initial formation of the *CO inter- 
mediate, followed by sequential hydrogenation steps, leading 
to the generation of key *CHO intermediates, and ultimately 
resulting in the formation of CH4 and C2 H4 . Consequently, the 
adsorption strength of *CO on the active sites plays a critical role 
in determining whether *CO evolves toward desorption or further 
hydrogenation for hydrocarbon generation. As anticipated, the 
intensity of the *CO peak initially increased in the case of 
NHCH -Cu7 , and then gradually leveled off (Figure S40 and

To further verify the *CO binding strength of the different

transform infrared spectroscopy (CO-DRIFTS) [ 51 ]. The intensity

adsorption ability. The CO adsorption capacity of NHCH -Cu7 
is comparable to that of NHCMe -Cu7 (Figure S42 ), plausibly 
owing to the similar number of active sites in both catalysts. Both 
samples were subjected to Ar purging at a constant flow rate 
while monitoring the intensity of the peak of adsorbed CO at 
regular intervals to characterize its desorption behavior. For both 
clusters, the recorded absorbance intensities were normalized 
against the initial value at t = 0 s and fitted using a first-order 
kinetic model, yielding a desorption rate constant ( kdes ) of 4.2 
× 10− 3 for NHCMe -Cu7 and 8.6 × 10− 3 NHCH -Cu7 (Figure 4f ).

behavior in this selectivity shift from preceding CO2 activation 
steps, we conducted comparative CORR experiments using CO 
as substrate. Under high CO coverage, NHCMe -Cu7 still showed 
negligible methane formation, indicating that the limiting factor 
for hydrocarbon generation is not CO2 activation but rather the 
intrinsic ability to stabilize and further hydrogenate adsorbed 
*CO (Figure S43 ). In contrast, NHCH -Cu7 exhibited a pro-

the CO2 /CO electroreduction performance tests and operando 
spectroscopic analyses demonstrate that the *CO intermediate 
can be stabilized by tuning the NHC ligand in the Cu clusters, 
thereby successfully steering the reaction pathway on NHCH -Cu7 
toward the production of hydrocarbons.

anism underlying the remote modulation of the Cu electronic 
structure and its influence on the catalytic activity for CO2

both clusters. The catalyst models derived from the optimized

to those in NHCMe -Cu7 , as supported by the above experimental 
findings. The d-band electronic structure of the Cu sites was 
clarified by projected density of states (PDOS) and integrated 
projected density of states (IPDOS) analyses of NHCH -Cu7 and 
NHCMe -Cu7 . Rather than forming a broad d-band, each 3d state 
exhibits a relatively localized electron distribution, resulting in 
five distinct 3d orbitals, with different numbers of electrons

Cu sites in NHCH -Cu7 compared to those in NHCMe -Cu7 , further 
supporting the enhanced orbital hybridization between *CO and 
NHCH -Cu7 (Figure S46 ) [ 10 ]. In general, the aforementioned 
analysis confirms that by subtly tailoring the functional groups 
in the NHC ligands, a remarkable effect on remote modulation 
of the d-band electronic structure of the Cu sites is achieved, 
thus facilitating *CO stabilization and further hydrogenation on 
NHCH -Cu7 , ultimately favoring the formation of deeper reduced 
products such as CH4 and C2 H4 . Finally, to further investigate 
the intrinsic relationship between the electronic structure and 
the selectivity for the CO2 RR products, the Gibbs free energy of 
the reaction at the different catalytic interfaces was calculated. 
For NHCMe -Cu7 , the Gibbs free energy barrier for the release of 
CO ( − 0.07 eV) is notably lower than that for *OCCO coupling 
( − 0.01 eV) and the protonation of *CO to *CHO (0.01 eV) 
(Figures 5d and S47 ). This indicates that CO desorption is 
thermodynamically favored, consistent with the experimental 
observations where CO is the main product, accompanied by 
a small amount of C2 H4 . In contrast, for NHCH -Cu7 , the free 
energy barrier for *CHO formation ( − 0.24 eV) is lower than that 
for *CO desorption ( − 0.15 eV) (Figures 5e and S48 ), suggesting 
that the pathway preferentially favors *CHO generation over CO 
release. In addition, the protonation of *CHO to form *CH2 O 
on NHCH -Cu7 is energetically favorable ( − 0.11 eV), exhibiting a 
significantly lower barrier than the *COCHO coupling reaction 
( − 0.04 eV). This explains the observed higher selectivity for CH4 
production than for generating C2 H4 in the electrochemical tests.

Conclusion 
In summary, a remote-control strategy for modulating the elec- 
tronic structure of Cu NCs by tuning the functional groups of 
NHC ligands was proposed. This ligand engineering switches 
the products of CO2 electroreduction from simple CO to higher- 
value h ydrocarbons. In the proof-of-concept study, two pseudo- 
isologous NHCH -Cu7 and NHCMe -Cu7 clusters featuring either 
-H or -CH3 groups on the NHC ligand were synthesized, resulting 
in structural deformation of the core architecture. Notably, 
NHCH -Cu7 exhibited exceptional FE toward hydrocarbon pro- 
duction (50.4% CH4 and 23.6% C2 H4 ) at − 1.5 V, with a high partial 
current density of 202.2 mA ⋅cm− 2 for hydrocarbon products, 
which starkly contrasts with the predominant CO selectivity 
observed with NHCMe -Cu7 . XAFS and UV-vis absorption anal- 
yses demonstrate that the variations in the electronic state of the 
two clusters coincide with the electronic effect of modulating 
the functional groups on the NHC ligand. In situ ATR-FTIR 
and CO-DRIFTS tests confirm stronger binding of the *CO 
intermediate on NHCH -Cu7 , favoring deep *CO protonation and 
the consequent generation of CH4 and C2 H4 . Systematic DFT 
analyses reveal that the electron-poor -H groups in the NHC 
ligands elevate the 3dx 
2 
-y 
2 orbital to the highest occupied d- 
orbital in NHCH -Cu7, which hosts a greater population of active 
electrons, thereby facilitating electron injection from Cu into 
the π* orbital of *CO. Compared to the 3dz 
2 orbital in NHCMe - 
Cu7 , the 3dx 
2 
-y 
2 orbital exhibits stronger orbital hybridization 
with *CO, which promotes *CO stabilization and activation, thus 
significantly impeding its desorption and lowering the energy 
barrier for *CHO formation. This ultimately steers the reaction 
pathway on NHCH -Cu7 toward hydrocarbon production. To the 
best of our knowledge, this work demonstrates the first remote 
modulation electronic structure in Cu NCs through NHC ligand 
engineering and establishes a definitive paradigm for atomic- 
level design of highly efficient CO2 RR catalysts for high-value 
hydrocarbons.
