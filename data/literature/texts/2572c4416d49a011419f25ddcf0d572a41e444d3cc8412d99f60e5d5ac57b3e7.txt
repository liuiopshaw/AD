ABSTRACT: Photochemical methods are effective for controllable synthesis of silver
nanoparticles with specific sizes and shapes. Whether they are capable of fabricating Ag
nanoclusters (NCs) with atomic precision is yet to be proved. In this work, we synthesize
an atomically precise Ag NC, [Ag25(4-MePhCC)20(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag25), via a
process mediated by visible light. Its total structure is determined by X-ray
crystallography. The investigation of the mechanism reveals that the formation of Ag25
is triggered by a photoinduced electron-transfer (PET) process. An electron of certain
amines is excited by light with wavelength shorter than 455 nm and transferred to Ag+.
The amine is oxidized to the corresponding amine N-oxide. Such a PET process is
supported by experimental and density functional theory studies. To expand the
application scope of the photochemical method, another three NCs, [Ag19(4-tBuPhC
C)14(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag19), [Ag32(4-tBuPhCC)22(Dppp)4](SbF6)3 (Ag32), and bi-
metallic [Ag22Au3(4-tBuPhCC)20(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag22Au3), are produced by
replacing certain ingredients. Furthermore, since the formation of Ag19 can be regarded
as a photochromatic process, a facile amine visual detection method is also presented based on this mechanism.
KEYWORDS: photochemical synthesis, silver, nanocluster, amine, photoelectron transfer
INTRODUCTION
The ultrasmall size of metal clusters places them in between
molecules and bulk. Strong nano-size effects are thus
manifested in their exceptional physicochemical properties,
such as multiple absorption bands, distinct photoluminescence,
well-defined catalytic sites, etc. These attractive features have
made them highly desirable models for research in catalysis,
biology, sensors, etc.1−14 To fundamentally understand their
physicochemical properties, resolving the total structure of
nanoclusters (NCs) is essential. In this aspect, single-crystal X-
ray crystallography has unparalleled advantages in terms of
providing structure images with atomic resolution. As a
premise, single crystals must first be obtained. To this end,
some brilliant synthetic approaches have been established, such
as solid-state synthesis,15 electrocrystallization,16 sonochemical
methods,17 catalytic routes,18 etc.19,20 One of the most widely
used methods is probably solution-phase synthesis, in which a
metal ion precursor is reduced by a chemical reagent (e.g.
NaBH4) in the presence of ligands.21,22 Various methods have
given birth to a wide variety of NCs with fascinating structures
and properties. Nonetheless, whether a photochemical route is
applicable for synthesizing atomically precise metal clusters
remains elusive.
Technically, photochemical synthesis is easily accessible and
has practical features, such as adjustable photon energy. It has
already been widely used to promote organic reactions as well
as inorganic synthesis.23−25 Historically, the photoresponsive
behavior of coinage metals has been known for hundreds of
years.26−28 A well-known example is black-and-white photog-
raphy, which was originally based on a photochemical process
where silver halide was reduced to Ag colloid when exposed to
visible light.29 The underlying chemical principle had been
developed into an efficient strategy for fabricating silver
nanomaterials. Not only the size but also the morphology of
Ag nanoparticles can be manipulated with light (e.g. photo-
induced conversion of Ag nanospheres to nanoprisms).27,30−35
Many related results have convincingly demonstrated the
efficiency of photochemical methods at the nanoscale.36 In this
work, we will prove that the photochemical route can achieve
atomic precision for Ag NCs, one ultimate goal in its
controllable synthesis.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Synthesis of an Ag25 Nanocluster. We first screened a
range of ligands, including alkyne, phosphine, thiolate, and
their combinations. It was found that only the combination of
alkyne and phosphine with an Ag(I) precursor was responsive
to visible light (Figure S1). The subsequent synthesis and
crystallization trials eventually led us to an atomically precise
Ag NC, [Ag25(4-MePhCC)20(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag25). No
reducing reagent (e.g. NaBH4) was used in the whole process.
A typical synthesis involves irradiating the solution containing
Ag(I) precursor, ligands, and amine with a 5-W white-light
LED (Figure 1). In brief, AgSbF6 was dissolved in ethanol
before 4-methylphenylacetylene, 1,5-bis(diphenylphosphino)-
pentane (Dpppe), and triethylamine (Et3N) were introduced.
The resulting slightly yellow solution rapidly turned blue upon
irradiation (Figure S2 and Video S1). The reaction was aged
for 24 h, during which the color got darker. The mixture was
then separated from the solution and subjected to the
crystallization procedure. Black block single crystals were
obtained in 28% isolated yield, which were then analyzed by X-
ray crystallography (Figure S3). As shown in Figure 2a, the
Ag13 core of Ag25 is in the shape of an anticuboctahedron, a
type of hexagonal close-packed (hcp) lattice.37 The 12 Ag
atoms of the outer shell are arranged into three distorted
tetrahedrons, which are trigonally distributed around the Ag13
core (Figure 2b). The Ag4 tetrahedrons and Ag13 core are
bridged by three bidentate Dpppe groups. Since the equatorial
positions are occupied by phosphine, alkyne ligands are
anchored on the upper and lower ends of Ag25, with their
methyls pointing in polar directions. The 10 alkyne ligands on
either side can be further divided into three groups: three
ligands are used to protect the Ag4 tetrahedrons, six alkynes
connect the Ag4 tetrahedrons to the anticuboctahedron core,
and the last one caps the polar Ag3 triangle (Figure S4). The
ESI-TOF-MS spectrum of Ag25 showed a prominent peak at
m/z = 2107.4, which was ascribed to the trication [Ag25(4-
MePhCC)20(Dpppe)3]3+ (calcd = 2107.1, Figure 2c). Thus,
electron counting suggests that Ag25 is a 2-electron closed-shell
system based on the superatom theory (Figure S5).38,39 The
cluster shows a main light absorption peak at 588 nm (Figure
2d). It should be noted that our Ag25 resembles another Ag25
cluster mediated by NaBH4. Both clusters have the same
anticuboctahedron Ag13 core and three distorted Ag4
tetrahedrons, although they employed different alkynes.40
Photoinduced Electron Transfer (PET) Mechanism.
Understanding the mechanism through which light induces the
formation of Ag NCs is crucial for the photochemical method
to be established. A series of control experiments revealed that
two factors were critical: one is Et3N and the other is the light.
In the absence of Et3N, irradiating the solution brought about
no observable change (Figure S6). When the synthesis was
carried out in the dark, the result was also negative. In contrast,
even ambient illumination induced a slow reaction, and LED
could greatly accelerate the progress (Figure S7 and Video S1).
Moreover, the formation of Ag NCs critically depended on the
light wavelength. By using optical filters, we were able to define
that only light with wavelength shorter than 455 nm resulted in
the formation of Ag NCs, which corresponds to a photonic
energy of ca. 2.7 eV (Figure 3). Also, GC analysis of the gas-
phase composition of the reaction vessel indicated there was
no H2 produced, excluding the influence of in situ-generated
H2 (Figure S8). On the other hand, the indispensability of
amine, a widely used electron donor in organic reactions,41
implies that it donates an electron to Ag(I). Ammonia and
most chosen aliphatic amines, regardless of their category
(primary, secondary, or tertiary), induced the formation of
Ag25 (Figures S9 and S10). When aniline was used instead of
Et3N, however, it took 1 day before a subtle color change could
be observed. 4-Fluoroaniline was completely inactive, while p-
anisidine with an electron-donating group functioned smoothly
(Figure S10). Their different performances were closely related
to the electron-donating ability of the amine group.42 The
electron pair of aliphatic amines is more prone to be excited by
photons. One of the aromatic amines delocalizes into the
benzene ring, resulting in a decreased Lewis basicity. Electron-
withdrawing substituents enhanced this effect and vice versa.
DFT Calculations. Hence, we propose that the formation
of Ag25 is initiated by a PET process.43,44 An electron of the
amine is excited by a photon with energy above a threshold.
Ag(I) accepts it and gets reduced.45,46 Such a process is
intrinsically an electronically excited system consisting of an
electron donor and an electron acceptor. Thus, the hole and
electron distribution of four representative amines (Et3N, 4-
fluoroaniline, p-anisidine, and 4-methylpyridine) and silver
ions was studied by time-dependent density functional theory
(TD-DFT, Figure 4).47,48 The results indicated that for major
excitation of Ag+-attached Et3N or p-anisidine, there is an
evidently large separation of hole and electron distributions
(Table S1). For 4-fluoroaniline and 4-methylpyridine, the
separation degrees (t index) are negative, implying holes and
electrons are not substantially separated. In Figure 4 the holes
and electrons are visualized as colored isosurfaces. For Et3N
and p-anisidine binding Ag+, the hole regions are associated
with Et3N and p-anisidine, while the electron regions are
associated with Ag. Clearly, charge-transfer (CT) excitation
exists in these two complexes and the Ag+ ion can obtain the
transferred electron. In contrast, for 4-fluoroaniline or 4-
methylpyridine binding Ag+, the hole and electron regions are
all located at amines, which means local excitation (LE) and
thus the Ag+ ion does not obtain an extra electron from the
amine. These results are in good agreement with experimental
observations.
In such a scenario, the amine that gives away the electron
should be oxidized. 1H NMR spectra of the resulting solution
showed that, compared with Et3N (δ = 2.58, 6H, δ = 1.05,
9H), the product had the same hydrogen count and multiplet
but the signals were significantly shifted downfield (δ = 3.09,
6H, δ = 1.38, 9H) (Figure S11). Such a notable change was
ascribed to the formation of Et3N N-oxide. The assignment
was further supported by both mass and FT-IR spectroscopy
(Figures S12 and S13).49 When the synthesis was conducted

Application Scope of the Photochemical Method.
Good applicability is vital for a synthesis method to be
generalized. To this end, by modifying the reaction parameters,
we have successfully synthesized another three NCs. Replacing
4-methylphenylacetylene with 4-tert-butylphenylacetylene
(TBA) while keeping the other conditions the same yielded
a [Ag19(4-tBuPhCC)14(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag19) NC (Fig-
ure 6a). Its overall structure is similar to that of Ag25. However,
Ag19 has an Ag13 cuboctahedron kernel rather than the
anticuboctahedron of Ag25. It can be viewed as a basic unit of a
face-centered cubic (fcc) lattice. Ag13 core is extended by six
Ag atoms by Ag−Ag bonds. Each pair of such Ag atoms are
bridged by a Dpppe (Figures 7a and S18). Furthermore,
replacing Dpppe with 1,3-bis(diphenylphosphino)propane
(Dppp) resulted in an Ag32(4-tBuPhCC)22(Dppp)4](SbF6)3
(Ag32) NC with a distinct kernel structure. As shown in Figure
6b, Ag32 contained an Ag24 kernel and two distorted Ag4
tetrahedrons. The Ag24 core can be decomposed into three
layers: a middle distorted Ag14 plane which is sandwiched by
two Ag5 subunits (Figure S19). Like Ag25, the equatorial
positions of Ag32 are occupied by phosphine ligands, while the
alkyne ligands are located at polar positions (Figures 7b and
S20). According to electron counting, Ag32 has 7 free valence
electrons (32Ag −22alkyne −3SbF
d6
−= 7e). The electron
paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectroscopy of Ag32 in
CH2Cl2 solution was then measured (Figure S21). The result
exhibits one local maximum and one local minimum, which is
characteristic of a system with one unpaired electron (s = 1/2),
with g = 1.945 and 1.901.
More importantly, the photochemical route is not limited to
Ag NCs. When HAuCl4 and AgSbF6 were introduced
simultaneously, a bimetallic Ag22Au3(4-tBuPhCC)20-
(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag22Au3) NC was synthesized (Figure
6c). Its core is an anticuboctahedron doped with three Au
atoms. They occupy three trigonally symmetric vertices of the
middle Ag3Au3 hexagon57,58 (Figure 7c). The Ag−Au bond
(3.465 Å) is significantly longer than the Ag−Ag (3.029 Å)
bond, which results in a structural distortion of the
anticuboctahedron (Figure S22). Both the changes of the
kernel structure and the heteroatom doping can lead to
significant changes of the spectral features. Although Ag19 and
Ag25 have similar features in UV−vis spectra with the main
absorption at 588 nm (Figure S23), Ag32 shows a main
absorption at 729 nm (Figure S24). The doping of Au atoms
into Ag22Au3 causes a main absorption shift from 588 to 603

Applications in Amine Detection. Amine detection has
wide applications in both research and daily life. Most methods
so far reported have relied on organic reagents (e.g.
benzenesulfonyl chloride).59−61 We can view the formation
of NCs as a photochromic process induced by amines.
Combined with their characteristic color, a facile amine
detection procedure can be proposed (Figure 8). Irradiating
the solution of AgSbF6 and Et3N resulted in black precipitate,
as mentioned. Introducing additionally an alkyne yielded a
suspension of Ag-alkyne polymeric complex. The combination
of phosphine and AgSbF6 was immune to visible light even
with excess Et3N. All their absorption spectra were, however,
featureless in the visible range. Only in the presence of both
ligands could we observe a broad peak from 500 to 650 nm
due to the absorption of Ag19 (Figure 8a). The analysis of
solutions with incrementally increasing amounts of Et3N (from
0.15 to 0.50 mmol/L) showed a good linear relationship

CONCLUSIONS
In summary, a photochemical method for synthesizing
atomically precise nanoclusters is established. Four atomically
precise NCs, [Ag25(4-MePhCC)20(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3
(Ag25), [Ag19(4-tBuPhCC)14(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag19),
[Ag32(4-tBuPhCC)22(Dppp)4](SbF6)3 (Ag32), and
[Ag22Au3(4-tBuPhCC)20(Dpppe)3](SbF6)3 (Ag22Au3), are
successfully produced by this method. The mechanism has
proved that the formation of NCs is induced by a
photoinduced electron-transfer process involving an amine.
Such a photoredox mechanism is supported experimentally
and theoretically. Based on this, an amine detection method
was also presented. Provided the intrinsic characteristics of the
photochemical approach such as energy adjustability and
technical maturity, it is optimistic that a light-controlled
synthesis of more silver and heterometallic NCs will emerge in
the future.
METHODS
Synthesis of Ag19 Nanocluster. AgSbF6 (0.1 mmol, 34 mg) in 2
mL of ethanol was added to a CH2Cl2 solution (2 mL) containing
4-tBuPhCCH (0.1 mmol, 18 μL) and Dpppe (1,5-bis-
(diphenylphosphino)pentane) (0.1 mmol, 44 mg). Then 14 μL
Et3N was introduced under vigorous stirring. Under the irradiation of
5-W white-light LED, the color of the solution changes from colorless
to blue instantly. The reaction mixture was stirred at room
temperature under irradiation for 24 h. The mixture was evaporated
to dryness to give a dark purple solid, which was dissolved in CH2Cl2
(2 mL) again. The solution was centrifuged for 10 min at 12000 r/
min. The supernatant solution was subject to the diffusion with n-
hexane and diethyl ether (volume ratio, 1:1) to afford the dark purple
rod-shaped crystals after 1 week (isolated yield 27%).
Synthesis of Ag32 Nanocluster. AgSbF6 (0.1 mmol, 34 mg) in 2
mL of ethanol was added to a CH2Cl2 solution (2 mL) containing
4-tBuPhCCH (0.1 mmol, 18 μL) and Dppp (1,3-bis-
(diphenylphosphino)propane) (0.1 mmol, 41 mg). Then 14 μL
Et3N was introduced under vigorous stirring. Under the irradiation of
5-W white-light LED, the color of the solution changes from colorless
to purple. The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature
under irradiation for 24 h. The mixture was evaporated to dryness to
give a dark brown solid, which was dissolved in CH2Cl2 (2 mL) again.
The solution was centrifuged for 10 min at 12000 r/min. The
supernatant solution was subject to the diffusion with n-hexane and
diethyl ether (volume ratio, 1:1) to afford the dark rod-shaped crystals
after 1 week (isolated yield 33%).
Synthesis of Ag22Au3 Nanocluster. AgSbF6 (0.1 mmol, 34 mg)
in 2 mL of ethanol was added to a CH2Cl2 solution (2 mL)
containing 4-tBuPhCCH (0.1 mmol, 18 μL) and Dpppe (0.1
mmol, 44 mg) and HAuCl4 (0.01 mmol), then 14 μL Et3N was
introduced under vigorous stirring. Under the irradiation of a 5-W
white-light LED, the color of the solution changes from colorless to
green instantly. The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature
under irradiation for 24 h. The mixture was evaporated to dryness to
give a dark green solid, which was dissolved in CH2Cl2 (2 mL) again.

DFT Study Methods. All DFT and TD-DFT computations are
carried out using the Gaussian 09 software package. The hole and
electron distribution of electron excitations are calculated with
Multiwfn wave function analysis code. The hybrid density functional
Cam-b3lyp have been used and the metal atom (Ag) is treated using
LanL2DZ basis set while Pople group basis set 6-31G is used for other
atoms (H, C, N, F).
