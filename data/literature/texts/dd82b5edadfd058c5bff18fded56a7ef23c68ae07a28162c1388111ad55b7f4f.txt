ABSTRACT
Bacterial colonization disrupts wound microenvironments, triggering oxidative stress and macrophage dysfunction that impede
healing. Addressing the need for adaptive therapies, we developed a pH-responsive metal-organic framework (MOF) decorated
with Ag nanoparticles (Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag) integrating 2-methylimidazole, Cu2+, and Ag nanoparticles. Here, we synthesized this
multifunctional material that autonomously modulates antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activities in response to wound pH
changes. Under acidic conditions, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag achieved a 99.99% antibacterial rate against drug-resistant Escherichia coli
(D–E. coli) at a concentration of 35 µg mL−1, and accelerated wound closure to 98% within 12 d. Its antibacterial mechanism arises
from the synergistic effect of surface positive charge, reactive oxygen species (ROS), and released metal ions. As the pH shifts to
alkaline, it scavenges excess reactive oxygen species, promoting macrophage polarization and tissue regeneration. In vivo, Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag accelerated the infected wound’s closure, reduced inflammation, and enhanced collagen deposition and angiogenesis
without systemic toxicity. This study presents a dynamic, infection-responsive platform that synchronizes antibacterial and anti-
inflammatory regulation, offering a promising strategy for managing chronic and infected wounds.

Introduction
Wound healing is a highly orchestrated physiological process
comprising four overlapping phases: hemostasis, inflammation,
proliferation, and remodeling [1–3]. However, this process is
highly susceptible to disruption by microbial infection. During
the hemostatic phase, the wound environment becomes moist
and accumulates necrotic cells, tissue debris, and blood exu-
dates, which together provide abundant sources of carbon and
nitrogen. This creates a favorable niche for bacterial colonization
[4, 5]. When the invasive capacity of pathogenic microorganisms
exceeds the host’s immune defenses, an infected wound devel-
ops. As bacteria proliferate, they secrete metabolic byproducts
such as lactic acid and pyruvic acid, leading to acidification of
the wound microenvironment. This reduction in pH activates
immune responses and triggers the recruitment of neutrophils to

the infection site to clear the pathogens [6, 7]. After bacterial elim-
ination, the wound milieu gradually transitions from acidic to
alkaline due to the release of inflammatory mediators and tissue
exudates. More critically, bacterial lysis releases cellular debris,
intracellular components, and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) frag-
ments that can further amplify inflammatory signaling [8, 9],
resulting in excessive inflammation, impaired angiogenesis, and
delayed or even arrested wound healing [10]. In clinical practice,
effective bacterial eradication and precise regulation of inflam-
mation remain central challenges in managing infected wounds.
Conventional strategies, including debridement, antibiotic ther-
apy, antioxidant treatment, and bioengineered skin substitutes,
provide only partial benefits [11–13].
Nevertheless, recurrent infection after treatment remains com-
mon. Moreover, traditional antibacterial and anti-inflammatory
drugs lack phase-specific responsiveness and cannot adapt to the
dynamic pH fluctuations and biochemical changes that occur
during different healing stages (i.e., antibacterial, inflammatory,
and proliferative phases). This limitation often leads to overmed-
ication, reduced efficacy, or even complete loss of drug activity
under changing microenvironmental conditions, thereby compli-
cating clinical management. Epidemiological data indicate that
approximately 300 000 deaths occur annually worldwide due to
bacterial infection-related complications [14]. Therefore, there is
an urgent need to develop next-generation antimicrobial systems
that not only achieve potent bactericidal activity but also dynam-
ically adapt to the evolving wound microenvironment, alleviate
excessive inflammation, and exert stage-specific antibacterial and
anti-inflammatory effects.
Antibacterial metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have attracted
considerable attention in biomedicine owing to their large surface
area, tunable ligand composition, and multiple antimicrobial
mechanisms [15]. Among these, iron-based MOFs (Fe-MOFs) are
regarded as promising candidatess for treating infected wounds
due to their intrinsic antibacterial activity, excellent biocompati-
bility, and ability to promote tissue regeneration [16, 17]. However,
single-component Fe-MOFs still face several critical challenges
in practical applications [18, 19]: First, many Fe-MOFs rely on
exogenous hydrogen peroxide to initiate the Fenton reaction,
which limits their catalytic efficiency in vivo. Second, bacteria can
upregulate glutathione (GSH) expression or exploit the abundant
GSH present at wound sites to competitively occupy Fe-MOF
active sites and scavenge reactive oxygen species (ROS), signifi-
cantly attenuating antibacterial efficacy and potentially inducing
resistance. Third, the formation of bacterial biofilms creates
dense physical barriers that severely hinder Fe-MOF penetra-
tion and bactericidal activity. Finally, the dynamically changing
wound microenvironment may cause structural collapse of the
framework, potentially increasing cytotoxicity.
To address these issues, recent studies have primarily focused
on ligand engineering and metal co-doping strategies to enhance
the overall performance of Fe-MOFs [20, 21]. One approach
involves introducing imidazole-based compounds with conju-
gated high-electron-density structures. These ligands improve Fe-
MOF adaptability and stability under fluctuating proton concen-
trations while endowing anti-inflammatory capability through
scavenging excessive ROS at infected sites. In parallel, metal-
ion doping (e.g., Cu2+, Zn2+) can further enhance antibacterial
performance by depleting bacterial GSH and reducing the risk
of drug-resistance [22, 23]. Moreover, the porous architecture of
Fe-MOFs provides abundant binding sites for the incorporation
of inorganic antimicrobial agents, thereby achieving synergistic
eradication of multidrug-resistant bacteria.
Among inorganic bactericides, silver nanoparticles (Ag NPs) are
particularly attractive due to their excellent biocompatibility, high
membrane permeability, and controlled ion-release behavior [24,
25]. Their slow-release profile prevents Ag+ accumulation in the
bloodstream, promotes epithelialization, and accelerates tissue
regeneration, making them a viable alternative to antibiotics
for treating infected wounds. Mechanistically, during the early
inflammatory phase, when the local environment is acidic, Ag
NPs release electrons and Ag+ ions. The strong surface plasmon
resonance and abundant lattice defects of Ag NPs facilitate the
reaction of released electrons with the oxygenated, proton-rich
environment to generate ROS. The synergistic action of ROS
and Ag+ disrupts bacterial cell walls, interferes with respiration
and metabolism, and ultimately induces bacterial apoptosis
[26]. Theoretically, Ag NPs can interact with organic molecules
containing electron-donating groups, such as hydroxyl and ether
functionalities, which may enhance their antibacterial efficacy
while mitigating toxicity [27]. In addition, spherical Ag NPs with
diameters below 6 nm exhibit superior antibacterial activity and
can be excreted via renal filtration [28, 29]. However, ultra-small
Ag NPs are prone to agglomeration into larger particles, which
can lead to bioaccumulation and potential heavy-metal toxicity.
Thus, the synthesis of monodisperse ultrasmall Ag NPs remains
technically challenging [30–32]. In addition, the pH-responsive
antibacterial mechanism of Ag NPs and the underlying pathways
are not yet fully understood.
Herein, we report the hydrothermal synthesis of Fe-MOF, fol-
lowed by coordination with 2-methylimidazole and Cu2+ ions to
obtain Cu-Fe-MOF, and subsequent adsorption of Ag nanoparti-
cles (5 ± 0.75 nm) to construct a pH-adaptive Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
nanocomposite capable of intelligently modulating antibacterial
and anti-inflammatory activities (Scheme 1A). During the early
inflammatory stage characterized by bacterial colonization, Cu-
Fe-MOF@Ag exhibits enhanced antibacterial potency through
modulation of surface charge and the synergistic release of Cu2+,
Ag+, and reactive oxygen species (∙OH, 1O2). These agents col-
lectively disrupt bacterial membranes, interfere with respiratory
and amino acid metabolic pathways, and achieve efficient bac-
terial eradication in a bacteria-concentration-dependent manner
(Scheme 1C,D). As the infection progresses to the inflammatory
phase, the nanocomposite shifts toward an anti-inflammatory
state, scavenging excess ROS, promoting macrophage polariza-
tion toward a reparative phenotype, and upregulating wound-
healing factors to accelerate tissue regeneration (Scheme 1B,E).
Moreover, our results reveal that the antibacterial activity of Ag
NPs exhibits distinct pH responsiveness, with H+ ions playing
a crucial role in promoting Ag+ dissolution and superoxide
dismutation reactions. Overall, this study introduces an innova-
tive, pH-dynamic material design strategy that enables adaptive
switching between antibacterial and anti-inflammatory functions
in response to the wound microenvironment. Unlike stimulus-
responsive materials that rely on exogenous triggers, this platform
enables autonomous switching between antibacterial and tissue-
repair modes by sensing the dynamic endogenous pH changes

Results and Discussion
2.1
Preparation and Characterization of
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
To
achieve
precise
therapeutic
adaptation
across
the
inflammatory-to-proliferative phases of infected wound healing,
a pH-responsive Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag nanocomposite was rationally
designed and constructed to regulate antibacterial and anti-
inflammatory activities in response to environmental pH. The
design of the material was based on the dynamic transition of the
wound microenvironment from acidic to alkaline during healing.
To construct the nanocomposite, Fe3+ ions with high charge
density were used as central metal nodes and coordinated with 2-
aminoterephthalic acid to form a bidentate chelating framework.
Next, 2-methylimidazole and Cu2+ ions were incorporated to
create the Cu-Fe-MOF nanocarrier. Ag nanoparticles were then
adsorbed onto the framework via coordination with amino
groups, which served as active binding sites. This approach
produced
a
stable
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
composite
with
both
antibacterial and anti-inflammatory functionalities. To verify
the structural integrity and compositional distribution of the
nanocomposite, a series of physicochemical characterizations,
including
transmission
electron
microscope
(TEM),
high
resolution transmission electron microscope (HRTEM), energy
dispersive
spectrometer
(EDS),
Fourier-transform
infrared
spectroscopy (FT-IR), ultraviolet–visible spectroscopy (UV–vis),
X-ray diffraction (XRD), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy
(XPS) were conducted.
We used TEM to investigate the morphology and distribution
of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag. The TEM images clearly reveal that Ag
nanoparticles with an average diameter of 5 ± 0.75 nm were
uniformly distributed on the surface of Cu-Fe-MOF nanorods
(Figure S1 and Figure 1A,B). In contrast, pristine Ag nanoparticles
tended to aggregate severely (Figure 1E). The HRTEM images
display lattice spacings of 0.209 and 0.235 nm (Figure 1C,D),
corresponding to the (222) plane of Cu-Fe-MOF and the (111)
plane of face-centered cubic Ag, respectively, which are consistent
with XRD patterns (Figure S3B). Elemental mapping and EDS
analysis confirmed the presence of Ag, C, N, O, Fe, and Cu in
the composite (Figure 1G), with respective atomic percentages of
0.89%, 52.30%, 0.29%, 35.90%, 10.36%, and 0.26% (Figure S2A,C).
Inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) analysis
further confirmed that the mass fractions of Fe, Cu, and Ag were
24.30%, 0.79%, and 0.98%, respectively (Figure S2B). The line-
scan analysis revealed that Cu and Fe were uniformly distributed
within the Cu-Fe-MOF framework (Figure 1F), whereas Ag was
evenly dispersed over the surface of Cu-Fe-MOF. Notably, Ag was
predominantly localized on the outer surface of the Cu-Fe-MOF
nanorods. To further evaluate the in vitro stability of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag, TEM observations revealed no obvious morphological
changes in the material after soaking in phosphate-buffered
saline (PBS) for 7 days, while the contents of Cu, Fe, and Ag
decreased due to ion release into the solution (Figure S3A,C).
These findings demonstrate that the material possesses high
stability.
We used UV–vis and FT-IR to determine the structure and
the elemental composition of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag. The character-
istic peak at 400 nm in the UV–vis spectrum further verified
the presence of metallic Ag NPs in the material (Figure 1I).
Whereas the stretching vibration band at 702 cm−1 in the
FT-IR spectrum could be attributed to the coordination inter-
action between Ag and the N atoms of the imidazole ring
[33] (Figure 1H). The pH-dependent surface charge behavior
was examined via zeta potential measurements (Figure S4A,B).
Bacterial metabolites render the local microenvironment acidic
in the infected wounds, with pH typically ranging from 4.7
to 6.0. Upon progression to the inflammatory phase, tissue
exudation and the accumulation of inflammatory mediators
shift the microenvironment toward alkalinity, raising the pH
to 7.5–8.5. During the proliferative phase, the wound maintains
the alkaline milieu to support fibroblast migration, collagen
synthesis, and granulation tissue formation. Collectively, infected
wounds exhibit an acidic microenvironment during the stage
of bacterial colonization (≈pH 5.0), whereas the pH gradually
increases to approximately 8.0 during the subsequent inflamma-
tory and proliferative phases. Based on this dynamic variation,
the present study further investigated whether Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
possesses the corresponding pH-responsive properties. Cu-Fe-
MOF exhibited a positive zeta potential (+40 mV) under acidic
conditions, while Ag sol displayed a negative potential (−38 mV).
After electrostatic self-assembly, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag exhibited an
overall positive potential (+27 mV). Remarkably, as the pH
increased from acidic to alkaline, the zeta potential shifted from
+31 to −8 mV, confirming the pronounced pH-responsive charge
inversion that enhances colloidal stability and biocompatibility in
physiological environments. To investigate the physicochemical
properties of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag and its potential application in
infected wounds, subsequent experiments will be conducted
under three pH conditions: pH 5, 7, and 8, to systematically
evaluate its pH-responsive behavior as well as the variations in
its antibacterial activity and anti-inflammatory performance. By
simulating the transition of the wound microenvironment from
the infection stage (acidic) to the inflammatory and proliferative
stages (alkaline), this study aims to elucidate the functional
adaptability of the material across different physiological phases.
To evaluate the physical and chemical properties of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag, metal-ion release profiles were evaluated under
various pH conditions (Figure S5A–C). In acidic medium (pH =
5), the release sequence followed Fe3+ > Ag+ > Cu2+, whereas
under alkaline conditions, the order shifted to Fe3+ > Cu2+
> Ag+, with all ions showing markedly reduced release rates.
Comprehensive XPS spectra confirmed the coexistence of Fe, Cu,
Ag, C, N, and O elements (Figure S6). High-resolution XPS further
revealed the presence of Fe2+/Fe3+, Cu2+, and Ag+ species, as well
as characteristic peaks corresponding to aminoterephthalate and
imidazole frameworks (Figure 1J–N and Figure S7A).
To investigate the relationship between ion release behavior and
the dynamic pH changes during wound healing, we designed a
two-stage release experiment. The material was first incubated
in a pH = 5.0 medium for 40 h, after which the medium was
replaced with a pH = 8.0 buffer, and release was continuously

Molecular docking results elucidated the mechanism underlying
the structural stability of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag (Figure S7B). Calcula-
tions indicated that Cu2+ and Fe3+ ions were stably anchored to
the Pluronic F127 (F127) framework through coordination with
2-methylimidazole. Fe3+, acting as a high-charge-density center,
possesses a [Ar] 3d5 configuration that endows it with strong
polarization capability and vacant orbital acceptor capacity, facil-
itating coordination with atoms containing lone pair electrons.
During the initial assembly, oxygen atoms in F127 donate lone
pairs to the vacant orbitals of Fe3+ to form σ bonds, enhancing
structural rigidity via chelation. Simultaneously, Cu2+ coordi-
nates with ether oxygen atoms of F127 through its vacant orbitals,
constructing a stable zigzag coordination motif that cooperates
with Fe3+ to form a symmetric double-zigzag configuration. 2-
Methylimidazole, as an extended ligand, further coordinates with
ether oxygens, reinforcing the framework stability. Subsequently,
Ag nanoparticles anchor via ether oxygen coordination, integrat-
ing into the double-zigzag structure while maintaining symmetry
with Fe3+ and Cu2+. This multilayered cooperative coordination
confers the composite with excellent overall integrity, resulting in
low release of Fe3+, Cu2+, and Ag+ ions.
However, the coordination strength is pH-dependent. Under
acidic conditions, protonation of 2-methylimidazole competes
with metal ions for coordination sites, reducing structural stabil-
ity. In contrast, under alkaline conditions, deprotonation of the
F127 framework increases the electron density of oxygen atoms
and enhances their negative charge, thereby more firmly lock-
ing metal ions within the double-zigzag structure and forming
coordination bonds more stable than in neutral environments.
Consequently, ion release is significantly enhanced in acidic
media but suppressed under basic conditions. These results
collectively validated the successful synthesis and structural
integrity of the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag nanocomposite.
2.2
Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Generation
and Mechanistic Insights
Theoretically, ultrasmall Ag nanoparticles (Ag NPs) possess high
surface energy due to their large specific surface area. In an acidic
and oxygenated medium, Ag NPs readily release Ag+ ions and
electrons, where protons (H+) act as electron acceptors to react
with dissolved oxygen, forming hydrogen peroxide (H2O2). Subse-
quently, this system undergoes a Fenton-like catalytic process that
efficiently converts H2O2 into highly oxidative species, including
hydroxyl radicals (∙OH) and singlet oxygen (1O2). Therefore,
an acidic environment is expected to significantly enhance Ag+
dissolution and increase ROS yield. To identify the types of ROS
generated, ascorbic acid (AA), 3, 3, 5, 5-tetramethylbenzidine
(TMB), and 1,3-diphenylisobenzofuran (DPBF) were employed as
selective probes [34, 35]. Ascorbic acid exhibits a characteristic
absorption peak at 265 nm, which diminishes upon oxidation to
dehydroascorbic acid; thus, the decrease in absorbance directly
reflects ROS consumption (Figure 2A). TMB is oxidized by ∙OH
to yield blue oxidized TMB (oxTMB) with a maximum absorption
at 652 nm (Figure 2B); upon quenching with strong acid, the
product turns yellow and exhibits a peak at 450 nm. DPBF,
whose furan ring reacts specifically with 1O2 to form an unstable
endoperoxide intermediate, undergoes decomposition to 1, 2-
dibenzoylbenzene, resulting in a gradual decrease of absorbance
near 415 nm (Figure 2C). In addition, electron paramagnetic
resonance (EPR) spectroscopy was performed to further confirm
the ROS species, and comparative experiments were conducted
under alkaline conditions to evaluate the pH dependence of ROS
formation. As shown in Figure 1O, the degradation of ascorbic
acid was greatly accelerated in the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag system
compared to Cu-Fe-MOF, indicating higher ROS production.
To further examine the ROS-generating capability of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag at different pH values, the results shown in Figure
S8A demonstrate that the production of ∙OH and 1O2 increased
with decreasing pH. Notably, by simulating the dynamic pH
changes of the wound microenvironment during healing, we
found that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag generated ∙OH and 1O2 under the
acidic conditions corresponding to the infection stage, while
scavenging excessive ∙OH and 1O2 under the alkaline conditions
corresponding to the inflammatory stage, thereby demonstrating
its pronounced antioxidant capability. The TMB and DPBF
colorimetric assays showed that, relative to Cu-Fe-MOF, the Cu-
Fe-MOF@Ag system generated markedly higher levels of oxTMB
and 1,2-dibenzoylbenzene, suggesting that the incorporation of
Ag nanoparticles effectively enhanced the material’s catalytic
capacity for ∙OH and 1O2 generation. Moreover, the electron para-
magnetic resonance (EPR) spectra exhibited characteristic signals
with intensity ratios of 1:2:2:1 and 1:1:1, respectively, further ver-
ifying the existence of these two ROS species (Figure 2D,E). The
scavenging experiments conducted under different pH conditions
showed that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag exhibited effective scavenging
activity toward both ∙OH and
1O2 at pH = 8. In contrast,
at pH = 5, the radical level in the system increased, which
was attributed to the material itself inducing the generation
of trace amounts of ∙OH and
1O2 under acidic conditions.
Notably, these signals were absent under alkaline conditions,
suggesting that such environments are unfavorable for ROS
generation.
To assess the composite’s capacity to deplete excessive glu-
tathione (GSH) in wound microenvironments, 5, 5-dithiobis (2-
nitrobenzoic acid) (DTNB) assays were performed (Figure S8B).
The results revealed that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag bound substantially
more GSH compared to Cu-Fe-MOF, confirming its enhanced
oxidative capability and potential to counteract bacterial antiox-
idant defenses. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the
antibacterial activity of Ag NPs is distinctly acid-responsive, high-
lighting their promising potential for combating drug-resistant
bacterial infections. To further elucidate the role of protons in
Ag-mediated ROS generation, we constructed a structural model

nation to 1.537 Å, which subsequently led to bond rupture and
∙OH generation. This reaction pathway proceeds barrierlessly,
with a monotonic energy decrease, confirming it as a spontaneous
process. Taken together, these computational and experimental
findings clearly demonstrate that protons play a pivotal role in
promoting ROS generation on Ag NP surfaces. Moreover, 1O2 is
more prone than 3O2 to transform into ∙OH, suggesting a proton-
coupled activation mechanism as the dominant pathway in acidic
environments.
2.3
Antibacterial Performance and Mechanistic
Evaluation
Based on the above findings, under acidic conditions, the surface
of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag becomes positively charged while simultane-
ously generating multiple reactive oxygen species (∙OH, 1O2) and
releasing metal ions. The positively charged surface and released
metal ions interact electrostatically with negatively charged
bacterial cell walls, altering their secondary structures and the
activity of functional proteins. Meanwhile, ROS species further
disrupt the phospholipid bilayer and transmembrane proteins,
collectively inducing irreversible bacterial damage. To systemati-
cally evaluate the antibacterial performance of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag,
Escherichia coli (E. coli), Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus), and
drug-resistant E. coli (D – E.coli) were selected as representative
model strains. The minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC),
agar disk diffusion assay, and bacterial colony-counting method
were employed to assess antibacterial efficacy, and the capability
of the composite to destroy bacterial biofilms and counteract
antibiotic resistance was also investigated. In comparison with
Fe-MOF, Cu-Fe-MOF, and Ag, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag showed the low-
est MIC value, demonstrating its superior antibacterial activity
(Figure S9B). As shown in Figure 3A, the MIC values of Cu-
Fe-MOF@Ag against E. coli, S. aureus, and D – E.coli were
24.5, 26.5, and 35 µg mL−1, respectively. The MIC of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag against D – E. coli was approximately three times
lower than that of commercial tetracycline. We investigated
the MIC of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag under different pH conditions
(Figure S9A). The results indicated that the MIC decreased
as the pH was lowered. Combined with the Zeta potential
measurements, it was evident that the surface of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
became increasingly positively charged under acidic conditions,
facilitating stronger electrostatic interactions with the negatively
charged bacterial cell walls and thereby inducing bacterial death.
Notably, as the bacterial density increased, metabolic byproducts
such as CO2 and lactic acid lowered the local pH, further
promoting electrostatic adsorption between the material and
bacteria and consequently enhancing the antibacterial effect.
Colony-counting assays further confirmed that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
achieved >99.99% bacterial inhibition within 20 min for all three
strains (Figure 3D,E). The inhibition zone diameter of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag was 1.5 times larger than that of Ag nanoparticles and
2 times larger than that of the mixed-ion group. Comparative
studies using Cu-Fe-MOF, Ag nanoparticles, and mixed ion
systems (Ag+ + Fe3+ + Cu2+) demonstrated that the superior
antibacterial activity of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag primarily originated
from the synergistic effects of its positively charged surface,
released metal ions, and generated ROS (Figure 3F,H,I).
Under environmental stress, such as macrophage phagocytosis or
exposure to antimicrobial agents, bacteria initiate biofilm forma-
tion as a survival mechanism. Biofilms, composed of structured
extracellular polymeric substances, serve as physical barriers
that shield bacteria from external stresses. Moreover, biofilm-
embedded bacteria can enter a metabolic dormancy state, reduc-
ing growth and energy consumption, thereby evading immune
clearance and antibiotic attack. To evaluate the biofilm disruption
capacity of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag, both crystal violet staining and
colony-counting assays were performed. As shown in Figure 3G,
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag exhibited a biofilm destruction rate exceeding
97%, far superior to Cu-Fe-MOF and Ag alone, and reduced
99.99% of residual bacteria within the biofilm (Figure 3J,K). As
shown in Figure S9C, compared with Fe-MOF, Cu-Fe-MOF, and
Ag, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag exhibited the strongest disruptive effect on
D – E.coli biofilms and was able to eliminate the vast majority of
the biofilm. Notably, dead bacteria were mainly distributed on the
biofilm surface, indicating that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag possesses strong
biofilm penetration capability. Repetitive antibacterial cycling
tests using D – E.coli demonstrated that after 25 consecutive cycles
(P < 0.05), Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag maintained >70% antibacterial
efficiency (Figure 3B,C), highlighting its remarkable stability
and sustained antimicrobial performance. After multiple reuse
cycles, the contents of Cu, Fe, and Ag ions in the material
gradually decreased, resulting in a progressive reduction in their
release concentrations and, consequently, a gradual decline in
antibacterial activity.
To further assess bacterial membrane damage, live/dead flu-
orescence staining and morphological characterization were
conducted (Green indicates live cells, whereas red indicates dead
cells). As shown in Figure 4A and Figure S10, untreated E. coli,
S. aureus, and D – E.coli maintained high viability (with partial
natural cell death observed). In contrast, after treatment with
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag, bacterial mortality markedly increased in all
groups (P < 0.05). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and TEM
images (Figure 4B) revealed that untreated bacteria exhibited
smooth and intact surfaces, whereas Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag treated
cells displayed severe membrane collapse, perforations, and
cytoplasmic leakage, confirming irreversible structural damage.
The outer structures of the bacterial cell wall, such as teichoic
acids and lipopolysaccharides, contain phosphate (─PO4
3−) and
carboxylate (─COO−) groups that become ionized under physi-
ological pH conditions, thereby conferring a negatively charged
surface. This surface charge state can be characterized by zeta
potential measurements [36–38]. In the present study, the surface
zeta potentials of untreated E. coli, S. aureus, and D – E. coli were
-16.72, -15.26, and -17.18 mV, respectively; after treatment with Cu-
Fe-MOF@Ag for 20 min, these values increased to -5.76, -6.32, and
-6.22 mV (Figure 4C), respectively, indicating a marked reduction
in surface negative charge. Because Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag itself is
positively charged, it can bind to the bacterial surface through
electrostatic attraction; meanwhile, the released Cu2+, Fe3+, and

2.4
Biocompatibility and Biosafety Evaluation
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag gradually releases Fe3+ and Cu2+ ions in phys-
iological media. Fe3+, as a key component of heme proteins
and iron-sulfur clusters, participates in essential biological pro-
cesses such as oxygen transport, energy metabolism, electron
transfer, and DNA synthesis. Cu2+, on the other hand, promotes
the expression of pro-angiogenic factors, thereby enhancing
endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and neovascularization.
As essential trace elements in the human body, both Fe and
Cu exhibit excellent biocompatibility and act as cofactors that
synergistically accelerate wound healing [39].
To systematically evaluate the biocompatibility of Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag, in vitro cytotoxicity, expressed as the half-maximal
inhibitory concentration (IC50), hemolysis ratio, macrophage
co-culture effects (RAW264.7), and cell migration assays were
conducted, followed by in vivo intramuscular injection studies
to
assess
potential
histopathological
alterations
in
major
organs. As shown in Figure 5A, the IC50 of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
toward L929 fibroblasts was 289.78 µg mL−1 (P < 0.05), while
the hemolysis rate was 3.41%, well below the 5% biosafety
threshold (Figure 5B,C). The nanocomposite exhibited negligible
cytotoxicity toward RAW264.7 macrophages (Figure 5E) and
significantly enhanced cell migration, with a scratch closure
rate exceeding 90% after 24 h (Figure 5D,F), demonstrating
excellent biosafety and potent cell-repair capability. Moreover,
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag displayed enhanced ROS-scavenging efficiency
under alkaline conditions (Figure S12), indicating superior
antioxidant performance during the late healing phase. In
vivo histological assessment (Figure 5G) revealed no apparent
muscle fiber rupture, degeneration, or necrosis at the injection
site. Hematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining of the heart, liver,
spleen, lungs, and kidneys was shown in Figure 5H. Compared
with the control group, glomerular structures in the treated
rats remained intact, with no obvious congestion or dilation
observed. Hepatocytes exhibited normal morphology without
apparent edema, degeneration, or necrosis. In the spleen, the
boundary between white and red pulp was well-defined, and the
tissue architecture was preserved. These results indicate that the
material does not induce significant tissue toxicity at therapeutic
concentrations and demonstrates good biocompatibility. These
findings confirm that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag possesses excellent tissue
compatibility and in vivo biosafety.
2.5
In Vivo Evaluation of Wound Healing
Efficacy
A full-thickness skin defect mouse model was established to
evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag against D
– E. coli infected wounds. Dynamic changes in blood metal ion
concentrations were monitored to elucidate their involvement in
the repair process. As illustrated in Figure 6A and Figure S12A,
circular wounds (≈8 mm diameter) were created on the dorsal
skin, and divided into four groups: uninfected control, D – E.coli
infected control, ciprofloxacin-treated, Fe-MOF-treated, Cu-Fe-
MOF-treated, Ag-treated, and Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag-treated groups.
Wound healing was monitored over time, and bacterial residues
were quantified via standard swab cultures. Healing analyses
(Figure 6B,D,F and Figure S12B) show that uninfected wounds
began closing on day 3, achieving 96% closure by day 12. In
contrast, all infected wounds exhibited delayed healing. By day
12, wound closure rates were 56% for the infection control, 76%
for ciprofloxacin, and an impressive 98% for Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
(P<0.05), demonstrating superior therapeutic efficacy-surpassing
even the uninfected group. Monitoring of wound pH dynamics
(Figure S13) indicated a transition from acidic during hemostasis
to alkaline in the proliferative phase, and eventual restoration to
mild acidity upon healing. Importantly, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag main-
tained morphological integrity throughout the healing process
(Figure S14), reflecting strong adaptability to the wound microen-
vironment. Bacterial culture of regenerated tissues (Figure 6C
and Figure S15) confirmed the complete eradication of D – E.coli
colonies in both Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag and control wounds. During
treatment, body weights across all groups exhibited an initial
decline followed by recovery (Figure 6G).

in the blood following treatment. As shown in Figure 6H,I,

2.6
Histological and Immunomodulatory
Analyses
To investigate tissue regeneration and immune modulation dur-
ing healing, hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E), Masson’s
trichrome, and immunohistochemical staining were performed
for markers including recombinant cluster of differentiation
86 (CD86), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin 6 (IL-
6), cluster of differentiation 206 (CD206), vascular endothelial
growth factor (VEGF), interleukin-10 (IL-10), cluster of differ-
entiation 31 (CD31), collagen I (COL-1), and fibronectin (FN).
H&E staining (Figure 7A) shows that on day 3, all infected
groups exhibited disordered tissue architecture. By day 6, Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag treated wounds displayed narrower scar widths and
reduced neutrophil infiltration, and by day 12, the wound sites
showed organized cell alignment with nearly complete epithe-
lial regeneration. Masson staining further indicated continuous
collagen deposition from day 3 onward, with significantly higher
collagen content in the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag group than in both
the infection and ciprofloxacin groups. These results collectively
demonstrate that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag facilitates accelerated wound
regeneration compared to conventional antibiotic treatment.
To elucidate the immunomodulatory mechanism, immunoflu-
orescence staining was performed on regenerated tissues at
day 8. As shown in Figure 7B, pro-inflammatory markers
(CD86, TNF-α, IL-6) were markedly suppressed in the Cu-Fe-
MOF@Ag group compared with the infection and ciprofloxacin
groups, whereas anti-inflammatory markers CD206 and IL-10
were strongly upregulated. Compared with the D – E.coli and
CIP-treated groups, the expression of the marker CD86 of classi-
cally activated macropoages (M1 macrophages) was significantly
decreased in the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag group, whereas the expression
of thmarker CD206 of alternatively activated macrophages (M2
macrophages) was markedly increased. Flow cytometry con-
firmed a higher M2/M1 macrophage ratio, indicating polarization
toward the pro-regenerative M2 phenotype (Figure 7C,D). During
the proliferative phase, key regenerative markers-VEGF, CD31,
COL-1, and FN-were also significantly upregulated (Figure 7E) (P
< 0.05). These findings indicate that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag suppresses
excessive inflammation, promotes macrophage polarization from
M1 to M2 (Figure S16), and enhances the expression of angio-
genic and extracellular matrix repair factors, thereby accelerating
infected wound healing (Figure 7F). H&E staining of major
organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys) across all
treatment groups (Figure S17) shows no detectable pathological
abnormalities, confirming that Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag induced no sys-
temic toxicity and exhibits excellent in vivo biosafety throughout
the healing process.
3
Discussion
Bacterial colonization initiates tissue damage and inflamma-
tion at infected wounds, driving a pH shift from acidic to
alkaline through immune oxidative stress and exudation, com-
pounded by the accumulation of bacterial metabolites. This
dynamic microenvironment undermines conventional drugs that
lack phase-specific responsiveness, impeding precise therapy. To
address this challenge, we developed a pH-responsive composite,
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag, and comprehensively evaluated its physico-
chemical features, antibacterial activity and mechanisms, bio-
compatibility, anti-inflammatory properties, and in vivo wound-
healing efficacy.
3.1
Rational Design and pH-Adaptive Behavior
Guided by mechanistic insights into pH responsiveness, we
selected Fe-MOF and ultrasmall Ag nanoparticles as the core
constituents. Fe3+ with a [Ar] 3d5 configuration provides high
charge density and strong polarization, favoring robust coordina-
tion with lone-pair-bearing donor atoms. In aminoterephthalate
derivatives, deprotonated carboxylate O atoms chelate Fe3+ in a
bidentate mode, while amine N donates a lone pair into vacant
Fe 3d orbitals to form σ-coordination bonds [40]. Incorpora-
tion of 2-methylimidazole-featuring dual protonation sites and
a pKa near physiological pH-together with Cu2+ (conferring
both reparative and antibacterial functions) yields a Cu-Fe-
MOF carrier whose surface charge reversibly inverts with pH
(positive in acid, negative in alkali), thereby matching the tem-
poral pH evolution of infected wounds. Under acidic conditions,
the positively charged surface and released metal ions electro-
statically perturb secondary structures of cell-wall components
and modulate functional proteins, resulting in potent, bacteria-
density-dependent killing. Residual uncoordinated amines act
as anchoring sites to immobilize Ag nanoparticles, producing
the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag antibacterial agent with programmable
switching between antibacterial and anti-inflammatory states.
The intelligent pH-responsive behavior of the nanocomposite
originates from the unique protonation-deprotonation prop-
erties of 2-methylimidazole. Owing to its dual protonation
sites and a pKa value close to physiological pH, the ligand
imparts excellent adaptability and stability to the Fe-MOF under
dynamically changing microenvironments. Consequently, the
surface chemistry and biological functionality of the material
can dynamically adjust to environmental pH. During the early
stage of infection dominated by bacterial colonization, the acidic
microenvironment induces surface protonation, resulting in a
positively charged surface. This facilitates the synergistic release
of metal ions (Cu2+, Ag+) and reactive oxygen species (∙OH
and 1O2), which cooperatively disrupt bacterial cell walls, impair
respiratory chains, and interfere with amino acid metabolism,
resulting in high antibacterial efficacy. As wound healing pro-
gresses to the late inflammatory and proliferative phases, the
microenvironment gradually shifts toward alkalinity, causing the
surface charge to invert to the negative polarity. This transition
enhances the electron cloud density of the π–π conjugated imi-
dazole rings, boosting ROS-scavenging capacity and contributing
to anti-inflammatory activity. The material thereby supports
macrophage polarization and tissue regeneration.

3.3
Multimodal Antibacterial Mechanism
Bacterial proliferation acidifies the milieu (e.g., lactic acid, CO2,
pyruvate), which boosts the formation of ∙OH and 1O2. These
ROS localize to bacterial membranes, overwhelming endogenous
antioxidant defenses and triggering lipid peroxidation, protein
oxidation, and DNA strand breaks. In parallel, the positively
charged surface enhances adhesion to the negatively charged
cell envelope, while released Cu2+/Fe3+/Ag+ coordinate with
key enzymes (especially thiol-containing targets), thereby per-
turbing pyrimidine nucleotide and amino-acid metabolism. Con-
sistent with transcriptomic and pathway analyses, disruptions
span alanine–aspartate–glutamate metabolism, branched-chain
amino-acid biosynthesis, and pyrimidine metabolism, collec-
tively collapsing the material and energetic bases for bacterial
growth. These multi-target synergisms explain the superior
activity of Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag over single-ion systems.
Compared to our previous study, CuFe2O4/Cu@PEI/Ag exhibits
potent antibacterial activity through the synergistic effects of
Cu/Cu+/Cu2+, Fe2+/Fe3+, Ag/Ag+, ROS, and PEI. However, high
levels of ROS may induce potential cytotoxicity. Ideally, antibac-
terial materials should maintain low-dose, sustained release of
metal ions and ROS within the infected microenvironment (e.g.,
weakly acidic conditions), sufficient to disrupt bacterial mem-
branes and interfere with bacterial metabolism, while preserving
host immune cell functions. In contrast, excessive concentrations
of Cu2+ or Ag+ ions may compromise the fibroblasts’ mito-
chondrial membrane potential, triggering apoptosis or necrosis.
The overproduction of ROS can inhibit keratinocyte migration
and proliferation, hindering wound edge closure. Moreover,
high doses of metal ions and ROS may interfere with VEGF
signaling, suppress angiogenesis, and exacerbate local ischemia
and hypoxia. ROS species such as ⋅OH are non-selective and,
besides attacking bacterial membranes, can also induce lipid
peroxidation and damage to proteins and DNA in host cells,
potentially leading to tissue necrosis. Elevated Cu2+ or ROS
levels may additionally disrupt transforming growth factor-β1
signaling, impairing fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation
and thereby hindering wound contraction. In contrast, the MOF-
Fe/Cu/Ag framework incorporates imidazole rings, enabling
the release of trace amounts of Fe3+, Cu2+, Ag+, and ROS
during the infection phase, effectively eradicating pathogens.
During the inflammatory phase, the imidazole rings scavenge
excess ROS, alleviating oxidative stress. Simultaneously, trace
Fe3+ and Cu2+ act as endogenous supplemental elements sup-
porting cellular functions and synergistically promoting wound
healing.
3.4
Immunomodulation and Tissue Repair
During healing, macrophages polarize between M1 (CD86, pro-
inflammatory) and M2 (CD206, pro-reparative) phenotypes to
orchestrate clearance and regeneration [44, 45]. In the inflamma-
tory phase, M1 macrophages phagocytose pathogens/debris and
release TNF-α and IL-6 to augment sterilization. During prolifera-
tion, M1-to-M2 transition elevates IL-10 and upregulates vascular
and matrix cues-CD31, VEGF, COL-1, and FN-to drive angio-
genesis and tissue remodeling [46, 47]. Persistent debris/DNA
fragments may prolong inflammation and upregulate Cu/Zn-
superoxide dismutase (SOD), contributing to systemic ion loss
(e.g., Cu2+, Fe3+) through sequestration and excretion, thereby
delaying repair. The electron-rich π system of surface imidazole
groups enhances ROS scavenging under alkaline conditions,
mitigating oxidative stress in late stages. Concurrently, Cu2+
promotes VEGF expression and endothelial activation, while Fe3+
supports electron transport, DNA synthesis, and enzyme cofactor
functions central to immune regulation (including modulation of
IL-6 and TNF-α).
Compared with the alkaline phosphatase-responsive antibacte-
rial agent developed by Xia’s group, the near-infrared (NI) light-
triggered intelligent spray system designed by Qian’s group, and
the pH/glucose dual-responsive hydrogel dressing reported by Li
et al., these enzyme- or light-responsive “smart” materials [48–
50], although capable of controlled release, still rely on external
intervention, which, to some extent, limits their efficiency in
clinical translation. Moreover, for pH-responsive hydrogels, the
interfacial adhesion between bilayer components and the overall
mechanical performance still require precise optimization, while
their response stability remains relatively limited. In contrast,
the Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag antibacterial agent constructed in this study
functions as an autonomously responsive, integrated platform
that directly responds to the natural pH fluctuations within
the wound microenvironment. During the infection stage (≈pH
5.0), it operates in a pro-oxidative antibacterial mode, and
subsequently, during the inflammatory stage (≈pH 8.0), it spon-
taneously switches to an antioxidative and anti-inflammatory
mode. This endogenously driven functional transition ensures
stage-specific therapeutic intervention, enabling temporally coor-
dinated antibacterial and anti-inflammatory regulation without
the need for exogenous triggering. In addition, its degradation
products actively participate in and accelerate the biological
processes involved in tissue repair. Therefore, while enhancing
autonomous antibacterial activity, this platform also distinctly

3.5
Limitations and Outlook
While long-term physiological toxicity and metabolism warrant
further study, this work provides a conceptual validation that
Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag can intelligently toggle antibacterial and anti-
inflammatory functions in a drug-resistant infection model,
efficiently clearing inflammatory mediators and enhancing repar-
ative factor expression to markedly accelerate wound healing.
Future efforts should expand pharmacokinetics/toxicokinetics,
assess chronic exposure and immune memory effects, and explore
scalable manufacturing and dressing integration to facilitate
clinical translation.
4
Conclusion
In summary, this study successfully developed a pH-responsive
intelligent antibacterial agent, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag, using Fe-MOF
as the structural scaffold, reinforced with dimethylimidazole
ligands, and synergistically integrated with Cu2+ and Ag. This
smart nanocomposite can autonomously switch between antibac-
terial and anti-inflammatory activities according to different
stages of infected wound healing, enabling precise therapy while
maintaining high stability throughout treatment. In vitro results
demonstrated potent antibacterial activity, as Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
could be activated by H+ and exhibited concentration-dependent
bactericidal performance, eradicating 99.99% of drug-resistant
bacteria at only 35 µg mL−1. Its antibacterial effect arises from
the synergistic action of surface positive charges, released metal
ions, and ROS, which collectively disrupt the bacterial cell
wall and membrane structure, interfere with respiratory chain
function, amino acid metabolism, and nucleic acid stability, and
ultimately lead to bacterial death. Moreover, Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag
showed excellent biocompatibility, with a hemolysis rate of 3.41%,
an IC50 value of 289.78 µg mL−1 toward L929 fibroblasts, a
scratch closure rate of over 90% within 24 h, and no obvious
in vivo accumulation. In vivo experiments further demonstrated
that the material accelerated wound healing to over 98% within
12 d by scavenging excessive ROS, suppressing the expression
of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α and IL-6, promoting the
secretion of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10, and upregu-
lating the expression of repair-related factors, including CD31,
VEGF, COL-1, and FN. In addition, the released Fe3+ and
Cu2+ could replenish metal ions depleted during the immune
response, thereby further promoting tissue regeneration. Overall,
this study proposes an intelligent material design strategy based
on Cu-Fe-MOF@Ag, providing a promising solution for fully
adaptive treatment throughout the course of infected wound
healing and demonstrating considerable potential for clinical
translation.
