Heteronuclear Single-Atom Nanozymes Overcome
Radiotherapy Resistance via Synergistic Multi-Enzyme
Activities
Bowen Yang, Yiyin Mai, Hongyan Su, Ruonan Li, Ge Qiao, Jiannan Wu, Pingyu Zhang,
Hanqing Chen, Tingting Wang, Guotao Yuan,* and Yue Pan*
Single-atom nanozymes have revolutionized catalytic tumor therapy by
maximizing atomic eﬃciency and enabling enzyme-mimetic reactive oxygen
species (ROS) generation. Yet, their single active-site architecture imposes
fundamental limitations on catalytic selectivity and intermediate adsorption–
desorption kinetics. Here, a manganese/ruthenium dual-metal single-atom
nanozyme (Mn/Ru BMSA) is reported that overcomes these constraints
through synergistic metal interactions, exhibiting unprecedented multienzyme
activities, including catalase, peroxidase, and superoxide dismutase. By
leveraging tumor-overexpressed hydrogen peroxide, Mn/Ru BMSA simulta-
neously alleviates hypoxia and produces cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals, creating
a self-sustaining catalytic cycle that ampliﬁes ROS for radiotherapy synergy.
Density functional theory calculations reveal that Mn coordination modulates
the d-band center of Ru, optimizing electronic structure to enhance substrate
activation. In radioresistant breast cancer models, Mn/Ru BMSA exhibits
a synergistic triple-modal antitumor action: 1) sustained oxygen generation
alleviates tumor hypoxia, 2) X-ray-enhanced ROS production ampliﬁes oxida-
tive stress, and 3) hypoxia-inducible factor 1𝜶pathway inhibition suppresses
cancer stem cell (CSC) stemness (as validated by transcriptomics). Patient-
derived organoid assays demonstrated complete radioresistance reversal
while maintaining exceptional biosafety. This work establishes Mn/Ru BMSA
as a potential radiosensitizer that overcomes the limitations of conventional
high-Z materials by targeting the hypoxia-CSC axis, providing a strategy for
developing multi-functional nanozymes against treatment-resistant cancers.

1. Introduction
Single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes) have
emerged as a promising class of cata-
lysts in tumor therapy due to their ex-
ceptional reactive oxygen species (ROS)
generation capabilities, high atomic uti-
lization eﬃciency, and enzyme-mimicking
catalytic kinetics.[1–5] These nanomateri-
als, characterized by well-deﬁned active
sites, exhibit peroxidase (POD)- and oxi-
dase (OXD)-like activities, enabling the con-
version of endogenous hydrogen perox-
ide (H2O2) and oxygen (O2) into cytotoxic
ROS—such as superoxide anions (•O2
−),
singlet oxygen (1O2), and hydroxyl radicals
(•OH)—within the tumor microenviron-
ment (TME).[6–9] To date, various SAzymes
incorporating diﬀerent metal centers (e.g.,
Fe,[10] Mn,[11] Cu,[12] Co,[13] Pt,[14]) coordi-
nated with nitrogen (N) ligands have been
explored for anticancer applications. How-
ever, conventional metal-N4 conﬁgurations
often suﬀer from suboptimal substrate
adsorption due to their symmetric elec-
tron distribution.[13,15–17] In contrast, asym-
metric coordination structures (e.g., Mn-
N5
[18] or Fe-N3PS[19]) or non-planar coordi-
nation structures (e.g., 3D FeN4C-SOx)[20]

demonstrate enhanced enzymatic activity owing to localized
charge polarization at the metal sites. Despite these advances,
a fundamental limitation of SAzymes stems from their single
active-site architecture, which imposes an inherent trade-oﬀbe-
tween the adsorption–desorption thermodynamics of reactive in-
termediates and peak catalytic selectivity and eﬃciency.[21–23]
Recent advances have led to the development of dual-atom
nanozymes (DAzymes), which exploit synergistic interactions
between adjacent metal sites to achieve enhanced catalytic per-
formance. Unlike single-atom nanozymes (SAzymes), DAzymes
preserve the advantages of atomic dispersion while enabling ad-
ditional geometric and electronic modulation. The presence of
two adjacent, yet diﬀerent, metal centers enables the precise and
independent tuning of adsorption energies for diﬀerent inter-
mediates at their respective sites. This conﬁguration oﬀers a vi-
able and sophisticated strategy to circumvent the scaling relations
and catalytic limitations inherent to single-atom architectures,
thereby unlocking new opportunities for designing highly eﬃ-
cient catalysts for complex reactions. Emerging DAzymes, such
as FeCo/Fe–Co,[24] FeMn-NCe,[25] and Mo-Cu,[26] demonstrate
superior catalytic activity by overcoming the inherent constraints
of single-atom architectures. Notably, Lin et al. demonstrate that
Mo-Cu diatomic sites not only synergistically enhance catalytic
therapeutic eﬀects but also induce oxidative stress cascade ampli-
ﬁcation through glutathione depletion and NADPH blockade.[26]
Parallel work by Guo et al. on edge-rich Fe1Mn1–NCe achieves
record nanozyme activity via d-band center tuning, where Mn co-
ordination optimizes substrate adsorption to accelerate H2O2/O2
activation.[27] While these advances highlight the promise of elec-
tronic structure modulation, a critical question remains: Can we
simultaneously amplify catalytic performance and overcome the
multifactorial limitations of radiotherapy resistance?
Radiotherapy remains a clinical mainstay for solid tumors, ex-
erting its cytotoxic eﬀects through direct DNA damage and in-
direct oxygen-dependent generation of ROS.[28–30] However, the
aberrant vasculature and metabolic demands of tumors create
severely hypoxic regions that undermine both mechanisms.[31]
Hypoxia not only reduces radiolytic ROS yields but also activates
hypoxia-inducible factor 1𝛼(HIF-1𝛼)-driven survival pathways in
cancer stem cells (CSCs), enriching a therapy-resistant subpopu-
lation responsible for tumor recurrence.[32,33] Current radiosen-
sitization strategies using high-Z nanomaterials (e.g., Ru, Au,
HfO2) enhance physical radiation dose deposition through pho-
toelectric eﬀects, yet fail to address the biological resistance con-
ferred by hypoxia and the toxicity of heavy metals.[28,34] These
limitations are particularly acute in CSC-enriched niches, where
a hypoxic environment promotes self-renewal. The resulting
“hypoxia-CSC axis” creates a pernicious feedback loop: radiation
kills oxygenated cells, selectively sparing hypoxic CSCs that re-
populate the tumor.[35,36] Consequently, strategic disruption of
the hypoxia-CSC axis now represents a transformative approach
in precision radiotherapy, with engineered DAzymes emerging
as uniquely capable therapeutic agents that simultaneously target
both the biochemical and cellular mediators of radioresistance.
In this study, we report a manganese-ruthenium dual-metal
single-atom nanozyme (Mn/Ru BMSA) engineered to break the
activity-limiting scaling relationships of single-atom nanozymes.
This diatomic pair was speciﬁcally selected to leverage the dis-
tinct catalytic tendencies of each metal, creating a synergistic sys-
tem where each site independently and cooperatively manages
a speciﬁc step in the catalytic cycle. The Mn/Ru BMSA eﬀec-
tively alleviates tumor hypoxia by converting overexpressed H2O2
into O2. Simultaneously, it catalyzes H2O2 decomposition into
·OH, which synergizes with X-ray irradiation for enhanced ra-
diotherapy. Moreover, its superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like activ-
ity replenishes the tumor microenvironment with H2O2 and O2,
thereby establishing a self-sustaining catalytic cycle that contin-
uously ampliﬁes therapeutic eﬃcacy. Theoretical calculations re-
veal that Mn modulates the d-band center of single-atom Ru, op-
timizing electronic structure and boosting enzymatic activity. In
radioresistant breast cancer models, Mn/Ru BMSA not only ame-
liorated hypoxia but also suppressed cancer stemness, as demon-
strated both in vitro and in vivo and further validated by transcrip-
tomic sequencing (Scheme 1). Notably, Mn/Ru BMSA exhibited
potent radiosensitizing eﬀects and successfully reversed radiore-
sistance in patient-derived organoid (PDO) models. Our ﬁndings
underscore Mn/Ru BMSA as a promising strategy to overcome
radioresistance, providing a robust approach to enhance radio-
therapy eﬃcacy in cancer treatment.
2. Results and Discussion
2.1. Characterization of the Mn/Ru BMSAs
The procedure used for the synthesis of the Mn/Ru BMSAs is
shown in Scheme 1. Mn/Ru BMSAs were synthesized by us-
ing manganese and ruthenium ions as the source with subse-
quent calcination to form Mn- and Ru-cointegrated nanoparti-
cles. The morphology of the Mn/Ru BMSAs was characterized via
transmission electron microscopy (TEM) (Figure 1a,b). The ZIF-
8-derived mesoporous carbon nanozymes had a particle size of
106.5 ± 1.4 nm by DLS measurement (Figure S1, Supporting In-
formation). Furthermore, the DLS particle size measurements of
Mn/Ru BMSAs dispersed in PBS, 50% FBS, and DMEM showed
no signiﬁcant diﬀerences during the 35-day redispersion test, in-
dicating excellent stability of the Mn/Ru BMSAs (Figure S2, Sup-
porting Information). The amorphous carbon framework of the
Mn/Ru samples and the atomic dispersion of the Mn and Ru
atoms were detected via aberration-corrected atomic resolution
high-angle annular dark-ﬁeld scanning transmission electron
microscopy (HAADF-STEM) (Figure 1c,d). High-density bright
dots were clearly observed throughout the carbon framework,
with multiple atomic pairs (indicated by red boxes in Figure 1d)
being distinctly identiﬁed, conﬁrming the formation of Mn/Ru
bimetallic atomic sites. HAADF-STEM and elemental mapping
(Figure 1e) revealed that C, N, Ru, and Mn were uniformly dis-
tributed throughout the matrix of the Mn/Ru BMSAs. More-
over, inductively coupled plasma‒optical emission spectrometry
(ICP‒OES) analysis revealed a Mn content of 1.1 wt.% and a Ru
content of 0.8 wt.%. The X-ray diﬀraction (XRD) (Figure 1f) pat-
tern of the Mn/Ru BMSAs exhibited two broad peaks at 23.3°
and 42.5°, corresponding to the (002) and (101) crystal facets of
graphitic carbon,[37] respectively, and excluding the formation of
Mn or Ru metal species. The Raman spectrum of the Mn/Ru
BMSAs (Figure 1g) featured two characteristic peaks at 1331.5
and 1583.2 cm−1, which were attributed to the D-band (sp3 dan-
gling carbon) and G-band (in-plane sp2 carbon), respectively.[38]

2.2. Enzymatic Activity and Catalytic Mechanism of the Mn/Ru
BMSAs
Capitalizing on the synergistic advantages of dual-metal sites
and accelerated electron transfer in Mn/Ru BMSAs, we system-
atically evaluated their multi-enzymatic cascade performance.
Given the elevated H2O2 and hypoxia levels in tumor microen-
vironments (TME), we ﬁrst assessed CAT-mimetic activity via
H2O2-driven oxygen generation. Mn/Ru BMSAs induced a rapid
increase in dissolved O2 from 8.12 to 27.34 mg L−1, whereas
Ru SAs showed signiﬁcantly attenuated O2 production rates
(Figure 3a). We next validated POD-like activity using 3,3′,5,5′-
tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) as a probe. Nanozyme activity is
critically pH-dependent; Mn/Ru BMSAs achieved maximal rel-
ative activity at pH 4.4 (Figure 3b), mirroring the acidic TME.
Under these conditions, Mn/Ru BMSAs + H2O2 elicited a pro-
nounced absorption peak at 652 nm (Figure 3c), conﬁrming su-
perior POD-mimetic eﬃciency. Notably, X-ray irradiation fur-
ther ampliﬁed this catalytic activity. Electron spin resonance
(ESR) with DMPO trapping revealed a characteristic 1:2:2:1 quar-
tet signal, verifying •OH generation. Mn/Ru BMSAs produced
stronger radical signals than Ru SAs, with further enhancement
under X-ray exposure (Figure 3d), consistent with catalytic as-
says. Quantitative analysis showed Mn/Ru BMSAs exhibited a
POD-like activity of 40.02 U nmol−1(Ru)—2.7 × higher than
Ru SAs (14.92 U nmol−1) (Figure 3e). Steady-state kinetics in-

2.3. Catalytic Mechanism of the Mn/Ru BMSAs by DFT
Calculation

ciency of the Mn/Ru BMSAs, the electronic structures and reac-
tion path mechanisms of the Ru SAs and Mn/Ru BMSAs were
investigated via density functional theory (DFT) calculations. A
comparative analysis of the calculated density of states (DOS)
revealed that the d-band center was closer to the Fermi level in
Mn/Ru BMSAs (−0.72 eV) than in Ru SAs (−0.81 eV) attributed
to the introduction of Mn atoms (Figure 4a,b). The upshift in the
d-band center implies that the interaction between the adsorbed
substrate and the surface of the Mn/Ru BMSAs was strength-
ened, thus facilitating the catalytic reaction (Figure 4c). The in-
troduction of Mn led to a change in the charge of Ru (gain of
electrons) according to diﬀerential charge analysis (Figure 4d),

2.4. Mn/Ru BMSAs Promote Radiosensitization In Vitro
Considering the signiﬁcant enzyme-like catalytic activity of the
Mn/Ru BMSAs and their high-Z X-ray absorption properties, we
next systematically investigated the in vitro therapeutic eﬀects of
Mn/Ru BMSAs on MDA-MB-231 human breast cancer cells. Ef-
fective uptake by tumor cells is crucial for maximizing the ther-
apeutic eﬃcacy of the nanozyme. Therefore, confocal laser scan-
ning microscopy (CLSM) was employed to observe the localiza-
tion of Cy5.5-labeled Mn/Ru BMSAs in tumor cells. The par-
tial colocalization of red (Mn/Ru BMSAs) and green (lysosomal
tracker) ﬂuorescence indicated that the Mn/Ru BMSAs function
within both lysosomes and the cytoplasm (Figure S6, Supporting
Information). Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs)
were used as normal controls to study the speciﬁc toxicity of
Mn/Ru BMSAs toward tumor cells. The results of the CCK-8 as-
say demonstrated that the Mn/Ru BMSAs exhibited minimal tox-
icity to normal cells even at high concentrations, conﬁrming their
high biocompatibility (Figure S7, Supporting Information). Fur-
thermore, the Mn/Ru BMSAs showed signiﬁcant toxicity toward
MDA-MB-231 cells at a concentration of 75 μg mL−1, demon-
strating their potent therapeutic eﬃcacy (Figure S8, Support-
ing Information). Based on these results, Mn/Ru BMSAs were
used at a constant concentration of 75 μg mL−1 for subsequent
experiments.

ing radiotherapy.[47] To mimic clinical radioresistance, we devel-

2.7. Mn/Ru BMSAs Enhance Radiosensitivity of Radioresistant
PDOs
The radioresistant nature of TNBC makes it particularly chal-
lenging to treat, leading to overall survival rates that are lower
than those for other breast cancer subtypes. Recently, PDOs have
emerged as powerful preclinical models for studying TNBC, as
PDOs replicate the histopathological and genetic features of the
primary tumor, including its radioresistant characteristics.[56,57]
In this study, we established PDOs from a TNBC patient and
investigated the ablation eﬃcacy of the Mn/Ru BMSAs. The
schematic in Figure 8a illustrates the construction process of
the TNBC organoids, and bright-ﬁeld images conﬁrmed their
successful formation (Figure S39, Supporting Information). Im-
munoﬂuorescence staining for Ki-67, ER, PR, and HER2 in the
generated TNBC organoids demonstrated that these organoids
were indeed triple negative and that the marker proﬁle of the orig-
inal tumor tissues was successfully replicated (Figure 8b). The
distribution of the Mn/Ru BMSAs was observed via CLSM. In the
TNBC PDOs, the Mn/Ru BMSAs were found in the cytoplasm,
indicating their successful uptake by the organoids (Figure 8c).
The viability assay indicated that exposure to 4 Gy of radiation
alone had a minimal eﬀect on the viability of the TNBC PDOs,
demonstrating their inherent radioresistance. In contrast, when
treated with 75 μg mL−1 of Mn/Ru BMSAs, a signiﬁcant reduc-
tion in PDO viability was observed upon the application of 4 Gy
of radiation, suggesting that the Mn/Ru BMSAs eﬀectively en-
hanced the radiosensitivity of the TNBC PDOs to reverse their
radioresistance (Figure S40, Supporting Information). CLSM im-
ages were taken of the TNBC PDOs after they were stained with
calcein AM (green, live cells) and PI (red, dead cells). While the
control, Mn/Ru BMSAs, and X-ray groups displayed minimal cell
death, the combination of Mn/Ru BMSAs and X-ray treatment
signiﬁcantly increased the red ﬂuorescence intensity, indicating
enhanced cell death and improved radiosensitization (Figure 8d).
Collectively, the Mn/Ru BMSAs successfully reversed the radiore-
sistance of TNBC PODs by eﬀectively enhancing their radiosen-
sitivity, demonstrating great potential as a radiosensitizer for
TNBC treatment.
3. Conclusion
In summary, we designed a heteronuclear single-atom nanozyme
with multienzyme activities via the pyrolysis of a MOF-based pre-
cursor. Through the incorporation Mn ions modulates the cat-
alytic activity of the Ru SA sites, Mn/Ru BMSA simultaneously
alleviates hypoxia and produces cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals, creat-
ing a self-sustaining catalytic cycle that ampliﬁes ROS for radio-
therapy synergy. Especially, Mn/Ru BMSA exhibits higher POD-
like catalytic eﬃciency (40.02 U nmol−1, Ru) than did the Ru SAs
(14.92 U nmol−1, Ru). The calculated Km of H2O2 for Mn/Ru
BMSAs and Ru SAs was 5.23 and 13.16 mm, respectively. More-
over, the catalytic mechanism of the Mn/Ru BMSAs was elu-
cidated via theoretical calculations, which revealed that Mn ad-
justs the d-band center of Ru SAs from −0.81 to −0.72 eV, illus-
trating the higher aﬃnity of Mn/Ru BMSAs for H2O2 than that
of Ru SAs and thereby improving the catalytic performance. In
both in vitro and in vivo radioresistant breast cancer mice mod-
els, the Mn/Ru BMSAs eﬀectively catalyzed the amelioration of
the hypoxic tumor microenvironment, downregulated HIF-1𝛼,
and suppressed the expression of stemness markers (NANOG,
SOX2, and OCT4). Additionally, the Mn/Ru BMSAs improved
the therapeutic response of radioresistant organoid models to ra-
diotherapy. These results establish the Mn/Ru BMSAs as a po-
tent agent to reverse radioresistance and enhance radiotherapy
eﬃcacy in breast cancer.
