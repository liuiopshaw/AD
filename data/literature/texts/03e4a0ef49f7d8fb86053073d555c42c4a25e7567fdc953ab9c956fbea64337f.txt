ABSTRACT: The surface functionalization of nanomaterial is relevant in terms of its
selectivity toward a bacterial strain. Recently, semiconducting nanomaterials have been
gaining much attention in the development of nanomaterial-based alternative
antibiotics. Among the various semiconducting materials, molybdenum disulﬁde
(MoS2) has been gaining considerable interest due to its unique electronic, physical,
and chemical properties. The semiconducting nature, high colloidal stability, and
biocompatibility make it a promising candidate in the ﬁeld of biomedical research. The
development of antibacterial material based on MoS2 quantum dots is still in its infancy.
Here, we have explored the eﬀect of hydrophobic amino acid-modiﬁed MoS2 quantum
dots toward their antibacterial activity. Functionalization enhances the antibacterial activity and selectivity depending on the amino
acid residue. MoS2 quantum dots with a leucine-functionalized ligand show selective antibacterial activity against methicillin-resistant
Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), whereas MoS2 quantum dots with a phenylalanine-functionalized ligand show almost equal
antibacterial eﬃcacy against both MRSA and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA).
KEYWORDS: 2D-MoS2, nanoantibiotics, quantum dots, antibacterial activity, ROS
■INTRODUCTION
Infectious disease caused by pathogenic bacteria possesses a
serious threat to public health.1 The extensive use of
conventional antibiotics makes bacteria more resistant, and
hence the number of drug-resistant bacterial strains is ever
increasing.2 Therefore, there is an urgent need to design and
develop alternative antibacterial agents with high antibacterial
eﬃcacy and bacterial strain selectivity.3,4 In this respect, the
rapid development of nanomaterial-based antibiotics opens up
a new avenue to solve the bacterial resistant problem.5,6 To
date, there are several kinds of nanomaterials, such as gold
nanoparticles, silver nanoparticles, graphene-based materials,
peptides, etc., that have been explored for their high
antibacterial activity but with limited bacterial strain
selectivity.7−15 Although there are various nanomaterial-based
antibiotics reported, there is always a need to develop novel
nanomaterials with eﬃcient antibacterial activity, speciﬁcally
with selectivity.16
During the past few years, semiconductor nanomaterials
have gained tremendous attention in the ﬁeld of biomedi-
cine.17,18 Due to the semiconducting nature of these
nanomaterials, they generate electron−hole pairs.19 These
electron−hole pairs have strong oxidative and reductive
abilities to produce reactive oxygen species (ROS) when
reacting with dissolved oxygen and water.20 Typically, singlet
oxygen (1O2), superoxide anion radicals (•O2
−), hydroxyl
radicals (•OH), and hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) are known as
reactive oxygen species. These ROS can damage important
cellular components such as phospholipids, proteins, nucleic
acids, etc., which leads to cell lysis.21 This kind of ROS-
generating property of the semiconducting materials can be
enhanced in the presence of visible light irradiation. Taking
advantage of these properties, several studies have been carried
out to develop various phototherapy agents based on quantum
dots and various hybrid nanostructures (e.g., ZnO, TiO2).22−26
Although each system has its own advantages and disadvan-
tages, MoS2 nanosheet, a semiconducting material, has been
widely investigated as an antibacterial agent because of its
tunable band gap, high surface area, low toxicity, and the
possibility of surface modiﬁcation.27−30 With a decrease in the
lateral size of MoS2 from a few layers or a monolayer
nanosheet to quantum dots its band gap can be tuned, which
accentuates its photocatalytic behavior due to the stronger
quantum conﬁnement and edge eﬀects.31−33 The enhanced
photoluminescence properties due to the reduction in size
make MoS2 QDs a promising candidate for ﬂuorescence
sensing, bioimaging, etc.34,35 Moreover, the reduction in the
lateral size improves the dispersibility along with the increase
of the surface-to-volume ratio. This increase in surface-to-
volume ratio enhances the ease of surface functionalization,
which further increases the stability of the functionalized

Earlier, we have shown the high antibacterial activity of
MoS2 nanosheets, and there are several reports related to
this.27,28,36−38 Tian et al. have ﬁrst reported the antibacterial
activity of MoS2 quantum dots in the presence of simulated
solar light irradiation at 50 μg/mL concentration through the
generation of reactive oxygen species (ROS).39 But the
eﬀective concentration is signiﬁcantly higher compared to
that of the conventional nanomaterial-based antibiotics, and
more speciﬁcally, the selectivity was not explored. Recently,
Chen and co-workers have shown bacterial strain selectivity
based on diﬀerent nanostructured MoS2 prepared using the
galvanostatic electrolysis method where it was found that MoS2
with nanoﬂower morphology has higher antibacterial activity as
compared to nanosheets.40 Apart from unmodiﬁed MoS2, the
same technique was also implemented to construct metal
(Ag)-doped MoS2 nanoarchitecture for selective antibacterial
activity.41 But tuning of bacterial selectivity based on the
surface modiﬁcation of MoS2 QDs has not been reported so
far. It was well established that surface functionalization holds
the key to improved activity and selectivity. For instance,
functionalized gold nanoparticles can show enhanced anti-

bacterial activity and strain selectivity.42,43 Surface functional-
ization of nanomaterials has always proved to be an eﬀective
tool to modulate the nature of the interaction of material with
the bacterial surface. Therefore, in this work, we have
designated and developed bacterial strain selectivity against
Gram-positive methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus
(MRSA) and Gram-negative Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA)
bacteria in a very low concentration range through the surface
functionalization of hydrothermally prepared MoS2 quantum
dots with diﬀerent amino acid-derivatized ligands. Amino acids
can provide an ideal way to introduce functional diversity in
functionalized nanomaterials. Rotello’s group has shown that
with the increase of hydrophobicity and positive charge on the
nanomaterial surface, the antibacterial eﬃcacy increases.44
Keeping this in mind, we decided to incorporate a naturally
hydrophobic moiety provided by two diﬀerent amino acids:
phenylalanine and leucine. Phenylalanine can provide aromatic
hydrophobicity, whereas leucine can provide aliphatic hydro-
phobicity. We chose lipoic acid as a starting material to
conjugate with the amino acids, which further coupled with
unsymmetrical N,N-dimethylethylenediamine for the incorpo-
ration of positive charge at the end of the ligand system. The
presence of the amino acid moiety in the ligand system imparts
bacterial strain selectivity. As MoS2 QDs can produce more
ROS in the presence of light, therefore, we have extended our
study to check the eﬀect of white light toward the
enhancement of the antibacterial activity of the functionalized
MQDs. We found that the leucine-functionalized MQDs show
selective antibacterial activity against Gram-positive MRSA in a
very low concentration range, i.e., 0.62 ppm (0.62 μg/mL),
whereas they did not show any antibacterial activity against
Gram-negative bacteria PA even at 30 ppm (30 μg/mL)
concentration. For phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs, we
found almost equal eﬀectiveness toward the antibacterial
activity against both MRSA and PA. A mechanistic study
revealed that the ROS-mediated oxidative stress from the core
of the material and the membrane depolarization due to
surface functionalization were responsible for the higher and
selective antibacterial activity.
■RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Characterization and Functionalization of MoS2
Quantum Dots. MoS2 QDs were synthesized according to
a reported hydrothermal method by heating sodium molybdate
(Na2MoO4·2H2O) and cysteine mixed in a 1:2 ratio at 200 °C
for 36 h (Figure 1a).45 After completion of the reaction, the
solution was centrifuged at a speed of 12 000 rpm for 30 min.
In the end, the supernatant, which contains MoS2 QDs, was
collected. As synthesized MQDs were characterized using
photoluminescence (PL), atomic force microscopy (AFM),
transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and infrared (IR)
and UV−vis spectroscopies. The ﬂuorescence emission spectra
of MQDs show excitation-dependent emission (Figure 1b)
consistent with an earlier report.45 The presence of an
absorption band at 275 nm in UV−vis spectra supports the
formation of MQDs (Figure 1c). The AFM measurement
shows a height distribution of 0.9−1.2 nm (Figure 1d), which
signiﬁes the presence of monolayers. The TEM analysis of the
MoS2 quantum dots shows a monodisperse and uniform
nanocrystalline structure with an average diameter of 3−4 nm
(Figure 1e). The d spacing (0.32 nm) in high-resolution TEM
(HRTEM) (Figure 1f) further conﬁrms the presence of the
MoS2 nanostructure. Moreover, in the IR spectra of MQDs,
the peak near 3403 cm−1 corresponds to the N−H stretching
vibration and the peak near 1564 cm−1 corresponds to the N−
H bending vibration of cysteine, which is consistent with the
earlier report (Figure 1i).45
After conﬁrming the material, we proceeded with surface
functionalization. Lipoic acid was chosen as the starting
material for the preparation of amino acid-terminated ligands.
The advantage of using lipoic acid is that it is biocompatible,
and two thiol groups can introduce a stronger anchoring eﬀect
on the MoS2 QD surface, which can enhance the colloidal
stability of functionalized materials.46,47 The incorporation of
the positive charge at the end of the ligand system helps to
increase the electrostatic interaction with the bacterial surface
(Figure 1g).48 The detailed synthetic procedure is provided in
the Supporting Information. The synthesized ligands (LA_Phe
and LA_Leu) were then added to MQDs and stirred for 48 h
to achieve surface functionalization. The change in ζ potential
from −31.1 mV (for MQDs) to +35.6 mV (for leucine-
functionalized MQDs) and +21.8 mV (for phenylalanine-
functionalized MQDs) conﬁrmed the surface functionalization
(Figure 1h). Apart from ζ potential, the IR spectra of the
functionalized MQDs also support the surface functionaliza-
tion (Figure 1i). The peak between 2959 and 2854 cm−1 for
MQD_Phe and MQD_Leu corresponds to the asymmetric
stretching vibrations of −CH3 and −CH2−of the function-
alized ligands. Moreover, the peaks near 1714 cm−1 for
MQD_Phe and 1649 cm−1 for MQD_Leu correspond to the
CO bond of amide. The peak near 1650 cm−1 for
MQD_Phe is due to the CC bond of the phenyl group.
However, all of these ligands’ characteristic peaks are absent in
the IR spectra of nonfunctionalized MQDs. The UV−visible
spectra of the functionalized MQDs show the same absorption
proﬁle as that of the native quantum dots (Figure 1c). This
clearly suggests that after functionalization, the MoS2
nanostructure remains intact. Furthermore, the Raman spectra
also support the functionalization of MQDs. Typically, Raman
spectra of bulk MoS2 have two prominent peaks located at
around 383 and 407 cm−1 for lower-energy out-of-plane
vibration E2g
1 and the high-energy in-plane vibration Ag
1,
respectively.49 Cao et al. have shown that the Raman spectra of
MoS2 quantum dots synthesized by a bottom-up approach
show deviation in both E2g
1 and Ag
1 modes compared to the
bulk MoS2.50 Along the same line, the as synthesized MoS2
quantum dots show two intense peaks near 340 and 380 cm−1
(Figure S6). In functionalized MQDs, these peaks are further
shifted. LA_Phe functionalized MQDs show the peaks at 344
and 388 cm−1, and LA_Leu functionalized MQDs show the
peaks near 365 and 402 cm−1 (Figure S6). This shifting of the
Raman peaks in the functionalized materials compared to the
native MQDs supports the functionalization. Following the
surface functionalization, the concentration of the materials
was measured using inductively coupled plasma-mass spec-
trometry (ICP-MS), and then we move toward antibacterial
activity.
Antibacterial Activity. The antibacterial eﬃcacy of
functionalized MQDs was measured from the growth kinetics
of MRSA and PA bacteria in the presence of functionalized
material. To check the growth kinetics, bacterial solutions
having OD = 0.01 (106−107 CFU/mL) were incubated with
diﬀerent concentrations of materials. Then, over a period of 14
h, the increase in optical density was measured. The
concentration at which no bacterial growth was observed in
the growth curve is known as the minimum inhibitory

signiﬁcant amounts of colonies are formed (Figure 2d). This

Next, we have investigated the antibacterial eﬀect of
phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs against MRSA and PA.
In contrast to the leucine-functionalized MQDs, phenyl-
alanine-functionalized MQDs show almost equal eﬀectiveness
toward the antibacterial activity against both MRSA and PA
(Figure 3). In the case of MRSA, the MIC value of
phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs was observed at 7 ppm
(Figure 3a), and in the case of PA, it was 4 ppm (Figure 3c).
Following the same trend, the MBC value for MRSA was 9
ppm (Figure 3b) and for PA, it was 5 ppm (Figure 3d). The
eﬀect of white light toward the enhancement of antibacterial
activity was further explored for phenylalanine-functionalized
MQDs also. The MIC value in the presence of light was
reduced to 4 ppm for MRSA and 2 ppm for PA (Figure S9).
Likewise, the MBC value was also reduced to 4 ppm for MRSA
and 2 ppm for PA (Figure S9).
Hence, we can conclude that the functionalized MQDs not
only show higher antibacterial activity but also impart bacterial
selectivity. In earlier studies, researchers have shown the
antimicrobial activity of MoS2 nanosheets and MoS2 quantum
dots but have never explored bacterial strain selectivity based
on surface modiﬁcation. The reason for the diﬀerent
antibacterial activities of the amino acid residue could be
because of the diﬀerent hydrophobicity indexes, structures, and
speciﬁc biomolecular interactions of the side chain.51,52 The
cell wall of Gram-positive bacteria is mainly composed of
peptidoglycan layers, whereas the cell wall of Gram-negative
bacteria is more complex than that of Gram-positive bacteria.
The Gram-negative bacteria contain an outer lipopolysacchar-
ide layer over the peptidoglycan layers. The presence of the
outer lipopolysaccharide layers leads to less cell permeability of
foreign particles. At experimental conditions (pH 7.4),
phenylalanine is more hydrophobic than leucine. Therefore,
phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs can depolarize both
Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria easily whereas
leucine-functionalized MQDs are unable to depolarize the
lipopolysaccharide layers of Gram-negative bacteria. This in
turn imparts the selectivity of leucine-functionalized MQDs
against Gram-positive bacteria MRSA.
Mechanistic Study of the High Antibacterial Activity
and Selectivity. To explore the origin of the higher
antibacterial activity and bacterial selectivity, we have carried
out Ellman’s assay to quantify the ROS generations from the
core of the material and membrane depolarization assay to
probe the eﬀect of surface functionalization on the bacterial
membrane. In Ellman’s assay, the percentage loss of
glutathione by the functionalized materials was estimated
using 0.4 mM glutathione incubated with 0.5 ppm of
functionalized MoS2 QDs in the dark and in the presence of
light. After incubation for over a period of 3 h, we found that
the loss of glutathione in the dark was 25% (Figure 4a)
whereas the loss in the presence of light was 50% (Figure 4b),
which directly correlates the higher antibacterial activity of the
functionalized materials in the presence of light. To evaluate
the origin of bacterial selectivity, we have used the 3,3-
dipropylthiadicarbocyanine iodide (DISC3(5)) ﬂuorescent
probe, which accumulates in the interior membrane when
incubated with bacterial cells. Any material that causes
depolarization of the bacterial surface resulted in the release
of the dye, which is estimated from the enhancement of
ﬂuorescence. In the case of MRSA, we can observe that
depolarization is more for leucine-functionalized MQDs,
followed by the phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs (Figure
4c), which matches the trend in the antibacterial activity.
Whereas in the case of PA, depolarization is signiﬁcantly high
for phenylalanine-functionalized MQDs, but leucine-function-
alized MQDs show insuﬃcient depolarization (Figure 4d),
which is directly related to bacterial selectivity.

Further, the morphology change of the bacterial cell was
observed through SEM imaging. MRSA bacteria when treated
with both leucine-functionalized MQDs and phenylalanine-
functionalized MQDs show a signiﬁcant change in morphology
(Figure 5b,c). When PA was treated with leucine-function-
alized MQDs, no signiﬁcant change in morphology was
observed (Figure 5e). But PA treated with phenylalanine-
functionalized MQDs causes serious damage with the loss of
membrane integrity (Figure 5f). Hence, all of the above studies
suggest that functionalized MoS2 QDs show excellent
antibacterial activity and bacterial selectivity. The cellular
toxicity of MQDs and functionalized MQDs was studied by
considering HeLa cell lines. No considerable cytotoxicity of
MQDs and functionalized MQDs was observed up to 20 ppm
concentration (Figure S10), which further supports the
biocompatibility of the functionalized MQDs.
■CONCLUSIONS
In conclusion, surface modiﬁcation plays a key role in the
development of nanomaterial-based antibiotics. In this report,
we have shown that amino acid residues can introduce
bacterial strain selectivity between Gram-positive MRSA and
Gram-negative PA and higher antibacterial activity. The
leucine-functionalized MoS2 QDs are selective against MRSA
whereas the phenylalanine-functionalized MoS2 QDs are
equally eﬀective against both MRSA and PA. The mechanistic
study revealed that both oxidative stress generation from the
core of the materials and membrane depolarization resulted in
high antibacterial activity and selectivity. This strategy can be
extended with various other functionalities, such as small
peptides, dendrimers, saccharides, etc., or even with diﬀerent
nanomaterials to tune the antibacterial activity together with
bacterial selectivity.
