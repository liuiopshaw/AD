ABSTRACT: Chiral metal nanoclusters have recently been attracting great
attention. It is challenging to realize asymmetric catalysis via atomically precise
metal nanoclusters. Herein, we report the synthesis and total structure
determination of chiral clusters [Au7Ag8(dppf)3(L-/D-proline)6](BF4)2 (L-/D-
Au7Ag8). Superatomic clusters L-/D-Au7Ag8 display intense and mirror-image
Cotton effects in their CD spectra. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
were carried out to understand the correlation between electronic structures and
the optical activity of the enantiomeric pair. Surprisingly, the incorporation of
proline in a metal nanocluster can significantly promote the catalytic efficiency in
asymmetric Aldol reactions. The increase of catalytic activity of Au7Ag8 in
comparison with organocatalysis by proline is attributed to the cooperative effect
of the metal core and prolines, showing the advantages of the integration of metal
catalysis and organocatalysis in a metal nanocluster.
■INTRODUCTION
Great attention has been paid to chiral metal materials in
nanoscience research because of the controllable chiral
response,1−4 unique chirality transfer mechanism,5 and their
broad applications in optical devices,6−8 medicine design,9
biorecognition,9 cell imaging,3 and enantioselective cataly-
sis.10−13 Among these nanomaterials, chiral metal nanoclusters
with precise structures have demonstrated advantages in
unraveling the relationship between structures and proper-
ties.14−19 In-depth investigations of novel chiral clusters are
triggered by advances in synthetic strategies and character-
ization techniques.17,20,21 Compared with chiral high-perform-
ance liquid chromatography (HPLC) column22−25 or counter-
ion-pairing26,27 strategies, chiral ligand induction provides a
practical and efficient protocol for the synthesis of chiral metal
nanoclusters.28−35 However, the contribution of atomically
precise nanoclusters to asymmetric reactions has not been
achieved due to the lack of specific catalytic sites.
Asymmetric reactions are powerful in constructing optically
active organic compounds with pharmaceutical activity.
Biomolecules such as amino acids (AAs) and quinine
derivatives have been tapped for asymmetric synthesis due to
their efficient catalytic activity and their abundance in
nature.36,37 Among different catalytic systems, biomolecule-
catalyzed C−C bond formation such as Aldol asymmetric
condensation has gained broad recognition. List and his co-
workers have demonstrated the power of prolines in the
asymmetric Aldol reaction,38 and organic chemists also tested
the catalytic activity and stereoselectivity of aldolases and other
AAs.39,40 The stereoselectivity of the Aldol reaction is in
general controlled by the steric environments of active AAs.
However, low turnover numbers might limit the potential
applications of AAs. To overcome this intrinsic drawback,
tremendous efforts have been directed toward the discovery
and design of proline derivatives with better efficiency and
higher turnover numbers in the last decades.41−45 To this end,
we have developed a strategy via covalently immobilizing AAs
on well-defined metal nanoclusters, which facilitates double-
fold advantages: (1) the integration of organocatalysis and
metal catalysis may lead to remarkable stereocontrol and high
efficiency in asymmetric Aldol reactions and (2) the well-
defined catalytic system benefits the understanding of the
mechanism. Herein, we report a well-defined hybrid catalyst

Computational Results. Au7Ag8 is an ideal model
providing insights into the effect and mechanism of AAs on
the optical activity of metal nanoparticles. The correlation
between the optical activity and geometrical structure of
Au7Ag8 was further studied by time-dependent DFT
calculations. As shown in Figure 5a, the calculated electronic
absorption spectrum of Au7Ag8 is in good agreement with the
experimental result. The peaks at 350, 400, and 484 nm are
well reproduced. Simulated CD spectra of L/D-Au7Ag8 are also
presented in Figure 5b. The prominent peak I (λexp = 350 nm)
arises primarily from HOMO −1 →LUMO + 7. This
absorption peak (λexp = 420 nm) accounts for CD band II,
which mainly corresponds to the HOMO →LUMO + 4
transition. The third CD peak around 480 nm is related to the
absorption peak III at 480 nm, which is mainly ascribed to the
HOMO →LUMO + 1 transition (1P state to 1D state).
Natural transition orbitals (NTO)52 show the feature of the
three signature transitions, and frontier molecular orbitals are
located over the metal core (Figure S9). Frontier molecular
orbital composition study can further confirm the result that
the HOMO −1, HOMO, LUMO, LUMO + 1, LUMO + 4,
and LUMO + 7 are mainly contributed by the metal core
(Table S2). In addition, frontier molecular orbitals contain
non-negligible contributions from proline and dppf. Therefore,
proline and dppf have a significant effect on the electronic
structure and the CD response.

List et al. reported the enamine-based mechanism for the
proline-catalyzed Aldol reaction.38 We propose a synergistic
mechanism, which is illustrated in Figure 6. Hirshfeld charge
analysis54 of Au7Ag8 reveals that partial remaining charge can
provide electrostatic force to anchor carbonyl compounds
including acetone and aldehyde (Figure S16 and Table S3). In
Figure S17, the r2scan-3c composite method55 was used to deal
with interactions between acetone and open sites of Au7Ag8
surface. Ag7 and Ag8 with high atomic charge are chosen as
simulated acetone adsorption sites (labeled as yellow atoms in
Figure S17). The results show that the CO···Ag7 and C
O···Ag8 distances are 2.56 and 2.64 Å, and the Mayer bond
order56 of CO···Ag7 and CO···Ag8 is 0.13, indicative of
weak coordination interaction between Ag and carbonyl
oxygen. Therefore, the carbonyl activation by the metal core
should be considered in the reaction mechanism. Initially, the
acetone is activated by a Au−Ag bimetallic core via
coordination of the carbonyl oxygen, and the intermolecular
nucleophilic attack by the amino group in L-proline gives rise
to L-enamine intermediate (III). In this case, the metal core
function as Lewis acids near the pyrrolidine sites and thereby
promote the generation of
L-enamine. Subsequently, the
aldehyde is activated by Ag, and the electron-rich −CH2
group in enamine stereospecifically adds to the aldehyde
group, leading to the generation of alkoxide via a six-membered
transition state (IV). The cyclic transition state consists of L-
enamine, carbonyl group, and proton provided by the
C4H7NH2
+COO−group. The aldehyde orientation in the
transition state contributes to the enantiofacial selectivity for
the (R)-product. Proton of C4H7NH2
+COO−is transferred to
the carbonyl group of aldehyde. Subsequently, the formation of
C−C and O−H bonds gives rise to the intermediate V. Hence,
the iminium ion of intermediate V is subject to the
nucleophilic attack of H2O, forming the hydramine inter-
mediate VI. The subsequent N−C bond cleavage gives directly
the chiral product. Generally, catalysts with multiple catalytic
sites can facilitate a cooperative effect. Considering the
synergistic effect of Ag salts and Au core in organic
synthesis,57,58 the carbonyl activation of Au7Ag8 core accounts
for the promotion of the catalytic activity. So far, there have
been various reports about the combination of aminocatalysis
and transition metal catalysis;59−61 however, there is no
precedent dealing with the integration of metal catalysis and
organocatalysis in a nanocluster with regard to asymmetric
catalysis. Based on the proposed reaction mechanism, the
asymmetric Aldol reaction can be accomplished by nano-
clusters via combining enamine catalysis and CO activation,
showing the power of synergistic catalysis.
Furthermore, compared with net ProAg or prolines, the
catalytic sites (pyrrolidine moieties) of prolines immobilized
on clusters are more accessible to substrates (Figure 7).
Usually, Ag clusters are unstable, while in the L-/D-Au7Ag8
nanocluster, Aun can act as a glue of ProAg to enhance its
stability. According to the strong-bond rule and heterobond
rule,62 the Au−Ag bond energy is greater than the Ag−Ag
bond energy, and aurophilicity is also accountable for the
stabilization of nanoclusters.63 Thereby, employment of Au can
contribute to the stability enhancement of the cluster skeleton.

activation by the Au−Ag bimetallic core. In addition, the
arrangement of prolines on the Au7Ag8 surface can increase the
accessibility of catalytic sites to substrates. We develop a
concept of the integration of metal core activation and
aminocatalysis in a chiral nanocluster catalyst, which is a novel
strategy for the dual activation in a synergistic manner.
Furthermore, the insights from this work are expected to take a
pivotal step toward rational design of nanocluster-based
asymmetric catalysts.
■EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
Synthesis of L-Au7Ag8. 3 mL CH2Cl2 solution of dppfAu2(BF4)2
(0.02 mmol) was added to a 3.0 mL methanol suspension containing
L-Pro-Ag (8.8 mg, 0.04 mmol). Then, a freshly prepared solution of
NaBH4 (0.30 mg in 0.5 mL of ethanol) was added dropwise under
vigorous stirring. After 5 min, 0.5 mL methanol solution of AgBF4
(0.02 mmol) was added to the mixture at room temperature. The
solution color changed from yellow to orange and finally to dark red.
Then the reaction continued for 12 h at room temperature in air in
the dark. The mixture was evaporated to dryness to give a dark red
solid, which was washed with water (2 × 5 mL), dried in vacuum
drying oven overnight, and dissolved in CH2Cl2 (1.5 mL) with two
drops of methanol and centrifuged for 3 min at 10,000 rpm. The
supernatant solution was subject to the diffusion of n-hexane to afford
11.8 mg of black crystals after one week (45% yield based on Au).
