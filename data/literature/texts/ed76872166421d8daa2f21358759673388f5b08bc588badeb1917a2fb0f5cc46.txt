Ultrasound Imaging Hot Paper
Enhanced Ultrasound Contrast of Renal-Clearable Luminescent Gold
Nanoparticles
Yue Tan+, Miaona Chen+, Huarui Chen, Juefei Wu,* and Jinbin Liu*
Abstract: Renal-clearable nanoparticles are typically fast
eliminated through the free glomerular filtration, which show
weak interaction with the renal compartments and negligible
ultrasound signals, raising challenges in direct imaging of
kidney diseases. Here, we report the ultrasmall renal-clearable
luminescent gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) with both pH-
induced charge reversal and aggregation properties, and
discover that enhanced ultrasound contrast could be facilely
acquired through the increased tubular reabsorption and in situ
aggregation of AuNPs in renal tubule cells in injured kidneys.
The tuning elimination pathway of the renal-clearable lumi-
nescent AuNPs is further demonstrated to provide a synerg-
istical fluorescence and ultrasound imaging strategy for
diagnosing early kidney injury with precise anatomical infor-
mation.
Renal clearance is a clear requirement for future success of
contrast agents in clinical practices[1] and involves the first
glomerular filtration, followed by tubular secretion and
tubular reabsorption (from tubular lumen to peritubular
capillaries).[2] The ultrasmall renal-clearable luminescent gold
nanoparticles (AuNPs, d < 3 nm)[3] following the glomerular
filtration pathway, are very suitable for targeting tumor[4] and
monitoring kidney clearance kinetics,[5] but fail to target and
permeate primary injured kidney compartments due to the
weak interaction with renal tubular cells.[6] Considering that
the renal tubular cell (RTC) is the primary target of injury and
progression of kidney disease,[7] the fundamental understand-
ing of the interactions and transport of renal-clearable
nanoparticles with renal tubules is essential to designing
strategies for targeting of injured RTC. Therefore, the design
of renal-clearable luminescent AuNPs with enhanced tubular
reabsorption is extremely important in interaction enhance-
ment with RTCs and further improvement of permeability
and retention time in kidney, allowing to fully investigate the
correlation between renal transport of AuNPs and their
ability of RTC targeting.
The intrinsic emission of AuNPs facilitates the fluorescent
bioimaging, but it is hard to provide anatomical information
of kidney compartments due to the limited penetration.
Contrast-enhanced ultrasound imaging is a noninvasive and
accurate tool for evaluation of internal organ lesions.[8] The
conventional ultrasound contrast agents are typically from
several hundred nanometers (e.g., perfluorocarbon emulsion
particles,[9] echogenic liposomes[10] and gas-filled polylactic
acid nano-dispersions[11]) to several micrometers (e.g., lipid
surrounded gaseous bubbles,[12] surfactant polymers[13] and
proprietary shell materials[14]) to maintain high innate reflec-
tion and backscattering capabilities required for high ultra-
sound imaging sensitivity.[15] However, the large sizes of
ultrasound contrast agents would raise a challenge to pass
through the vessels to renal parenchyma.[16] Although aggre-
gation of particles could enhance the ultrasound contrast in
solution,[17] the micrometer-sized aggregates could not be
filtered through glomeruli and also cause severe accumulation
in healthy organs.[18] Therefore, how to generate enhanced
ultrasound contrast with renal-clearable luminescent AuNPs
is of great importance in fundamentally understanding of the
in vivo nanoparticle transport, and further guides to design
multi-modality nanoprobes toward precise diagnosis of
kidney injure.
The acidosis-induced early kidney injury exhibits urinary
acidification in renal tubule,[19] but it is difficult to differ-
entiate using the routine clinical makers (e.g., blood urea
nitrogen (BUN) and creatinine (Cr)),[20] which need timely
renoprotective intervention to prevent from progressing into
more severe stages including inflammatory and fibrosis over
time.[21] Herein, we designed the renal-clearable luminescent
PEGylated AuNPs co-coated with pH-responsive zwitterionic
imidazole groups (PMIZ-AuNPs, Figure S1a), which showed
both charge-reversal and aggregation capabilities toward
kidney acidic microenvironments to enhance tubular reab-
sorption of the particles and generate both fluorescence and
enhanced ultrasound signals. The PMIZ-AuNPs were effi-
ciently eliminated out of the normal kidney through the
glomerular filtration, but being retained in tubular cells of
injured kidney due to enhanced reabsorption induced by the
acidic environments of renal tubule lumens. The increased
reabsorption of PMIZ-AuNPs not only promoted the high
retention of AuNPs, but also resulted in situ aggregation of
particles in renal tubular cells, enhancing the ultrasound
contrast. These results provided a facile and robust bimodal
imaging strategy through tuning the elimination pathways of
renal-clearable nanomedicines to diagnose early kidney
injury.

To acquire the anatomical information, ultrasound imag-
ing was performed after iv injection of AuNPs into the injured
mice (Figure 3a). Negligible ultrasound signal was found for
typical glomerular-clearable GS-AuNPs[5c,d] in injured or
normal kidneys, similar to those with saline as background.
However, the injured kidney after iv injection of PMIZ-
AuNPs exhibited an obviously enhanced ultrasound signal at
10 min p.i. and remained up to 240 min p.i., suggesting the
enhanced tubular reabsorptions of PMIZ-AuNPs in acidic
microenvironment. Interestingly, the normal kidney showed
no significant change in ultrasound intensities, indicating the
elimination of PMIZ-AuNPs in normal kidneys through rapid
free glomerular filtration, consistent with those of the
glomerular-clearable GS-AuNPs. The values of increased
ultrasound intensity (DI, Figure 3b) of the injured kidney
treated with PMIZ-AuNPs increased firstly to  20 at 30 min
p.i. and remained steady at this high value until to 240 min p.i.
The values of increased ultrasound intensity in parenchyma
were much higher than those in pelvis (Figure 3c and d),
which resulted in 4 times increase of the area under curve
(AUC) than those of the pelvis. These results confirmed that
the charge reversal of PMIZ-AuNPs induced by acidic
microenvironment in tubular lumen improved the lumen
cell uptake (tubular reabsorptions) of AuNPs, and the pH-
induced aggregation in tubular cell not only prolonged the
retention time of AuNPs in the injured kidney, but also
enhanced the ultrasound signal for precisely and noninva-
sively imaging of kidney injury. As control for the AuNPs with
large core size (13 nm) with the similar coating of both PEG-
SH and MIZ-H (LPMIZ-AuNPs, Figure S13), undetectable
ultrasound signal enhancement was observed in injured or
normal kidneys (Figure S14), further demonstrating ultra-
small size of the renal-clearable luminescent AuNPs would
benefit the ultrasound imaging of kidney injury.
The histological analysis of PMIZ-AuNPs distribution in
different kidney compartments further confirmed the suc-
cessful quantification of early kidney injury, resulting from
the enhanced tubular reabsorption of the PMIZ-AuNPs
(Figure 4a). With silver enhancement, the PMIZ-AuNPs
were mainly found inside the tubular cells in the injured
kidneys at 7 h p.i. (Figure 4a and S15), different from the
normal kidney that appeared in glomeruli, tubular lumens
and peritubular capillaries (Figure 4a and S16), which were
also distinct from the injured kidneys at 5 min or 24 h. p.i. that
mainly appeared in tubular lumens (Figure S17). In addition,
the glomerular-clearable GS-AuNPs (Figure 4a) as control
were visualized in the glomeruli and tubular lumens and
peritubular capillaries of injured or normal kidneys, but few
inside tubular cells in injured or normal kidney. The increased
blood concentrations of PMIZ-AuNPs were observed from
the injured mice other than the normal ones from 3 to 12 h p.i.
(Figure 4b), demonstrating that the PMIZ-AuNPs could be
transported back into the blood plasma, which confirmed the
reabsorption of PMIZ-AuNPs in injured kidney. Therefore, it
could be speculated that unlike the glomerular filtration
happened in normal kidneys (Figure 4c), the tubular lumen
acidification caused the charge reversal of PMIZ-AuNPs from
negative to positive to increase lumen cell uptake of AuNPs,
followed by aggregation of AuNPs in tubular cell to enhance
the retention in injured kidneys, which promoted the ultra-
sound signal to provide a feasible strategy for both fluores-

In summary, we discovered that ultrasound signal of
ultrasmall renal-clearable luminescent AuNPs with pH-
induced charge reversal and aggregation could be acquired
for both noninvasively fluorescence and ultrasound imaging
of early kidney injury. The pH-induced tubular reabsorption
enhancement and in situ aggregation of luminescent AuNPs
in tubular cells of the injured kidney was demonstrated to
show prolonged retention of AuNPs, resulting in enhanced
ultrasound signal for noninvasively imaging of kidney injury
with precise anatomical information. The discovery of tubular
reabsorption of the renal-clearable metal nanoparticles in
acidic kidneys showed uniqueness in synergistical fluores-
cence and ultrasound imaging toward early diagnosis and
treatments of specific renal diseases. Future work is highly
demanded to further improve the ultrasound imaging contrast
of the ultrasmall renal-clearable metal nanoparticles to
stimulate more advanced biological applications.
