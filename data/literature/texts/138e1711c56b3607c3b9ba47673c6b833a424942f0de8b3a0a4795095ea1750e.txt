ABSTRACT: Ever increasing infectious diseases caused by
pathogenic bacteria are creating one of the greatest health
problems. The extensive use of numerous antibiotics and
antimicrobial agents has prompted the growth of multidrug-
resistant bacterial strains. The ancient biomedical application of
metals and the recent advancement in the ﬁeld of nanotechnology
have encouraged us to explore the antimicrobial activity of
nanomaterials. Herein, we have synthesized a magnetically
separable superparamagnetic nickel nanocluster-loaded two-dimen-
sional molybdenum disulﬁde nanocomposite (Ni@2D-MoS2). It
can selectively bind with Gram-positive bacteria such as
methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and Enter-
ococcus faecalis over Gram-negative bacteria such as Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. After the functionalization of
Ni@2D-MoS2 with a positively charged ligand, it showed an excellent Gram-selective antibacterial activity toward MRSA and E.
faecalis. Furthermore, the superparamagnetic property of the synthesized material can be used for the simultaneous removal and
killing of the microbes and recycled for further use. This study demonstrates strategies to develop hybrid antimicrobial nanomaterial
systems for selective antibacterial activity with recyclability.
KEYWORDS: 2D molybdenum disulﬁde (2D-MoS2), nickel nanocluster, antibacterial activity, gram selectivity, magnetic nanocomposite
1. INTRODUCTION
The emergence of antimicrobial resistance in pathogenic
bacteria has become a global threat and is leading to a serious
medical crisis.1 In the conventional treatment of infectious
diseases, boundless usage of antibiotics and antimicrobial
agents has compelled microorganisms to develop resistance to
these drugs, and endangering the biomedical ﬁeld.2 Hence,
there is an urgent need for a new generation of antimicrobial
materials to confront the prevalence of infectious diseases.
These drug-resistant microbes also cause environmental threat
by contaminating natural water resources. The current
progress in the ﬁeld of nanotechnology has helped to probe
safe, cost-eﬀective, and potent antimicrobial treatment
strategies to combat against bacterial infections. Silver metal,
which possesses antimicrobial ability, was ﬁrst reported in
1676; subsequently, silver-coated containers were used for
storing water, wine, etc. to control bacterial contamination.3,4
Recently, nanomaterials owing to their unique structure,
properties, and good biocompatibility have attracted a great
deal of attention as an antimicrobial system, where nanosized
materials exhibit superior antibacterial activity compared to
that of their bulk counterparts.5−7 Metal-based nanomaterials
such as palladium and silver nanoparticles,8,9 functionalized
gold nanoparticles,10 carbon-based nanomaterials including
graphene and graphene oxide,11,12 carbon nanotubes, and their
derivatives have been widely reported to show a promising
outcome for ﬁghting against microbial infections.13,14 In
particular, the photothermal activity of iron oxide-loaded
graphene oxide and photothermally activated antimicrobial
peptide has been used to kill bacteria.15,16 Several metal oxide-
based nanomaterials including ZnO, TiO2, and NiO also
exhibit bactericidal activity.17−20 Lately, it has been reported
that the photoregulated oxidase-like activity of palladium-based
cage molecules showed high antibacterial characteristics
against MRSA.21 Two-dimensional supramolecular polymers
were also used to eradicate the bioﬁlm.22
Moreover, various transition metal dichalcogenides, partic-
ularly MoS2 nanosheets due to their diverse properties such as
easily tunable colloidal behavior, nature of speciﬁc interactions,
and reactivity through surface modiﬁcation, helped to explore

their potential applications.23−25 The high drug loading
eﬃciency, physiological stability, and biocompatibility of 2D-
MoS2-based nanomaterials have encouraged their application
in various biomedical ﬁelds.26−28 In our earlier studies, it has
been shown that chemically exfoliated MoS2 nanosheets
functionalized with diﬀerent charge-terminated thiol ligands
were used in combination therapy against multidrug-resistant
bacteria MRSA.29 Surface-functionalized 2D-MoS2 has also
shown great potential to eradicate the pathogenic bacteria.30,31
However, most of these reported materials showed bactericidal
activity against multidrug-resistant bacterial strains without any
Gram selectivity. The use of nonselective antimicrobial agents
would disrupt the balance of microbial ﬂora and may cause
unanticipated outcome. This also creates the prevalence of
antimicrobial resistance.32 To minimize these fearsome
developments, scientists have introduced strain-selective
bactericidal strategies, which are based on the structure,
composition, and surface properties of the antimicrobial
agents.33−38
As the Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria diﬀer in
their cell wall composition, they may interact with the
nanomaterials in a diﬀerent fashion.39 Hence, these bacteria
can show a diﬀerent adhesion propensity toward the
nanomaterials. The bacterial adhesion on the surface of the
nanomaterial helps to disrupt the cell wall integrity and leads
to cell death.40−43 Barman et al. showed that the self-assembled
polyurethane capsule exhibits selective antibacterial activity
against the Gram-negative bacteria Escherichia Coli.44 Latter
from our group, it has been reported that amino acid-
functionalized MoS2 quantum dots showed selective anti-
bacterial activity.45 Furthermore, nanomaterials having mag-
netic properties, such as iron/nickel/cobalt-based nano-
particles, have been widely used as nanocarriers for drug
delivery.46,47 In addition, various surface-functionalized mag-
netic nanoparticles have been used to separate bacteria
magnetically from the bacterial solution.48,49 Until now, to
the best of our knowledge, using superparamagnetic nano-
composite materials that exhibit high Gram-selective bacterial
adhesion and antibacterial activity has not been reported so far.
However, recently, one report came where they have prepared
superparamagnetic nickel colloidal nanocrystals that can bind
with bacteria without any signiﬁcant antibacterial activity.50
In this study, we have reported the synthesis of a
superparamagnetic nickel nanoparticle-loaded MoS2 nano-
composite material (Ni@2D-MoS2) that showed Gram-
selective bacterial binding ability in the case of Gram-positive
bacteria such as MRSA and Enterococcus faecalis, whereas
Gram-negative bacteria such as E. coli and Pseudomonas
aeruginosa did not eﬀectively interact with Ni@2D-MoS2.
Utilizing the very high tendency of the MoS2 nanosheets
toward the thiol ligand molecule, Ni@2D-MoS2 has been
functionalized with a positively charged head group containing
a thiol ligand. As a result, the positively charged ligand-
functionalized composite material, positive-Ni@2D-MoS2,
exhibits selective antibacterial activity against MRSA and E.
faecalis bacterial strains. The combined eﬀect of the bacterial
adhesion ability along with the surface functionalization of the
Ni@2D-MoS2 composite enhanced the bactericidal activity
manifold. Additionally, after the intended use of the super-
paramagnetic positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite material,
its magnetic property permits magnetic separation of positive-
Ni@2D-MoS2 from the solution using an external magnet,
which was recycled for further application in antimicrobial
activity. The central objective of this study is represented in

2. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION
2.1. Materials and Instruments. Molybdenum disulﬁde (MoS2),
n-butyllithium, tetra ethylene glycol (TEG), diethylene glycol (DEG),
nickel (II) chloride (NiCl2, 98%), 11-bromo-1-undecene, trimethyl-
amine, and sodium hydride were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. All
other chemicals including tri-sodium citrate dihydrate (NaCit, ≥99%)
and sodium hydroxide pellets (NaOH) were purchased from SDFine
(India) and used as received. Milli-Q water was used throughout our
studies. Atomic force microscopy (AFM, JPK instrument), scanning
electron microscopy (SEM, Ultra-55 FE-SEM Karl Zeiss EDS), and
transmission electron microscopy (TEM, JOEL JEM-2100F) were
performed to characterize exfoliated MoS2 and the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite. Image J software was used to determine the d
spacing. Raman spectra (LabRAM HR) and PXRD (PANalytical
XRD) spectra were analyzed to ﬁnd out the phase and the
composition of the materials. XPS (AXIS ULTRA) was used to
conﬁrm the valence state of the elements present in the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite. The magnetic measurements were done with a
Quantum Design MPMS−XL EverCool SQUID magnetometer. The

2.2. Preparation of Exfoliated MoS2. The monolayer exfoliated
MoS2 was obtained from the exfoliation of bulk MoS2 using a
modiﬁed lithium intercalation method.51 In brief, 300 mg of bulk
MoS2 powder was transferred into a vial that contains a magnetic bar
and set on a magnetic stirrer inside a glovebox under an inert
atmosphere. About 6 mL of n-butyl lithium solution (1.6 M, in
hexane) was added to it and stirred for 48 h. Then, the lithium-
intercalated MoS2 solution was centrifuged and washed with hexane
repeatedly to remove excess n-butyl lithium. Afterward, 100 mL of
water was added followed by sonication for 1 h to hydrolyze the
intercalated Li atoms. Then, the solution was centrifuged at 10,000
rpm three times, and the supernatant was discarded. To remove any
nonexfoliated MoS2, the black precipitate was redispersed in water
and centrifuged at 2000 rpm, and ﬁnally, the exfoliated MoS2 (2D-
MoS2)-containing supernatant solution was collected.
2.3. Synthesis of the Ni@2D-MoS2 Nanocomposite. The
synthesis of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite was carried out by in
situ preparation of the superparamagnetic nickel nanoparticle
followed by adsorption of nickel nanoparticle on 2D-MoS2. First,
thermal hydrolysis and reduction of NiCl2 in DEG with NaCit and
NaOH were performed. In short, NaOH (26 mg) was dissolved in
DEG (2 mL) by heating at 150 °C. In a 50 mL double-neck ﬂask
containing 10 mg of exfoliated MoS2 along with a magnetic pellet,
22.1 mg of NiCl2, 22 mg of NaCit, and 10 mL of DEG were
transferred and heated at 200 °C for 30 min under a N2 atmosphere
with mechanical stirring. Then, the previously prepared NaOH/DEG
mixture solution was rapidly injected into the reaction mixture. After
2 h of continuous heating at 200 °C, the reaction was cooled down to
room temperature. The superparamagnetic Ni@2D-MoS2 nano-
composite was magnetically separated and repeatedly washed with
ethanol and water and ﬁnally stored in ethanol.
2.4. Ligand Functionalization of Materials. The ligand
functionalization of layered Ni@2D-MoS2 and 2D-MoS2 was
accomplished by following our previously reported protocol.52 Brieﬂy,
10 mg of the ligand molecule was transferred into a vial and dissolved
in 8 mL of water. Then, the aqueous solution of Ni@2D-MoS2 and
2D-MoS2 containing 5 mg each was added to the ligand solution. The
mixture was sonicated for few minutes. Next, the solutions were set on
the magnetic stirrer and vigorously stirred for 24 h. After stirring,
dialysis of the functionalized material solutions was carried out using
snakeskin dialysis membrane 10,000 MWCO (Thermo scientiﬁc) to
remove any unbound ligand molecules.

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1. Preparation of the Ni@2D-MoS2 Nanocomposite.
Chemically exfoliated 2D-MoS2 (2D-MoS2) was prepared from
the bulk MoS2 using the lithium intercalation method.51
Atomic force microscopy (AFM) imaging exhibits the single-
layer MoS2 nanosheet formation with an average thickness of
1.0 nm (Figure 1a). Afterward, the superparamagnetic nickel
nanocluster-loaded 2D-MoS2 was prepared by pyrolysis of
NiCl2 under a nitrogen atmosphere at 200 °C in the presence
of 2D-MoS2 in diethylene glycol (DEG). The nickel nano-
cluster was formed by the reduction of Ni2+ ions into zero-
valent Ni in the presence of NaOH and DEG. Subsequently,
the in situ generated nickel nanocluster was embedded onto
the surface of the MoS2 nanosheets to form the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite. In the nanointerfaces between the nickel
nanoclusters and the MoS2 nanosheets, the Ni atoms are
coordinated with the S atoms through donation of a lone pair
of electrons. Hence, the S atoms in 2D-MoS2 act as bridging
atoms between the Ni and Mo atoms in the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite.53 Trisodium citrate was used to stabilize the
synthesized nickel nanocluster.
The morphology of the synthesized nickel nanocluster-
loaded Ni@2D-MoS2 composite was characterized by AFM,
transmission electron microscopy (TEM), and scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) imaging. The AFM image of
Ni@2D-MoS2 indicates that the monolayer nature of 2D-MoS2
remains in the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite with an average
thickness of 1.2 nm (Figure 1b). The lateral size of the Ni@
2D-MoS2 nanosheets reduced to some extent compared to that
of native 2D-MoS2. This might be due to the high-temperature
pyrolysis reaction conditions used for the preparation of the
Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite material. The TEM and the
high-resolution TEM (HRTEM) images of the Ni@2D-MoS2
composite reveals the successful loading of the Ni nanocluster
with the average diameter of 18 nm on the MoS2 nanosheets,
which has the average lateral size of 106 nm (Figure 1c,d). In
the HRTEM image of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite
(Figure 1d), two diﬀerent crystal lattices have been observed
for the MoS2 and Ni nanocluster (inset). The d-spacing value
of 0.204 nm corresponds to the Ni (111) atomic plane in
nickel nanocluster, and the value of 0.276 nm is consistent to
the Mo (100) atomic plane in 2D-MoS2. This coexistence
clearly indicates the presence of the nickel nanocluster on the
2D-MoS2 nanosheets.
The SEM image of Ni@2D-MoS2 also supported that the
nickel nanocluster was eﬀectively and uniformly embedded on
the layered 2D-MoS2 (Figure S1). The energy dispersive X-ray
(EDS) elemental mapping analysis of the Ni@2D-MoS2 layers
represented the even distribution of the Ni atoms throughout
the 2D-MoS2 (Figure 1e,h). The EDS survey spectrum of the
Ni@2D-MoS2 composite also indicates the presence of the
elements including Mo, S, and Ni atoms (Figure S2). In
addition, Raman spectroscopy has been utilized for the
determination of the phase and composition of the MoS2
layer in the Ni@2D-MoS2 composites. MoS2 from the bulk
phase to the monolayer state directly inﬂuences the Raman
active E2g
1 and A1g vibration modes. While the in-plane
vibration mode of two S atoms with respect to the Mo atom
(E2g
1) showed upshifts, the other out-of-plane vibration mode
associated with the vibration of S atoms in opposite directions
(A1g) showed downshifts.54,55 Figure 1i indicates the presence
of monolayer MoS2 in the Ni@2D-MoS2 composite, where the
peak at 376.1 cm−1 for the E2g
1 mode of bulk MoS2 is upshifted
to 377.1 cm−1 and the peak at 402.6 cm−1 for the A1g mode of
bulk MoS2 is downshifted to 401.0 cm−1. We have also
performed X-ray diﬀraction analysis, which further supports
the formation of the Ni@2D-MoS2 composite. The evident
diﬀraction peaks for the (002) plane of MoS2 and the (111)
plane of the nickel nanocluster are observed in the Ni@2D-
MoS2 nanocomposite (Figure 1j).
Furthermore, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was
used to determine the valence state of the constituent elements
present in the Ni@2D-MoS2 composite. The wide spectrum in
Figure 2a clearly indicates the copresence of the Mo, C, O, S,
and Ni elements. The high-resolution XPS spectrum of Mo 3d
exhibits two characteristics peaks at 232.8 and 229.6 eV; these
were attributed to Mo 3d3/2 and 3d5/2, respectively. This
signiﬁes the predominant presence of Mo4+ in the Ni@2D-
MoS2 composite. The appearance of a small peak at around
236.2 eV due to Mo6+ indicates the presence of a small amount
of MoO3 formed from the oxidation of MoS2 (Figure 2b). In
Figure 2c, the high-resolution XPS spectrum of Ni 2p showed a
complex structure that comprised peaks at 856.0 eV for Ni

Nickel is an important magnetic material. Thus, the
magnetic property in the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite has
been incorporated by the nickel nanocluster. The magnetic
measurement of the synthesized Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite
was performed at room temperature. In Figure 3a, the
magnetization vs magnetic ﬁeld plot revealed no hysteresis
loop formation. This phenomenon of the magnetization plot
conﬁrmed the superparamagnetic nature of the composite
nanomaterial Ni@2D-MoS2.56 This result also attributed that
the size of the nickel nanoparticles is so small that they behave
as a single magnetic domain. The conﬁned individual magnetic
dipoles in the Ni nanograins are randomized by the suﬃciently
higher thermal energy at 298 K. The inset super-expanded plot
for the mass magnetic moment (M) at the very low external
magnetic ﬁeld (H) from −500 to 500 Oe represents a very
small hysteresis loop that indicates a minute ferromagnetic
property of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite. This might be
due to the presence of few agglomerated nickel nanoclusters.
In Figure 3b, in the ﬁeld-cooled (FC) mode, the magnetic
susceptibility of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite decreases
with increasing temperature. However, under zero-ﬁeld cooling
(ZFC) conditions at very low temperature, the magnetic
susceptibility increases with the increase in temperature and
showed a maximum at the Néel temperature (TN). The TN for
Ni@2D-MoS2 is obtained at 10 K. Thus, the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite exhibits superparamagnetic property along
with weak ferromagnetism, which is a characteristic of an
antiferromagnetic material.57 The blocking temperature (TB)
at the transition point for the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite
was found to be 98 K. The atomic composition of the
synthesized nanocomposite was obtained from the inductively
coupled plasma−mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) analysis and
showed the weight ratio of around 8% of the Ni nanocluster
loaded on the 2D-MoS2 nanosheet.

3.3. Selective Bacterial Binding Ability of Ni@2D-
MoS2. Based on Peng and Dullens et al. work, the Ni
nanocluster exhibits strong adhesion toward various bacterial
strains with low antimicrobial activity.50 Therefore, we expect
that Ni@2D-MoS2 will exhibit strong adhesion with bacteria
due to the Ni nanocluster and high antibacterial activity owing
to the 2D-MoS2 core material. However, to our surprise, we
observed strong binding of MRSA (Gram positive) with Ni@
2D-MoS2 compared to E. coli (Gram negative). This may be
due to a certain diﬀerence in the bacterial cell wall functionality
of Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacterial strains that
determined the mode of interactions with the materials.59 To
probe the Gram-selective bacterial binding, the Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite was treated with both the Gram-positive
bacteria MRSA and the Gram-negative bacteria E. coli. We
observed very little adhesion of E. coli with Ni@2D-MoS2 as
the turbidity of the supernatant solution remains unchanged
after magnetic separation (Figure 5a), whereas in the case of
MRSA bacteria, a transparent supernatant was obtained after
magnetic separation (Figure 5b). Moreover, the scanning
electron microscopy (SEM) image of the bounded MRSA
bacteria with the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanosheets clearly revealed the
close adherence of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanosheets on the
bacterial surface (Figure S6). The concentration-dependent
bacterial binding ability of Ni@2D-MoS2 was also established
by measuring the turbidity of the supernatant bacterial solution
after magnetic separation. Figure 5c shows that the bacterial
capturing ability of Ni@2D-MoS2 increased with an increase in
the concentration. In the case of the Gram-positive bacteria
MRSA, over 98% of bacteria were captured by the Ni@2D-
MoS2 with a concentration of 105 μg/mL, while the Gram-
negative bacteria E. coli was captured at only around 17% by
the same amount of Ni@2D-MoS2. We have extended our
study for the selective bacterial capturing ability of the Ni@
2D-MoS2 nanocomposite toward the other Gram-negative and
Gram-positive bacterial strains such as P. aeruginosa and E.
faecalis, respectively (Figure S7). E. faecalis bacteria were
eﬀectively captured (∼92%) by the same amount of Ni@2D-
MoS2 and separated from the solution. However, P. aeruginosa
bacteria were barely separated from the solution. These results
indicate that the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite can eﬀectively
interact with the Gram-positive bacteria compared to the
Gram-negative bacteria, exhibiting Gram-selective bacterial
binding ability.
3.4. Selective Antibacterial Activity of Functionalized
Ni@2D-MoS2. The remarkable bacterial binding ability of
Ni@2D-MoS2 was exploited for the antimicrobial activity
against the drug-resistant pathogenic bacteria MRSA using
ligand functionalization. We have established that positively
charged ligand-functionalized 2D-MoS2 has shown better
antibacterial activity than that of the native 2D-MoS2.60 The
quaternary ammonium group-based positively charged head
group containing the ligand not only helps the nanomaterial to
interact with the bacterial cell but also helps the nanomaterial

3.5. Mechanism of Gram-Selective Bacterial Binding.
The probable origin for the bacterial binding ability of Ni@2D-
MoS2 can be explained by considering the interaction of
bacterial cell membrane functional groups with the Ni
nanocluster present in the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite.
The Gram-positive bacterial cellular surface is markedly
diﬀerent from the surface of Gram-negative bacteria.61 The
cell wall in Gram-positive bacteria is composed of
peptidoglycan, which contains mainly teichoic acid and
lipoteichoic acid that possess a strong chelating capability
toward metal ions.62 Hence, it can easily interact with the Ni
nanocrystals. However, in the case of Gram-negative bacteria,
the major component of the outer membrane is lip-
opolysaccharide.63 Moreover, the relative compositions of
cell wall functional groups such as carboxyl, amino, and
hydroxyl groups also vary with diﬀerent bacterial strains.64−66
The comparative aﬃnity of the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite
toward these functional groups containing molecules such as
oleic acid, oleyl amine, and oleyl alcohol was characterized
using a phase transfer experiment. All these amphiphilic
molecules contain an identical apolar part (−C17H33) but
diﬀerent polar head groups: −COOH (oleic acid), −NH2
(oleylamine), and −OH (oleyl alcohol). The aﬃnity of these
molecules to the Ni@2D-MoS2 was reﬂected from the amount
of Ni@2D-MoS2 transferred from aqueous medium to
chloroform medium. Figure S9 indicates that oleylamine
showed the highest aﬃnity, followed by oleic acid and oleyl
alcohol. Thus, the Gram-selective bacterial binding ability of
the Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite is generated through the
diﬀerent extents of bacterial cellular interactions. However, the
choosing of ligand molecules with the functional groups that
can preferably interact with the lipopolysaccharide and
phosphate moieties may target selectively Gram-negative
bacteria. For example, the Wang group has reported that the
modiﬁed Au NPs with diﬀerent ratios of amine- or thiol-
tethered phenylboronic acids showed selective antibacterial
activity by binding to lipopolysaccharide (Gram-negative) or
lipoteichoic acid (Gram-positive), respectively.67 Moreover,
gold nanoparticles with tunable charge also showed Gram-
selective antibacterial activity as reported by the Grzybowski
group by tuning electrostatic interaction with the phosphate
group.68

3.7. Recyclability. The magnetically separable antimicro-
bial agents are environment-friendly. These materials can be
easily recycled using an external magnet without discharging
into the environment. This method reduces the usage of raw
materials, and it is also cost-eﬀective. After the treatment of
positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 against MRSA bacteria, the material
was separated using a magnet. Then, the supernatant
containing bacteria was cultured on an agar plate to check
the bacterial survivability. Afterward, the separated positive-
Ni@2D-MoS2 was recycled by repeated washing with PBS
buﬀer. Due to the reversible nature of bacterial adsorption on
the nanomaterial surface, the residual bacteria were separated
from the positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 nanosheets during washing.
Then, the recovered material was redispersed in the MRSA
bacterial suspension to check further antibacterial activity. This
procedure was repeated six times. After each cycle, positive-
Ni@2D-MoS2 was collected by magnetic separation, washed,
and reused. In Figure 9, the results reﬂected that even after the
completion of the sixth cycle, the bactericidal activity of the
positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite remains intact without
any signiﬁcant loss of activity.
3.8. Biocompatibility Study. The hemolysis assay was
performed to investigate the biocompatibility of the function-
alized positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite. In this assay, the
amount of hemoglobin released from the red blood cell lysis
was determined by measuring absorbance at 570 nm. The
positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 showed only a small amount of
hemolysis (∼15%) with the material concentration of 6
times higher MBC value for MRSA (Figure 10). This result
suggests good biocompatibility of the positive-Ni@2D-MoS2
nanocomposite. However, in the case of cellular toxicity study
using the HeLa cell line, the positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 showed
some extent of toxicity, ∼50%, with the treatment of 7 times
higher MBC concentration, which can be modulated with
further surface modiﬁcation (Figure S11).

4. CONCLUSIONS
Herein, we demonstrate a facile synthesis of the super-
paramagnetic nickel nanocluster-loaded 2D-MoS2 nanocom-
posite. The Ni@2D-MoS2 nanosheets showed selectivity in
bacterial adhesion ability toward the Gram-positive bacterial
strains such as MRSA and E. faecalis over the Gram-negative
bacterial strains E. coli and P. aeruginosa. Furthermore, the
antibacterial activity and colloidal stability of Ni@2D-MoS2
nanosheets were remarkably enhanced through positive thiol
ligand functionalization. The superparamagnetic property of
the positive-Ni@2D-MoS2 nanocomposite also facilitates
magnetic separation, to recycle for further application. This
study would prompt the research for the selective extraction of
microbes, water treatment, and the eﬀective strategy for
antibacterial activity.
