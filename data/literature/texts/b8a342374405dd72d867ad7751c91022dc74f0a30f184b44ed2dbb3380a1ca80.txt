A B S T R A C T
Fluorescent CdTe nanocrystals (NCs) capped with beta-cyclodextrin (β-CD) are successfully synthesized by host-guest
supramolecular assembly of the hydrophobic alkyl chains of N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) on the surface of CdTe NCs and
eco-friendly β-CD via the promising simple hydrothermal method in our experiments. The as-prepared NCs display
better stability and lower toxicity compared with traditional those only capped with NAC. Specially, cytotoxicity
experiments to human umbilical vein endothelial cells in vitro and zebrafish embryo toxicological tests in vivo are
performed to determine the toxicity of CdTe NCs. For their practical applications, the promising red-luminescent NCs
are employed as stable and low poison red phosphors to fabricate white light-emitting diodes (WLEDs) with remarkable
color-rendering index (CRI) being 91.6. This research offers significance for solving the difficulty in toxicity and in-
stability of heavy metal based NCs, which has potential applications in future optoelectronic devices and biomarkers.

1. Introduction
Cadmium (Cd) based nanocrystals (NCs) have gained special at-
tention owing to their unique attributes of broad excitation wavelength,
narrow full-width-at-half maximum (FWHM) and controllable photo-
luminescence [1–4]. The excellent properties make them suitable can-
didates for applications such as solar cells [5,6], sensors [7,8] and white
light-emitting diodes (WLEDs) [9–12]. However, the heavy metal Cd
ions contained NCs are terribly deleterious to human body health,
limiting their massive industrial applications in photoelectric devices.
One possible solution is to develop many low or non-toxic Cd-free NCs
as benign alternatives, such as CuInS2 [13,14], AgInS2 [15,16] and InP
[17,18]. But they are no match for Cd-based NCs due to their relatively
poorer fluorescent properties of wide FWHM, low quantum yield (QY)
and narrow tunable emission, which fail to meet the requirements of
the photoelectric devices. Therefore, it makes great sense to minimize
toxicity, along with keeping high-performance of Cd-based NCs.
Up to now, much effort has been developed to reduce toxicity of
CdTe NCs. For example, one solution is to design core/shell structural
NCs such as CdTe/ZnS [19], CdTe/ZnSe [20] and CdTe/ZnTe [21].
Another alternative approach is to encapsulate NCs with stable in-
organic compounds (such as SiO2 [22], Ca(OH)2 [23] and BaSO4 [24])
and organic ligands (such as glutathione [25], thioglycolic acid [26],
dithizone [27]). Although both methods have all contributed to the
decreased toxicity and improving stability of CdTe NCs to an extent,
there still exists some problems that the synthetic routes are complex
and the prepared NCs still harm on organisms. To solve it, the host-
guest supramolecular assembly is highly expected for the merits of ro-
bust coordination interaction towards heavy metal ions, biocompat-
ibility and molecular recognition. Beta-cyclodextrin (β-CD) is a pro-
mising bio-molecular host candidate due to its advantages of good
water-soluble [28], inexpensive [29], commercially available and
nontoxic natural product [30]. Its special cyclic molecular structure of
hydrophobic internal cavity and hydrophilic external surface endow it
high capacity for forming host-guest inclusion complexes with specific
organic molecules [31–33]. Currently, many researchers have applied
the grafted β-CD into the surface modification of CdTe NCs [34,35].
Zhang et al. prepared mono-6-thio-β-CD functionalized CdTe NCs with
uniform dimension and high quantum yield as detectors of nitrophenol
isomers [36]. Chen et al. prepared CdTe NCs encapsulated with 6-azido-
β-CD clicked silica via layer-by-layer modification, which were applied
in the recognition of amino acids [37]. However, the reported proce-
dures were normally costly and time-consuming owing to the fact that
β-CD was needed to be modified with functional groups (such as thiol,
azido, carboxyl groups). It is necessary to simplify the synthesitic pro-
cess for the matter of energy conservation and time-saving. Moreover,
the fluorescent stability of the resulting NCs are still short of research
and the further reduction of the toxicity of the resulting NCs is im-
perative as well.
In this work, a facile one-pot method was designed to synthesize β-
CD stabilized CdTe NCs. This method had no need to separate the in-
termediate products and the final complex products could be obtained
directly. The β-CD without any modification was used to coat NCs by
host-guest supramolecular assembly of β-CD and the hydrophobic alkyl
chains of N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC) on the surface of CdTe NCs.
Therefore, our synthetic process might be more convenient and time-
saving. The as-prepared NCs are biocompatible and their toxicity is
tesitified significantly low though cytotoxicity and vivo tests. Besides,
the β-CD loaded CdTe NCs are of excellent fluorescence and fluorescent
stability under ultraviolet light and heating conditions. Taken ad-
vantage of their excellent properties, the innocuous red-luminescent β-
CD coated NCs were applied as stable red phosphors to fabricate white
light-emitting diodes (WLEDs) with bright light, indicating their great
potential application in photovoltaic devices. This work has the fol-
lowing characteristics: (1) Host-guest supramolecular assembly with
affordable β-CD as a host molecule is exploited to facilely synthesize β-
CD coated CdTe NCs via a simple preparation process. (2) The toxicity
of the β-CD coated CdTe NCs is more than 160 times lower than tra-
ditional NAC capped CdTe NCs by cytotoxicity experiments to human
umbilical vein endothelial cells in vitro and zebrafish embryo tox-
icological tests in vivo. (3) The ultraviolet and thermal stability of β-CD
coated CdTe NCs are also greatly enhanced comparing with control
sample of NAC capped CdTe NCs. (4) The red-luminescent β-CD coated
CdTe NCs of low toxicity are first applied as superb red phosphors in
fabrication of WLEDs with high color-rendering index (CRI) of 91.6.
This method offers a path route to facilely prepare biocompatible
fluorescent NCs by the host-guest supramolecular assembly, which
might have great potentials in optoelectronic devices.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials
Cadmium chloride (CdCl2·2.5H2O), elemental tellurium powder
(Te), N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC), beta-cyclodextrin (β-CD), sodium bor-
ohydride (NaBH4), sodium hydroxide (NaOH), dimethyl sulfoxide
(DMSO) were purchased from Aldrich and used as received. Reagent-
grade absolute ethanol was supplied by Sinopharm Chemical Reagent
Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and used as purchased without further
purification. High-purity water with the resistivity of greater than 18 M
Ωcm−1 was used in the experiments. Dulbecco’s modified eagle
medium (DMEM) was obtained from hyclone. Fetal bovine serum (FBS)
was brought from Gibco, Grand Island, NY. Penicillin, streptomycin,
and
3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium
bromide
(MTT) were brought from Sigma Chemical Company.
2.2. Preparation of fresh oxygen-free NaHTe solution
Generally, 6.0 mmol (0.23 g) NaBH4 was transferred to a small flask,
and then 2.0 mL ultrapure water was added. After 1.5 mmol (0.19 g) Te
powders were added, the reaction flask was cooled by ice. During the
reaction, a small outlet connected to the flask was kept open to dis-
charge the pressure caused by the resulting hydrogen. After several
hours, the black Te powders disappeared and white sodium tetraborate
precipitate appeared on the bottom of the flask instead.
2.3. Synthesis of CdTe NCs capped with β-CD
Firstly,
CdCl2·2.5H2O
(0.46 g,
2.0 mmol)
and
NAC
(0.49 g,
3.0 mmol) were dissolved in ultrapure water (150 mL) and the vigor-
ously stirred for 15 min. Then, β-CD (3.40 g, 3.0 mmol) was added and
the pH value of the mixture solution was approximately adjusted to
11.0. After that, such mixed solution was degassed with N2 for 30 min,
heated at 95 °C and the fresh oxygen-free NaHTe was rapidly added
with stirring in sequence. Finally, the different fluorescent colloidal
CdTe NCs capped with β-CD ligands were obtained through varying
reaction time. After cooling to a room temperature, an excess amount of
the absolute ethanol was added into the reacted solution and the mix-
ture was centrifuged at 550 rpm for 30 min. The NCs solid powers were
achieved by repeating those steps over a few times and drying at 45 °C.
2.4. Synthesis of CdTe NCs capped with NAC ligands
The synthetic procedure of CdTe NCs capped with NAC ligands are
same as above, except for no β-CD added.
2.5. Stability test of CdTe NCs
Under strong UV light irradiation (365 nm, ∼1 W), the PL intensities
of NAC capped CdTe and β-CD coated CdTe NCs powders were detected
at different time, respectively. In addition, their PL intensity variations
after heat treatment at 150 °C for 1 h were investigated respectively.

2.6. Toxicity tests of CdTe NCs to HUVECs
Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) were obtained
from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and grown in high
glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented
with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin.
Cells were seeded in a 96-well plate with a density of 5 × 104 cells/mL
and grown for approximately 16 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere
of 5% CO2. After culture medium was removed, cells were cultured
with DMEM (containing 2% FBS) in the presence of NAC capped CdTe
NCs or β-CD coated CdTe NCs at a concentration gradient of
0.005–8.0 mg/mL for 24 h, respectively. Then, cells were incubated
with MTT (final concentration of 0.5 mg/mL) for 4 h at 37 °C.
Afterwards, the media was carefully removed and 150 μL DMSO was
added to each well. The amount of viable cells in each well was de-
termined by measuring the absorbance of solubilized formazan at
490 nm with a microplate reader (Tecan Safire, Crailsheim, Germany).
Media only treated cells served as the indicator of 100% cell viability.
For each treatment, eight replicate wells were performed.
2.7. Toxicity tests of CdTe NCs to zebrafish embryos
Zebrafish embryos were exposed to the as-fabricated NAC capped
CdTe NCs or β-CD coated CdTe NCs aqueous solution with graded
concentrations. The tests were conducted in a 96-well plate with a
single embryo per well at 28 °C. Static method was adopted to record
the death rate of zebrafish after 24 h. Five parallel experiments were
performed and the corresponding experimental data were statistically
calculated.
2.8. Preparation of WLEDs devices
The as-prepared β-CD coated CdTe NCs powders with red fluores-
cence were directly used as red nanophosphors to fabricate WLEDs. In a
typical process, blue LED chips (∼460 nm) were fixed on the LED bases
and the gold threads were made to connect the LED chips with the
power supply. Afterward, the silicones mixed with CdTe NCs and
YAG:Ce yellow phosphors (NCs/phosphors = 1/5.5 (wt/wt)) were va-
cuumed to remove the bubbles formed during mixing. Then, the mix-
tures were loaded on the LED chips and heated at 150 °C for 1 h.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. Synthesis of CdTe NCs
The high-quality fluorescent CdTe NCs coated with β-CD were
prepared by a simple and efficient one-pot process. Separation and
purification of intermediate products were not required in the synthetic
process. Besides, the inexpensive and green β-CD was directly used to
coat NCs, where the modification of β-CD with functional groups (such
as carboxyl groups) is not needed. Thus, our synthetic process is greatly
simplified compared with references [36–38]. The NCs with different
fluorescence colors from green to red were obtained by changing re-
action time from 1 to 5 h (Fig. 1(a)). Then, the role of the β-CD was
preliminarily investigated by exploring the effect of the β-CD con-
centrations on the performances of CdTe NCs. As shown in Fig. 1(b),
with the increased amount of β-CD, the PL intensity of CdTe NCs gra-
dually increases firstly and descends eventually. When the mole weight
of β-CD is equal to that of NAC, the PL intensity reaches a maximum,
which is possibly attributed to the host-guest supramolecular assembly
of β-CD and the alkyl chains of NAC on the surface of CdTe NCs.

According to the thorough characterizations above, the possible
mechanism for formation of CdTe NCs capped with β-CD is proposed.
Primarily, the sulfhydryl groups of NAC strongly coordinate with cad-
mium (II), resulting in the stable existence of NAC capped Cd precursor.
Then, the eco-friendly β-CD is added to modify Cd surface. β-CD is a
promising bio-molecular host candidate due to its special cyclic mole-
cular structure of hydrophobic internal cavity and hydrophilic external
surface endow it high capacity for forming host-guest inclusion com-
plexes with hydrophobic organic guest molecules in aqueous solution
[31–33]. Therefore, the Cd surfaces are coated with β-CD by supra-
molecular host-guest interaction between β-CD and hydrophobic alkyl
chain of NAC. Finally, the Te precursors react with β-CD coated Cd
precursors. The as-prepared CdTe NCs possess a special structure that
CdTe cores are surrounded with a great many β-CD molecules similar to
a shell, which may lessen the surface imperfection and improve the
stability of NCs.
3.3. Stability analysis of CdTe NCs
The photo-stability of light conversion materials is essential for
application in photoelectric devices such as WLEDs. Herein, the stabi-
lity of β-CD capped CdTe NCs powders with red fluorescence under UV
and heating conditions was evaluated by analyzing their PL spectra.
The fluorescence emission peak of the used CdTe NCs powers coated
with β-CD is observed at 648 nm shown in Fig. 5(a) and the variations
of PL intensities for NCs powders under strong UV light irradiation
(365 nm, ∼1 W) are exhibited in Fig. 5(b). The PL intensity of NAC
capped CdTe NCs powders falls drastically to only 50% of the initial
intensity after 3 h and their fluorescence completely disappears at 5 h.
However, with the load of β-CD on the surface of CdTe NCs, the
emission intensity for the NCs powders still keeps at about 50% of their
initial intensity after about 9 h. The results suggest that the photo-sta-
bility of CdTe NCs under strong UV light irradiation is raised by about 3
times with the addition of β-CD.

3.4. Toxicity assessment of CdTe NCs
High toxicity of Cd based NCs is another critical issue for their
practical applications [44–46]. Their toxicity mainly derives from the
release of the Cd2+ ions from NCs particles, which are poisonous to
vertebrate systems at relatively low concentrations. One possible solu-
tion is to employ NCs coated with biomolecules or polymers. Herein,
the toxicity of the NCs was evaluated by zebrafish embryo toxicological
tests in vivo. Fig. 6(a, b) show the survival rates of zebrafish embryos
versus the concentrations of CdTe NCs coated with NAC and β-CD,
respectively. The median lethal concentration (LC50) value of NAC
capped CdTe NCs is about 18.2 μg/mL. Commonly, the modification of
CdTe NCs with organic ligands lowers their toxicity to a certain extent,
and their LC50 value are about tens of micrograms per milliliter
[38,47]. To further reduce the toxicity of NCs, eco-friendly β-CD was
added as a shell by supramolecular host-guest interaction between β-CD
and hydrophobic alkyl chain of NAC. the LC50 value of the as-prepared
β-CD coated CdTe NCs is increased to more than 3000 μg/mL. It could
be ascribed that CdTe NCs wrapped with β-CD efficiently lower the
toxicity of CdTe NCs by decreasing the release of the toxic Cd 2+ ions
from NCs as well as the exposure of heavy metals to living organisms.
The toxicity of the NCs was further investigated by cytotoxicity
experiments to human umbilical vein endothelial cells in vitro. Fig. 6(c,
d) demonstrate the cytotoxicity of the β-CD coated CdTe NCs and NAC
capped CdTe NCs with different concentrations by MTT viability assay.
The LC50 of the CdTe NCs only capped with NAC is 16 μg/mL after 24 h
of incubation. Although the incorporation of NAC ligands obviously
increases the LC50 of CdTe NCs (approximately 0.04 μg/mL) [48], NAC
capped CdTe NCs are still virulent. As to β-CD coated CdTe NCs, the
viability of human umbilical vein endothelial cells is plummeting along
with the decreased NCs concentration. However, a dramatic increase of
the cell viability appears by further increasing the concentration of β-
CD coated CdTe NCs from 1 mg/mL. Particularly, as the NCs con-
centration is more than 4 mg/mL, the cell viability exceeds 50% again,
which may be explained by that β-CD as a cyclic oligosaccharide made
up of seven glucopyranose units through α-1,4-glucosidic bond linkages
has the ability of promoting cell proliferation. Therefore, the obtained
β-CD coated CdTe NCs are excellent low-toxic fluorescent materials for
further applications.

4. Conclusions
In summary, the red-luminescent β-CD capped CdTe NCs were
successfully synthesized by supramolecular host-guest interaction be-
tween β-CD and NAC ligands via a simple one-pot method. The as-
prepared NCs have excellent advantages at low toxicity and great
photo-stability under heating and UV irradiation. It is worth mentioned
that the toxicity β-CD coated CdTe NCs is reduced by 160 times com-
pared with that of NAC capped CdTe NCs. Furthermore, the pure white
light emitted WLED with remarkable color-rendering index of 91.6 was
practically fabricated based on red-luminescent β-CD capped CdTe NCs
as novel red phosphors. In this way, this supramolecular assembly
method may constitute a promising way to synthesize high-quality
fluorescent material with low toxicity and great stability for future
photovoltaic devices and biomarker applications.
